EL26-70-000 · 2026-08-07 · Organization of MISO States, Inc.

Answer of the Organization of MISO States, Inc. in Support of Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.'s et al. 08/03/2026 Motion to Hold Proceeding in Abeyance

The Organization of MISO States answers in support of the MISO abeyance motion, on the condition that the stakeholder process and later filings fully address the issues in the E-8 order.

Asks FERC to: Grant the abeyance, without narrowing the scope of FERC's inquiry or prejudging whether MISO's filings resolve each issue.

Answers the order. Supports the MISO abeyance request that postponed the E-8 show cause deadline, while asking that cost-shift and transparency directives get full treatment in MISO's coming filings.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6)

Key points

  1. OMS supports abeyance because the issues are interconnected and suited to a stakeholder process followed by section 205 filings.
    the issues identified by the Commission’s Show Cause Order are significant, highly interconnected, and best addressed through a coordinated stakeholder process PDF p. 1
  2. OMS conditions its support on full treatment of the Commission's issues and no prejudgment of resolution.
    Granting abeyance should not narrow the scope of the Commission’s inquiry or prejudge whether any particular issue has been adequately resolved PDF p. 2
  3. OMS lists the issues it wants addressed, including cost transparency, cost shifting if load does not materialize, flexible service and alternative transmission technologies.
    addressing the potential for inappropriate cost shifting when anticipated load does not ultimately materialize or materializes at a different scale or schedule than expected PDF p. 4
  4. OMS says MISO's stakeholder workstreams so far have favored issues supported by parties interested in developing and serving large loads.
    workstreams prioritized by MISO and stakeholders to date have been limited to those issues supported by parties largely interested in developing and serving large loads. PDF p. 4
