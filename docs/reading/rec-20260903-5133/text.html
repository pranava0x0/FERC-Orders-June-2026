COMMENTS OF THE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION OF WEST VIRGINIA

The Public Service Commission of West Virginia (“PSCWV”) respectfully files these comments to address concerns regarding PJM’s August 13, 2026, filing to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) — a service to be implemented by PJM solely during periods when there are insufficient resources to reliably meet demand in real time operations. PJM explains that “IRAS will enable PJM to prioritize load reduction in zones/areas where New Large Loads have not brought their own new capacity or had their capacity needs covered through the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”). New Large Loads can avoid this condition on taking transmission service by bringing new capacity to the system.”

Thus, the only New Large Load (NLL) that will be at risk of prioritized or first-in-line curtailment under the new PJM proposal are those that have failed to cover their own capacity requirement (“Uncovered NLL”).1  Although the IRAS is targeted to Uncovered NLL, PJM attempts to navigate the maze between clear Federal jurisdiction over interstate transmission, implicit Federal jurisdiction over retail transactions that might affect wholesale electricity markets, and clear state jurisdiction over retail service and  rates.  PJM states that the IRAS “has been carefully designed to respect the cooperative federalism approach established under the FPA and animating several other recent landmark Commission determinations.”  PJM supports its filing to involve the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission”) in retail service describing

The PSCWV does not fully support PJM’s structure to address unusual and historic capacity needs created by growth in NLL, mostly associated with the growth of data centers, however, it does agree with the goals enumerated in the Administration’s Ratepayer Protection Pledge and similar ratepayer protections plans put forth by the Governors of the thirteen PJM States.  PJM refers to the Pledge and ratepayer protection in its filing and appears to understand the need to protect non-NLL customers (“Other Customers”) from costs associated with all NLL generally and  Uncovered NLL particularly.  Yet, its filing contains a provision to provide a wholesale rate credit to effectively pay Uncovered NLL for mandatory curtailments that are applicable to NLL only because they elected to add Uncovered Load.  It is that credit that the PSCWV opposes and requests that the Commission reject.

The concept of compensation through any rate to be paid based on uncovered NLL that is interrupted is, if given to the Uncovered NLL,  the antithesis of everything that is intended to be achieved by the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.  PJM admits in its filing that: “A rate of zero dollars for IRAS would apply where Eligible New Large Loads “waive”2 in their retail rates receipt of compensation for the provision of wholesale Interim Resource Adequacy Service—an outcome that would be consistent with the Ratepayer Protection Pledge signatories’ commitment ‘to protect American consumers from price hikes due to data center energy and infrastructure requirements, and lower electricity costs for consumers in the long term.’”  Yet, by offering a rate, or

compensation, to Uncovered NLL for IRAS the PJM tariff establishes an unnecessary potential conflict at the retail level.

If the Uncovered NLL receives the compensation, a cost is created that would fall on Other Customers.  Because that “cost” is solely due to payments to the Uncovered NLL, the PJM tariff, if approved, creates a cost that would not exist, but for the Uncovered NLL.  The only choice that States would have to prevent  rate increase on Other Customers is to prohibit the compensation from going to the Uncovered NLL and use it to offset the cost when setting rates for Other Customers.

PJM defends its complicated and unnecessary payment to Uncovered NLL by assuming altruism of the Uncovered NLL.  PJM explains: “PJM’s proposal also allows Eligible New Large Loads to agree upon a retail rate between 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate and zero dollars, to further facilitate implementation of the Ratepayer Protection Pledge and retail level arrangements.”  What happens if the Eligible New Large Load does not agree to a lower rate or zero?  If the Commission approves the PJM proposal it will set up a potential conflict if an Eligible New Large Load does not “agree” to waive its Commission-approved wholesale compensation for interpretability and a State retail regulatory authority denies it that compensation, which it would have to do to protect other Customers. This regulatory free-for-all could be further complicated in multi-state PJM Zones where the states have conflicting interests and policies for passing through the PJM IRAS compensation to Uncovered NLL or Other Ratepayers under their individual jurisdiction.

The complexity, pitfalls, and potential for adding costs on Other Customers created by the PJM proposal can be simply resolved by rejecting the IRAS compensation.  It is unjust and unreasonable to pay Uncovered NLL for a capacity deficiency-driven curtailed when it could have

CONCLUSION
The State of West Virginia, the other states in PJM and the White House have similar public policies that NLL, mostly data centers, must not come at the expense of residential or commercial utility customers. The PSCWV fully supports the West Virginia State policy that, consistent with the national Ratepayer Protection Pledge, developers will build, bring, or procure sufficient power resources and assume responsibility for infrastructure investments necessary to serve their projects. This approach reinforces the State’s commitment to ensuring that utility costs associated with large-scale data center development are not shifted to West Virginia ratepayers while promoting a stronger, more resilient electric grid.  While we believe there could be improvements to the PJM proposal to manage Uncovered NLL, it generally  furthers the goal of protecting Other Customers, except for the IRAS compensation to Uncovered NLL.  While West Virginia as a fully retail regulated state with vertically integrated electric utilities has regulatory tools to set retail rates for Uncovered NLL that protect Other Ratepayers, those tools could be challenged if the Commission approves the PJM wholesale compensation for interruption of Uncovered NLL and those loads do not “voluntarily” turn down receiving that compensation.  The PSCWV would have to order that the payments that were specifically calculated based on the Uncovered NLL not be assigned and paid to that load in order to protect Other Customers.  The federal/state tension that such action would set up is problematic, both administratively and legally. That tension is easily avoided if the Commission rejects that portion of the PJM tariff, which it should because the payments are not only unnecessary, they are unjust and unreasonable.
