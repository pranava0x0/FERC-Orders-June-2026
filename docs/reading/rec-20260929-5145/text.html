II. ANSWER

United's answer addresses three distinct flaws in how PJM’s Answer treats Peak Shaving Adjustments (“PSA”) under IRAS, each independent of the others. First, PJM’s rejection of PSAs as a verifiable tool to reduce resource adequacy need rests on a distinction PJM does not apply consistently elsewhere in its own Answer. Second, PJM defends against a broader proposal to treat PSA as a substitute for large loads supporting new capacity through Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”), rather than the narrower one United actually requested, which was that PSAs be taken into account when calculating IRAS exposure. Third, PJM treats PSA and BYONC as equivalent rather than recognizing that they address the same capacity gap from different sides and thus crediting one does not require relaxing requirements for the other. A. PJM’s Rejection of PSA Credit to Displace IRAS Need Rests on an Internal Inconsistency in PJM’s Own Answer

PJM’s Answer rejects crediting PSA against a New Large Load’s IRAS exposure on the ground that PSA amounts are “adjustments to PJM’s load forecasts, not resources that may participate in RPM Auctions,” and are not a “real-time dispatchable product.”7 PJM reasons that this distinction settles the question: because PSA does not clear an auction and cannot be called upon in real time, it categorically cannot reduce the capacity need giving rise to IRAS curtailment.

PJM’s own Answer, however, shows that this distinction does not actually settle anything. In the same filing, PJM confirms that it already permits qualifying PSA MW “that qualify for an opt-out to potentially reduce the identified 2027/2028 Delivery Year capacity shortfall for which the RBP will procure.”8 That is, PJM already treats a PSA value, the identical kind of forecast adjustment PJM now calls too unreliable to be taken into account under IRAS, as sound enough to shrink the actual, binding quantity of capacity PJM goes out and procures through the companion Reliability Backstop Procurement mechanism.9 Just as a PSA value serves to reduce how much capacity PJM is targeting to procure under the RBP, it should be factored into how much of a load’s demand counts as unmet capacity need under IRAS. PJM offers no explanation for why the same PSA value should be treated as germane in one proceeding and irrelevant in the other. Both the RBP procurement target and the IRAS

B. PJM’s “Not Dispatchable” Rationale Answers a Request United Did Not
Make

PJM’s Answer builds its rejection of PSA credit around PSA’s unsuitability as a substitute for BYONC, a tradable, must-offer capacity resource that PJM’s Tariff requires to satisfy specific capacity market obligations.10 On that framing, PSA fails because it cannot be dispatched, cannot clear an auction, and carries none of the ongoing capacity-market obligations BYONC carries.

PJM is drawing an unnecessary parallel between BYONC and PSA, and responding to a request United never made. United’s comments did not ask PJM to treat PSA as BYONC, to permit PSA to participate in RPM Auctions, or to make PSA dispatchable during an IRAS Action. United asked only that PJM’s Tariff “specify that the deduction under this formula for a qualifying Peak Shaving Adjustment or comparable state-authorized reduction shall equal the MW value calculated and verified by PJM pursuant to its applicable procedures.”11 That is a

The distinction matters because PJM’s own Eligible New Large Load Amount

It is a forecast-based determination, built from registration data, load forecasts, and capacity commitments, that fixes a load’s exposure before any Interim Resource Adequacy Alert is ever called.12 A qualifying PSA reduces the underlying capacity need that calculation is meant to measure before any such Alert occurs, in exactly the same way BYONC and Allocated RBP UCAP MW do. United respectfully requests that the Commission consider the argument in our original filing, which was that the IRAS exposure calculation should take into account a verified reduction in resource-adequacy need resulting from PSA, rather than PJM’s mischaracterization of that request as an ask to treat PSA as a dispatchable capacity resource.

C. PSA and BYONC Are Complementary, Not Competing, Categories
PJM’s Answer proceeds as though recognizing PSA within the Eligible New Large Load Amount formula would require treating PSA as a substitute for BYONC, competing for the same role BYONC already fills. It would not. BYONC and PSA address the same underlying problem, i.e., the gap between a New Large Load’s demand and the capacity available to serve it, from two different directions, and nothing in United’s request asks PJM to blur that distinction.

BYONC works on the supply side: it adds new capacity to the system to match or offset a load’s demand. PSA works on the demand side: it reduces the capacity PJM must otherwise procure, or the amount of curtailment that may be necessary. A load that brings 50 MW of BYONC and a load that supports the reduction of the zonal or EDC peak by 50 MW through a qualifying PSA arrive at an identical outcome, from PJM’s perspective: the load’s otherwise- unmet capacity need shrinks by the same amount. The mechanism differs, but the resource- adequacy benefit does not, and thus large loads should be given the same credit. PJM itself recognized PSA and BYONC as different sides of the same coin in its Transmittal Letter when it excluded any locations from “new” BYONC status that had previously helped “reduce the amount of capacity needed to meet the PJM Region Reliability Requirement through a Peak Shaving Adjustment or Price Responsive Demand.”13

Recognizing the verified benefit of PSA does not require PJM to loosen any of the requirements applicable to BYONC, which would remain the only pathway to recognize a New Large Load’s contribution to bringing actual new capacity to the system, subject to must-offer requirements, capacity market participation, and the ten-year commitment discussed above.14 PSA would remain what it already is under PJM’s own rules, a verified reduction to the forecast peak demand from which a load’s capacity need is calculated.15 Because PJM already relies on PSA as a demand-side decrement in the RPM today, alongside other demand-side measures such as Price Responsive Demand (“PRD”),16 it is not a speculative or unverified figure. The success and impact of PJM’s IRAS proposal depends on getting that net figure right. The purpose of the Eligible New Large Load Amount formula is to identify how much of a load’s demand remains genuinely uncovered.18 A formula that credits new supply but ignores a verified reduction in peak demand does not measure that figure accurately, it systematically overstates it for any load that has taken real, verified steps to reduce the regional shortfall. PJM should be directed to correct the formula so that it reflects both halves of a New Large Load’s actual net capacity need. Failure to do so will result in a harmful over-estimation of IRAS need while diminishing the incentive for New Large Loads to pursue PSA as a mechanism to affordably and quickly reduce pressure on the strained PJM grid.
