sufficient but in fact what is just and reasonable and consistent with the public interest. And the Commission has found that alternative configurations indeed meet this standard. The Transmission Owners fail to acknowledge the Commission’s finding that Talen’s decision to transition to front- of-meter “in fact, might support” a showing “that there is a lack of sufficient clarity related to Co- Location Arrangements writ large,”7 while at the same time seeking to perpetuate continued regulatory uncertainty through multiple rehearing requests recycling the same arguments. The Transmission Owners also renew their challenge to the Commission’s denial of PJM’s unauthorized replacement of “Point of Interconnection” with “Point of Change in Ownership” in the definition of Co-Located Load.8 The Commission should reject this argument, as it did PJM’s, for failure to timely raise it on rehearing of the December 2025 Co-Location Order.9 As the Commission observed recently, “an attack on a judgment in a proceeding other than a direct appeal” is a collateral attack and “is generally prohibited.”10

Looking forward, the Commission should grant Constellation’s Request for Rehearing and implement three reforms to expedite interconnection: (1) establish study deadlines binding both PJM and Transmission Owners, (2) require PJM to spell out its methodology and process for completing Necessary Studies in its Tariff, and (3) require all Transmission Owner standards, requirements, guidance, and practices related to necessary control technologies and/or protection
