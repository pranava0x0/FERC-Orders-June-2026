I. 
BACKGROUND 
On July 31, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) submitted revisions to the PJM 
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among 
Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”).3

Constellation joins the chorus of voices raising concerns about the proposed LSE credit

Importantly, the approximately $10.2 billion collateral estimate captures only collateral 
posted by LSEs to PJM. It does not capture the additional credit support that LSEs are almost 
certain to require from the large-load customers whose obligations they serve. In other words, if 
an LSE is required to post significant collateral to PJM as a result of its large load retail customer, 
the LSE is likely to then require its large load retail customer to provide the same or similar 
collateral to the LSE, more or less doubling the total collateral required for an RBP customer.  
Given the magnitude of the collateral PJM proposes to require LSEs to post, this “double 
collateral” problem will strain liquidity and add significantly to already-significant financing and 
transaction costs.

1. 
PJM’s Fixed $1.5 Million/MW Rate Is Based on Contract Notional, 
Not Measured Credit Exposure 
PJM has a legitimate interest in protecting the RBP against reasonably foreseeable 
nonpayment. But the amount of collateral imposed on LSEs must bear a reasoned relationship to 
the exposure being secured. PJM has not made that showing for its fixed $1.5 million/MW rate, 
and it is not “reasonably tailored to the actual credit risk presented”14 nor “derived from measured 
exposure.”15 
PJM derives the proposed rate from approximately one-half of the gross notional value of 
a hypothetical fifteen-year RBP commitment priced at $555/MW-day. PJM also performs a 
discounted-cash-flow calculation using the capacity-market weighted average cost of capital. 
Those calculations explain how PJM arrived at the $1.5 million figure. They do not demonstrate

The methodology has five material gaps. First, it assumes that every committed RBP 
megawatt is priced at the $555/MW-day maximum willingness to pay, although accepted RBP 
prices may be lower and may vary by resource and Delivery Year. Second, it uses fifteen years of 
gross contract value rather than the net RBP Charges that LSEs actually will be billed, should RBP

charges to be secured, the time needed to draw collateral and reallocate any residual default, or the 
stress assumptions used to estimate net exposure. Fourth, it applies the same $1.5 million/MW 
base rate without explaining why credit quality may increase—but never reduce—the base 
requirement. Finally, although PJM may reduce the rate over time, the proposed Tariff establishes 
no mandatory recalculation schedule, prescribed inputs, or objective collateral-release standard. 
PJM’s proposed default rule further demonstrates the disconnect. Proposed Tariff section 
18.9(a) provides that an unpaid RBP Charge will be reallocated to the remaining LSEs responsible 
for RBP Charges and that the defaulting LSE’s RBP Collateral may be applied as an offset. PJM 
does not explain why the exposure arising before collateral can be drawn and any residual default 
reallocated should be measured by one-half of a fifteen-year gross payment stream.

2. 
Large Load Customers Should Be Permitted to Post Collateral for the 
Account of the Responsible LSE  
An excessive collateral requirement can create the very liquidity risk it is intended to 
mitigate. Under the filed design, an LSE must furnish RBP Collateral to PJM or PJMSettlement 
and may then prudently obtain corresponding support from the large-load customer associated with 
the obligation. That structure can encumber two balance sheets and impose two layers of financing 
and transaction costs for the same payment obligation. 
The Tariff therefore should permit the applicable large load customer, or an affiliate acting 
on its behalf, to post RBP Collateral directly with PJM or PJMSettlement for the account of the 
responsible LSE. The LSE should remain fully responsible for payment of RBP Charges; PJM or 
PJMSettlement should have direct and unconditional draw rights; and the customer-posted amount 
should satisfy the LSE’s collateral requirement dollar-for-dollar. The same collateral should not 
be credited against more than one obligation, and disputes between the customer and the LSE 
should not impair PJM’s draw rights. 
This option would not reduce PJM’s credit protection or change the entity responsible for 
paying RBP Charges. It would add the option for another entity18 to furnish the collateral and 
thereby avoid unnecessary intermediation through the LSE and duplicative credit postings. In 
addition to the advantages described above, allowing the large load to post collateral will facilitate 
a seamless transition if the customer changes its LSE, the applicable RBP Obligation changes, or

3. 
The Commission Can Preserve the RBP Procurement Schedule While 
Resolving the LSE Collateral Provisions in an Expedited Paper 
Hearing  
The procurement is urgent; the future LSE collateral methodology is not. RBP Sellers must 
satisfy the seller-side credit requirements in connection with their RBP Sell Offers. By contrast, 
the first LSE collateral requirement will not arise, as currently proposed, until forty-five days 
before the 2028/2029 Delivery Year. The Commission therefore can accept the RBP solicitation, 
selection process, commitments, and seller-side credit provisions while severing or deferring its 
decision on only proposed Tariff section 18.8(b). That disposition would not displace 
Constellation’s separate request in its Limited Protest that the initial RBP Obligation allocations 
be determined 365 days before the 2028/2029 Delivery Year.20 
The present record is sufficient to conclude that PJM has not supported the fixed $1.5 
million/MW rate. The record is not sufficiently developed, however, to prescribe a durable 
replacement methodology.21  The replacement must be conformed to the RBP settlement structure 
accepted by the Commission and supported by evidence addressing the relevant exposure horizon, 
stress assumptions, credit-quality treatment, customer-posted collateral, collateral already held by 
PJM, switching and transfer mechanics, and mandatory recalculation and release standards.

III. 
CONCLUSION 
In accordance with PJM’s willingness to accept changes under NRG Power Marketing,23 
the Commission should (i) accept the RBP subject to the modifications Constellation discussed in 
its Limited Protest in this proceeding, with the additional clarification discussed above permitting 
Energy Resources seeking new capacity rights to satisfy the criteria for resources eligible to supply 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW, and (ii) sever the LSE credit issue discussed herein for further 
proceedings and refer that issue to an expedited paper hearing.
