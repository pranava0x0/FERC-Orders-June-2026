A. PJM’s Peak-Shaving Opt-Out Recognizes and Gives Effect to Efforts By
States To Establish Peak-Shaving Adjustment Programs.
Policymakers and regulators across the country are working diligently within their respective jurisdictions to address unprecedented energy demand, striving to preserve the reliability and affordability of energy for everyday Americans. Coordinating these overlapping efforts will amplify their effectiveness, to the benefit of ratepayers.  PJM’s peak-shaving opt-out does precisely this by appropriately recognizing state-authorized peak-shaving programs, in a manner consistent with ordinary-course capacity-procurement auctions in PJM. Two such state- authorized peak-shaving programs are emblematic of this opportunity.

In Virginia, Va. Code Ann. § 56-596.7 establishes a framework for “voluntary demand flexibility program[s]” under which large loads may partner with third parties to peak-shave in order to maximize or reasonably maximize “electric grid system savings and improve reliability for retail electric service customers.”7 The law expressly states that under such programs, large loads may “secure measurable and verifiable electric load reductions from other retail electric service customers during time periods of peak system demand or other events that cause strain on the electric grid,”8 directly authorizing large loads to contract with, fund, or purchase peak-shaving measures from third parties under the program.

Similarly, New Jersey codified its peak-shaving programs in the DCFSA, which Governor Sherrill signed into law on July 7, 2026.9  The DCFSA establishes a demand-reduction program for “data center customers [to] contract directly with a third party for, or otherwise fund, verified

B. Base Requests PJM Make Two Narrow Clarifications on the RBP Proposal.
To ensure PJM’s peak-shaving opt-out captures these benefits, Base requests that PJM make two discrete clarifications to its proposal.

1. Clarification that the Peak-Shaving Decrement is Not Discounted
First, Base asks PJM to clarify that the peak-shaving amount submitted by the Electric Distributor is not discounted.  Section 18.1(d) provides that the Initial RBP Target “shall be further decremented . . . by up to the associated MW quantity of load that participates in a peak shaving adjustment program.”14 By contrast, the transmittal letter says that “the Initial RBP Target will be decremented by the amount specified by the Electric Distributor,”15 which implies a 1:1 decrement with no discount.  Likewise, the Tariff’s own structure supports a reading that no discount was intended.  The preceding sentence of section 18.1(d) requires that an Electric Distributor’s opt-out request “shall specify the associated MW quantity of load in the zone/area that will be opted-out” (emphasis added).

Clarification on the value of the opt-out decrement is needed to allow commercial arrangements to be finalized.  Otherwise, Base and other providers that seek to facilitate peak- shaving cannot determine what decrement a given amount of peak-shaving will produce. And without that certainty, peak shaving arrangements with large load counterparties cannot be priced or executed.  This clarification can be easily effectuated by removing the phrase “up to” in proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, Section 18.1(d), as follows: “shall be further decremented . . .

2. Clarification that the Deadline to Meet Attachment DD-2’s Requirements

will be September 2027

Second, Base seeks clarification from PJM that, to qualify for the peak-shaving opt-out in the RBP, the deadline for any Peak Shaving Adjustment plan submitted pursuant to the requirements of Attachment DD-2 will be September 2027 (to be effective for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year), not September 2026.  This is a narrow clarification necessary to align the RBP opt-out with the procedural deadlines already established in Attachment DD-2.

Section 18.1(d) conditions the peak-shaving opt-out on programs “that are codified in state law and that also meet the requirements of Tariff, Attachment DD-2.”17  The challenge is that Attachment DD-2 was designed for a different purpose on a different schedule,18 and several of its requirements cannot be satisfied in time for the opening of the RBP bid window if not clarified.
