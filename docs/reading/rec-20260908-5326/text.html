II. PJM Fails to Specify the Process for Interconnection Customers with Existing or Ongoing Necessary Studies

PJM fails to address the Commission’s finding that “PJM’s proposed revisions lack clarity with respect to how they apply to Interconnection Customers that have already executed Necessary Study Agreements.”19 As the June 2026 Co-Location Order found, the “scope” of the Necessary Studies “has not changed.”20 And PJM has not “otherwise demonstrate[d]” why “Interconnection Customers seeking to serve Co-Located Load cannot rely on the results of Necessary Studies that have already been performed if the scope of the study has not changed.”21

The Commission should specifically order PJM to clarify that Necessary Studies need not be repeated where their scope is the same as previously conducted Necessary Studies. The Necessary Studies process can take years.22 Requiring duplicative studies without justification would unnecessarily delay the interconnection of large loads and cannot be just and reasonable.

CLARIFICATION
I. PJM’s Comment That It Can Provide “Partial NITS” Requires Further Explanation On compliance, PJM states that “[a]t present, PJM can provide partial NITS, i.e., NITS in an amount less than requested and/or for a term shorter than requested, to Co-Located Load through the Necessary Study process and therefore is proposing an October 17, 2026 effective date for the bulk of the revisions proposed in this filing.”23 The Commission should require PJM to clarify several aspects of this proposal.

First, PJM should clarify what “partial NITS” is, whether it is a new or existing transmission service, and which revisions are included in “the bulk.”

Second, PJM should clarify its reference to the Necessary Study process, as it is unclear how that process applies to PJM’s provision of “partial NITS” or Interim NITS. As the Commission explained, “the studies applicable to a transmission service request … are distinct from the Necessary Study process, which studies the modification of a generator interconnection.”24 In addition to explaining what it means, PJM also should clarify that the Necessary Study process can proceed at the same time as the study process for NITS and Interim NITS conducted by PJM. This is particularly appropriate when the co-location arrangement subject to the Necessary Study is one that will use NITS and Interim NITS services, which will not require delisting of the generator. This in turn will make it much less likely that the results would identify additions, modifications, or replacements to the Transmission System necessary

Considerations
