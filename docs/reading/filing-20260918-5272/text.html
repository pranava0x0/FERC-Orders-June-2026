II. 
COMMENTS 
A. 
Midwest TDUs seek confirmation that the proposed requirements 
apply only to those Large Loads directly connected to the MISO 
Transmission System.  
MISO should confirm that its proposed requirements apply only to those Large 
Loads directly connected to the MISO Transmission System. To the extent MISO’s 
proposed Tariff language does not clearly impose this limit, the Commission should 
direct MISO to revise its proposed Tariff language to do so. 
MISO’s filing appears to impose the proposed Large Loads Interconnection 
Reliability Requirements on only those Large Loads that are interconnected to the MISO 
Transmission System, and there is no indication that MISO intends to extend these 
requirements to any loads interconnected to distribution facilities. There is no mention of 
distribution-level interconnection in MISO’s transmittal letter, proposed Tariff revisions, 
or supporting testimony.

For example, while direct implementation of the proposed Large Load

directly meet MISO’s proposed Large Load requirements (including the additional 
requirements applicable to Computational Loads) would impose a disproportionate 
financial and administrative burden. Smaller utilities are unable to spread the significant 
costs and personnel needed to perform these specialized tasks in a rapidly evolving 
terrain. Thus, requiring small Transmission Customers to directly perform the primary 
role in complying with the proposed Large Load reliability requirements would impose a 
particularly heavy and undue burden on municipal and consumer-owned electric systems 
and their often rural customers.

Second, MISO should clarify the full scope of potential consequences for non-
compliance with these new requirements, particularly for Transmission Customers that 
have entered into agreements with Large Loads to effectuate compliance. Proposed 
section 37A(I) states that “[f]ailure to comply with the requirements established in this 
Section 37A may result in a delayed Service Commencement Date, a period of time 
needed for corrective action, operational limitations, or other remediation action(s) by 
Transmission Provider as may be necessary to maintain Transmission System 
reliability.”30 It is appropriate for the consequences for non-compliance to be tailored to 
address and prevent the reliability risks posed by Large Loads. Indeed, that should be the 
guiding principle for implementing these requirements. In contrast, it would be 
inappropriate to impose additional consequences or other unduly punitive penalties (such 
as financial penalties) on the Transmission Customer if these requirements are not met by 
the Large Load assigned responsibility for complying with these requirements under an 
agreement.  
Accordingly, MISO should clarify that the scope of “other remedial action(s)” is 
limited to actions that address the reliability impacts caused by Large Loads assigned 
compliance responsibility under their agreement with the Transmission Customer (rather 
than punitive measures against the Transmission Customer). Thus, the remedial actions 
should be targeted to measures that impact the Large Load that is in the best position to 
effectuate compliance. In addition, MISO should confirm that this language establishes 
the exclusive list of consequences for a Transmission Customer’s failure to comply with

the Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads where the Transmission

Third, where a Transmission Customer demonstrates its ability to effectuate

Loads themselves to ensure that they fully understand these new requirements. Although 
MISO’s proposed Tariff language makes the Transmission Customer responsible for 
compliance with these requirements, MISO cannot ignore that it is the actions taken by 
the Large Loads themselves that are the source of the reliability risks that need to be 
addressed. MISO has developed these new requirements to protect the MISO 
Transmission System against the impact of new Large Load interconnections, and MISO 
should play a central role in making sure that Large Loads fully comprehend these 
requirements and are well positioned to act consistently with them.

It would not be just and reasonable for MISO to avoid any direct interactions with 
these Large Loads and instead solely rely on Transmission Customers to communicate

details getting lost in translation, undermining the reliability protection these 
requirements are meant to provide. The support MISO provides Transmission Customers 
and other stakeholders—such as service representatives, the MISO Help Center, and 
Customer and System Operator Training—should also be made directly available to 
Large Loads to facilitate compliance with these requirements, as Large Loads are best 
positioned to address reliability risks upon receipt of MISO’s guidance.

ATTACHMENT A: DESCRIPTION OF MIDWEST TDUS 
Central Minnesota Municipal Power Agency d/b/a Central Municipal Power 
Agency/Services (“CMPAS”) is a public power joint action agency organized under 
Minnesota law. CMPAS operates as a project-oriented agency. CMPAS and certain of its 
members own and operate transmission assets which have been turned over to the 
functional control of MISO. 
The Indiana Municipal Power Agency (“IMPA”) is a body corporate and 
politic, and a political subdivision of the State of Indiana.  IMPA was created in 1980, 
under the provisions of Indiana Code § 8-1-2.2, by a group of municipalities for the 
purpose of jointly financing, developing, owning, and operating electric generation and 
transmission facilities which are appropriate to the current and projected electric power 
needs of such municipalities.  IMPA became an operating utility in 1983, when it began 
to sell electric power to its member utilities under power sales contracts.  IMPA’s 
members currently comprise 60 Indiana cities and towns and one Ohio Village. 
IMPA is a member of Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (MISO).  
IMPA has ownership interests in generation resources within MISO, including co-
ownership with Duke Energy Indiana and Wabash Valley Power Alliance in the Gibson 
Station Unit 5, ownership interest in the Prairie State Energy Campus, ownership interest 
in Trimble County Units 1 and 2, and ownership of natural gas combustion turbine 
peaking units.  IMPA, Duke Energy Indiana, and the Wabash Valley Power Alliance 
have entered into a joint ownership agreement of the Joint Transmission System (JTS) in
