Motion to Intervene and Comments of Central Municipal Power Agency/Services et al. re Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.'s 08/28/2026 filing

Four Midwest municipal and cooperative power agencies intervene and comment on MISO's large-load reliability requirements, seeking two clarifications: the rules apply only to transmission-connected loads, and they do not unduly burden Transmission Customers.

Asks FERC to: Have MISO clarify scope and customer burden, or direct it by deficiency letter and Tariff revisions to do so.

Answers the order. Comments on MISO's first filing after the E-8 order. They invoke the order's suggested large-load definition, which refers to interconnection at the transmission system, to argue the scope should be stated explicitly.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. The Midwest TDUs support swift MISO action and ask for two clarifications.
    Midwest TDUs therefore seek clarification on two issues PDF p. 2
  2. First, the requirements should reach only loads on the MISO Transmission System, not distribution-connected loads.
    apply to those Large Loads interconnected to the MISO Transmission System (and not to loads interconnected at to the distribution system). PDF p. 2
  3. Second, Transmission Customers should be able to rely on agreements with Large Loads to show compliance without undue burden.
    But additional clarity is needed to ensure that Transmission Customers can rely on these agreements to avoid undue burdens. PDF p. 2
  4. They ask FERC to order revisions through a deficiency letter if MISO does not clarify on its own.
    To the extent that MISO does not clarify these issues on its own, the Commission should direct it to do so in a deficiency letter. PDF p. 2
  5. They say the word "behind" in MISO's Large Load definition could be read to reach loads behind a distribution transformer.
    the term “behind” could potentially be read broadly to encompass anything behind such Point(s) of Withdrawal PDF p. 7
