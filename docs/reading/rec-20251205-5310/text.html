ANSWER OF PJM INTERCONNECTION, L.L.C. TO
MOTION FOR EXPEDITED ACTION OF CONSTELLATION
ENERGY GENERATION, LLC

Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission” or “FERC”), 18 C.F.R. § 385.213, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) submits this answer to Constellation Energy Generation, LLC’s (“Constellation”) November 21, 2025, Motion for Expedited Action (“Motion”).1

The Motion recognizes that the subject matter of the captioned proceedings overlaps the issues in the recently-initiated, industry-wide ANOPR2 proceeding.3  The Motion nonetheless seeks a “full order on the merits” applicable solely in the PJM Region, “even while the Commission develops a comprehensive rule in response to the ANOPR”

applicable nationwide.4  The Motion seeks relief prior to any Commission determinations prompted by the ANOPR on foundational issues, including jurisdictional boundaries.  The Motion invites the Commission to run afoul of the principle that agencies should defer taking action in specific adjudicatory matters where such action would supplant ongoing generic rulemakings.5  The Commission should not take the bait.

If that was not enough, PJM has been engaged with its stakeholders—including Constellation—in a Critical Issue Fast Path (“CIFP”) process to assist PJM’s Board of Managers in determining how to address this important, yet extremely complex, issue.  The ANOPR clearly states that it was “not intended in any way to discourage public utilities from making filings to address these and similar issues under FPA section 205.”6

By seeking an expedited order on the merits in these proceedings, the Motion requests relief that not only pre-empts the ANOPR docket but also the CIFP process and any section 205 filings the ANOPR goes out of its way to avoid discouraging.  The Motion attempts to lock in its movant’s desired outcome while foreclosing possible solutions under consideration by PJM’s Board of Mangers as developed in the expedited CIFP process. PJM encourages clear Commission guidance on these important issues, but not in the Motion’s requested fashion.

Should the Commission feel inclined to address the issues raised by Constellation in its Motion through action on the Section 206 filing, the Commission should be cognizant that a set of rules or guidelines that are applicable only to the PJM market will not create a rational, durable, and predictable pathway for the national issues associated with large load interconnection. A “PJM-only” solution will only encourage data centers to shop for locations based not on the underlying values of a particular site but instead on exploiting regulatory arbitrage created by inconsistent regulatory treatment across regions.  Such an approach is not consistent with the President’s Executive Order which focuses on the need for development of AI infrastructure as a national, not regional, imperative. ANSWER

PJM’s CIFP process concerning the integration of large loads has been ongoing since September, culminating in presentations before PJM’s Board of Managers on November 19, 2025.  Various stakeholders or stakeholder groups presented a total of twelve proposal packages to the Board. Among those groups were the “Joint Stakeholders,” a group including Constellation (Constellation included the Joint Stakeholders’ package as an exhibit to its comments on the ANOPR).7  The Board is now considering the stakeholders’ various proposals and will soon determine and announce how it plans to proceed.

In addition, initial comments in the ANOPR process initiated by the Commission at the request of the Secretary of Energy were filed on November 21, 2025.  As Constellation acknowledges, the ANOPR process involves issues identical to those at issue

Rather than await the results of the ANOPR processes, without regard for City of Anaheim v. FERC, and indifferent to the ongoing deliberative CIFP process, the Motion asks the Commission to make a merits decision that would implement its movant’s preferred outcomes in these consolidated dockets immediately.  The Motion’s requested relief includes a proposed finding that PJM’s Tariff is unjust and unreasonable and an accompanying order establishing a “full replacement rate.”8  While the components of the replacement rate are not specified in the Motion, the Constellation Comments in the ANOPR proceedings request the Commission to adopt certain options that PJM proposed in its Answer to the Commission’s order to show cause in these dockets.9  Specifically, the movant seeks adoption of PJM options 2,10 4,11 5,12 and 713 “as clarified and modified by

But participation alone should not translate into pre-emption of orderly regulatory processes and collaborative, industry-wide efforts to inform the PJM Board of Managers’ action.
