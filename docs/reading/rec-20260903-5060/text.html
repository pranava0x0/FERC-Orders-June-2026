COMMENTS OF THE SOLAR ENERGY INDUSTRIES ASSOCIATION

Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (Commission),1 the Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA) submits these comments in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (PJM) filing to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS).2  IRAS is a new transmission service that will be implemented solely during periods when there are insufficient resources to reliably meet demand in real time operations.3 When triggered, IRAS will enable PJM to prioritize load reduction in zones where New Large Loads that have not brought their own new capacity or have their capacity needs met through the Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP).4 New Large Loads may avoid IRAS by bringing new capacity to the system through the Bring Your Own New Capacity (BYONC) construct.5 BYONC would allow New Large Loads that bring new capacity to the PJM Region to remove themselves—in whole or in part—from IRAS.6

A. Surplus Resources: Barriers to BYONC Participation and Solutions
As explained by SEIA, Advanced Energy United, and Google in the Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP) filing, in Docket No. ER26-3380, existing PJM rules limit the ability for Surplus Resources to add capacity to the PJM system.18 Currently, PJM only grants capacity interconnection rights (CIRs) and capacity accreditation to surplus resources that merge with the existing facility as a single hybrid resource,19 a developer seeking capacity value through surplus interconnection must elect hybrid treatment—which potentially requires unwinding existing financial arrangements. The alternative—interconnecting independently as a co-located resource—preserves independent financing but limits the surplus resource to energy-only market participation, forfeiting capacity value entirely. Because hybrid treatment requires combined market participation as a single resource,20 it does not allow for component-level settlement, and capacity performance penalty risk cannot be isolated from the host resource. Lenders to the existing facility will rarely consent to the addition of a surplus resource under these conditions, because it exposes the existing project to performance liability arising from a resource it does not own or control. This effectively renders the surplus interconnection pathway commercially unavailable for many projects.

In the RBP proceeding, SEIA and Advanced Energy United each make recommendations
on how to resolve these concerns and make the surplus interconnection pathway commercially

First, PJM should enable surplus resources to obtain independently recognized and bankable capacity positions—distinct from the host resource— without requiring the wholesale restructuring of existing commercial arrangements. This could be achieved through several mechanisms, including: (i) split UCAP settlement between component accounts; or (ii) CIR transfer mechanisms between co-located resources.22 Whichever mechanism is adopted, the objective should be to provide each component of a surplus arrangement with independent settlement, revenue allocation, and penalty isolation necessary to support separate financing.

Second, PJM should establish clear protections ensuring that a default or performance issue arising from the surplus resource cannot create cross-default risk or otherwise interfere with the existing resource’s interconnection agreement or operations. This protection is essential to obtaining lender consent: Absent an explicit structural separation of liability, lenders to the existing facility will not approve the addition of a surplus resource that could jeopardize the project’s existing financing and operational arrangements.

II. CONCLUSION
The IRAS proposal is another necessary response to PJM’s growing resource adequacy issues. But there is much to do to ensure the program is successful. These comments do not propose an alternative to what PJM filed. These are recommendations that will allow New Large Loads to leverage what is already on the grid, or what can be added quickly, so that PJM can unlock the full potential of its proposal. Accordingly, SEIA requests that the Commission and PJM consider the market refinements identified in these comments.
