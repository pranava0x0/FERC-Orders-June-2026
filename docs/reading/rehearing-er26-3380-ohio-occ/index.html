ER26-3380-001 · 2026-10-29 · Ohio Consumers' Counsel

Request for Rehearing to Protect Consumers from Excessive Transmission Rates by Office of the Ohio Consumers' Counsel

The Office of the Ohio Consumers' Counsel asks for rehearing of FERC's September 29, 2026 order accepting PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement, challenging the $555/MW-day offer cap, pay-as-bid clearing, the 20% shortage penalty and cost allocation to consumers rather than data center loads.

Asks FERC to: Direct PJM to set a lower offer cap, adopt single-price clearing, replace the 20% shortage penalty, and design an RBP that allocates costs to the large loads causing the need.

Parallel proceeding. Rehearing in PJM's backstop procurement docket ER26-3380, the capacity-adequacy track running beside E-7's request that PJM report how it will ensure generation for large loads.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. OCC says the accepted RBP design does not match concurrences by Commissioners LaCerte and See that new load, not existing customers, should bear its costs.
    Existing customers should not be left paying costs attributable to new demand. PDF p. 4
  2. The order accepted a one-time procurement of 6,831 MW and postponed the auction five months to February 28, 2027.
    it postponed that auction for five months, until February 28, 2027. PDF p. 5
  3. OCC lists the paper hearing topics FERC set: zonal cost allocation, muni and co-op opt-outs, TO exit fees and LSE collateral.
    set these issues for further paper hearings and additional comments PDF p. 6
  4. OCC says the $555/MW-day cap and pay-as-bid clearing lack record support and do not mitigate seller market power.
    There is no evidence in the record to support this offer cap, and there is no evidence to support the pay-as-bid clearing mechanism design. PDF p. 6
  5. It says the cap is more than $200/MW-day above the collared cap in the 2028/2029 base residual auction.
    more than $200/MW-day higher than the current collared price cap applied in the 2028/2029 RPM base residual auction. PDF p. 12
  6. OCC says the 20% shortage penalty is far below RPM's non-performance penalty and shortfall charges are limited to three years against a 15-year commitment.
    FERC accepted a proposal to limit the proposed shortage penalty to only 1/5th of the capacity sell offer PDF p. 18
  7. It says costs should fall on data centers and FERC ignored a claimed $23.1 billion cost shift from the past three auctions.
    data center demand increased capacity costs to other PJM customers by approximately $23.1 billion PDF p. 23
  8. OCC's prayer asks FERC to lower the cap, adopt single clearing price, reject the 20% penalty and allocate RBP costs to cost causers.
    directing PJM to set a lower market seller offer cap for the RBP auction consistent with protests filed by OCC and others on this issue PDF p. 25

PDF p. 22

FirstEnergy and NOVEC ask FERC to reject the procurement and replace it.

FERC should have rejected the RBP auction proposal and directed PJM to meet again with stakeholders to consider a model that would provide appropriate allocations of costs PDF p. 22

PDF p. 15

Pay-as-bid without market power screens lets sellers bid the cap, not cost.

There is nothing in that structure that will prevent sellers from just bidding at the $555/MW-day offer cap regardless of the sellers’ actual marginal costs. PDF p. 15

PDF p. 15

FERC abandoned its single-clearing-price precedent for capacity auctions without a reasoned explanation.

FERC also failed to provide a reasoned explanation for its departure from its long-held policy of requiring a single clearing price auction design for capacity markets. PDF p. 15

PDF p. 13

The $555/MW-day cap is borrowed from the BRA with no evidence it fits.

The fact that the accepted auction design will pay each seller whose offer clears the auction its offer bid price for a period of up to 15 years only encourages rather than discourages offers above competitive levels. PDF p. 13

PDF p. 19

Shortfall charges end at commercial operation, leaving customers unprotected for the rest of 15 years.

FERC never addressed the IMM’s concerns that there is nothing to protect consumers from shortfall in capacity that could occur in years four through fifteen. PDF p. 19

Shortfall charges end at commercial operation, leaving customers unprotected for the rest of 15 years.

FERC did not explain why the shortage penalty, set at 20% of the price bid by the seller times the cleared capacity, was appropriate. PDF p. 18

OCC: PJM's wording suggests consumers, not sellers, may end up paying the shortfall charges.

FERC also ignores that under PJM’s proposal, it is consumers who may pay the shortage penalties. PDF p. 19

PDF p. 21

OCC: RBP costs should fall on the data centers that caused the shortfall, not other consumers.

FERC never addressed OCC’s main concern that the RBP auction model is inappropriate, unjust and unreasonable because it does not target procurement and allocation PDF p. 21

OCC: RBP costs should fall on the data centers that caused the shortfall, not other consumers.
