Motion to Intervene and Comments of CPV Power Holdings, LP 
Pursuant to Rules 212 and 214 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC” 
or “Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 CPV Power Holdings, LP (“CPV”) moves 
to intervene and comment on the July 31, 2026 filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) in 
the above-captioned proceeding.2 In the RBP Proposal, PJM proposes revisions to the PJM Open 
Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load 
Serving Entities to implement the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) framework—a 
one-time mechanism for procuring new capacity to address PJM’s resource adequacy shortfalls.   
I. 
SUMMARY 
CPV supports the RBP Proposal and believes it is a critical step toward restoring resource 
adequacy in the PJM region. PJM is facing a significant capacity shortfall as observed by the 
2028/29 Base Residual Auction, which fell 6,831 MW short of the Reliability Requirement. 
Load forecasts indicate that this shortfall is expected to grow considerably over the next 5–10 
years, primarily driven by large load additions and data center growth. The RBP is a necessary 
and timely response to this growing resource adequacy challenge.  
CPV participated throughout the months-long Critical Issues Fast Path (“CIFP”) 
proceeding that culminated in the RBP Proposal and appreciates PJM’s efforts to develop a 
necessary and timely response to the resource adequacy shortfalls facing the PJM region. The

RBP Proposal combines feedback from the diverse group of parties that participated in the CIFP

A confluence of conditions in recent years, including interconnection queue delays, 
significant increases in equipment and development costs, supply chain constraints, and extended 
construction durations, has made bringing new generation resources online increasingly difficult. 
Despite these conditions, companies including CPV have continued to develop new generation 
projects, many of which are in the late stages of development and have the potential to enter 
construction in the near term. Absent immediate action to facilitate the financing and 
construction of these late-stage projects, it is unlikely that new generation will be built at the 
scale necessary to keep pace with expected load growth. The RBP Proposal will use gating 
criteria to evaluate sponsor credentials and development progress and determine which are most 
likely to enter service, while distinguishing between advanced and more speculative projects. 
The selection process will then select projects in least-cost order, subject to prioritization based 
on expected in-service dates,3 and provide them with the long-term commitments needed in 
today’s environment to enable them to reach financial close, commence construction, and 
ultimately reach commercial operations.

II. 
MOTION TO INTERVENE  
CPV and its affiliates develop, own, and operate electric generating facilities that 
participate in the PJM markets. PJM’s RBP Proposal and associated Tariff changes will directly 
and substantially affect CPV and its affiliates. Accordingly, CPV has a direct and substantial 
interest in the outcome of this proceeding that no other party can adequately represent. CPV’s 
participation is in the public interest, and it respectfully requests that the Commission grant this 
timely filed motion to intervene. 
III. 
COMMENTS  
Although CPV generally supports the RBP Proposal, it respectfully submits the following 
comments addressing one discrete issue: PJM’s proposed site control requirement applicable to

Under the RBP Proposal, a seller must satisfy certain gating criteria to submit a valid 
RBP Sell Offer for RBP UCAP MW.5 Among those criteria, PJM proposes to require a seller to 
provide evidence of 100% site control in Attachment DD § 18.5(f)(i)(2) of its proposed Tariff 
changes: 
Evidence of 100% Site Control for each of the Generating Facility (including the 
location of the main step-up transformer), the Interconnection Facilities, and the 
Interconnection Switchyard (as defined in the PJM Manuals) for a term of at least 
three years from the date of submission of the Application.6 
 
As discussed below, CPV has identified two related aspects of this provision that warrant 
clarification to avoid unnecessarily narrowing the pool of eligible sellers and 
undermining the effectiveness of the RBP. 
The proposed section 18.5(f)(i)(2) requires a seller to demonstrate site control for a term 
of at least three years from the date of submission of the “Application.” The Tariff’s existing 
definition of Application, however, has no connection to the RBP.7 Notably, PJM does not use 
the term “Application” in the RBP Proposal to describe a seller’s submission; instead, PJM 
consistently uses “RBP Sell Offer” to describe the mechanism by which sellers offer to sell RBP 
Resources. This strongly indicates that PJM intended to reference the date of submission of the 
RBP Sell Offer.8  
Second, as drafted, the proposed section 18.5(f)(i)(2) does not appear to account for 
sellers with generation projects currently active in PJM’s interconnection queue that have already

satisfied the applicable site control requirements under PJM’s existing Tariff. PJM’s existing 
interconnection procedures require generation project developers to demonstrate site control as a 
condition of advancing through the queue.9 Requiring such developers to separately satisfy a new 
three-year requirement on limited notice could impose an  unnecessary additional burden on 
sellers that have already demonstrated to PJM’s satisfaction that they possess the requisite site 
control for their generating facility and related infrastructure under PJM’s existing generator 
interconnection procedures, and risks disqualifying advanced projects from the RBP. 
For example, projects in Transition Cycle #1 and Transition Cycle #2, which have 
reached advanced stages of the interconnection process , have already demonstrated site control 
as a prerequisite to their continued advancement through the queue. Yet under section 
18.5(f)(i)(2) as currently proposed, these projects could inadvertently be excluded from 
participation in the RBP based solely on the discrepancies between the two sets of site control 
requirements. Such an outcome would unnecessarily limit the pool of eligible resources, contrary 
to the RBP’s stated objective of selecting resources “most likely to go into service and provide 
needed capacity.”10 Based on this objective, and the extensive discussions during the CIFP 
proceeding, CPV does not believe that this was PJM’s intent with the site control gating 
criteria.11

To remedy these concerns, CPV respectfully requests that the Commission direct PJM to 
submit revisions to section 18.5(f)(i)(2) in a compliance filing,12 as follows: 
For projects without an active queue position, eEvidence of 100% Site Control 
for each of the Generating Facility (including the location of the main step-up 
transformer), the Interconnection Facilities, and the Interconnection Switchyard 
(as defined in the PJM Manuals) for a term of at least three years from the date of 
submission of the Application RBP Sell Offer. 
This limited revision would preserve PJM’s site control requirement for projects without 
active queue positions while recognizing that projects already in the interconnection queue are 
subject to existing site control requirements under the Tariff. The revision would therefore 
clarify the requirement by using terminology applicable to the RBP Proposal and avoid 
unintentionally excluding advanced projects without altering the overall design of the RBP 
Proposal.
