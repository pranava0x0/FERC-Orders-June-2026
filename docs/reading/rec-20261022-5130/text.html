Pursuant to Rules 212 and 214 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or the “Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Northern Indiana Public Service Company LLC (“NIPSCO”), a subsidiary of NiSource Inc., moves for leave to intervene in this proceeding and submits these Comments in support of the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc’s (“MISO”) filing in the above-captioned docket to establish a Large Load Addition Resource Study (“LARS”) process.2  MISO’s LARS Proposal is designed to be a permanent study process that optimizes the MISO generator and load interconnection processes by providing expedited review of the requested addition of 200 MW or greater loads together with new generation resources that are electrically proximate to those loads.  The LARS Proposal is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory.  Therefore, the Commission should approve MISO’s filing, effective December 2, 2026, as requested by MISO.

II. MOTION TO INTERVENE
A. Description of NIPSCO
NIPSCO is an Indiana limited liability company headquartered in Merrillville, Indiana, and is a subsidiary of NiSource Inc.  NIPSCO serves more than 877,800 natural gas customers and 492,700 electric customers across Northern Indiana. NIPSCO owns and operates approximately 3,880 megawatts of electric generating capacity and 2,981 miles of electric transmission lines. NIPSCO is an electric load-serving entity and a transmission-owning member of MISO. NIPSCO has transferred functional control of its transmission system to MISO, which serves as NIPSCO’s Reliability Coordinator.

B. Grounds for Intervention
NIPSCO is a transmission-owning member of MISO and owns and operates generation facilities in the MISO footprint. NIPSCO also serves loads across Northern Indiana and expects to serve the type of loads that are at issue in the LARS Proposal.  NIPSCO therefore has a direct and

III. BACKGROUND
Recent projections of electricity demand growth and the Commission’s recent Show Cause Order related to large load interconnections underscore the need for MISO to develop a process for studying large loads and the generation intended to serve them.4  Specifically, MISO’s 2026 Reliability Imperative Report found that data centers, manufacturing expansion, and electrification will drive annual load growth by 1% to 2% through 2044. To address long-term resource adequacy issues, the fast connection of new resource additions is paramount for supporting large load growth and ensuring reliability as reflected in MISO’s 2026 Planning Resource Auction Results. The Department of Energy’s (“DOE”) Speed to Power Initiative, likewise reflects the need to accelerate the development of generation and transmission infrastructure to serve growing load. In the Show Cause Order issued by the Commission on June 18, 2026, the Commission identified potential gaps in the MISO Tariff concerning study procedures and services for the interconnection of generation with electrically proximate large loads.5 The Commission directed MISO to provide

For its part, NIPSCO, like many other electric utilities in MISO, is facing an unprecedented level of interest from customers that desire to locate in NIPSCO’s electric service territory. While historically, a new customer may inquire about adding a few MW of service and an inquiry for 10- 20 MW was considered a large load addition, new inquiries are now seeking to establish hundreds or in some cases thousands of MW of new load. These new customers are also emphasizing the desire for reliable and stable service to begin as soon as possible and the ability to interconnect quickly. New generation in the MISO footprint is urgently needed in order to reliably accommodate this growth, and accordingly new processes designed to expedite and integrate the study process for the interconnection of large loads and their associated new generation are critical. The LARS Proposal addresses these resource adequacy concerns and the need to streamline the MISO generator and load study process. Under the LARS Proposal, MISO would provide an expedited pathway for studying qualifying large loads together with associated new generation resources. While LARS Interconnection Customers will remain responsible for meeting the requirements of MISO’s standard Generator Interconnection Queue, generation and load studies will be conducted together under a single application.7 Also, Large Load Generator Interconnection Agreements would be executed in 120 days.8 Leveraging the successes of MISO’s existing Expedited Project Review process for new electric transmission projects (“EPS”), and Expedited Resource Addition Study process for new generation facilities (“ERAS”), this LARS

Importantly, the LARS Proposal contains several requirements that allow MISO to study the new load impacts with greater certainty and reduce the risks of speculative generation or load. After receiving the request, the transmission owner must meet with the interconnection customer and any impacted parties.11 These pre-submission coordination activities are designed to increase the efficiency of MISO’s review by having all of the necessary details agreed upon prior to filing the LARS Application.

A. The LARS Proposal is Necessary to Address MISO’s Short-Term Needs
MISO’s existing interconnection process as set forth in Attachment X of MISO’s Tariff (the Definitive Planning Phase (“DPP”) process) alone is insufficient to bridge the corresponding needs of new generation and large expected load growth throughout the MISO footprint. While the DPP process is well-suited to address NIPSCO’s interconnection needs for normal load growth, the scale and timing of large load interconnections present a different challenge. NIPSCO and other MISO Market Participants are working with potential large load customers, such as data centers, that have unprecedented service needs on short timeframes. NIPSCO, for example, is considering requests to add between 3,000-8,000 MW primarily to serve megaload customers.18  The DPP process was simply not designed to process the needs for both the interconnection of new large load additions and the interconnection of new generation to supply energy to those loads in an organized, coordinated and comprehensive fashion.

Large loads present operational and reliability challenges and therefore require detailed studies that enable affected parties to plan for reliability challenges. The LARS Proposal would allow MISO to evaluate the associated load and generation using aligned assumptions and to identify the upgrades required to accommodate both, through a single process. B. The LARS Proposal is a Just and Reasonable Solution
As MISO outlines in its filing, the LARS Proposal is tailored to meet a specific need identified by the Commission.19  It is self-limiting in that it is designed solely to study generation/load pairs and maintains several guardrails in order to protect the DPP process and DPP interconnection customers. MISO developed the LARS Proposal to ensure that the process works in tandem with the DPP process and existing interconnection customers are not adversely impacted. For instance, the LARS Proposal is designed to keep the transmission capacity available for LARS applications separate from the transmission capacity headroom available throughout the DPP cycle.20

Further, LARS Applicants must commit to a substantially higher financial commitment including a non-refundable $250,000 application fee. This payment will be used to fund the additional tools, staffing, and process enhancement MISO needs to support its existing process and LARS.21

Finally, LARS applications cannot be modified after submission and prior to the execution of the Large Load Generator Interconnection Agreement, ensuring that LARS Applicants avoid sudden changes causing delays and withdrawals.22 The LARS Proposal is a just and reasonable solution to MISO’s current resource adequacy dilemma.
