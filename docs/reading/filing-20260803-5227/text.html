I. MOTION FOR ABEYANCE
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, thereby initiating a proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act.  The Show Cause Order found that the Open Access Transmission Tariff of the California Independent System Operator Corporation (“CAISO”) and/or the Transmission Owner (“TO”) Tariffs of each of the CAISO Participating TOs appear to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential to the extent that they do not include provisions establishing the rates, terms, and conditions for the interconnection of and service to certain large and co-located loads.4  The Show Cause Order therefore directed the CAISO and its Participating TOs to either explain how their respective tariffs remain just and reasonable without specific provisions for the interconnection of large and co-located loads or to develop such provisions.5

As explained in the Motion to Rescind, each of the Six Cities is a non-public utility that is exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”).6  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission acknowledged that some of the

Participating TOs in the CAISO are not subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction and explained that it would “rescind the show cause order” against such entities at their request, provided that the CAISO “respond[s] to this order regarding the non-public utility’s CAISO Tariff provisions on file with the Commission.”7  Accordingly, the Motion to Rescind requested that the Commission rescind the Show Cause Order against each of the Six Cities.  The Motion to Rescind, which is supported by the CAISO, is pending at this time.

Because the Commission has yet to act on the Motion to Rescind, the Six Cities conditionally request a 90-day abeyance of the 60-day deadline to respond to the Show Cause Order.  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission explained that it would consider requests “to hold, for up to 90 days, all or certain aspects of this FPA section 206 proceeding in abeyance, including the deadline to respond to this order.”8  The purpose of the abeyance, according to the Commission, is to allow sufficient time for the CAISO and Participating TOs to develop FPA section 205 filings proposing revisions to their respective TO Tariffs to address the issues in the Show Cause Order.9

Good cause exists for the Commission to grant the Six Cities’ conditional request for abeyance.  As an initial matter, this abeyance request will be moot in the event the Commission grants the Motion to Rescind.  Recission is appropriate because each of the Six Cities is a non- public utility exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant to Section 201(f) of the FPA.10

If the Motion to Rescind is not granted, the Six Cities will need an additional 90 days to prepare responses to the Show Cause Order, develop potential revisions to each of their TO Tariffs to address any modifications that are determined to be needed, and coordinate both their responses to the Show Cause Order and proposed tariff revisions with the CAISO (which has advised that it intends to conduct a stakeholder proceeding to develop responsive policies and tariff changes) and the other Participating TOs.  Moreover, the Six Cities’ facilities and Entitlements in the CAISO generally represent either shares of jointly-owned facilities with, in some cases, entities that are not CAISO Participating TOs and/or are also not Commission- jurisdictional, or contractual rights to transmission service on other parties’ systems governed by those parties’ tariffs and/or bilateral agreements.11  Therefore, the Six Cities may potentially need to coordinate their responses to the Show Cause Order with these parties as well.  Finally, each City will need to evaluate whether proposed revisions to their TO Tariffs to address the Show Cause Order require review and approval by the City’s Public Utility Board or the City Council (or both) and obtain any necessary authorizations from those regulatory authorities. Given that the issues raised in the Show Cause Order are both important and novel, it is critical that the Cities have adequate time to fully consider and prepare responsive filings if the Show Cause Order is not rescinded.  For these reasons and in light of the circumstances described above, the Six Cities conditionally seek a 90-day abeyance of the 60-day response deadline if and to the extent that the Commission does not grant the Six Cities’ pending Motion to Rescind.
