ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23 · Bloom Energy

Limited Protest of Bloom Energy Corporation

Bloom Energy files a limited protest of MISO's LARS filing. It supports LARS and a December 2, 2026 effective date but objects to the 200 MW minimum load size and asks FERC to align it with MISO's 50 MW Large Load definition.

Asks FERC to: Accept LARS effective December 2, 2026 and direct a 60-day compliance filing that either aligns eligibility with the 50 MW Large Load definition or supplies evidence for the 200 MW minimum.

Answers the order. Protests one eligibility element of MISO's LARS response to the E-8 show cause order, arguing the 200 MW floor departs from the order's 50 MW large load definition.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. Bloom supports LARS and its December 2 effective date, and protests only the 200 MW minimum.
    Bloom protests one element only: the 200 MW minimum load size. PDF p. 1
  2. Bloom says MISO lowered the threshold from 250 MW to 200 MW without new analysis.
    MISO presents no EPR results or other grid impact study for 200 MW, and none for loads below it. PDF p. 2
  3. The 200 MW floor departs from the 50 MW definition in the show cause order and in MISO's pending IRR filing.
    The Show Cause Order identified a reasonable large load definition as a single-site commercial or industrial customer with peak load of 50 MW or greater. PDF p. 2
  4. Bloom argues paired loads below 200 MW are similarly situated and that the ten-pair cap already focuses each study cycle.
    And the ten-pair cap, not a size threshold, already preserves the focus of each study cycle. PDF p. 3
  5. Bloom says the right screen is connection to the Transmission System, so LARS should cover loads of 50 MW or more.
    LARS should therefore apply to loads of 50 MW or more that connect to the Transmission System. PDF p. 4
  6. If the minimum falls, Bloom asks FERC to conform the 200 MW generation-side minimum in Section 3.12.2.
    it should direct a conforming change to Section 3.12.2, which separately requires at least 200 MW of Network Resource Interconnection Service at each Point of Interconnection. PDF p. 5
