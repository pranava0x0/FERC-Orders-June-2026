II. ARGUMENT
A. BTMG Parties and PJM TOs Agree That Workable and Broadly Construed
Grandfathering Provisions Are Necessary to Avoid Substantial Harm due to Reasonable Reliance on Retail Behind-the-Meter-Generation (BTMG) Rules.

The PJM TOs support PJM’s 50 MW threshold but advocate that “this threshold be paired with grandfathering provisions that appropriately recognize that industrial and commercial customers have relied on Retail BTMG rules and that eliminating the ability to continue relying on those provisions could cause them substantial financial harm.”3 While the BTMG Parties contest PJM TOs’ mischaracterization of the BTMG Parties’ protest, they agree with PJM TOs on a critical point: the limits on BTMG arrangements must be paired with workable grandfathering for projects that “would have been eligible for Retail BTMG treatment” but for imposition of the materiality threshold and “have made substantial progress toward completion” in order to protect the reasonable investment-backed expectations of PJM customers (and the BTMG developers) and to ensure just and reasonable rates.

1. PJM TOs Mischaracterize BTMG Parties’ Position on the Materiality
Threshold.
As an initial matter, PJM TOs do not accurately state the position of the BTMG Parties. PJM TOs claim that “[s]everal commentors argue that a nameplate-based threshold is inappropriate and urge the Commission to adopt a materially higher threshold, such as 200 MW.”4 PJM TOs do not address the substance of the BTMG Parties’ argument: BTMG Parties protested PJM’s proposed netting threshold because it did not comply with the directive in the Commission’s order directing PJM’s compliance in this proceeding (Co-Location Order).5 PJM TOs do not demonstrate that PJM’s proposal imposes a “MW threshold for the amount of load

2. BTMG Parties and the PJM TOs Agree that Workable
Grandfathering Provisions Are Essential to Ensure Just and Reasonable
Rates.
In any event, the BTMG Parties and the PJM TOs agree on a critical point regarding PJM’s proposed tariff revisions: the grandfathering provisions must be implemented so as to avoid substantial harm to the investment-backed expectations of customers who relied on the existing BTMG provisions, as the Commission required in its Co-Location Order. BTMG Parties agree with PJM TOs that:

• Grandfathering treatment should extend to projects that are not yet in service and would have been eligible for Retail BTMG treatment, but have made substantial progress towards completion.

• The “existing contract” exception to the application of the new Retail BTMG rules should be construed broadly to cover a wide range of contracts and recognition of renewal rights in those contracts.

B. The Record Supports Expanding Transmission Services Eligibility to
Similarly Situated Customers that Do Not Have Generation with a PJM
Interconnection Agreement.
PJM TOs urge the Commission to “reject the Joint Parties’ request that it reject or defer action on PJM’s proposal to limit the new transmission service options to Co-Located Load.”13 They argue that “there is absolutely no evidence in the record to suggest that load-only customers should be entitled to utilize these transmission services”14 and that “the new services are tied to generator-specific attributes that ordinary load-only customers do not share, including an associated generator, separate metering from that generator, and the ability and willingness to prevent or limit withdrawals through the co-location configuration itself.”15 But the PJM TOs are wrong on both points:

(1) There is robust evidence in the record that load-only customers should be entitled to utilize these transmission services. The Commission’s core findings draw from pleadings that demonstrate why load-only customers should be allowed to access the new transmission services.

(2) The new services are not tied to generator-specific attributes. Even if they were, that would not justify denying service to co-location configurations where the generator does not inject into the PJM system.
