II. INTRODUCTION
DCC appreciates MISO’s continued proactive engagement with the data center industry, and its efforts to develop reliability requirements that facilitate the integration of large loads while preserving the reliable operation of the transmission system.  DCC member facilities depend upon reliable electricity service, and DCC shares MISO’s objective of ensuring that the interconnection and operation of large load customers does not adversely affect reliability, system operations, or other customers.  DCC recognizes that large loads may present operational characteristics that warrant distinct reliability requirements and standards to maintain transmission system reliability. DCC is committed to working collaboratively with MISO, the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”), and stakeholders to establish requirements that maintain reliability, protect customers, and avoid creating barriers to large load growth.

The issue presented by MISO’s Filing is not whether transmission customers taking service for large loads should be subject to distinct visibility and operational requirements.  The Commission already resolved this issue in its June 2026 show cause order finding the MISO tariff unjust and unreasonable because it does not include provisions establishing ride-through, ramping, and other operational requirements to address the challenges of large load integration.5  Instead, the issue is whether the framework that MISO has proposed in this proceeding represents a just and reasonable means of mitigating these reliability risks.  While DCC appreciates MISO’s efforts to develop a compliance framework and recognizes the difficulty of the task, DCC respectfully

A. MISO’s Filing Is Inconsistent With The Show Cause Order, The FPA, And
The Rule Of Reason
1. MISO’s Proposal To Relegate The Substance Of Its Requirements To
Its BPMs Is Inconsistent With The FPA
Section 205 of the FPA requires public utilities to file the rates, terms, and conditions of FERC-jurisdictional service with the Commission.10  This obligation includes “classifications, practices, and regulations affecting such rates and charges[.]”11  Additionally, “no change shall be made” in any rate, term, condition, or practice without a Section 205 filing providing notice of the change to the Commission and the public, and only after the Commission determines that the change is just and reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential.12  These basic statutory requirements are reflected in the Filed Rate Doctrine, which prohibits public utilities from imposing any rates, terms, and conditions other than those that have been filed with and approved by the Commission.13

Section 205 and the Filed Rate Doctrine are designed to ensure that customers receive
advance notice of the rates, terms, and conditions of service and the Commission can exercise its authority necessary to maintain just and reasonable rates.14  As the courts have recognized, the

But the FPA and Filed Rate Doctrine are not satisfied merely because a public utility has filed a tariff with FERC.  The FPA, FERC’s regulations, and the Filed Rate Doctrine require that the tariff “clearly and specifically” set forth the rates, terms, and conditions, and practices affecting such rates, terms, and conditions, of FERC-jurisdictional service.16  A tariff that omits key terms and conditions of service in favor of vague or open-ended references to a public utility’s procedures and practices is inconsistent with the FPA and the Filed Rate Doctrine, and ultimately unjust and unreasonable.17

MISO’s proposed tariff revisions do not satisfy these requirements.  While DCC recognizes that not every ministerial implementation detail must be included in the tariff, MISO’s tariff revisions do not include the substantive standards that MISO proposes to apply, leave key aspects of MISO’s proposed framework unaddressed, and grant MISO broad discretion to apply the standards to customers as it deems appropriate without any objective requirements to constrain this discretion. The Commission’s Rule of Reason does not justify MISO’s decision to leave the substance of its proposed requirements to the BPMs.  Instead, the Commission’s Rule of Reason requires public utilities to file those practices or provisions that “significantly affect rates and services”20 while permitting only implementation details of “practical insignificance”21 to be included in BPMs or other manuals. The practical effect of the tariff revisions that have been proposed is that neither the Commission nor commenters have the information necessary to determine whether the substance of MISO’s proposed framework is just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential. B. MISO Has Not Demonstrated That Its Proposed Definition Of Large Load Is
Just, Reasonable, And Not Unduly Discriminatory Or Preferential
MISO’s proposed definition of Large Load also creates ambiguity regarding the scope and application of MISO’s proposed requirements.  Because classification as a “Large Load” carries substantial regulatory and commercial consequences, it is critical that any definition of Large Load provide clear notice to transmission customers and the loads they serve of whether they will be subject to these requirements.  At the same time, the definition of Large Load should be limited to customers serving load whose size, location, and operating characteristics present the reliability risks that the Commission sought to address in the Show Cause Order.  MISO’s proposed definition of Large Load does not meet these standards.

Indeed, MISO has proposed a definition that departs in material respects from the definition identified in the Show Cause Order without adequately explaining those departures, clarifying their effect on the scope of the proposed requirements, or demonstrating how they correspond to the reliability concerns underlying this proceeding. In the Show Cause Order, the Commission stated that it would be reasonable to define a large load as

a new, commercial or industrial customer, located at a single site behind one or more points of interconnection, and that has a peak load of 50 MW or greater, interconnects to the transmission system at a voltage level of greater than 69 kV, and is not part of a co-location arrangement.46

In its Filing, however, MISO proposes a definition of Large Load that omits any voltage thresholds, is not expressly limited to transmission-connected load, and provides MISO the authority to aggregate customers based on vague and subjective standards:

Load with Demand of 50 MW or more associated with any facility or aggregation of facilities located at a single substation behind one or more Point(s) of Withdrawal from the Transmission System that is owned, operated, or controlled by: (a) a single entity, or (b) a group of entities that will coordinate with respect to the profile of

C. MISO Has Not Demonstrated That Its Grandfathering Proposal Will
Minimize Disruption To Existing Commercial Arrangements
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission recognized that while changes to the MISO tariff are warranted, large loads already are working with transmission owners and other entities to interconnect their projects and obtain transmission service, including that customers are “negotiating, entering into, and/or have executed various agreements related to interconnecting to the transmission system and/or the provision of transmission service.”56  Accordingly, the Commission directed MISO to address in its response to the Show Cause Order how MISO would “ensure minimal disruption to such existing commercial arrangements” and allow a “reasonable amount of time to finalize ongoing agreements that are nearing completion as of the date such Tariff provisions are filed with the Commission.”57

MISO proposes to respond to this directive by exempting certain resources from the reliability requirements proposed in its Filing.  Specifically, MISO proposes that:

• A Large Load, or portion of a Large Load, with a Service Commencement Date on or before December 4, 2026 would be fully exempt from Section 37A.

• A Large Load, or portion of a Large Load, with a Service Commencement Date after December 4, 2026 that is associated with a transmission project that either (i) has been approved by the MISO Board in MTEP 2026, or a prior MTEP, or (ii) has been submitted to MISO for EPR on or prior to June 18, 2026 (the “MTEP/EPR Condition”) would be exempt from ride-through and ramp requirements.

• A Large Load, or portion of a Large Load, that does not have a Service Commencement Date prior to December 4, 2026 and does not meet the MTEP/EPR Condition but is associated with a transmission project submitted to MISO for EPR between June 19, 2026 and October 31, 2026 would be subject to all of the

DCC appreciates MISO’s effort to recognize the potential impact that its proposed reliability requirements could have on existing large load customers that already have entered commercial arrangements based on existing market rules.  In practice, requiring customers that have already commenced the development of their facilities to comply with new ride-through, ramp limits, and other requirements has the potential to create significant disruption by forcing customers to redesign their projects, modify protection and control systems, replace equipment, and implement other project-specific engineering modifications.  As a result, it is critically important that any new reliability requirements that are adopted include measures to respect the settled expectations of customers, including appropriate exemptions and transition periods for customers that are required to comply with these requirements.

DCC believes, however, that MISO has not demonstrated that its proposal is appropriately tailored to avoid disruption to existing commercial arrangements that the Commission sought to address in the Show Cause Order.  MISO’s proposed grandfathering framework turns entirely on the status of an associated transmission project in the MTEP or EPR process rather than on the commitments or progress made by Large Load customers.  In practice, whether a transmission project satisfies the MTEP/EPR Condition may reflect little more than the timing or nature of a transmission upgrade rather than the maturity of the underlying project.  Indeed, a customer may execute utility service agreements, secure financing, procure major equipment, commit substantial capital, and make irreversible business decisions prior to when an associated transmission project reaches the applicable MTEP or EPR milestone.

MISO’s proposal also has the potential to result in arbitrary differences in treatment between similarly situated transmission customers serving Large Loads.
