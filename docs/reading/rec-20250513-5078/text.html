I. ANSWER

A. PJM’s Tariff Will Become Just and Reasonable Once it Incorporates
Language Effectuating All Eight Co-Located Load Configuration Options
Presented in its Answer to the Show Cause Order.
Talen does not agree that PJM’s Tariff is just and reasonable as it currently stands today.  PJM presented eight options for co-located load configurations in its Answer to the Show Cause Order.5  The eight options presented by PJM include both front-of-the-meter and behind-the-meter arrangements, including options that allow for co-located load to not be network load and/or not take any FERC-jurisdictional services.  Taken together, the eight configuration options permit entities seeking to enter into co-located load arrangements to select the configuration that best meets their needs from a considerable variety of configuration options.  Thus, this framework, when adopted as a whole, will result in PJM’s Tariff being just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory.  There is an ample record of evidence already to support this finding.

However, PJM’s current Tariff language only allows entities pursuing large load arrangements to configure these arrangements in three ways: as network load with its own point of interconnection (“POI”) to the grid (Option 1); as network load that shares the same POI with the generator but is separately metered (Option 2); or as network load that has a behind-the-meter generation agreement with a co-located generator (Option 3).6 These three options do not presently outline regulatory guardrails for many of the potential configurations that co-located load arrangements may take, despite there being an immediate need for such guidelines.  The absence of any tariff language that outlines how these loads may interconnect under other configurations results in the current PJM Tariff being unjust and unreasonable.

PJM’s Tariff will be just and reasonable only if the Commission orders PJM to make all necessary Tariff modifications to effectuate the selection of all eight configuration arrangements it presented in its Answer to the Show Cause Order, including those arrangements where: co-located load is non-network load connected directly to a generator connected to the PJM transmission system with various protections in place to avoid the potential of delivery of system energy to serve the load (Option 4); co-located load is configured the same as Option 4 and also has conditional approval from PJM to take back- up power from the grid if necessary (Option 5); co-located load is network load and it brings additional generation to the PJM transmission system that could serve as a capacity resource for PJM (Option 6); co-located load is network load but it uses a new transitional non-capacity backed service offering until any resource adequacy issues associated with

B. The Commission Should Deny the Motion to Stay Proceedings and Request for Settlement Judge Procedures and Order PJM to Modify its Tariff Within 30 Days.

Time is of the essence for the Commission to act.  PJM and its diverse stakeholders rely on the Commission to support to PJM in administering its transmission system in a reliable and stable way that also promotes economic growth.  As Talen has previously explained, it is essential for the Commission to provide clarity and regulatory certainty on the critical issues presented by the co-location of large load as quickly as possible.8 Talen does not agree that the Commission should establish a 90-day period of settlement judge procedures to further discuss the issues.  The record already before the Commission has eight strong, viable options for co-located load configuration proposed by PJM which are just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory.  A 90-day settlement period will only further delay crucial regulatory certainty for all stakeholders involved. Instead of establishing a 90-day period of settlement judge procedures, the Commission should order PJM to file amended Tariff language no later than thirty (30) days after the order is issued.
