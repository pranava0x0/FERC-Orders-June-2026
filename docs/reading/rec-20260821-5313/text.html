Following the conclusion of the CIFP, on July 31, 2026, PJM submitted the instant filing to establish what it characterizes as a “critical safety net designed to address current resource adequacy shortfalls” observed in the base residual auctions for the 2028/2029 delivery year.9 According to PJM, the RBP “will only procure the capacity necessary to cover any remaining shortfall from the 2028/2029 BRA after PJM has accounted for new capacity being brought by new load or that otherwise qualify for a limited opt-out from the RBP.”10 Tract generally supports and commends PJM addressing the BRA shortfall by requiring loads to either curtail, bring their own capacity, or enter the RBP.

IV. REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION
A. PJM Should Clarify the Scope of Agreements Load Can Provide As Evidence
of Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and Annual Demand Resources or
DER Capacity Aggregation Resources.
Tract respectfully requests that PJM clarify that the undefined terms “electric service agreement and transmission service agreement” encompass executed agreements with load serving entities or transmission owners that evidence a binding commitment by new load to achieve commercial operation by or during the relevant delivery year. As currently drafted, the proposed tariff language is not clear whether functionally equivalent agreements that serve the same evidentiary purpose as electric and transmission service agreements would satisfy the Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources eligibility requirements set forth in section 18.2(c). Absent such clarification, PJM risks understating the volume of legitimately committed new load that will achieve commercial operation by or during the relevant delivery year and that satisfies the requirements for Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MWs or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources, thereby inflating the Final RBP Target beyond that necessary to address any actual resource

As drafted, the requirement for load to provide an executed electric or transmission service agreement serves an evidentiary function: to “indicat[e] that the load will be coming online” in the relevant delivery year.15 The requirement screens out speculative load and ensures that only load that has assumed binding obligations sufficient to demonstrate it will achieve commercial operation within the applicable timeframe may be credited as Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources. While electric service and transmission service agreements satisfy that standard, other executed agreements—including transmission security agreements and construction letters of authorization—impose binding commitments equally probative of genuine load commitment. That some EDCs either do not utilize those agreements or do not require loads coming online in the 2028/2029 delivery year to execute them so far in advance does not make such loads any less likely to reach commercial operation. Of course, such loads must demonstrate some evidence of commitment to come online, and the Commission should find that transmission security agreements and construction letters of authorization suffice.
