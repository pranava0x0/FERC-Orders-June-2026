discriminatory or preferential, and direct modifications consistent with the Commission’s

Assurance Agreement to institute the RBP, a “one-time, supplementary mechanism to procure

IV. 
REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION 
A. 
PJM Should Clarify the Scope of Agreements Load Can Provide As Evidence 
of Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and Annual Demand Resources or 
DER Capacity Aggregation Resources. 
Tract respectfully requests that PJM clarify that the undefined terms “electric service 
agreement and transmission service agreement” encompass executed agreements with load serving 
entities or transmission owners that evidence a binding commitment by new load to achieve 
commercial operation by or during the relevant delivery year. As currently drafted, the proposed 
tariff language is not clear whether functionally equivalent agreements that serve the same 
evidentiary purpose as electric and transmission service agreements would satisfy the Qualifying 
RBP Offset UCAP MW or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources 
eligibility requirements set forth in section 18.2(c). Absent such clarification, PJM risks 
understating the volume of legitimately committed new load that will achieve commercial 
operation by or during the relevant delivery year and that satisfies the requirements for Qualifying 
RBP Offset UCAP MWs or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources, 
thereby inflating the Final RBP Target beyond that necessary to address any actual resource

As drafted, the requirement for load to provide an executed electric or transmission service 
agreement serves an evidentiary function: to “indicat[e] that the load will be coming online” in the 
relevant delivery year.15 The requirement screens out speculative load and ensures that only load 
that has assumed binding obligations sufficient to demonstrate it will achieve commercial 
operation within the applicable timeframe may be credited as Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW 
or Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources. While electric service 
and transmission service agreements satisfy that standard, other executed agreements—including 
transmission security agreements and construction letters of authorization—impose binding 
commitments equally probative of genuine load commitment. That some EDCs either do not 
utilize those agreements or do not require loads coming online in the 2028/2029 delivery year to 
execute them so far in advance does not make such loads any less likely to reach commercial 
operation. Of course, such loads must demonstrate some evidence of commitment to come online, 
and the Commission should find that transmission security agreements and construction letters of 
authorization suffice.

Commonwealth Edison Company (“ComEd”) and PECO Energy Company (“PECO”), two 
transmission owners in PJM.16 ComEd and PECO utilize these agreements to govern the terms

V. 
LIMITED PROTEST 
A. 
To The Extent Not Clarified As Argued Above, PJM’s RBP Filing Is Unduly 
Discriminatory Because It Excludes Similarly Situated New Loads From 
Eligibility For Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and Annual Demand 
Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources. 
The Commission has long held that a rate, term, or condition is unduly discriminatory 
where it treats similarly situated customers differently absent a legitimate basis for doing so.24 
Should PJM decline to provide the requested clarification, the Commission should reject the RBP 
filing as unduly discriminatory or preferential under Section 205 of the FPA.25  
By restricting the types of executed agreements new loads may present to obtain eligibility 
for Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and Annual Demand Resources or DER Capacity 
Aggregation Resources, PJM’s proposal draws an impermissible distinction among similarly
