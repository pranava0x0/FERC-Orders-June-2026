(“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure, the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”), Midwest Reliability Organization (“MRO”), ReliabilityFirst Corporation (“RF”), and SERC Reliability Corporation (“SERC”) (collectively, “NERC and the Regional Entities”) move to intervene and comment on the IRR Proposal.4  NERC and all six Regional Entities (collectively, the “ERO Enterprise”) are dedicated to ensuring effective and efficient reduction of risks to the reliability and security of the grid, including supporting entities in identifying and mitigating potential threats to BPS reliability.

The Commission certified NERC as the ERO in 2006.12 As the ERO, NERC’s mission is to ensure effective and efficient reduction of risks to the reliability and security of the grid.13 NERC is responsible for developing and enforcing mandatory Reliability Standards applicable to BPS entities on NERC’s compliance registry and conducting assessments of the reliability and adequacy of the BPS.14 NERC develops Reliability Standards in accordance with Section 300 (Reliability Standards Development) of the NERC Rules of Procedure (“ROP”) and the NERC Standard Processes Manual.15 NERC’s compliance registry includes those entities which meet registry criteria under Section 500 and Appendix 5B of the ROP.  The ERO Enterprise is responsible for monitoring, assessing, and enforcing compliance with Reliability Standards in the United States in accordance with Section 400 (Compliance Enforcement) of the ROP and the NERC Compliance Monitoring and Enforcement Program.16

III. COMMENTS
Consistent with the July 16th Order, the ERO Enterprise has prioritized near-term measures, such as foundational Reliability Standards and registry criteria addressing computational loads, while preserving flexibility for subsequent refinements, such as plans to develop ride-through requirements next year, as additional experience is gained with large load interconnections.20 The ERO Enterprise is working as quickly as possible with stakeholders to advance integration of computational load in a manner that sustains continued reliability and resilience. MISO’s filing is an example of contributions by those across the electric and computational load industries to advance reliability in the face of this change.  Due to the urgency of these near-term measures, the ERO Enterprise, MISO, and other stakeholders working to mitigate risk are acting in parallel rather than sequentially, creating potential for some differences. While all stakeholders collaborate to minimize these differences, different timelines and internal governance processes may yield slightly different outcomes.

Regardless of the parallel development, MISO’s IRR Proposal seems generally consistent with pending Reliability Standards efforts.  The ERO Enterprise and RTO/ISO stakeholders, including MISO, have forums and groups established to continue ensuring consistency to the extent possible. For example, NERC’s Reliability and Security Technical Committee (“RSTC”) subgroups, computational load drafting team Project 2026-02, the short-term advisory group for foundational standards, and an ERO Enterprise and RTO/ISO computational load advisory group provide crucial input to help synchronize and harmonize parallel efforts advance.  In particular, the RTO/ISO computational load advisory group was developed and designed to foster coordination on these issues, with a specific focus on the potential performance requirements for computational loads across interconnections.21

At each step of the pending project for foundational Reliability Standards, the ERO Enterprise expedited processes where feasible, while maintaining transparency and effective consensus-building through a concerted effort to involve computational load entities in the standards development process.  At the inception of the Reliability Standards development project, for example, NERC’s Standards Committee appointed a drafting team based on diverse industry experts that included computational load entities, RTOs/ISOs, and members of NERC Standing Committees.  In light of the July 16th Order, the Standards Committee also granted waivers to permit shorter balloting and comment periods in the standards development process. Recently, the RSTC applied expedited processing for a computational load disturbance ride-through performance Standard Authorization Request in anticipation of presenting the request to the Standards Committee in the fall of 2026. The RSTC meetings in September included multiple presentations on the work being done to study and respond to the needs associated with computational load.

The ERO Enterprise anticipates that industry will continue to rapidly refine its understanding of what is needed to support reliable computational load integration.  As the ERO Enterprise and industry learn more about the needs associated with computational load, criteria (such as ride-through requirements) may need to be adjusted. Earlier in the year, the RSTC approved a roadmap of upcoming high-priority standards development projects – with the most urgent being the disturbance ride-through performance project mentioned above. To that end, the ERO Enterprise requests that the Commission consider expedited evaluation of future efforts stating that they seek to harmonize with NERC Reliability Standard requirements.
