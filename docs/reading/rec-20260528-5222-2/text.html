Q. 
Does the National Association of Regulatory Utility Commissioner’s Electric Cost 
3 
Allocation Manual (“NARUC Manual”) state that the purpose of the minimum 
4 
system method is to develop a result that can be applied to a revenue requirement?
5 
A. 
No. The NARUC Manual clearly explains that the minimum system method’s  purpose is 
6 
to classify distribution plant. The NARUC Manual states the following: 
7 
Classifying distribution plant with the minimum-size method assumes that a 
8 
minimum size distribution system can be built to serve the minimum loading 
9 
requirements of the customer. The minimum-size method involves determining the 
10 
minimum size pole, conductor, cable, transformer, and service that is currently 
11 
installed by the utility. Normally, the average book cost for each piece of equipment 
12 
determines the price of all installed units. Once determined for each primary plant 
13 
account, the minimum size distribution system is classified as customer-related 
14 
costs. The demand-related costs for each account are the difference between the 
15 
total investment in the account and customer-related costs.12
16 
17 
The minimum system study, as explained above, serves to classify portions of certain 
18 
accounts as either customer (i.e., pertaining to the minimum distribution system) or demand 
19 
related. Mr. Ming’s result of 6.2% merely means that, of the plant accounts included in his 
20 
study, he determined that 6.2% of the balances are related to a hypothetical minimum 
21 
system. Therefore, Mr. Ming’s calculation does not represent true revenue requirement for 
22 
the minimum system represented by that 6.2%.

Q.

the Grid Reliance Charge consistent with cost causation principles?

A. 
No. Mr. Ming states that his recommendation to employ the minimum system method in

Mr. Ming’s proposal to employ the minimum system method in calculating the Grid 
12
