I. GOOD CAUSE EXISTS TO GRANT AN EXTENSION

The Commission may extend filing deadlines for good cause.6 Here, good cause exists for the Commission to grant the Community Organizations’ and Clean Energy Advocates’ request for at least two independent reasons.

First, Congress is actively debating the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (“BAAJA”).7 Enactment of that statute would constitute a significant legal change that could directly bear on MISO’s Section 205 filing and FERC’s authority to approve it. For instance, BAAJA Section 2107 contains directives regarding transmission cost allocation that may be inconsistent with MISO’s filing. An extension would allow parties and the Commission time to see whether BAAJA passes and, if so, permits interested parties to comment on how BAAJA’s passage would affect MISO’s pending 205 filing. Indeed, granting an extension may also serve overall efficiency as, if BAAJA passes, utilities themselves may be required, or otherwise seek, to revisit or revise their filings.

Declining to grant an extension, by contrast, would only complicate this proceeding. If the extension were granted and BAAJA does not pass, the parties will not have wasted significant time, and indeed, will have time to address the already complex issues presented by the status quo (see below). If, by contrast, BAAJA passes after the currently set 30-day comment period has closed, parties will have to raise issues concerning how BAAJA affects this proceeding on rehearing. Having to address BAAJA on rehearing is not efficient for either parties or the Commission. The significant uncertainty created by the Congress’ active consideration of BAAJA constitutes good cause to extend the comment period on this proceeding beyond the end of the current legislative session (January 3, 2027).8

Second, good cause also exists to extend the deadline because the ordinary comment period is insufficient to allow parties to address the many issues raised by this complex and lengthy proceeding. Even absent the legal uncertainty created by Congress’s consideration of BAAJA, parties would benefit from additional time to consider the filing and prepare their responses. MISO’s section 205 filing follows from a lengthy and complex multi-stage rulemaking process9 initiated by the United States Department of Energy. This complexity is demonstrated by the fact that
