I. REQUEST FOR CUU/PRIVIIIIC TREATMENT
In accordance with section 388.112 of the Commission's regulations,5 Exelon requests privileged treatment for a portion of this Answer containing confidential information concerning pending retail customer service applications. Exelon understands that the involved customers may view this information as competitively sensitive and ComEd (the relevant Exelon utility) also treats such information as non-public. Accordingly, good cause exists for the Commission to grant this request for privileged treatment of this information.

Exelon is filing both a public version of this Answer with necessary redactions and a non- public version of this Answer that contains the confidential information. The non-public version has been labelled "CUI//PRIV//HC" and should be made subject to privileged document protection by the Commission. In accordance with 18 C.F.R. § 388.112(b)(2)(i), a proposed form of protective agreement modeled on the Commission's Model Protective Order is enclosed as Attachment A. II. ANSWER
In its motion, Constellation points to the Commission's statement that there are "allegations in the record that Exelon has delayed work on Constellation's requests to co-locate load with its generators at certain facilities due to Exelon's concerns that such requests are not allowed under the [PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff ("PJM Tariff')] without arranging for wholesale transmission or retail distribution service."' To be clear, these are merely allegations. Exelon has not "delayed" or refused service in response to any lawful request.

In Attachment 1 of Exhibit 5 to Constellation's November 22, 2024 Complaint filed in Docket No. EL25-20-000,8 Constellation produced a November 12, 2024 letter from ComEd to Constellation concerning interconnection requests at the LaSalle and Byron nuclear facilities. In that letter, ComEd details certain design issues with Constellation's proposal and then explains that the "end use load is retail load under both Illinois and federal law and cannot be interconnected solely through amendments to the LaSalle or Byron [interconnection service agreements]."9 ComEd went on to explain:

We continue to believe there is no lawful path to interconnect end
use load at LaSalle and Byron without that load taking service as a
retail customer of ComEd and we cannot continue to review
proposals for co-location of end use load at LaSalle and Byron on
some other basis.1°

Further, to leave no doubt that Exelon is working to help interconnect data centers, last month, in Docket No. ER25-3492-000, FERC issued an Order Accepting Transmission Security Agreement, an agreement by and between PECO and Amazon Data Services, Inc. ("Amazon"). Amazon is constructing a new data center campus in Falls Township, Pennsylvania, and PECO will provide retail electric delivery services for the data center campus, pursuant to its electric retail service tariff on file with the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission. In addition, Network Integrated Transmission Service will be procured by the responsible Load Serving Entity, as such term is defined in the PJM Tariff. The Transmission Security Agreement "ensures that a contribution to the transmission revenue requirement is made by Amazon commensurate with its requested end-use load relating to the construction of and service to its Data Center."18 This is another example of Exelon, in compliance with the PJM Tariff, providing a feasible path forward for data centers.

Constellation's Motion and requested PJM Tariff changes are unwarranted and should be denied.
