I. RELEVANT BACKGROUND
On November 22, 2024, Constellation filed a complaint under Federal Power Act (“FPA”) section 2064 against PJM in Docket No. EL25-20-000. In short, Constellation argued that the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) is unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory because it does not contain specific rules for interconnecting generators that will serve co-located loads. Constellation requested that the Commission grant the complaint on a fast-track basis, determine that the PJM Tariff is unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory or preferential, and direct PJM to adopt in its Tariff certain portions of PJM’s Guidance on Co-Located Load. On November 1, 2024, the Commission held a technical conference in Docket No AD24- 11-000, to discuss issues related to co-location of loads at generating facilities. The Commission noted several proceedings initiated by entities in the PJM Region that involved similar issues. Initial and reply comments were filed following the technical conference.5

On February 20, 2025, the Commission issued an order in Docket No. EL25-49-000, instituting a proceeding under FPA section 206.6 The Show Cause Order followed several filings that addressed the co-location of large loads at generating facilities in PJM, as well as the Commission’s technical conference in Docket No. AD24-11-000. the FPA section 206 burden has not been met. Moreover, the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ Answer demonstrates that Constellation’s request for the Commission to make determinations regarding jurisdiction and adopt Constellation’s list of demands is both premature and raises significant concerns regarding reliability and affordability. Instead, as ODEC recommended in its Reply Comments on the Proposed ANOPR, the Commission should (1) act swiftly to provide necessary clarity, adopt generally applicable rules and/or standards, and direct compliance filings;

(2) include specific directives to ensure reliability and resource adequacy; and (3) adopt requirements to ensure that large loads pay their fair share of the costs. PJM can then work with stakeholders, as it has in its ongoing Critical Issue Fast Path (“CIFP”) process, to develop a proposal for consideration by its Board of Managers that can be submitted to the Commission in compliance with the Commission’s guidance and directives.

The Show Cause proceeding should be closed without further directive and Constellation’s complaint should be dismissed because there has been no demonstration that the existing Tariff is unjust, unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential. However, even if the Commission finds that this FPA section 206 burden has been met, Constellation’s specific relief has not been shown to constitute a just and reasonable replacement rate. In addition to the issues raised in the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ Answer, ODEC submits that Constellation’s request for the Commission to direct that large load interconnection requests must be processed without regard to whether the load has arranged for wholesale transmission or distribution service, could lead to unreasonable cost allocation, as well as threats to reliability. In the Show Cause Order, the Commission stated that “[t]he record demonstrates that different co-location arrangements are likely to use or benefit from the transmission system in different ways depending on how they are configured and, moreover, that those different configurations will themselves likely have different
