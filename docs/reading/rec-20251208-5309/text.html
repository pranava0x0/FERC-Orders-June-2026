I. 
RELEVANT BACKGROUND 
On November 22, 2024, Constellation filed a complaint under Federal Power Act (“FPA”) 
section 2064 against PJM in Docket No. EL25-20-000. In short, Constellation argued that the PJM 
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) is unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory 
because it does not contain specific rules for interconnecting generators that will serve co-located 
loads. Constellation requested that the Commission grant the complaint on a fast-track basis, 
determine that the PJM Tariff is unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory or preferential, 
and direct PJM to adopt in its Tariff certain portions of PJM’s Guidance on Co-Located Load.  
On November 1, 2024, the Commission held a technical conference in Docket No AD24-
11-000, to discuss issues related to co-location of loads at generating facilities. The Commission 
noted several proceedings initiated by entities in the PJM Region that involved similar issues. 
Initial and reply comments were filed following the technical conference.5  
On February 20, 2025, the Commission issued an order in Docket No. EL25-49-000, 
instituting a proceeding under FPA section 206.6 The Show Cause Order followed several filings 
that addressed the co-location of large loads at generating facilities in PJM, as well as the 
Commission’s technical conference in Docket No. AD24-11-000. The Show Cause Order directed

and establish a durable framework for the interconnection of large loads to the transmission system 
in a manner that ensures reliability and resource adequacy, and ensures that large loads pay their 
share of the costs for the benefits they will receive. 
II. 
ANSWER 
In response to the Show Cause Order, the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, including 
ODEC, demonstrated that (1) neither the Commission nor any of the parties supporting initiation 
of the Show Cause proceeding have met the FPA section 206 burden to demonstrate that the

Commission guidance might be appropriate.11 To that end, PJM requested that if the Commission 
decides to take action, it would issue guidance so that PJM can work with stakeholders to develop 
a solution. To address timeliness, PJM suggested that the Commission could issue a compliance 
directive to set a deadline for future filings, such as an FPA section 205 filing by PJM consistent

the FPA section 206 burden has not been met. Moreover, the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ 
Answer demonstrates that Constellation’s request for the Commission to make determinations 
regarding jurisdiction and adopt Constellation’s list of demands is both premature and raises 
significant concerns regarding reliability and affordability. Instead, as ODEC recommended in its 
Reply Comments on the Proposed ANOPR, the Commission should (1) act swiftly to provide 
necessary clarity, adopt generally applicable rules and/or standards, and direct compliance filings; 
(2) include specific directives to ensure reliability and resource adequacy; and (3) adopt 
requirements to ensure that large loads pay their fair share of the costs. PJM can then work with 
stakeholders, as it has in its ongoing Critical Issue Fast Path (“CIFP”) process, to develop a 
proposal for consideration by its Board of Managers that can be submitted to the Commission in 
compliance with the Commission’s guidance and directives.  
The Show Cause proceeding should be closed without further directive and Constellation’s 
complaint should be dismissed because there has been no demonstration that the existing Tariff is 
unjust, unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential. However, even if the Commission 
finds that this FPA section 206 burden has been met, Constellation’s specific relief has not been 
shown to constitute a just and reasonable replacement rate. In addition to the issues raised in the 
Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ Answer, ODEC submits that Constellation’s request for the 
Commission to direct that large load interconnection requests must be processed without regard to 
whether the load has arranged for wholesale transmission or distribution service, could lead to 
unreasonable cost allocation, as well as threats to reliability. In the Show Cause Order, the 
Commission stated that “[t]he record demonstrates that different co-location arrangements are 
likely to use or benefit from the transmission system in different ways depending on how they are 
configured and, moreover, that those different configurations will themselves likely have different

load arrangements with the existing PJM provisions for netting with regard to behind-the-meter

similar and, therefore, different treatment of co-located load from behind-the-meter generation is 
not unduly discriminatory.17 ODEC urges the Commission to reject Constellation’s attempt to 
argue otherwise.

the PJM Region only, also fails to address critical issues regarding resource adequacy with co-
located loads. In the Show Cause Order, the Commission advised that “the risk that co-located 
loads will not be reflected in resource adequacy planning mechanisms with sufficient forward 
notice is particularly concerning given how long it takes to add new generation capacity or upgrade 
transmission facilities.”18 Constellation’s request for the Commission to immediately adopt

III. 
CONCLUSION 
WHEREFORE, for the foregoing reasons, ODEC urges the Commission to reject 
Constellation’s Motion to Expedite, deny Constellation’s complaint and terminate the Show Cause 
proceeding without further action because the FPA section 206 burden has not been met, as 
discussed above and in the pleadings in these proceedings by the Indicated PJM Transmission 
Owners.
