A. Eddystone and Similarly Situated Energy Resources Should Qualify as RBP
Resources and Resources Eligible to Supply Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP
MW
On September 11, 2026, the D.C. Circuit issued a unanimous ruling vacating the
Department of Energy’s (“DOE”) order issued under section 202(c) Federal Power Act (“FPA”) requiring Consumers Energy to continue operating the J.H. Campbell power plant (the “Campbell Order”).6  The Campbell Order could have implications for other electric generators currently operating pursuant to the Secretary’s authority under section 202(c), including Constellation’s Eddystone Units 3 & 4 (“Eddystone”).  The court rejected the DOE’s interpretation of section 202(c), finding that no “emergency” existed to warrant a section 202(c) order.7  Legal challenges to other DOE orders under section 202(c), including with respect to Eddystone, remain pending. In light of this development and its implications, Constellation provides additional clarification regarding its earlier request to modify the criteria for resources eligible to provide Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and RBP Sell Offers.8

The Secretary has extended its 202(c) order for Eddystone continuously since, most recently on August 21, 2026.9  Notably, those DOE orders direct that the Eddystone units “shall not be considered capacity resources.”10  As a result, Constellation requested, and the Commission granted, a request for waiver to remove Eddystone Units 3 and 4 from Capacity Resource status and transferred the Capacity Interconnection Rights (“CIRs”) from Eddystone to Crane Clean Energy Center earlier this year.11  Eddystone is now an energy-only resource with no capacity rights.

To be clear, from a PJM perspective the path to becoming a new capacity resource for Eddystone would look no different from that of a new build resource or an uprate to an existing capacity resource:  it would require entry into the interconnection queue and payment to upgrade the transmission system as necessary.  Network upgrade costs are uncertain but could be significant—potentially in the tens of millions or even hundreds of millions of dollars. Requirements in Prior Proceedings
The PJM Answer claimed that the Commission should disregard the concerns raised in Constellation’s Limited Protest regarding the RBP’s price taker requirement because the

Commission has found in other proceedings, particularly in the context of Reliability Must Run (“RMR”) resources, that offering resources into the PJM Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) as price takers is just and reasonable.15  This comparison is misguided.  The Commission should not rely on its limited RMR precedent to guide its decision here for the following reasons:

• PJM Ignores the Commission’s Balancing Analysis in its RMR Proceeding.  In its RMR proceedings (and other proceedings considering the impact of price taker offers by resources with out-of-market support), the Commission engages in a balancing analysis to determine whether it is just and reasonable to require out-of-market resources to offer as price takers, wherein it balances market and resource adequacy impacts and cost impacts to determine whether a proposal is just and reasonable.16  This balancing analysis is fact specific, and the RMR proceedings considered, among other things, the unique nature of RMR units, the nature of the service provided, and the magnitude and duration of such service.  Here, PJM has not engaged in any meaningful balancing that considers the impact of RBP resources offering as price takers on markets, resource adequacy or costs, let alone provided evidence that the specific facts applicable to RBP resource (including, the uncertainty as to when or if the associated load will materialize, the duration and magnitude of the RBP service, etc.) justify PJM’s proposed result.  As described below, the Commission should find that the balancing analysis here, unlike in the RMR proceedings, weighs against finding that PJM’s price taker requirement is just and reasonable.

• Load Served by RMR Resources Already Exists.  Resources subject to RMR agreements are already serving existing load, with such generation operating past planned deactivation dates in response to a well-studied transmission reliability problem.  Such RMR resources continue to operate to ensure transmission reliability for existing load until specific and necessary transmission system upgrades are completed.  On the other hand, the load expected to be served by RBP resources is not yet energized, and as explained in Constellation’s Limited Protest and Answer and Dr. Shanker’s supporting testimony,17 there is risk this load may be delayed or never energized at all.  Others agree this distinction between existing load and new large load is a meaningful concern.  For example, in PJM’s Interim Resource Adequacy Service proceeding,  Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. explained that new large loads “are, as a structural matter, disproportionately likely to be projects still working through interconnection, construction, or commissioning, rather than loads already drawing their full expected demand, if any demand at all based upon the state of project development.”18  Thus, the impact on markets, resource adequacy provided by existing resources, and costs overall could be far greater in the RBP context where there is no load certainty than in the RMR context where the load already exists.

C. Constellation Supports PJM’s Suggestion to Sever Credit and Collateral
Issues
Constellation and others raised concerns about the load serving entity (“LSE”) collateral and credit requirements in the PJM Proposal.26  In the PJM Answer, PJM acknowledged that “reasonable minds may differ on the credit approach,” and “consent[ed] to the Commission resolving the applicable buyer-side LSE RBP Credit Rate through additional procedures.”27  Given the complexity and enormity of the potential LSE credit requirements, and given that “the LSE credit rules do not need to be effective for the RBP to be conducted,”28 Constellation appreciates PJM’s openness on these issues and supports, as described in its Answer, severing these credit issues and establishing an expedited paper hearing limited to proposed Tariff section 18.8(b).29
