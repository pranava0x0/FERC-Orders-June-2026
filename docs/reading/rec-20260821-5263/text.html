COMMENTS OF SOUTHERN MARYLAND ELECTRIC COOPERATIVE, INC. 
 
Pursuant to the Combined Notice of Filings #1 issued by the Federal Energy Regulatory 
Commission (“Commission”) on August 3, 2026, Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. 
(“SMECO”)1 respectfully submits these comments regarding the filing that PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. (“PJM”) submitted in the above-captioned docket on July 31, 2026 (the “RBP Filing”).2  
PJM proposes revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the 
Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish a one-time 
“Reliability Backstop Procurement” (“RBP”) of new capacity, the costs of which PJM proposes 
to recover through a new charge (the “RBP Charge”) assessed against a subset of Load Serving 
Entities (“LSEs”) in the PJM Region.    
SMECO acknowledges that additional capacity is needed to address the shortfall reflected 
in the 2028/2029 Delivery Year (“DY”) Base Residual Auction (“BRA”) and PJM's decision to 
pursue that capacity through a one-time backstop mechanism, the RBP.   SMECO’s comments 
focus on the roles the RBP Filing assigns to Electric Distributors in making those determinations, 
and certain aspects of the RBP credit and collateral requirements proposed in the RBP Filing.

I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY  
PJM's RBP proposal responds to an actual reliability concern – namely, that the 2028/2029 
DY BRA cleared 6,831.3 MW short of the PJM Region Reliability Requirement.  This shortfall, 
as PJM notes, was driven in part by the rapid interconnection of large loads, including data 
centers.3  SMECO agrees that this shortfall must be addressed.   
However, SMECO has comments in two areas.  First, PJM’s proposal assigns Electric 
Distributors a series of functions that exceed the metering and data-reporting functions Electric 
Distributors have historically performed for PJM under the PJM Operating Agreement (“OA”). 
Second, as to RBP credit, PJM proposes vastly different RBP collateral requirements as  
between RBP load versus RBP supply. SMECO serves a service territory that has attracted data 
center interest, and as such, has an interest in seeing a rational PJM RBP credit policy established.  
II. BACKGROUND 
PJM filed the RBP Filing on July 31, 2026, requesting that the Commission act by 
September 29, 2026 -- sixty days after filing -- so that PJM may commence the RBP on September 
30, 2026.4  PJM proposes to set an “Initial RBP Target” equal to the 6,831.3 MW UCAP shortfall 
reflected in the 2028/2029 DY BRA (excluding Fixed Resource Requirement load and supply), 
and to decrement that Initial RBP Target by two categories of MWs to arrive at a “Final RBP 
Target”: (1) “Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW,” meaning new capacity, matched with new load, 
that PJM verifies was not previously counted as either an RPM commitment or an FRR resource; 
and (2) “Opt-Out UCAP MW,” a narrow opt-out available in limited circumstances.5 PJM 
proposes to procure that Final RBP Target quantity through the RBP.

must post this RBP Collateral no later than forty-five days before the start of the relevant DY -- 
just fifteen days after the underlying RBO allocation on which the collateral requirement depends 
is reported (i.e., the RBO must be reported no later than sixty days before the DY).10

B. RBP Credit and Collateral Issues.  
PJM’s proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, section 18.8 imposes a credit requirement on 
LSEs.  Per the Affidavit of PJM witness Danielle Croop27, PJM's proposed initial allocation of 
RBP UCAP MW to each Electric Distributor zone/area is based on the Load Adjustments in PJM 
Table B9b of the 2026 PJM Load Forecast, applied against the region-wide Initial RBP Target of 
6,831.3 MW. 28    PJM proposes an RBP LSE Credit Rate "initially at $1.5 million" per MW.29 
Applied to the 6,831.3 MW of RBP target capacity, approximately $10.25 billion (6,831.3 MW × 
$1,500,000/MW = $10,246,950,000) would need to be posted by LSEs as collateral no later than 
45 days ahead of the start of the 2028/2029 Delivery Year. PJM derived the $1.5 million buy-side 
credit requirement as a full 15-year, $555 MW-day contract payment (discounted at 9.5%), 
yielding an RBP LSE required posting  ~ 7.5 years equivalent buy-side collateral.    
PJM proposes that the Electric Distributor’s RBO allocation be reported at no later than 
sixty days before the start of the DY; PJM proposes to require the affected LSE to post the resulting 
collateral just fifteen days later, at forty-five days before the DY begins.30  That leaves just a 
fifteen-day window to arrange collateral for an obligation whose amount it could not have 
predicted in advance, using a $1.5 million/MW RBP Credit Rate”31   Thus, once an LSE knows 
the outcome of the state/RERRA reporting process administered by the Electric Distributor, but it 
then only has two weeks to post significant RPM buy-side collateral.

requirement of RBP supply resources.   
Moreover, if an RBP-procured generator fails to deliver or is delayed, the RBP load it 
serves is exposed to higher risk of load curtailment.  An RBP load that wants to hedge that risk by 
buying replacement capacity cannot do so,  practically speaking; a 20% shortfall penalty times 
$555 MW-day is only $111 MW-day, which is insufficient compensation to purchase new 
capacity.  Further, if an RBP resource misses its commercial operation date (“COD”) because of 
network-upgrade delays are excused from shortfall penalty charges -- no RBP shortfall charge is 
collected to compensate the load sitting exposed to “IRAS” or other elevated curtailment risk in 
the meantime.34
