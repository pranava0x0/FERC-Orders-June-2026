I. INTRODUCTION
The PJM Transmission Owners’ proposals in this proceeding are grounded in two main principles: (1) rates for the new services must be consistent with longstanding cost allocation principles, and (2) the new services must be implemented in a manner that does not compromise system reliability.  Co-Located Load2 customers must pay their fair share of transmission system costs, including those related to the benefits of being connected and synchronized with the extensive PJM Transmission System.  Otherwise, other ratepayers will be forced to subsidize Co- Located Load customers.  Additionally, reliability must be a key consideration of these new services, not a secondary issue or afterthought.  Co-Located Loads will be fully connected and synchronized with the transmission system, and can have serious impacts on the system.  The laudable goals of promoting speed to power and economic efficiency for data centers and other large load customers will not be well-served by implementing new transmission services in a manner that results in significant risks and uncertainty around the ongoing reliability of the system. Moreover, such a result risks undermining continued reliable service to existing customers. In this reply brief, the PJM Transmission Owners respond to a number of issues raised with respect to PJM’s proposed non-rate terms and conditions for the three new transmission services and their rate proposals for the new transmission services.  The PJM Transmission Owners express support for PJM’s proposed scope of the new transmission services, on the basis that they provide reasonable flexibility to Co-Located Load customers while recognizing the need to implement these new services in a reliable fashion.  They also respond to criticisms of PJM’s proposed study process, pointing out the important role that PJM Transmission Owners will continue to play with respect to conducting load-related studies, and explaining why allegations of potential PJM

In their comments, Invenergy Renewables LLC and Invenergy Thermal LLC (together, “Invenergy”) request clarification on how the Commission and PJM view the definition of Eligible Customer in the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) when applied to the co-located generator and co-located load.11  Specifically, whether a co-located generator would qualify as an Eligible Customer on behalf of a Co-Located Load, and whether a co-located generator’s status as an Eligible Customer depends on whether the co-location arrangement is in a jurisdiction that has mandated retail choice.12

The penalty rachet applies when multiple Exceedance Hours occur during a particular time period.50  It is designed to discourage recurring reliance on unreserved transmission capacity.51 Under the penalty rachet framework, the penalty charges escalate as the frequency of unreserved transmission use increases.52  Specifically, if more than one Exceedance Hour occurs on the same calendar day, an additional daily penalty applies.53  If exceedances occur on multiple calendar days within the same calendar week, an additional weekly penalty applies; likewise, if exceedances occur in multiple calendar weeks within the same calendar month, an additional monthly penalty applies.54  By progressively increasing the economic consequences of repeated penalties, the penalty ratchet reinforces the expectation that Co-Located Load customers manage their load in a manner consistent with their reserved transmission service limits.55
