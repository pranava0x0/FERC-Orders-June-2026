ANSWER OF 
AMERICAN MUNICIPAL POWER, INC. 
 
Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission (“Commission” or “FERC”)1 and the Commission’s notice,2 
American Municipal Power, Inc. (“AMP”) respectfully submits this Answer in response to 
the abeyance motion filed by Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (“MISO”) 
and the MISO Transmission Owners on August 3, 2026, in the above-captioned 
proceedings (“Motion”). The Motion fails to provide the information required by the 
Commission’s June 18 Show Cause Order in the event abeyance is sought,3 and 
therefore the Commission has grounds to deny the Motion.  
However, in the interest of expediting resolution of this matter, the Commission 
should instead direct MISO to present within twenty days to its stakeholders a proposal 
for their consideration that addresses in substantial detail the critical cost-transparency 
and cost-shifting issues identified in the Show Cause Order, and immediately file that 
proposal with the Commission on an informational basis, along with an update describing 
MISO’s detailed plan for consideration of its proposal within the stakeholder process, to 
demonstrate progress in support of further abeyance. The Commission should also 
impose a deadline of November 16, 2026 for MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners

I. 
ANSWER 
A. 
The Show Cause Order identified critical issues for MISO to address 
in Tariff revisions to be proposed through a compliance filing that 
remains due on August 17, 2026. 
The Show Cause Order identified cost transparency and cost-shifting protections 
as central deficiencies, among others, in MISO's Tariff. Missing protections include, inter 
alia, public disclosure of large-load-driven network upgrades and their costs, cost 
recovery agreements, financial security requirements, and protection of existing 
transmission customers from stranded costs caused by speculative large-load projects.4 
The Show Cause Order discusses each of these problem areas in support of the 
Commission’s decision to require submittal of proposed revisions to the Tariff by August 
17, in the absence of a demonstration that the existing Tariff is just and reasonable. 
B. 
The Show Cause Order adopted reasonable standards for evaluating 
the abeyance request presented in the Motion. 
The Commission anticipated that MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners may 
need additional “time while they work through the stakeholder processes to develop FPA 
section 205 filings to revise the Tariff to address the issues raised in [the Show Cause 
Order].”5 The Show Cause Order indicated “that successful abeyance requests, if any, 
will include both (1) a robust description of the content of a potential future filing under 
FPA section 205 and (2) a reasoned and specific explanation of when such a filing is

C. 
MISO’s Motion fails to meet the standards for abeyance established in 
the Show Cause Order.

Whereas the Commission has thoughtfully outlined its expectations in terms of cost 
transparency and preventing cost shifting, MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners

The Motion asserts that there will be six meetings of MISO’s LLWG between now 
and November, and that “[a] detailed management plan outlining the specific agenda for

discussed at each meeting. For example, the listing for the August 27, 2026 meeting

Costs associated with transmission upgrades to serve large loads are likely to be 
in the billions of dollars. Solutions to ensure proper cost allocation that will avoid cost 
shifting cannot be solely developed by MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners through 
proposals that are presented to the rest of the stakeholder community with almost no time 
for meaningful input. This is especially true when the Show Cause Order’s directives have

D. 
To expedite resolution of this matter, the Commission should 
condition abeyance on a further compliance filing by MISO. 
There is a clear gap between the existing broad cost allocation methodologies 
applied to transmission projects in MISO generally, and a targeted allocation that is 
necessary to protect against cost shifting due to the addition of large loads. Transmission 
dependent utilities such as municipalities and cooperatives are not protected by state 
utility commissions and retail tariffs because the allocation of costs between individual 
load serving entities within MISO transmission pricing zones is performed at the 
wholesale level. Transmission service rates are, of course, FERC-jurisdictional and 
protections for these wholesale customers (and by implication, their associated retail 
customers) cannot be ignored or given short shrift.  
Accordingly, the Commission should direct MISO to: (1) bring forward a specific 
proposal to meet its obligations under the Show Cause Order to address cost 
transparency and cost shifting within the next twenty days in the MISO stakeholder 
process and provide the Commission with a detailed report regarding that proposal; (2) 
thoroughly vet its proposal with state regulators, consumer advocates, and other 
representatives of customers and load serving entities that might bear the costs of 
upgrades required to serve large loads; and (3) work collaboratively with these parties to 
develop just and reasonable revisions to the MISO Tariff in compliance with the Show 
Cause Order, to be submitted for the Commission’s approval by the revised deadline, 
which should be no later than the maximum ninety-day abeyance period contemplated in 
that order (i.e., no later than November 16, 2026).

related stakeholder forums that are organized during the term of this Working Group.
