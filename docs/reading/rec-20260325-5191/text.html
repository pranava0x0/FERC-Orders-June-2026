I. BACKGROUND
i. Justification for Interim NITS
In response to the Commission’s February 20, 2025 order to show cause,10 several parties recommended that “the Commission explore the potential to develop a new level of non-firm or interruptible transmission service that prioritizes connection of new flexible loads, regardless of whether they are co-located with generation.”11  Among these parties was Enchanted Rock, which filed extensive comments supporting the establishment of a new, interim non-firm transmission service to enable large load customers to interconnect faster while maintaining overall reliability of the transmission system.12

As the Commission recognized in its December 18 Order, “Enchanted Rock supports a transitional non-firm service where the load relies on PJM to provide energy, but where the load

ii. Commission Directives Related to Interim NITS
In response to these parties’ comments, the Commission directed PJM to “create a new interim, non-firm transmission service  . . .  [to] allow an Eligible Customer that has requested NITS on behalf of a Co-Located Load to take non-firm transmission service that can be interrupted until the Network Upgrades necessary to provide NITS are complete and the Co- Located Load can be designated as Network Load.”16  This “new interim, non-firm transmission service” is what PJM defines as “Interim NITS.”

Notably, in his concurring opinion to the December 18 Order, Commission Rosner described the Commission’s rationale behind ordering the establishment of Interim NITS in further detail, as well as how it is intended to be implemented in practice: today’s order directs PJM to establish a new interim service (see Figure 4) to provide a bridge while the infrastructure needed to serve a large load with traditional front-of-meter network transmission service is being built.  This interim service leverages speed in the near-term and unlocks the benefits of a full grid connection in the long-term.  It will allow co-located loads and generators seeking front-of-meter service to access the grid faster than they can today.  In exchange for increased speed, these loads will bear the risk of being curtailed when the grid is constrained until the transmission upgrades needed to provide service are constructed.  But that doesn’t mean that the load will always have to shut down completely during peak periods.  Rather, if the co-located load is paired with a new generator, and that generator is still waiting to begin its own front-of-meter service, the load can take service directly from the generator in the interim until both “graduate” to full, front-of-meter service.  This pathway is important because traditional, front-of-meter network transmission service provides the broadest benefits to both load and generators—a network load can be served by any generator on the grid, and a network generator can sell its power to any customer on the grid.20

Accordingly, as made unequivocally clear by Commissioner Rosner’s concurring statements, the Commission intended for a Co-Located Load taking Interim NITS to be able to utilize energy and capacity provided by a Co-Located Generating Facility during the period in which the Co- Located Load and Co-Located Generating Facility are being studied for full NITS (i.e., during the temporary Interim Period). II. PROTEST
i. The Proposed Interim NITS Limitation Cannot Be Imposed by PJM Because it is
Not Part of PJM’s Filed Rate

Proposed Interim NITS Limitation through means not specified in the PJM Tariff.  However, any prohibition on a Co-Located Load’s ability to be served by a Co-Located Generating Facility during the Interim Period must be specified in the PJM Tariff, as the prohibition significantly impacts how Eligible Customers taking Interim NITS must operate their Co-Located Load

during periods in which they are curtailed, and as discussed further below in Section II.ii, renders Interim NITS entirely unworkable.  Therefore, because the Proposed Interim NITS Limitation is certainly a practice “that affect[s] rates and service significantly, that [is]

ii. PJM’s Proposed Interim NITS Limitation Would Render Interim NITS
Unworkable and Conflicts With the Commission’s Directives in the
December 18 Order

As noted previously, the Commission established Interim NITS in order to allow “an Eligible Customer that has requested to take NITS on behalf of a Co-Located Load to . . .receive an interruptible transmission service before the Network Upgrades necessary to designate and serve the Co-Located Load as Network Load are complete.”28  The core purpose of Interim NITS is to allow a large load customer to interconnect faster in exchange for being interruptible (i.e., curtailable) when the transmission system is stressed and firm transmission service must be prioritized for Network Customers taking NITS.  Importantly, Interim NITS is designed to be temporary,29 and only be in place until Network Upgrades are constructed, and both the Co- Located Load and Co-Located Generating Facility can take or provide full Network Service, respectively. This is reflected clearly in Commissioner Rosner’s concurrence to the December 18 Order, in which he stated that “if the co-located load is paired with a new generator, and that generator is still waiting to begin its own front-of-meter service, the load can take service directly from the generator in the interim until both ‘graduate’ to full, front-of-meter service.”30 PJM’s Proposed Interim NITS Limitation would prohibit Co-Located Generating

Facilities from serving Co-Located Load during the Interim Period, which would mean that the Co-Located Load would be exposed to actual shutdowns when curtailed.  Absent the ability to be

Based on Enchanted Rock’s experience, large load customers, which Interim NITS is designed to serve, will accept the risk of being curtailed in exchange for faster interconnection. However, these customers cannot commercially bear the risk of being completely shut down when curtailed.  Data centers and similar critical infrastructure facilities typically operate under contractual reliability requirements demanding continuous uptime of 99.999% or greater. Market rules that would require these customers’ Co-Located Loads to be curtailed, but that would allow a Co-Located Generating Facility to serve the Co-Located Load while it is being curtailed, would be operationally and commercially manageable. However, PJM’s Proposed Interim NITS Limitation forecloses that option and would effectively force large load customers taking Interim NITS to risk actually being shut down during periods in which they are curtailed, rendering Interim NITS commercially impractical and unworkable.

Other parties agree with Enchanted Rock’s assessment of PJM’s Proposed Interim NITS Limitation.  As noted by the Data Center Coalition (“DCC”) in their answer to PJM’s February 23 Compliance Filing, the DCC stated that the Proposed Interim NITS Limitation “is unworkable because curtailment should limit use of the grid, not prevent the customer from using the co- located resource it supplied, which supports Interim NITS in the first place. If the customer cannot use that generation during curtailment, Interim NITS provides no practical value.”33
