II. ANSWER
i. IRAS Should Not Exceed Amount of New Large Load Demand in Real Time
Protests and comments submitted by Buckeye Power, SMECO, and EKPC raise issue with a specific component in PJM’s IRAS Proposal.  In its Proposal, PJM proposes to issue load reductions to electric distribution companies (“EDCs”) in an amount “up to, and capped at, the pre-Delivery Year IRAS obligations they were assigned” when conducting an IRAS Action.12

However, Buckeye Power, SMECO, and EKPC identify an important concern, which is that this pre-determined amount may not match the actual New Large Load demand being used in real-time.13  SMECO’s Comments highlight that this is not merely a hypothetical scenario, as IRAS amounts are set at a pre-Delivery Year MW quantity, yet an EDC’s assigned New Large Loads may not be yet energized, or may have only just begun a ramp or testing period that is below its expected draw when PJM issues an IRAS Action.14  If this is the case and PJM directs an amount of IRAS that corresponds to the peak load reported by the New Large Load, and this obligation is higher than the real-time demand of large loads in that zone, it is unclear whether EDCs will be required to reduce other customers in addition to New Large Load customers in order to meet the required IRAS MW amount.  Buckeye Power, SMECO, and EKPC properly point out that this is clearly not PJM’s intent,15 with Buckeye Power’s Comments highlighting that “PJM’s goal of ‘prevent[ing] Manual Load Dump’ properly recognizes that keeping existing customers, particularly residential loads, online is the primary goal in the event of a grid emergency.”16  Ultimately, however, PJM’s IRAS Proposal does not provide the clarity needed for EDCs’ IRAS obligations in real-time.  To this end, the Board requests clarification on how PJM will use real-time demand from New Large Loads to inform curtailment directives, either through a more dynamic database in PJM’s proposed Large Load Registry, or through the development and maintenance of a different system entirely to assist in this process. ii. PJM Should Clarify How IRAS Directives Interact with Peak Shaving Adjustments The Board appreciates PJM’s consideration of state policies when it added a Peak Shaving Adjustment (“PSA”) provision in its Reliability Backstop Procurement Proposal (“RBP”) that would decrement a zone’s RBP obligation commensurate with its PSA arrangements.18  This provision recognizes the reliability benefits PSA programs provide to the region, and the Board submitted Comments in appreciation of this component that compliments the newly passed Data Center Fair Share Act in New Jersey.19  However, while PSA arrangements reduce RBP obligations, PJM has not explained how they will interact with Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) obligations or IRAS obligations.  Several commentators have raised concerns about this ambiguity.20  The Board likewise seeks clarity regarding how PSA arrangements interact with IRAS.

The Board thinks that New Large Loads with PSA agreements must be accounted for explicitly in PJM’s IRAS operations with real-time information regarding whether New Large Loads have already reduced their load due to PSA directives.  In a scenario in which a New Large Load has already offset its load due to a PSA directive, it cannot reduce its total registered load amount in the event the EDC requires it during an IRAS Action. The Board recommends that PJM clarify that EDCs will not be required to curtail more than their New Large Load demand in real time, after accounting for any real-time load reductions achieved through programs such as PSA.  Furthermore, the Board recommends that a more dynamic data-sharing method is developed for PJM, states, utilities, and New Large Loads to locate and utilize real-time New Large Load demand, IRAS, and PSA dispatch information for better information-sharing and accountability.  In addition, the Board agrees with Equinix that the Large Load Registry should account for New Large Loads that are sponsoring PSAs at another site in the same zone in addition to PSAs associated with the load itself, which is not something PJM’s Proposal appears to do currently.21

III. CONCLUSION
The Board reiterates that it strongly supports PJM’s decision to propose a PJM-led resource adequacy service for New Large Loads that do not offset their registered peak load with sufficient capacity arrangements, and thinks its Proposal is largely workable.  However, the Board underscores concerns other entities have raised and seeks clarity on specific elements that currently provide confusion and challenges in implementing IRAS.  The Board is hopeful that this clarification will provide entities in New Jersey with the information they need to effectuate PSA programs, and that clarification pertaining to the IRAS MW amount will provide EDCs with the confirmation that they are not expected to curtail other customers during an IRAS Action.  The Board thinks these elements can be addressed without fundamentally altering PJM’s IRAS
