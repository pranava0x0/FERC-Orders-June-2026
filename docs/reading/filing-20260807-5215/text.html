ANSWER OF CORPORATE ENERGY BUYERS ASSOCIATION
IN SUPPORT OF THE MOTIONS FOR ABEYANCE

Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (Commission) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Corporate Energy Buyers Association (CEBA) respectfully submits this Answer in support of the motions for abeyance submitted separately by California Independent System Operator Corporation (CAISO);2 certain CAISO transmission owners (Joint PTOs);3 DCR Transmission, L.L.C.;4 and the Cities of Anaheim, Azusa, Banning, Colton, Pasadena, and Riverside, California (Six Cities)5 (collectively, Movants). Movants ask the Commission to hold this proceeding in abeyance by 90 days and, in so doing, extend certain deadlines for responding to the June 18, 2026 Order to Show Cause (Show Cause Order).6 The issues raised in the Show Cause Order raise complex and substantial issues that require due consideration to craft solutions that are appropriately tailored to the region. Granting the requested abeyance will promote administrative efficiencies by allowing time for CAISO and stakeholders—including CEBA and its members—to work collaboratively, as opposed to a rushed proposal that would unnecessarily shift stakeholder debate to the Commission docket.

5 Six Cities August 3, 2026 Conditional Motion for Abeyance. CEBA does not take a position on Six Cities’ related July 31, 2026 Motion to Rescind. 6 Cal. Indep. Sys. Operator Corp., 195 FERC ¶ 61,214 (2026).

Lastly, the requested abeyance strikes an appropriate balance: a 90-day extension would not materially impede the Commission’s objectives for advancing reforms quickly yet offers significant benefits in terms of enabling proposals to receive sufficient stakeholder feedback to promote both commercial viability and legal durability of the proposals that CAISO and CAISO transmission owners will each file in mid-November.

For these reasons, CEBA respectfully requests that the Commission grant these motions in an expedited manner.
