In the June 18 Order, the Commission set aside the December 18 Order, in part, concluding that “the Tariff is unjust and unreasonable because it does not include transmission services that reflect that Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of load with BTMG are willing and able to limit their energy withdrawals from the transmission system under certain conditions.”11 The Commission indicated that it would be unjust and unreasonable to require Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of load with BTMG to take Network Integration Transmission Service (“NITS”), without the option to instead take the alternative transmission services that FERC adopted in the December 18 Order for Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-Located Load.12

In response to these directives, PJM proposes to amend the Tariff to add Eligible Load as a defined term.16 PJM also proposes to add the term “Eligible Generating Facility,” which PJM says would “encompass both a Co-Located Generating Facility or Retail BTMG.”17 Further, PJM offers a number of other conforming changes to the Tariff to reflect the adoption of these new definitions.18

AMP supports the Commission’s decision to extend eligibility for the new transmission services to Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of load with BTMG. AMP is concerned, however, that PJM’s proposed Tariff provisions – particularly the proposed definition of “Eligible Generating Facility” and related conforming changes could limit eligibility for the new transmission services in contravention of the Commission’s June 18 Order.

II. PROTEST
A. The Commission should direct PJM to revise the proposed definition
of Eligible Generating Facility because it could exclude otherwise
eligible Non-Retail BTMG from taking new transmission services.
The PJM Filing proposes to define Eligible Load as “Co-Located Load or load connected to Behind the Meter Generation.”19 This definition appears to comply with the Commission’s directives in the June 18 Order inasmuch as it would encompass all “load

As noted above, AMP and AMP’s load-serving utility members own and operate generating units in the PJM region that qualify as Non-Retail BTMG. It is not clear – and PJM does not explain – why such units should be categorically excluded from taking advantage of the benefits that may be available to co-located generation (subject to the associated requirements), which appears to be the result under PJM’s proposal. AMP recognizes that an Eligible Customer taking service on behalf of load with Non-Retail BTMG may have little incentive to utilize one of the three new types of transmission service to the extent that all or a portion of a municipal, cooperative, or electric distribution company generator is engaged in netting under the existing Non- Retail BTMG Tariff provisions. There may be circumstances, however, in which a generating facility that qualifies as Non-Retail BTMG might wish to utilize one of the new transmission services. For example, section 34.3 of the PJM Tariff caps the total amount of Non-Retail BTMG in the PJM Region eligible for netting in any specific year, and sets a maximum ceiling of 3,000 MW (unless increased through a section 205 filing).27 If and when the netting cap is reached, there may be a greater incentive for generators that qualify as Non-Retail BTMG to consider one of the new transmission services. Excluding Non-Retail BTMG from this opportunity would frustrate the Commission’s objective of making the new transmission service options available to load with BTMG.28
