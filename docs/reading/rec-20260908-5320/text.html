C. ATC’s local planning reforms exclude pro forma agreements although
ATC requires the parties to execute these contracts, further
exacerbating the risk of undue discrimination and preferential
treatment.
ATC’s proposal contemplates that transmission customers requesting transmission service for an ATCLLC Large Load will be required to enter into a Minimum Transmission Charge Agreement (“MTCA”) and one or more Large Load Project Commitment Agreements (“LLPCAs”). Microsoft protested these agreements for its Racine County data center campus submitted by ATC in currently pending Docket No. ER26-3265 because as- filed, those agreements contained blatant errors and deficiencies, and raised cost-of-service and open access red flags. As in this proceeding, the agreements at issue in Docket No. ER26-3265 were negotiated without any input or participation from Microsoft, and the consequences of this fact are axiomatic in their structural shortcomings. Microsoft understands that ATC’s preference is that its transmission customers remain subject to the same terms and conditions. Yet Microsoft cannot reconcile this stated

preference with ATC’s decision to omit the MTCA and the LLPCA as pro forma contracts in Attachment FF-ATCLLC. These agreements establish the substantive rights and obligations that will govern the planning, construction, financing, and operation of facilities necessary to serve large loads, and ultimately their terms will flow through to the large load retail customer. Their absence from the Tariff is significant. By requiring customers to enter into agreements that are not part of a generally applicable Tariff in order to receive electric service, ATC introduces a level of discretion that creates a substantial risk that similarly situated customers may be offered materially different terms and conditions. The danger is particularly acute when the agreements are negotiated exclusively between ATC and its affiliates (like WEPCo) without any input from the applicable large load customer, as is the case for the agreements currently pending in Docket No. ER26-3265. The Commission recognizes that pro forma contracts have a role in ensuring that transmission providers administer jurisdictional services on a transparent, consistent, and non-discriminatory basis. Recently, in PJM Interconnection, L.L.C., the Commission explained that where specific contractual provisions provide benefits to interconnection customers, those benefits should be made available through a pro forma agreement “to ensure that all similarly situated interconnection customers are treated on a consistent and fair basis.”33 Likewise, in Southwest Power Pool, Inc., the Commission reminded the grid operator that if it elects to offer a particular form of service, the applicable terms and benefits should be memorialized in a pro forma agreement “to ensure that all similarly-situated interconnection customers are treated on a consistent and fair basis.”34 These precedents  recognize the concerns here—

transparency and comparability are undermined when contracts for a tariff service are negotiated outside of a generally applicable tariff framework. Because ATC intends the MTCA and the LLPCA to become a standard feature of local transmission planning and service, the terms governing those arrangements should be set in Commission-approved pro forma agreements rather than through individualized negotiations or website-posted contracts that may change over time.

The Commission reached a similar conclusion in MidAmerican Energy Co., where it rejected non-conforming provisions in an interconnection agreement, explaining that the insurance requirements the transmission provider wished to adopt for one interconnection customer should instead be incorporated into the pro forma Large Generator Interconnection Agreement.35 The Commission emphasized there that pro forma agreements are necessary to ensure that all similarly situated customers are “treated on a consistent and fair basis.”36 That safeguard is particularly relevant here because the agreements contemplated by ATC will govern matters that may ultimately determine a large retail customer’s financial exposure, project development obligations, termination rights, security requirements, and long-term service costs. Without Commission-approved pro forma agreements, large retail customers have no assurance that these provisions will be applied consistently from one large project to the next.

In the alternative, Microsoft respectfully requests that the Commission reject the proposed tariff changes without prejudice to be refiled.
