II. 
PROPOSED INTERCONNECTION PROCESS REFORMS 
 
The Show Cause Order identifies concern with the lack of “clear and consistent” tariff 
provisions governing requests for service by Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads.  SDG&E 
addresses this concern directly through the development of new and enhanced procedures 
governing service requests by Eligible Customers under the TO Tariff.  SDG&E’s proposed tariff 
revisions do not ask the Commission or stakeholders to learn entirely new vocabulary.  Instead, 
SDG&E’s framework borrows the familiar architecture of generator interconnection, which the 
Commission and market participants already understand well and that has been found to be just 
and reasonable, and applies it to Eligible Customers requesting service on behalf of large loads.  
Elements of SDG&E’s proposed framework include: (1) an application process featuring a time-
stamped queue, a scoping meeting, and a Site Control requirement including allowance for 
deposits in lieu of Site Control; (2) sequential study phases with defined timelines and deposits, 
and a single amendment right; and, (3) pro forma interconnection agreement with milestones, 
options for limited operation pending completion of upgrades, financial security and possible 
withdrawals, and queue forfeiture upon default. 
 
The proposed framework also reflects the complementary roles of SDG&E as a PTO and 
the CAISO.  SDG&E owns the relevant transmission facilities and will administer the 
interconnection process established under its TO Tariff.  The CAISO operates the CAISO 
Controlled Grid and is vested with responsibility for matters assigned to it under the Transmission 
Control Agreement and the CAISO Tariff.  For instance, interconnection service under the TO 
Tariff is not the same thing as and does not supplant transmission service governed by CAISO’s 
tariff; rather, the interconnection customer requesting transmission service over the CAISO 
Controlled Grid must take such service from CAISO through a Scheduling Coordinator.  
SDG&E’s proposed pro forma Interconnection Agreement confirms this point (Article 4.3).  
Additionally, the proposed procedures provide for coordination between SDG&E and the CAISO 
where necessary to secure the CAISO’s concurrence that the transmission upgrades identified by 
SDG&E through its study process are not duplicative of transmission upgrades the CAISO has 
identified through its annual TPP, and that the upgrades and operational requirements identified 
by SDG&E through its study process are consistent with safe and reliable operation of the CAISO 
Controlled Grid (pro forma IA, Article 5.5.2). 
 
Importantly, the proposed revisions are limited to matters properly addressed through 
SDG&E’s Commission-jurisdictional TO Tariff.  They do not alter the respective federal and state

A. 
Definition of Large Load 
 
The Show Cause Order at P 57 states that CAISO and PTO tariffs “lack a definition of 
large load, as a new category of load[,]” and suggests that “[a]n appropriate definition, including 
a load size and interconnection voltage threshold, should be based on the characteristics of 
CAISO’s transmission system.”  The Show Cause Order suggests that a reasonable definition 
would be “a new commercial or industrial customer, located at a single site behind one or more 
points of interconnection, and that has a peak load of 50 MW or greater, interconnects to the 
transmission system at a voltage level of greater than 69 kV, and is not part of a co-location 
arrangement.”  Rather than adopt the Show Cause Order’s definition, SDG&E proposes to include 
the following definition of “Large Load” in its TO Tariff: “a new customer, located at a single site 
behind one or more points of interconnection to the CAISO Controlled Grid, and that has a peak 
load of 50 MW or greater.”4   
 
SDG&E’s proposed definition differs from the definition in the Show Cause Order in three 
respects.  First, in SDG&E’s view, the Show Cause Order’s call out to “commercial and industrial 
customers” is not necessary because, as a practical matter, SDG&E expects that loads of 50 MW 
or greater will likely fall into these two general categories of customers.  Second, SDG&E believes 
that the express call-out to the CAISO Controlled Grid in its definition is preferable to the general 
reference to the “transmission system” in the Show Cause Order definition.  This call-out is 
necessary because some sub-transmission facilities within the CAISO Balancing Authority Area 
are operated at voltages greater than 69 kV but are not part of the CAISO Controlled Grid, while 
certain of SDG&E’s transmission facilities that are part of the CAISO Controlled Grid operate at 
69 kV.  Thus, the Show Cause Order definition is both over- and under-inclusive relative to 
SDG&E’s proposed definition.  Finally, SDG&E’s proposed definition does not exclude Large 
Loads that may be co-located with generation.  In SDG&E’s view, such an exclusion could limit 
the ratepayer protections that are a key feature of SDG&E’s proposed framework.

Intake and Initiating the Study Process

reforms to this existing framework, including through the following modifications: 
 
• 
Rolling applications.  The existing TO Tariff interconnection process is serial in nature 
and does not include any “windows” during which applications must be submitted.  
Accordingly, SDG&E already accepts applications at any time on a rolling basis.  To 
address the Commission’s suggestion regarding the need for an application process that 
accepts applications on a rolling basis, SDG&E proposes to maintain its current serial study 
process that accepts and studies each application at any time on a rolling basis, with priority 
based on the date SDG&E, as the PTO, receives the application.  SDG&E has modified 
Section 10.2 of the TO Tariff to state clearly that applications shall be accepted on a rolling 
basis.  As part of its framework to deter speculative or duplicative requests discussed in the 
Readiness Requirements section below, SDG&E proposes a $50,000 non-refundable 
application fee, also in Section 10.2. 
• 
Scoping Meeting.  SDG&E has added language to Section 10.3 of the TO Tariff 
establishing a Scoping Meeting that may occur following receipt of the application fee but 
before commencement of the System Impact Study.  The Scoping Meeting is proposed to 
occur at the request of either SDG&E or the Interconnection Customer for the purpose of 
discussing interconnection options and refining information and models. 
• 
Single pro forma Study Agreement.  SDG&E proposes a pro forma Study Agreement 
that is referenced in and attached to its TO Tariff (Appendix XIII).  The Study Agreement 
establishes the terms and conditions applicable to the System Impact Study and, as 
applicable, subsequent Facilities and Electromagnetic Transient (“EMT”) Studies.  In 
particular, the Study Agreement treats sixty (60) calendar days as the default good faith 
estimate for each of the System Impact and Facilities Studies.  Appendix XIII, Section 8.0. 
The use of a single pro forma Study Agreement streamlines the study process by 
eliminating the need to separately tender, negotiate, and execute a new agreement between 
study phases, thereby reducing administrative delays and facilitating more timely 
progression of the required studies.  In addition, assuming Commission acceptance of the 
pro forma Study Agreement, execution of conforming Study Agreements would not 
require individual filings with, and acceptance by, the Commission.  This will allow the 
applicable study to proceed without the need for specific Commission action, thereby 
reducing potential delays for customers.  After providing the required notice and study

2. 
Readiness Requirements 
 
To deter speculative or duplicative requests and ensure that projects progressing through 
the study process continue to demonstrate readiness, SDG&E proposes a readiness framework that 
includes both upfront financial commitments and ongoing project-development milestones.  
Specifically, SDG&E proposes a $50,000 non-refundable application fee, an initial $100,000

proceed with a Facilities Study, and an additional $200,000 study deposit if SDG&E determines 
that an EMT Study is required.5  Each study deposit will be applied toward the actual cost of the 
applicable study and will be subject to reconciliation in accordance with the Study Agreement. 
 
SDG&E would also require an ECLL applicant to identify a definitive Point of 
Interconnection to the CAISO Controlled Grid.  In addition, the proposed TO Tariff revisions 
include provisions governing an applicant’s demonstration of Site Control, including a limited 
opportunity to provide documentation and a deposit in lieu of such demonstration, which will 
expire within 180 Calendar Days of the effective date of its Interconnection Agreement.  SDG&E 
has reflected these modifications in proposed Sections 10.5 and 10.6 of the TO Tariff.
