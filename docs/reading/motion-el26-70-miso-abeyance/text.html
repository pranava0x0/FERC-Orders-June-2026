II. 
MISO SHOW CAUSE PROCEEDING  
The Show Cause Order identifies an unprecedented increase in requests for transmission 
service to serve large load, as well as the growing interest in the co-location of loads with 
generation facilities (i.e., co-location arrangements), while acknowledging the challenges 
associated with providing the transmission service to reliably serve the ongoing influx of large

III. 
REQUEST FOR ABEYANCE 
The Show Cause Order demonstrates the high priority the Commission has placed on 
ensuring that jurisdictional regional grid operators work quickly to meet the challenge of 
integrating large loads to support the artificial intelligence and manufacturing related national 
security and economic opportunity goals.  Notwithstanding the need for speed, the Commission 
invited MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners to seek to hold the show cause proceeding in 
abeyance, for up to 90 days, to provide MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners time while they 
work through the stakeholder process to develop FPA section 205 filings to revise the Tariff to 
address the issues raised in the Show Cause Order.13  MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners 
agree that an aggressive timeline is needed to address these issues, and that the breadth and 
complexity of the concerns raised in the Show Cause Order will require a more expedited 
stakeholder process and feedback loop than is typical before filing solutions with the Commission.

MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners appreciate the important role that states play in 
the MISO region, especially in managing the ultimate retail costs to customers and affordability.  
It is also because of the MISO states’ continued focus on resource adequacy that the MISO region 
is not facing an immediate crisis related to large load integration as has been experienced in other 
parts of the nation.  MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners are committed to continued 
collaboration with the states as solutions are finalized and brought before the Commission. 
Given the volume of work to be accomplished and the need to engage stakeholders, MISO 
and the MISO Transmission Owners respectfully request that the Commission grant a 90-day 
abeyance to allow MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners to bring regionally tailored solutions 
to the Commission to address the issues identified in the Show Cause Order.   The abeyance will 
allow MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners the opportunity to leverage the LLWG further 
to collaborate with the states and stakeholders to develop robust, durable solutions to meet the 
needs in the MISO region.  Importantly, MISO is not starting from scratch—the current MISO 
stakeholder process has already developed several prioritized proposals to address large load 
integration, as further described below.  MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners are confident 
that the abeyance approach will provide the most successful path forward to achieve the 
Commission’s desired outcomes to ensure the integration of large loads in an efficient, reliable, 
and affordable manner. 
Although MISO, the MISO Transmission Owners, the OMS, and stakeholders have been 
working on several speed to power initiatives, the 90-day abeyance requested herein is essential to

permit MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners, in collaboration with member states and other 
stakeholders, to adjust planned solutions to fully address the Commission’s directions and, where 
necessary, to develop additional Tariff revisions responsive to the Show Cause Order. The 
challenges and opportunities presented by large loads are multi-faceted and implicate numerous 
parts of MISO’s Tariff.  Therefore, while speed is important, it also is critical to ensure that 
individual solutions align properly with each other and with existing MISO procedures.  This is a 
complex task. 
Good cause exists to hold the show cause proceeding in abeyance.  MISO has established 
a dedicated stakeholder forum to address the issues identified in the Show Cause Order associated 
with the reliable integration of large loads.  Holding the show cause proceeding in abeyance will 
promote administrative efficiency, allow for more carefully-crafted solutions, and avoid 
unnecessary litigation of matters that would be more appropriately addressed through the ongoing 
MISO stakeholder process.  Ultimately, granting the abeyance will not prejudice any party, as all 
parties will have an opportunity to participate in MISO’s public stakeholder process and intervene 
in future FPA section 205 filings.

MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners will address the issues raised in the Show

period.14  Below is a description of the FPA section 205 filings that MISO and the MISO 
Transmission Owners are currently contemplating.  As shown below, some proposals have already

undergone significant stakeholder vetting and as such, will be filed prior to the end of the 90-day 
abeyance.  Other proposals are still in their infancy and may require the entire 90-day abeyance.     
First, MISO expects to submit tariff changes establishing a new Zero Injection Generator 
Interconnection Agreement (“ZGIA”) and related study process to facilitate faster resource 
additions when those resources are designated to serve load at the same substation, thereby 
accelerating the addition of generation resources that have little or no impact on the Transmission 
System.  With these proposed Tariff changes, the ZGIA will partly address several of the issues 
raised by the Commission related to co-location of generation and large loads by proposing a 
permanent solution for generation resources seeking to interconnect to the Transmission System 
at the same substation as an associated load.15  MISO expects to submit the ZGIA filing by the end 
of August. 
 
Second, MISO anticipates submitting Tariff changes establishing new large load 
Interconnection Reliability Requirements (“IRR”).  The proposed IRR Tariff changes will establish 
operational requirements for transmission customers serving large loads to ensure visibility and 
reliable operation of the Transmission System.16  Specifically, the IRR filing will include 
requirements for large loads to provide basic facility data, modeling, telemetry, and forecasting 
information.17  In addition, the proposed IRR Tariff changes will provide detail regarding Phasor 
Measurement Unit (“PMU”) requirements, as well as requirements for ramp and ride-though for 
certain types of large loads.

Third, at the July 16, 2026, LLWG meeting, MISO presented and stakeholders accepted a 
formal issue submission and conceptual design for a load addition and resource study (“LARS”) 
process.18  LARS will assess the transmission infrastructure needed to meet new large loads and 
new generation in an efficient process that can be executed on a shorter timeline, further 
supplementing the opportunities available for members to facilitate new large spot loads 
effectively.  This process is expected to position MISO and its members to ensure resource 
adequacy and protect customers from unnecessary investments relating to the uncertainty of spot 
large load siting. 
More specifically, LARS will analyze generation and transmission using the same study 
assumptions applied in the standard generator interconnection process and MISO’s MTEP.  The 
studies will be run in parallel, and will finish at approximately the same time—within 120 days—
with the load/transmission model incorporating both the planned generation and planned load.19  
LARS, as currently conceived, will address similar issues to those the Show Cause Order identified 
related to interconnection customers serving electrically proximate large load or co-located load.20  
MISO will continue to develop the LARS process through the LLWG and expects to submit the 
LARS filing by November 16, 2026, or earlier, if feasible.

V. 
REQUEST FOR SHORTENED COMMENT PERIOD AND EXPEDITED TREATMENT 
MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners respectfully request that the Commission issue 
an order granting the requested 90-day abeyance on an expedited basis by August 14, 2026.  
Prompt action on this Motion will provide certainty to MISO and its stakeholders regarding the 
need to submit a response to the Show Cause Order by August 17, 2026, and avoid the expenditure 
of time and resources on preparing filings that may prove unnecessary.  With such certainty, MISO 
and its stakeholders can confidently spend critical time and resources developing the FPA section 
205 filings discussed herein.    
To enable the Commission’s consideration of this Motion on an expedited basis, MISO and 
the MISO Transmission Owners also request that the Commission establish a 5-day comment 
period for this Motion—i.e., by August 10, 2026.  The Commission may shorten or eliminate 
answer periods altogether for good cause shown.25  Good cause exists to establish a 5-day answer 
period and grant expedited treatment of this Motion because the Show Cause Order’s compliance 
deadline is fast approaching and parties need certainty regarding the compliance timeline.
