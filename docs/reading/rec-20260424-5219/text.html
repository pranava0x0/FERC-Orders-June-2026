I. ANSWER TO PROTESTS AND COMMENTS
A. Behind-the-Meter Generation
In its 60-Day Compliance Filing, PJM proposes revisions to the Behind the Meter
Generation (“BTMG”) provisions in its Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to comply with the Commission’s December 18, 2025 Order, which directed PJM to propose a new materiality threshold for Retail BTMG, establish a three-year transition period, and provide legacy treatment for certain existing contractual arrangements above the new threshold.3 PJM proposes to establish a materiality threshold of up to 50 MW for Retail BTMG eligibility based on the cumulative nameplate rating of generation used to serve retail load by a Network Customer4 at the at the same electrical location, excluding qualifying backup generation, while otherwise preserving existing substantive netting rules.5  Several commenters argue that a nameplate-based threshold is inappropriate and urge the Commission to adopt a materially higher threshold, such as 200 MW.6

The Commission should reject those arguments because the 50 MW threshold strikes the appropriate balance between allowing continued use of Retail BTMG and appropriate limitations to ensure reliability.  The Order expressly directed PJM to propose a new MW threshold for Retail BTMG and explained that such a threshold should reduce the reliability and resource adequacy

B. Transmission Services for Co-Located Load
Consistent with the Order, PJM proposes Tariff revisions establishing the transmission- service framework applicable to Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co- Located Load, including three new transmission service options in addition to Network Integration Transmission Service (“NITS”).10

1. The Commission Should not Mandate a Combined Interconnection
and Transmission Study Process for Co-Located Loads and Generation
In its comments, Invenergy Renewables LLC and Invenergy Thermal LLC argue that the Commission and PJM should clarify how the transmission and generator interconnection study processes can work in tandem.11  In Invenergy’s view, developers and lenders will be reluctant to commit to projects without both sets of study results showing that the project is viable and identifying expected upgrade costs, and coordinated studies would better align generation and load, improve efficiency, reduce system costs, and provide the certainty needed for co-location projects to be financed and developed at scale.12

It is unclear what Invenergy is asking for here, but to the extent it is requesting that the Commission impose a combined transmission and interconnection study process for Co-Located Load and generation, the Commission should decline.  In its initial brief in the paper hearing process, PJM has already proposed a coordinated load and generation study process, with the PJM Transmission Owners conducting load integration studies and PJM performing generator interconnection and transmission service studies, as necessary.  Requiring PJM to administer a single study process goes beyond the issues set for hearing by the Commission in the Order, as well as the scope of compliance directed by the Commission in the instant proceeding. Finally, end-use load is still retail load and existing load interconnection rules set forth in state jurisdictional tariffs and agreements cannot be ignored or sidestepped.  However, there is no need for the Commission to entangle itself in questions regarding the division between its jurisdiction over interstate transmission matters and state jurisdiction over retail service in this proceeding.  The coordinated study approach proposed by PJM will provide a just and reasonable opportunity for Co-Located Load to interconnect and assess the appropriate type and level of transmission service that it wishes to obtain.
