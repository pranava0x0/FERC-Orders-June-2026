To the extent required, the PJM Transmission Owners respectfully move for leave to answer.  The 
Commission’s procedural rules generally prohibit answers to protests.  Specifically, Rule 213 states that “[a]n answer 
may not be made to a protest . . . unless otherwise ordered by the decisional authority.”  18 C.F.R. § 385.213(a)(2).  
The Commission has the discretion to waive its prohibition of answers to protests for good cause shown, i.e., to assist 
the Commission’s understanding and resolution of the issues raised or to ensure a complete record.  See, e.g., Pioneer 
Transmission, LLC v. N. Ind. Pub. Serv. Co., 140 FERC ¶ 61,057, at P 94 (2012); Midwest Indep. Transmission Sys. 
Operator, Inc., 120 FERC ¶ 61,083, at P 23 (2007); Morgan Stanley Cap. Grp., Inc. v. N.Y. Indep. Sys. Operator, Inc., 
93 FERC ¶ 61,017, at 61,036 (2000); CNG Transmission Corp., 89 FERC ¶ 61,100, at 61,287 n.11 (1999).  This 
Answer is submitted to ensure a complete and accurate record.  Accordingly, good cause exists to permit this answer.

I. 
ANSWER TO PROTESTS AND COMMENTS 
A. 
Behind-the-Meter Generation 
In its 60-Day Compliance Filing, PJM proposes revisions to the Behind the Meter 
Generation (“BTMG”) provisions in its Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to comply 
with the Commission’s December 18, 2025 Order, which directed PJM to propose a new 
materiality threshold for Retail BTMG, establish a three-year transition period, and provide legacy 
treatment for certain existing contractual arrangements above the new threshold.3 
PJM proposes to establish a materiality threshold of up to 50 MW for Retail BTMG 
eligibility based on the cumulative nameplate rating of generation used to serve retail load by a 
Network Customer4 at the at the same electrical location, excluding qualifying backup generation, 
while otherwise preserving existing substantive netting rules.5  Several commenters argue that a 
nameplate-based threshold is inappropriate and urge the Commission to adopt a materially higher 
threshold, such as 200 MW.6   
The Commission should reject those arguments because the 50 MW threshold strikes the 
appropriate balance between allowing continued use of Retail BTMG and appropriate limitations 
to ensure reliability.  The Order expressly directed PJM to propose a new MW threshold for Retail 
BTMG and explained that such a threshold should reduce the reliability and resource adequacy

towards completion. 
 The “existing contract” exception to the application of the new Retail BTMG rules 
should be construed broadly to cover a wide range of contracts and recognition of

B. 
Transmission Services for Co-Located Load 
Consistent with the Order, PJM proposes Tariff revisions establishing the transmission-
service framework applicable to Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-
Located Load, including three new transmission service options in addition to Network Integration 
Transmission Service (“NITS”).10   
1. 
The Commission Should not Mandate a Combined Interconnection 
and Transmission Study Process for Co-Located Loads and Generation 
In its comments, Invenergy Renewables LLC and Invenergy Thermal LLC argue that the 
Commission and PJM should clarify how the transmission and generator interconnection study 
processes can work in tandem.11  In Invenergy’s view, developers and lenders will be reluctant to 
commit to projects without both sets of study results showing that the project is viable and 
identifying expected upgrade costs, and coordinated studies would better align generation and 
load, improve efficiency, reduce system costs, and provide the certainty needed for co-location 
projects to be financed and developed at scale.12 
Invenergy’s comments primarily address PJM’s initial brief in the paper hearing process 
imposed by the Commission in Docket Nos. EL25-49, et al.,13 and the PJM Transmission Owners 
will respond fully to those comments in that proceeding.  However, because Invenergy also

It is unclear what Invenergy is asking for here, but to the extent it is requesting that the 
Commission impose a combined transmission and interconnection study process for Co-Located 
Load and generation, the Commission should decline.  In its initial brief in the paper hearing 
process, PJM has already proposed a coordinated load and generation study process, with the PJM 
Transmission Owners conducting load integration studies and PJM performing generator 
interconnection and transmission service studies, as necessary.  Requiring PJM to administer a 
single study process goes beyond the issues set for hearing by the Commission in the Order, as 
well as the scope of compliance directed by the Commission in the instant proceeding.   
Finally, end-use load is still retail load and existing load interconnection rules set forth in 
state jurisdictional tariffs and agreements cannot be ignored or sidestepped.  However, there is no 
need for the Commission to entangle itself in questions regarding the division between its 
jurisdiction over interstate transmission matters and state jurisdiction over retail service in this 
proceeding.  The coordinated study approach proposed by PJM will provide a just and reasonable 
opportunity for Co-Located Load to interconnect and assess the appropriate type and level of 
transmission service that it wishes to obtain.

3. 
Permitting Interim NITS Customers to Serve On-Site Load with On-
Site Generation During a Curtailment 
The Data Center Coalition and Crusoe argue that PJM’s proposed Interim NITS framework 
is unworkable unless a curtailed customer may continue to serve on-site load with co-located 
generation.21  They contend that curtailment should limit withdrawals from the transmission 
system, not prevent a customer from using its own co-located generation or Retail BTMG, because 
otherwise Interim NITS would provide little or no practical value and significantly undermine the 
viability of co-location arrangements.22  They therefore urge the Commission to clarify that an 
Interim NITS customer may continue to use co-located generator during curtailment, including 
while the generator remains in the interconnection study process.23
