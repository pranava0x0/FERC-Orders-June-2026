II. PROTEST
A. PJM’s Proposal Unlawfully Requires the Mandatory and Involuntary Designation of End-Use Retail Customers (Eligible New Large Loads) to Provide a FERC- Jurisdictional Wholesale Rate Service (IRAS), and the Compulsory Provision of that FERC-Jurisdictional Wholesale Rate Service to an RTO, Pursuant to FERC- Approved Rates, Terms, and Conditions in a FERC-Approved Tariff.

The acute appellate risk presented by the IRAS Filing can be summarized by the following five sequential points:

1. IRAS is a new wholesale rate service, subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Commission, with rates, terms, and conditions established by the Commission under the Commission-approved PJM Tariff;

2. PJM’s Tariff language makes clear that Eligible New Large Loads are the entities that provide IRAS as a wholesale rate service—not the Electric Distributor or Load Serving Entity;

3. PJM’s Tariff language requires the mandatory and involuntary designation of Eligible New Large Loads to provide IRAS to PJM, and the compulsory provision of IRAS to PJM when called upon by PJM (through an implementing directive issued to the applicable Electric Distributor);

4. Eligible New Large Loads have no contractual privity with PJM, are not subject to a FERC-jurisdictional load interconnection process, and are not public utilities under FPA section 201;12 and

5. The FPA does not grant the Commission the legal authority to force end-use retail customers to be designated to provide a FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service, or to compel the provision of that wholesale rate service, pursuant to a FERC- approved Tariff.

The absence of these jurisdictional and contractual “hooks” leads to only one possible outcome—PJM’s proposal would use the Commission’s general rate and transmission jurisdiction under FPA section 201 to directly impose requirements on entities that are excluded from that jurisdiction by the same statutory section, and have not voluntarily agreed to become subject to those requirements.  For the reasons described below in the next section, this is beyond the Commission’s legal authority under the FPA, and is not legally durable on appeal.

v. The FPA does not grant the Commission the legal authority to force end-
use retail customers to be designated to provide a FERC-jurisdictional
wholesale rate service, or to compel the provision of that wholesale rate
service, pursuant to a FERC-approved Tariff.
Simply put, in the IRAS Filing PJM is asking the Commission to do something that it is not statutorily permitted to do under the FPA.  As the D.C. Circuit has noted on more than one occasion, “as a federal agency, FERC is a creature of statute, having no constitutional or common law existence or authority, but only those authorities conferred upon it by Congress.”38  There is nothing in the FPA that grants the Commission the legal authority to force end-use retail customers (in this case, Eligible New Large Loads) to be designated to provide a FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service (IRAS), or to compel the provision of that wholesale rate service, pursuant to a FERC-approved tariff.  PJM cites to two primary legal authorities in support of the IRAS Filing, but as explained below, neither of these supports the action that PJM is suggesting the Commission take here.

First, PJM cites to the Supreme Court’s ruling in EPSA, correctly noting that there “the Supreme Court held that the Commission’s regulation of demand response ‘addresses—and
