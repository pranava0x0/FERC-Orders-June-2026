POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF  
THE ELECTRIC POWER SUPPLY ASSOCIATION 
In accordance with the July 30, 2026 notice issued by the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or the “Commission”),1 the Electric Power Supply 
Association (“EPSA”)2 submits these post-conference comments following up on the 
Chairman- and Commissioner-led technical conference held on July 23, 2026 (the 
“Technical Conference”), to discuss the governance and stakeholder processes of PJM 
Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”).  EPSA appreciates the Commission’s efforts to address 
these matters and this opportunity for input on the important issues discussed at the 
Technical Conference.  EPSA is submitting these comments to address two questions 
posed by the Commission about the allocation of filing rights under Section 205 of the 
Federal Power Act (the “FPA”),3 and to underscore the critical importance of ensuring 
that any attempted reallocation of such rights is fully in accordance with the statutory 
framework of the FPA.  EPSA also supports and incorporates by reference the

Introduction 
 
1. My name is Joseph T. Kelliher.  I am the principal of Joseph Kelliher Consulting, an advisory 
and consulting business. 
2. My Affidavit supports the Post-Conference Comments of the Electric Power Supply 
Association in the PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) Governance and Stakeholder 
Reforms proceeding initiated by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission” 
or “FERC”) earlier this year.  In this Affidavit I review Sections 205 and 206 of the Federal 
Power Act, previous Commission approvals and rejections of state requests for Section 205 
filing rights, relevant legal precedent, and discuss whether the Commission has the legal 
authority to grant requests by the Organization of PJM States, Inc. (“OPSI”) and states for 
Section 205 filing rights.  My conclusion is the Commission is barred from granting states 
direct or indirect Section 205 filing rights by the Federal Power Act.

stakeholder process were developed for a different era.”15  It may be a different era, but it is

13. The courts have also held that states cannot exercise indirect Section 205 filing rights.  This
