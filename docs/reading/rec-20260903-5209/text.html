COMMENTS OF CALIBRANT ENERGY

Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (Commission) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Calibrant Energy (Calibrant) respectfully submits these comments requesting clarifications of PJM Interconnection, LLC’s (PJM) proposed revisions to its Open Access Transmission Tariff (Tariff) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (RAA) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) rules and procedures.2 Calibrant seeks only a narrow subset of clarifications to provide the regulatory certainty needed to ensure that the IRAS proposal incentivizes the entry of any and all resources that are able to help address PJM’s resource adequacy challenges.

Calibrant develops energy generation and storage assets throughout the United States, including in the PJM footprint. Calibrant participated in PJM’s stakeholder process to ensure that

I. REQUIRED CLARIFICATIONS
A. Calibrant requests clarification that incremental Demand Resource capacity at prior-participating locations may provide BYONC.

PJM proposes to allow Demand Resources or DER Capacity Aggregation Resources to serve as BYONC under two circumstances: (1) the Demand Resource or DER Capacity Aggregation Resource is composed of locations that did not previously support a Capacity Resource (or participate as Peak Shaving Adjustment or Price Responsive Demand), or (2) the Demand Resource or DER Capacity Aggregation Resource is composed of “any location not registered in or prior to the 2026/2027 Delivery Year at which an incremental amount at that location is the result of the registration of new generation or storage assets at the location” (at least until the capacity market restabilizes, resetting the deadline from which prior-participation is measured).3 This second participation pathway is not clear. In order to give this provision

independent effect and based on PJM’s position in stakeholder discussions, Calibrant believes that PJM intended this provision to allow prior-participating Demand Resources and Distributed Energy Resource (DER) Capacity Aggregation Resource to offer incremental BYONC capacity where enabled by registration of new generation or storage resources at the location. Clarifying this intent will incent the new entry of reliable capacity supported by new behind-the-meter resources and avoid otherwise unnecessary involuntary curtailment exposure. In addition, Calibrant requests clarification that the “incremental amount” of Demand Resource capacity supported by installation of new behind-the-meter resources will be measured against the Demand Resource’s most recent participation level.

Absent such clarification, PJM’s proposed second qualification pathway for Demand Resources is confusing.4 It is not clear how new generation or storage could provide incremental capacity at a location that has never before participated. “Incremental” usually would imply additional capacity on top of existing capacity. Otherwise, it would just be new Demand Resource or DER capacity, not incremental capacity. Moreover, such new Demand Resource or DER capacity can already participate under PJM’s other proposed participation pathways for locations that have not previously participated in the PJM capacity construct—whether or not supported by new generation or storage. To give the incremental participation provision independent meaning, it must allow for some form of participation that adds to the other option, i.e., at some locations that have participated in the RPM in prior Delivery Years. Thus, the most natural reading of the language is that the limit on prior year registration is a bar on BYONC qualification only to the extent underlying storage and generation assets previously supported the registration (i.e., where such storage and generation is not new). B. Calibrant requests clarification that a New Large Load and BYONC’s
obligations under the IRAS program extend at most only through the required 10 years of RPM participation.

PJM proposes to require that “BYONC that is in an arrangement with a New Large Load must remain in such arrangement with that New Large Load unless and until the New Large Load is permanently out-of-service.”9 Calibrant seeks clarification, based on PJM’s statements in its transmittal letter and other parts of the proposed Tariff, that this tariff proposal is meant only to prohibit BYONC transferability and not to suggest that a BYONC must remain in service for the life of the New Large Load that it serves.

PJM explains in its transmittal that it proposes to require a BYONC to remain in an arrangement with its New Large Load for the life of the load to effectuate its “propos[al] that once the subject of an arrangement[,] BYONC cannot change to a different New Large Load— i.e., BYONC is non-transferable.”10 That is, proposed Tariff Attachment DD-4 section VII(e) is
