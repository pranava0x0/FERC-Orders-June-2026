LIMITED COMMENTS OF NORTH CAROLINA ELECTRIC 
MEMBERSHIP CORPORATION 
Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure1 and 
the Commission’s Combined Notice of Filings #2 of August 13, 2026, North Carolina 
Electric Membership Corporation (“NCEMC”)2 submits the following comments on the 
filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) proposing revisions to its Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load 
Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”).3
PJM states that it will implement IRAS only when there are insufficient resources to 
reliably meet demand in real-time operations, and, when implanted, IRAS will enable 
PJM “to prioritize load reductions in zones/areas where New Large Loads have not 
brought their own new capacity or had their capacity needs covered through the 
Reliability Backstop Procurement (‘RBP’).”4

Thus, PJM is proposing IRAS as an operational tool to address the reality that 
New Large Loads, which are developing more quickly than generation and transmission 
can be built to provide them firm service, may want to connect to the grid in a provisional 
manner on an interim basis. Practically speaking, PJM seeks to create an optional path 
forward for New Large Loads that choose to be curtailable in the interim in exchange for 
quicker interconnection to the grid. As PJM notes, “the IRAS framework is not motivated 
by any desire to single out New Large Loads for reduction because of their size or 
vintage, but rather for the valid and legitimate reason that New Large Loads have led to 
periods of insufficient resource adequacy and should, justifiably, play a significant role in 
the remedy.”5 The IRAS framework is intended to allow PJM to identify the megawatt 
(MW) amount of New Large Load that exceeds new generation to ensure that in 
emergency conditions the New Large Load can be shut off before existing load is 
impacted. In order to avoid perceived jurisdictional limitations, PJM proposes that it 
would issue an IRAS Action directive to the Electric Distributor, who will then, in 
coordination with the relevant Load-Serving Entities (LSEs) in the zone/area and the 
appropriate state and/or Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authority (“RERRA”), 
determine which loads will be shut off. 
I.
NCEMC 
NCEMC is a generation and transmission cooperative responsible for the full or 
partial power supply requirements of its 25 members throughout the state of North 
Carolina. Those 25 distribution cooperatives, in turn, supply electricity to approximately 
one million homes, farms, and businesses in which more than 2.5 million North

II.
The Commission should direct PJM to clarify that an IRAS Action 
directive applies only to New Large Loads provisionally interconnected to 
the grid and only to the extent of a provisionally interconnected New 
Large Load’s load at the time of the IRAS Action directive. 
Buckeye Power, Inc. (“Buckeye”), East Kentucky Power Cooperative, Inc. 
(“EKPC”), and Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. (“SMECO”) are submitting  
filings responsive to PJM’s IRAS filing on even date herewith. NCEMC generally 
supports the need for greater clarity highlighted in the Buckeye, EKPC, and SMECO 
filings. NCEMC specifically supports a concern articulated by Buckeye in its filing. 
Buckeye, in its Comments filed contemporaneously with these NCEMC Comments, 
states as follows: 
PJM’s filing is largely silent as to exactly how it will 
determine the MW amount when issuing an IRAS Action. PJM

states that the “IRAS reduction MW quantity … will be based on 
the IRAS quantity PJM determined for the Delivery Year.” PJM’s 
filing describes how it determines the yearly IRAS quantity based 
on Eligible New Large Load Amounts, which will be less than or 
equal to the Electric Distributor’s Eligible New Large Load 
Amount for that year. But PJM’s Filing does not explain how PJM 
will determine the IRAS quantity it directs an Electric Distributor 
to cause to be reduced in a given emergency event in real-time.  
…  
Buckeye is concerned that directing the Electric Distributor to 
reduce a specific amount of MW quantity based on registered peak 
load of New Large Loads determined for the Delivery Year will 
not match the actual amount of load being used by the New Large 
Loads in real-time. If PJM were allowed to direct IRAS quantities 
in real-time that exceed the real-time demand of New Large Loads, 
this mismatch between the peak load reported by the New Large 
Load in the registry and the amount of load the Large Load is 
using in real-time could require the Electric Distributor to reduce 
existing customers, including residential loads, to make up the 
difference. 
…  
Buckeye asks that PJM clarify how the MW amounts Electric 
Distributors will be asked to curtail will be determined in a real-
time IRAS Action, and whether this determination will consider 
the actual demand of the New Large Loads in real-time to avoid 
requiring impacts to existing customer load described above. 
Specifically, PJM should ensure that the amount of MWs the 
Electric Distributor is required to drop in response to an IRAS 
Action will not exceed the amount of MWs being used by the New 
Large Loads in real-time.  This clarification will still allow the 
Electric Distributors, working with its applicable state or RERRA, 
to determine how the MW drop will be assigned to individual 
loads, while at the same time ensuring that Electric Distributors are 
not put in the position of being forced to drop existing load (the 
action of last resort to maintain grid integrity) to comply with an 
IRAS Action.6
NCEMC shares Buckeye’s concern. An IRAS Action directive should in no way 
require any existing load that is not provisionally interconnected to be curtailed. Put
