II. LIMITED ANSWER
This proceeding concerns PJM’s July 31, 2026 filing6 proposing revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish the RBP to address previously identified resource adequacy challenges that have arisen as a result of insufficient capacity commitments in the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) Capacity Market Auctions (“RBP Proposal”). EKPC filed a protest to PJM’s RBP Proposal.7

As explained in the RBP Protests, PJM proposed new rules that “set forth the
responsibilities of various parties in the event a Transmission Owner exits from PJM”8 (“TO Exit Provisions”) and that would impose new financial obligations on any TO that exits PJM during the fifteen-year term of the RBP commitment.9 For example, the TO Exit Provisions would require an exiting TO with net load to pay, on an accelerated basis, all future RBP charges it would have owed, or enter into a binding agreement with the owners of the RBP resources to preserve the RBP commitments.10 Or, if the exiting TO leaves with net generation, the TO must ensure that the RBP-

A. The TO Exit Provisions Violate the Cost Causation Principle.
EKPC agrees with the RBP Protestors that the TO Exit Provisions violate the cost causation principle. The Commission’s cost causation principle requires that “all approved rates reflect to some degree the costs actually caused by the customer who must pay them,” in order to be found just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential under FPA section 205.13 Here, the TO Exit Provisions impose costs that do not align with an exiting TO’s obligations under the RBP Proposal.14

In its Protest, AEP highlights the disconnect between the potential financial obligations an exiting TO may be responsible for relative to the RBP Charges it would pay if it remained in PJM, assuming the Commission accepted the RBP Proposal cost allocation methodology and the costs that would be imposed if it exited PJM.15 The exit fee requires the TO to take on costs driven by “where PJM happens to procure or locate new RBP resources.”16 EKPC echoes AEP’s concerns that if PJM acquired new RBP resources in the exiting TO’s zone, the exiting TO would be required to pay for such RBP resources regardless of whether such RBP resources are tied to that TO.17 To require TOs to pay more than their obligation would be inconsistent with the Commission’s cost

B. The TO Exit Provisions Lack the Required Clarity and Specificity to be
Found Just and Reasonable.

As proposed, the TO Exit Provisions lack the clarity and specificity required for a TO to determine its obligations upon withdrawal from PJM. EKPC agrees with PPL Electric that any exit provision should not run afoul of the Commission’s requirement that rates be “clear and specifically set forth.”20 PPL Electric identifies language, such as “all costs” and “all forward obligations,” that exemplifies the financial uncertainty created by the TO Exit Provisions.21 The proposal does not include definitions for such costs nor include a limiting principle associated with the costs sufficient to provide certainty for the exiting TO.

Similarly, EKPC agrees with AEP that any RBP exit provisions must provide “a

C. The TO Exit Provisions “Unnecessarily Constrain Future Decision-Making.”
PJM concedes that a TO withdrawing is a “remote” possibility yet proposes rigid obligations that very likely would need to be revisited if a TO sought to withdraw over the 15-year RBP commitment period. EKPC agrees with PPL Electric that the proposed TO Exit Provisions would “unnecessarily constrain” the parties involved in effectuating a TO’s withdrawal from PJM, including the Commission and state regulatory authorities.29 Any withdrawal would require extensive negotiations and communications amongst the parties taking into account the specific facts and circumstances at that time.30 Additionally, any withdrawal would already need to comply with the CTOA provisions, which require Commission authorization of the withdrawal.31 As PPL Electric argued, “preemptively mandating how the costs and contractual arrangements of generation resources and load within an exiting Transmission Owner’s zone must be treated” is not necessary and likely will need to be revisited when the actual, specific circumstances are considered.32 Accordingly, EKPC urges the Commission to reject PJM’s proposed TO Exit Provisions.
