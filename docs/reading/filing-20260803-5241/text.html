I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC” or 
“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 LS Power Grid New York Corporation I and 
New York Transco LLC (collectively, “Non-Incumbent TOs” or “NTOs”) respectfully submit 
this Motion for Abeyance in response to the Order Instituting Proceeding Under Section 206 of 
the Federal Power Act, issued in the above-captioned proceeding on June 18, 2026.2  The NTOs 
seek a 90-day full abeyance of the current 60-day deadline to respond to all issues raised in the 
Show Cause Order so that the NTOs may have the additional time necessary to prepare an FPA 
section 205 filing alongside New York Independent System Operator, Inc. (“NYISO”) and 
relevant stakeholders.  The NTOs further request that the Commission shorten the answer period 
for this Motion to five days and issue an order on this Motion by August 13, 2026, so that NTOs 
will have certainty regarding their obligations in advance of the August 17, 2026 deadline to 
respond to the Show Cause Order.

The NTOs appreciate the Commission’s attention and guidance on issues relating to the 
interconnection of large loads, including data centers, to the FERC-jurisdictional transmission 
system.  The NTOs believe the potential tariff changes discussed in the Show Cause Order may 
provide opportunities for the NYISO and the transmission owning members of NYISO (“NYISO 
TOs”) to integrate large loads efficiently and further protect ratepayers across the NYISO region 
from transmission-related costs associated with speculative requests or large-load additions that 
do not materialize as planned.  The NTOs also agree that speed to interconnection should be a 
priority as the stakeholders work through this process.  The NTOs respectfully move to hold the 
obligation to respond to the Show Cause Order in abeyance in order to allow NYISO and the 
TOs sufficient time to ensure the proposed tariff revisions are workable, transparent, and 
responsive to the Commission’s identified areas of concern. 
II. 
ARGUMENT 
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission states that it will consider requests for 
abeyances of up to 90 days, which should include both (1) a robust description of the content of a 
potential future filing under FPA section 205 and (2) a reasoned and specific explanation of 
when such a filing is expected to be made.3 
The NTOs seek additional time to ensure they can develop comprehensive, coordinated 
tariff amendments in accordance with the Commission’s directives.  The issues the Commission 
has raised are complex and substantial.  Addressing the Show Cause Order will require 
substantial revisions to the NYISO Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”)—potentially 
including new pro forma contract(s)—and incorporate study and application processes tailored to 
the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads.  The Non-
Incumbent TOs must therefore closely coordinate with the NYISO and other NYISO TOs as the 
parties draft new provisions and, as applicable, revise existing provisions.  NYISO is separately 
seeking an abeyance to engage in its stakeholder process, which requires a corresponding motion

A. 
Proposed Tariff Revisions 
The Show Cause Order sets out five preliminary findings.  First, the Commission 
preliminarily found that the NYISO Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks 
provisions that address, with sufficient clarity and consistency, how NYISO and/or the 
Transmission Owners will timely study — that is, within 60 to 90 days of receiving the request 
— the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads.6  The 
Show Cause Order directs NYISO and the Transmission Owners, to the extent the matters 
addressed implicate aspects of the Tariff over which they hold the filing rights, either to explain 
whether the Tariff remains just and reasonable without an application process, study procedures, 
and ongoing operational requirements that address the unique characteristics and challenges of 
providing transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads, or to propose 
appropriate Tariff revisions.7 
Second, the Commission preliminarily found that the Tariff appears to be unjust and 
unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among 
transmission customers, specifically (1) transparency regarding the assignment of Network 
Upgrades, and their associated costs, that are needed to provide transmission service to Eligible

The issues for the NYISO and NYISO TOs to address are interconnected; therefore, 
successful FPA section 205 filings will require significant coordination among the NYISO, the 
NYISO TOs, and other stakeholders, such as state regulators.  The NTOs understand that the 
NYISO plans to file responsive tariff amendments in November (assuming its abeyance request 
is granted), and the NTOs’ cooperation in developing this proposal is vital.  For example, the 
roles, responsibilities, and processes to interconnect and serve large loads taking new types of 
transmission services will likely be dependent on modifications to existing services under the 
NYISO Tariff.  NYISO-specific regional differences must also be considered.  The NTOs have 
already been regularly meeting with the NYISO to discuss the issues expected to be addressed, 
the contemplated process for implementing appropriate FPA section 205 revisions, and the 
anticipated filing schedule.  The NTOs intend to continue working together through the 
stakeholder process to identify appropriate revisions to the NYISO Tariff.14  The NTOs expect to 
propose revisions to the Tariff and related agreements addressing the application process, study 
procedures, and ongoing operational requirements applicable to Eligible Customers seeking 
transmission service on behalf of large loads.  Those revisions are expected to concern, among 
other matters, the definition of a large load; readiness and information requirements; the

B. 
Briefing Questions 
The Commission also identifies several issues for further briefing.15  The NTOs 
understand that, if the Commission grants the requested full abeyance, the deadline to respond to 
the briefing questions will likewise be held in abeyance.16  The NTOs believe an abeyance is 
appropriate as to the briefing questions as they are wholly interrelated with the issues to be 
addressed in the NTOs’ Section 205 filings, as described above. 
III. 
REQUEST FOR SHORTENED ANSWER PERIOD AND EXPEDITED 
TREATMENT 
NTOs respectfully request that the Commission shorten the answer period for this Motion 
to five days and grant expedited treatment so that it may issue an order on this Motion by August 
13, 2026.  Rule 213(d)(1) of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure provides that an 
answer to a motion must be made within 15 days after the motion is filed unless the Commission 
orders otherwise.17  The Commission should order otherwise here, for three reasons.

First, a shortened answer period and expedited action will provide regulatory certainty in 
advance of a deadline that is now imminent.  The Show Cause Order directs NYISO and the 
NYISO TOs to respond within 60 days of issuance, which is August 17, 2026—14 days from the 
date of this filing.18  If the ordinary fifteen-day answer period applies, the Commission will not 
be in a position to rule on this Motion until after NTOs’ response is already due.  NTOs would 
then be required to divert resources to preparing a merits response to the Show Cause Order 
rather than to the coordinated work with NYISO and stakeholders that is necessary to produce 
the contemplated FPA section 205 filing.  The need for certainty is heightened here, because the 
Commission has emphasized that it will not grant abeyances reflexively.19 
Second, interested entities have been on notice of the need to respond to abeyance 
requests promptly.  The Commission expressly contemplated such requests in the Show Cause 
Order and directed that they be submitted within 45 days of issuance so as to allow the 
Commission time to act on them.20  Because the Commission itself contemplated that an 
abeyance request could be filed as late as 15 days before the response deadline, any entity with 
an interest in this proceeding has been on notice since June 18, 2026, that it would need to 
respond to such a request on an expedited basis.  A five-day answer period is therefore consistent 
with the schedule the Commission established and will not prejudice any participant.21 
Third, expedited action will promote administrative efficiency.  The NTOs understand 
that NYISO and the other FERC-jurisdictional transmission owners have or intend to similarly 
request that the Commission hold this proceeding in abeyance.  These requests are interrelated.

IV. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons described above, the NTOs respectfully request that the Commission (i) 
grant this Motion and hold this proceeding in abeyance for 90 days beginning August 17, 2026, 
and extend NTOs’ deadline to respond in full to the Show Cause Order to November 16, 2026; 
(ii) shorten the answer period for this Motion to five days; and (iii) grant expedited treatment and 
issue an order on this Motion by August 13, 2026.
