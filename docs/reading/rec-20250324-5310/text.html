Dear Secretary Reese:

Pursuant to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission” or “FERC”) February 20, 2025 order issued in the above-captioned proceeding,1 Silver Run Electric, LLC (“Silver Run”), as PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) Transmission Owner, respectfully submits this response in accordance with the Commission’s directive and explains that it takes no position on the matters that the Commission raises in its order.  Silver Run commits to working with PJM, the other PJM Transmission Owners, and the other stakeholders in this proceeding as necessary to implement any Tariff changes that may be required as part of this proceeding.

I. BACKGROUND
The February 20 Order instituted a proceeding under section 206 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”), wherein the Commission directed PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners to show cause as to why the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff, Amended and Restated Operating Agreement of PJM, and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities in the PJM Region (collectively, the “Tariff”) (a) remains just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential with regards to the rates, terms, and conditions of service applying to co-location arrangements or (b) explain what changes would remedy the Commission’s identified concerns if FERC were to determine the Tariff has become unjust and unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential and, therefore, establishes a replacement Tariff.

Silver Run is an electric transmission-only utility company established to develop, own, and operate transmission facilities within the PJM region, and is organized under the laws of the State of Delaware. Silver Run is an indirect subsidiary of LS Power Associates, L.P.  Silver Run owns and operates discrete and limited transmission facilities.  Specifically, Silver Run owns and operates one substation in New

Castle County, Delaware (the Silver Run Substation) and a 5.5-mile, 230 kV transmission line, which includes an approximately 3-mile submarine crossing of the Delaware River, connecting the Silver Run Substation to the Hope Creek Nuclear Plant Switchyard located in Salem County, New Jersey.  Silver Run built these transmission assets as part of PJM’s Order No. 1000-compliant competitive solicitation process associated with the Artificial Island Project, which was needed to improve transmission system performance in the area.2  These transmission assets, which commenced operations on April 28, 2020, were the first and, thus far, only transmission assets owned by Silver Run.

II. RESPONSE
Silver Run has carefully reviewed each of the issues raised in the February 20 Order, including the briefing questions reflected in Section VI.D of the February 20 Order.  Silver Run, however, is asserting no position in this proceeding but is committed to working alongside PJM, the other PJM Transmission Owners, and other stakeholders in this proceeding to implement any related Tariff changes that the Commission may require to be made.

Silver Run is taking no position on this matter, because it is a PJM Transmission Owner with no service territory, no load, and no generator interconnection customers, pending or otherwise. Furthermore, as explained above, Silver Run’s sole assets are transmission facilities under PJM’s operational control.  Consequently, if and when any load or generator customers seek to interconnect to Silver Run’s transmission facilities, co-located or not, PJM will oversee the processing thereof, and Silver Run will abide by any Tariff provisions effective at that time.

III. CONCLUSION
Silver Run looks forward to the opportunity of working with the Commission, its staff, PJM, and other stakeholders in this proceeding on any Tariff revisions that the Commission may find are necessary.  Please contact the undersigned with any questions regarding this filing.
