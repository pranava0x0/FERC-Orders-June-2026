PROTEST OF VISTRA CORP.

Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission”),1 Vistra Corp. (“Vistra”) submits this protest regarding PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) proposed Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) proposal.  Although Vistra recognizes PJM’s concerns regarding the resource adequacy challenges facing the region, those concerns demand durable solutions that provide investors with the certainty they need to maintain and expand PJM’s generation fleet.2  That is not IRAS. IRAS is ill-conceived, inconsistent with the Federal Power Act (“FPA”), and will stymie investment in PJM.  With IRAS, PJM ignores the jurisdictional boundaries that Congress carefully crafted and proposes rules that are unjust, unreasonable, and unduly discriminatory—each of which is a sufficient basis for the Commission to reject the proposal and for a court to overturn an order accepting IRAS on appeal.  In rejecting IRAS, the Commission should order PJM to develop a durable solution that is faithful to sound market fundamentals to ensure that the region has the capacity resources needed to meet load growth.

A. IRAS Violates the FPA’s Division of Jurisdiction
The Commission is a creature of statute and can act only within the scope of authority conveyed by Congress.10  IRAS exceeds the scope of the authority conveyed to the Commission under the FPA because the entire scheme is based on and triggered by the characteristics and behavior of retail customers (i.e., new large loads), which are within the states’ exclusive sphere to regulate.  In this way, IRAS explicitly “aims” at or “target[s]” specific retail customers, in violation of the FPA.11

The FPA gives the states exclusive authority over retail sales.12  This means that the Commission is prohibited from specifying the rates, terms, and conditions of retail sales “no matter how direct, or dramatic, [their] impact on wholesale rates” as that “is a job for the States alone.”13 Nevertheless, “[w]holesale and retail markets in electricity . . . are not hermetically sealed from each other,” so to determine whether an action that affects both wholesale and retail spheres oversteps the FPA’s bright jurisdictional line, the Supreme Court has considered “the target at

B. IRAS Is Unduly Discriminatory
IRAS is also unduly discriminatory because it treats similarly situated entities differently.38 “Similarly situated” does not mean that there are no differences between the two entities, but rather that there are no differences “that are material to the inquiry at hand.”39  PJM bears the burden to explain how its proposal to treat entities differently is justified by factors that render those entities not “similarly situated.”  PJM fails to meet that standard for several reasons.

1. IRAS Unduly Discriminates Between EDCs
To start, IRAS would unduly discriminate among EDCs based on the characteristics of
retail customers within those EDCs’ footprints.  Specifically, IRAS would subject EDCs to curtailment based on the size, timing, and contracting decisions of its retail customers.  Those retail customers are outside the Commission’s jurisdiction to consider and therefore cannot be “material to the inquiry at hand.”40  Providing differing levels of wholesale market treatment depending on legally irrelevant characteristics of entities sitting outside the Commission’s jurisdiction is, by definition, unduly discriminatory.

Even if the characteristics of the retail customers within an EDC’s territory could, in theory, be a lawful basis to discriminate among wholesale customers, the retail characteristics on which

2. IRAS Unduly Discriminates Against Existing Resources
When determining whether new large loads are subject to IRAS, PJM would value identical new and existing capacity resources differently even though they provide the same resource adequacy benefits to the system.  PJM has not explained why large loads that contract with new generation resources should be non-curtailable while those that contract with existing generation resource with the same capacity value should be curtailed when system conditions warrant.  Nor could it.  One MW of accredited capacity from a new resource provides the same resource adequacy benefits as one MW of capacity from an otherwise identical existing resource.  Whether the otherwise identical resources are bilaterally contracted or selling exclusively in the PJM capacity market does not change the resource adequacy value to the PJM system as a matter of physics.

D. IRAS Should Not Apply to Behind the Meter Load
Finally, the Commission should require PJM to confirm that IRAS will not apply to new large loads that are co-located behind the meter of generation resources that are not capacity resources.  For loads in such a configuration, there is nothing to curtail because the load is not using PJM capacity.  The Commission must direct PJM to provide this confirmation to ensure that this proceeding does not undermine the Commission’s parallel efforts to facilitate co-location. PJM’s filing introduces significant ambiguity about whether it intends to subject co-located loads to IRAS because it deviates from the Commission’s proposed definitions concerning co- located configurations in the PJM Large Load Order to Show Cause in Docket No. EL26-67-000. There, the Commission defined a “large load” as “a new, commercial or industrial customer,
