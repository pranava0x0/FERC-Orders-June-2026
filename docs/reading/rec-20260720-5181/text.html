LIMITED REQUEST FOR REHEARING AND CLARIFICATION
OF CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC
Constellation submits this limited request for rehearing and clarification of the June 2026 Co-Location Order.1 We raise four discrete points on rehearing to reinforce the Commission’s objective of establishing sufficiently clear tariff terms to allow the speedy connection and service to Co-Located Loads in a way that “facilitate[s] more efficient use of the existing transmission system” and “limit[s] unnecessary transmission system buildout” in PJM.2

First, the Commission should modify the June 2026 Co-Location Order to require study deadlines, which are the most critical path for the speedy interconnection of Co-Located Loads and would be logically consistent with the ANOPR Show Cause Order.3

Second, the Commission should require PJM to spell out in its Tariff the methodology and process (including deadlines) for completing Necessary Studies. These terms belong in the Tariff, and not the Manuals, because they “significantly affect rates, terms, and conditions” of service and are “readily susceptible to specification.”4

Third, the Commission should modify the operational penalty for the misoperation of control technologies and/or protections to include only a two-strike disqualification as opposed to a 120-day suspension on top of a two-strike disqualification. III. A Two-Strike Disqualification Is a Sufficient Deterrent and Alone Should Be the Just and Reasonable Replacement Rate.

The Commission failed to adequately support its finding that suspending new transmission service for an Eligible Customer up to 120 days after the first misoperation of control technologies and/or protection systems is just and reasonable.30 While an investigation of the misoperation is appropriate, imposing a four-month suspension while the investigation is ongoing is not. The Commission should reject the 120-day suspension and approve only the two-strike disqualification penalty for the misoperation of control technologies and/or protection systems as the just and reasonable operational penalty.

First, suspending service for an Eligible Customer for 120 days will have no added deterrence value or reliability benefit on top of imposing a two-strike disqualification penalty.31 A two-strike disqualification penalty will sufficiently incentivize Co-Located Loads to make sure they are “willing and able to limit their energy withdrawals.”32 Faced with the commercial risk

CLARIFICATION
I. The Commission Should Direct PJM to Revise the Definition of Co-Located Generating Facility to Conform to the December 2025 Co-Location Order.

We seek clarification on the Commission’s denial51 of Constellation’s request to revise PJM’s definition of Co-Located Generating Facility to “mean the portion of an existing or planned Generating Facility that the Interconnection Customer designates to serve and is physically connected to a Co-Located Load.”52 Constellation proposed this revision to conform the definition of Co-Located Generating Facility with the December 2025 Co-Location Order that specifically contemplated a “portion of the generating facility [will] be designated to serve any Co-Located Load that is not receiving NITS.”53 No party opposed. Based on these facts, it is unclear why the Commission disagreed. The Commission should direct PJM to revise its definition on compliance. A more precise definition in the PJM Tariff is critical to reduce uncertainty and potential litigation, expedite the interconnection of large loads, and ensure the intent of the December 2025 Co-Location Order is achieved. Without adding a modifier to “Generating Facility,” some could interpret Co-Located Generating Facility as including the entire capacity at a generation site even if only a portion of the capacity is contracted to serve the Co-Located Load.
