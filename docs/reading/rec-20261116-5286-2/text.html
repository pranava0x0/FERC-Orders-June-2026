Q. 
HOW WOULD THE MINIMUM CONTRIBUTION WORK? 
9 
A. 
The CRA establishes an Annual Minimum Transmission Revenue Contribution (“Annual 
10 
MTRC”) for each Covered Upgrade using the separate MTRC Calculation Template in 
11 
Schedule 5. The calculation uses a levelized fixed charge rate (“FCR”) over a twenty-year 
12 
MTRC Term, applied to the assigned capital cost, with the capital recovery, income-tax, 
13 
and other capital-related components specified in Schedule 5, plus any fixed annual 
14 
components not included in the FCR. The calculation excludes the Segment A project-risk 
15 
incentive adder. Estimated capital cost would initially support an Estimated Annual MTRC. 
16 
LSPGNY would establish the Final Annual MTRC using actual capital cost within one year 
17 
after the Covered Upgrade enters service, with estimated charges reconciled under the 
18 
CRA. The finalized contractual MTRC would not be reset through routine annual formula-
19 
rate updates, subject to the CRA’s expressly permitted adjustments. 
20 
Q. 
WHY DOES LSPGNY PROPOSE A TWENTY-YEAR, LEVELIZED MTRC? 
21 
A. 
A large-load request may require a substantial transmission investment before the load 
22 
reaches its expected operating level. A twenty-year commitment provides an extended 
23

PDF page 16

13 
period of customer accountability for that investment, while establishing a defined period 
1 
over which the Eligible Customer can evaluate its obligation. Levelization spreads the 
2 
calculated revenue contribution over that period rather than concentrating it in the initial 
3 
years. The purpose is to secure a meaningful contribution toward the investment 
4 
undertaken for the requested service and reduce the exposure of other customers if the load 
5 
is delayed, reduced, or discontinued. 
6 
 
The twenty-year MTRC Term is a contractual revenue-support period. Under CRA 
7 
Sections 3.2 and 6.7, earlier Break-Even is available if a final Reconciliation Statement 
8 
determines that the Total Required Revenue Commitment has been satisfied before the end 
9 
of that term. Operational payment and security obligations then terminate, subject to final 
10 
true-ups and correction of errors or fraud for periods before satisfaction. Once finally 
11 
satisfied, the responsibility does not revive because of a later change in return on equity, 
12 
expense, load, forecast, or other formula-rate input. It does not establish the upgrade’s 
13 
accounting depreciation rate or require removal of the facility from rate base at the end of 
14 
that term. The facility’s continued inclusion in the formula rate would remain governed by 
15 
the applicable tariff and Commission requirements. Amounts recovered under the CRA 
16 
would receive the credits and other treatment provided in the formula rate template so that 
17 
the contractual commitment does not create duplicate recovery. 
18 
 
The twenty-year term is supported by Operational Security based on the remaining 
19 
Cumulative Revenue Responsibility and, when the applicable termination provisions are 
20 
triggered, a Termination Fee equal to the remaining contractual commitment less any 
21 
Supplemental MTRC already billed and remaining unpaid. Those unpaid bills remain 
22 
separately payable and are not discharged by payment of the Termination Fee. It does not 
23

PDF page 17

14 
eliminate all risk over the Covered Upgrade’s depreciable life. Neither a levelized Annual 
1 
MTRC nor the single-year illustration below, standing alone, demonstrates that the total 
2 
commitment equals or exceeds the upgrade’s cost of service over its entire life. Such a 
3 
conclusion would require a comparison using consistent cost, revenue, timing, and 
4 
discount-rate assumptions.

Q. 
WHY CAN THE FIXED ANNUAL MTRC DIFFER FROM THE UPGRADE’S 
6 
ANNUAL FORMULA-RATE REVENUE REQUIREMENT? 
7 
A. 
The Annual MTRC is a levelized contractual amount calculated using the inputs specified 
8 
in the CRA. The formula rate separately determines the annual cost of service using the 
9 
applicable rate base, depreciation, expenses, taxes, return, and credits. As net plant declines 
10 
and other formula-rate inputs change, the annual cost of service can differ from the fixed 
11 
contractual amount. The Annual MTRC is therefore not a representation that the 
12 
customer’s minimum contribution will equal the actual incremental revenue requirement in 
13 
every year. 
14 
 
The effect on other customers depends on the actual revenue requirement, qualifying large-
15 
load revenues, Supplemental MTRC, and the credit and allocation provisions in the 
16 
template and tariff. The contractual floor, security, and reconciliation provisions mitigate 
17 
exposure to the large load; they do not support an unconditional assurance that every other 
18 
customer’s rate will remain unchanged under every operating or cost scenario. 
19 
Q. 
WHY DOES THE FIXED CHARGE CALCULATION INCLUDE CAPITAL 
20 
RECOVERY, INCOME TAXES, AND OTHER SPECIFIED COMPONENTS? 
21 
A. 
Capital cost alone does not measure the full revenue contribution associated with an 
22 
upgrade. Schedule 5 calculates the Annual MTRC using a levelized capital charge rate plus 
23

PDF page 18

15 
an expense allocator, consistent with the FCR worksheet in Exhibit No. GNY-101. The 
1 
calculation first determines an after-tax discount rate using the applicable capital structure, 
2 
return on equity, cost of debt, and composite income-tax rate. It then subtracts the present 
3 
value of federal and state tax-depreciation benefits from allocated capital cost and applies 
4 
an income-tax revenue conversion factor. The resulting present-value capital revenue 
5 
requirement is annualized over the twenty-year MTRC Term using the after-tax discount 
6 
rate and divided by allocated capital cost to determine the levelized capital charge rate. The 
7 
expense allocator reflects operating and maintenance expense, less project-specific 
8 
regulatory asset amortization included in that expense, plus taxes other than income taxes, 
9 
divided by total gross transmission plant in service. The levelized capital charge rate and 
10 
expense allocator are each rounded to six decimal places and added to determine the Fixed 
11 
Charge Rate. The Annual MTRC equals allocated capital cost multiplied by that rate, plus 
12 
any separately identified fixed annual costs not already included. Supporting workpapers 
13 
identify the inputs, their sources, and each calculation, and prevent duplicate inclusion of 
14 
any cost. 
15 
 
The FCR calculation establishes the contractual minimum contribution; it is not an 
16 
additional formula-rate charge imposed on top of the same cost of service without an offset. 
17 
LSPGNY would continue to calculate its revenue requirement through the formula rate, 
18 
and Supplemental MTRC and other applicable amounts would receive the treatment 
19 
specified in the template. Schedule 5 and its supporting workpapers provide the contractual 
20 
calculation and input reconciliation; the separate rate testimony of Eric Arzola explains 
21 
how the resulting Annual MTRC is used in the formula rate template. 
22

PDF page 19

16 
The MTRC is a contractual revenue floor calculated separately from LSPGNY’s annual 
1 
formula-rate cost of service. Supplemental MTRC is the greater of zero and the applicable 
2 
Annual MTRC less Qualifying Transmission Revenues for the corresponding period. The 
3 
CRA and Schedule 2 define the qualifying RTFC revenues attributable to the Large Load 
4 
Facility and the methodology for attributing or allocating those revenues among Covered 
5 
Upgrades. Each dollar would count only once. LSPGNY would maintain customer- and 
6 
Covered Upgrade-specific records of the commitment and its satisfaction. That tracking 
7 
does not require formula-rate credits to be applied exclusively to the revenue requirement 
8 
of the corresponding upgrade. Instead, CRA revenue is a general revenue credit that 
9 
reduces LSPGNY’s overall revenue requirement rather than the revenue requirement of any 
10 
individual Covered Upgrade. 
11 
Qualifying Transmission Revenues are not a separate retail or load-based charge imposed 
12 
by LSPGNY. They are the portion of RTFC revenues assessed on the Large Load Facility’s 
13 
Actual Energy Withdrawals, collected from the Responsible LSE or LSEs serving that 
14 
facility, and remitted to LSPGNY that is attributable to the applicable Covered Upgrade 
15 
and reduces the RTFC otherwise collected from other Responsible LSEs. Schedule 2
