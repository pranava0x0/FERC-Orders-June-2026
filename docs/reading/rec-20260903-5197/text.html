ACORE COMMENTS ON PJM INTERCONNECTION LLC  
INTERIM RESOURCE ADEQUACY SERVICE PROPOSAL 
 
 Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (Commission) 
Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 ACORE submits these comments on the PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. (PJM) proposal to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) and a large load 
registry (IRAS Proposal) submitted on August 13, 2026 in the above captioned docket.  
ACORE provides these supportive comments directed at the specific IRAS requirements 
for Bring Your Own New Capacity (BYONC). ACORE recognizes the immediate need for PJM 
to address the dramatic increase in demand resulting from the expansion of large loads within its 
footprint. Any reforms developed to address this need must incent and be aligned with a longer-
term contracting paradigm rather than reliance on the short-term volatile pricing in PJM’s annual 
Reliability Pricing Model (RPM) capacity market. 
ACORE therefore supports the requirement for a new large load to contract for new 
capacity under BYONC to avoid being subject to potential curtailments under IRAS.  Such a 
requirement will strongly incent longer-term (at least 10 year) contracting with BYONC capacity 
by the large loads availing themselves of the IRAS BYONC provision.  ACORE is also 
supportive of the requirement that the BYONC resources participate as a price taker in the Base 
Residual Auction (BRA) for 10 consecutive Delivery Years and be subject to the RPM’s must

In PJM’s May whitepaper, Powering Reliability Through Market Design, PJM accurately 
describes the importance of revenue predictability and stability for the development of capital-
intensive generation and storage capacity: 
In conversations with generation developers, project finance lenders, equity investors and 
power project consultants in furtherance of this initiative, PJM heard a strikingly 
consistent message: High capacity prices alone do not drive investment decisions. The 
barriers cited – repeatedly and across investor types – were revenue durability over the 
project financing horizon and regulatory stability sufficient to make long-term revenue 
projections credible.3 
In addition to the ten-year price taker requirement, ACORE also appreciates the 
expansive scope of BYONC to include not only needed new builds, but also capacity obtained 
through the utilization of Surplus Interconnection Service (SIS), as well as new demand response 
and distributed energy resources, and other types of capacity expansion. ACORE has long 
supported a greater use of SIS, especially for storage, but more improvements are needed.4 In a 
recent letter, PJM President and CEO David Mills stated that: “PJM understands that some 
aspects of our existing market rules for facilities interconnecting via the SIS process make it 
challenging for these facilities to provide capacity. Given the abundant interest in leveraging SIS 
to provide additional capacity, PJM staff and stakeholders recently initiated a workplan to

Finally, while not directly within the scope of IRAS but highly relevant to its success, 
ACORE continues to emphasize that a holistic approach is needed to address both the challenges 
and opportunities of large load integration. Such an approach will entail additional reforms and 
improvements that provide greater certainty and stability in the interconnection process,6 
Commission action on PJM’s Order No. 1920 compliance filing, and greater development of 
high-voltage interregional transmission between PJM and other regions. ACORE and our 
membership look forward to ongoing engagement with the Commission on these important 
issues.
