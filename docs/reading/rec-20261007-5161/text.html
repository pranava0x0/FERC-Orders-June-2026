I. Introduction and Summary
Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission ("Commission"), 18 C.F.R. §§ 385.212, 385.213, Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. ("SMECO") respectfully moves for leave to file this limited answer (“Limited Answer”) and answer to the September 23, 2026 Answer of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. ("PJM") filed in the above-captioned docket on September 23, 2026 ("PJM Answer"). On September 3, 2026, SMECO filed Comments and Limited Protest ("SMECO Limited Protest")1 regarding PJM's August 13, 2026 proposal to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service ("IRAS") and a Large Load Registry ("IRAS Filing").  SMECO's Limited Protest raised three discrete issues affecting Electric Distributors:

1. The real-time Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action mechanism does not confirm that the New Large Load underlying an Electric Distributor's assigned obligation is actually energized, risking curtailment of the Electric Distributor's core native load.2

2. The billing indemnification provision in proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section X(b)(iii) shifts compensation default risk to Electric Distributors, including for payments owed by unaffiliated entities.3

3. The Large Load Registry rests on Manual provisions not yet developed, leaves unclear the scope of Electric Distributor liability for third-party data, and identifies no cost-recovery mechanism (Section IV.D). 4

III. Answer
A. PJM Did Not Respond to SMECO's Showing That an IRAS Action Could Require
Curtailment of Core Native Load.
SMECO showed that, under the Affidavit of Christopher Pilong, an Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action directs an Electric Distributor to reduce load up to its full pre-Delivery Year IRAS obligation. That quantity is fixed before the Delivery Year and is not tied to whether the underlying New Large Load is energized and taking service. No IRAS Alert need precede an Action.10 Eligible New Large Loads are, by definition, loads that have not brought their own capacity, and are disproportionately likely to still be under construction or commissioning.11 If such a load is not yet drawing power when an Action issues, the Electric Distributor's only means of compliance would be to curtail its existing, non-Large-Load native customers.12 SMECO asked the Commission to direct PJM to clarify: (1) whether an Electric Distributor's obligation under an IRAS Action is capped by the New Large Load MW actually energized and taking service at the time of the Action; (2) if not, what safeguard prevents an Electric Distributor from being directed to reduce more load than its energized New Large Load represents; and (3) whether PJM will adjust or suspend an assigned IRAS obligation when the underlying New Large Load's in-service date is delayed.13

The PJM Answer does not respond to any of these questions. Instead, it restates the zone-level design that gives rise to SMECO's concern:

•
PJM "will be issuing broad load reduction directives to Electric Distributors."14
•
Electric Distributors "will retain full discretion" as to how PJM's MW reduction instructions "will ultimately be carried out just as they do today."15

•
PJM's directives "will be informed by the degree to which load in a certain zone/area's IRAS responsibility has been reduced through MW of BYONC or Allocated RBP UCAP MW."16

These statements do not account for New Large Load that is not yet energized or is still ramping. Discretion over which retail customers to curtail is of no help when the load that created the obligation is not consuming power. PJM identifies offsets for BYONC and Allocated RBP UCAP MW, but no corresponding adjustment for delayed in-service dates.

This gap matters because it runs counter to IRAS's stated purpose. PJM describes IRAS as protecting "native loads from the cost and reliability impacts" of New Large Loads that have not brought capacity.17 Under the design as filed, an IRAS Action could instead fall on native load. PJM also states that “[i]n particular, the in-service date is a critical piece of information" collected through the Large Load Registry.18 The data needed to cap an Action at energized New Large Load MW will therefore be available to PJM.

SMECO is not alone in this concern. In its answer, the NJBPU observed that SMECO's Limited Protest "highlight[s] that this is not merely a hypothetical scenario," because an Electric Distributor's "assigned New Large Loads may not be yet energized, or may have only just begun a ramp or testing period that is below its expected draw when PJM issues an IRAS Action."19 The

B. PJM Did Not Respond to SMECO's Protest of the Billing Indemnification Provision. In its Limited Protest, SMECO showed that proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section X(b)(iii) provides that any default in the payment of IRAS compensation "shall be assessed to the Electric Distributor," not to PJM or its membership. 22  In restructured states, the defaulting LSE may have no affiliation, and no commercial or credit relationship, with the Electric Distributor.23 The proposal includes no collateral requirement or PJM-administered mutualization to protect the Electric Distributor.24

While the PJM Answer addresses IRAS compensation25,  the discussion does not mention section X(b)(iii), default allocation, or Electric Distributor credit exposure.  What the PJM Answer does say, however,  underscores the problem. PJM describes the mechanism as "compensating LSEs, which are NITS customers."26 PJM further states that the entities to be paid and the loads to be charged are "determined at the state level."27 If the wholesale compensation flows to LSEs, and payors and payees are set by state authorities, PJM offers no reason why an Electric Distributor should guarantee payment by other entities.

In its Answer, PJM consents to clarifying proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section X on compliance, and asks the Commission to "prescribe the compliance requirement in as much detail as possible."28 SMECO agrees that compliance is the appropriate vehicle. The Commission should direct PJM, in that compliance filing, to revise section X(b)(iii) so that an Electric Distributor is not assessed defaults on IRAS compensation owed by other entities. Alternatives include limiting an Electric Distributor's payment obligation to amounts it actually collects, or establishing collateral requirements or PJM-administered credit support for IRAS compensation billing.

Registry information "is borne by the LSE, not the Electric Distributor directly, although PJM would welcome information provided by an Electric Distributor."30   SMECO indeed welcomes that clarification. It does not, however, resolve SMECO's concern, for three reasons:

•
Proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section III(b) still provides that an Electric Distributor "shall" provide Registry information and coordinate with PJM on its validity. A voluntary role described in an answer does not modify mandatory tariff language.

•
PJM states that Electric Distributors "may provide the information for the Large Load Registry on behalf of their relevant LSEs," and that PJM may reach out to Electric Distributors to supplement LSE submissions.31 That is precisely the relay role for which SMECO sought clarity on exposure under FPA section 222 and 18 C.F.R. § 35.41(b), which the IRAS Filing itself invoked.32  PJM does not address that exposure.

•
PJM acknowledges that the RAA's definition of "Party" is broader than LSEs, and that PJM "will continue to evaluate this."33 The identity of the obligated entity therefore remains unsettled.

The Commission should direct PJM to conform proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section III(b) to PJM's stated position that the LSE bears the Registry reporting obligation, and to clarify that an Electric Distributor that relays, in good faith, information originating with an LSE or Large Load is not responsible for the accuracy of that information.
