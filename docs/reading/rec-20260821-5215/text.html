COMMENTS OF
AMERICAN MUNICIPAL POWER, INC.
American Municipal Power, Inc. (“AMP”) hereby provides its comments on the July 31, 2026 filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) in the above-captioned docket,1 in which PJM proposed revisions to its Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish a Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) for the PJM region.

The RBP is “a one-time time procurement of new capacity to address resource adequacy challenges in the PJM Region.”2 The mechanism is designed to procure a targeted quantity of capacity under fixed agreements with terms up to fifteen years beginning with the 2028/2029 Delivery Year and ending with the 2042/2043 Delivery Year.3 PJM has set this Initial RBP Target at 6,831.3 MW,4 which may be decremented to account for certain new offsetting capacity (Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW), as well as for any qualifying Electric Distributor zone/areas that opt out of procuring capacity through the RBP. While the RBP will procure capacity on an RTO-wide basis, the capacity target (and associated costs) will be allocated to specific Electric Distributor zone/areas 1

PJM has effectively confirmed that neither AMP, nor any AMP member, will be allocated any of the costs of procuring the Initial RBP Target (i.e., RBP Charges) under PJM’s proposal, as all AMP members appear to be within zone/areas that do not receive any allocation of the Initial RBP Target. AMP strongly supports PJM’s proposal in this respect, as any allocation of RBP Charges to AMP or its members would be inconsistent with cost causation principles and the Ratepayer Protection Pledge. Because the PJM Filing is potentially ambiguous regarding allocation of the Initial RBP Target among the zone/areas, however, AMP submits these comments to clarify and complete the record. Additionally, PJM’s Filing demonstrates an administrative error with regard to the zone/area definition that PJM should address.

I. COMMENTS
A. PJM appropriately allocates the Initial RBP Target to zone/areas with large load growth documented in forecast load adjustments.

The critical first step in assigning cost responsibility for RBP Charges is PJM’s allocation of the Initial RBP Target, which PJM appropriately proposes to assign “to each Electric Distributor’s zone/area using that zone/area’s pro-rata share of the difference between the forecasted Load Adjustments for the 2026/2027 Delivery Year and the 2028/2029 Delivery Year.”5 In other words, the Initial RBP Target of 6,831.3 MW will be

allocated to those Electric Distributor zone/areas that have provided to PJM projections of large load growth between 2026 and 2028.6

Although there are several more steps in determining ultimate liability for RBP Charges, zone/areas that do not receive any allocation at this first step are effectively excluded from responsibility for RBP Charges. As PJM explains, this allocation framework is consistent with cost causation principles and the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.7

B. PJM should clarify the definition of “zone/area” is the one accepted by
the Commission in Docket No. ER26-2447.
While AMP supports PJM’s proposal to allocate the Initial RBP Target to
zone/areas with large load growth, there appears to be a ministerial issue with the definition of “zone/area” in the PJM Filing. Specifically, the filing includes a definition of zone/area that does not reflect revisions accepted by the Commission in Docket No. ER24-2447.8 In that proceeding, PJM proposed to amend the RAA to add the new defined term “Large Load Adjustment,” which, in turn, included a definition of zone/area.9 In 6
