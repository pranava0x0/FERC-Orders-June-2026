I.  INTRODUCTION AND STATEMENT OF INTEREST 
Maven Solutions is a Seattle-based decision-intelligence practice specializing in AI 
infrastructure, large-load interconnection, and grid governance. Founded in 2014, Maven has 
developed the Civic Bankability Rating Standard (“CBRS”), a statute-grounded scoring 
methodology for evaluating large-load and data-center projects against the ratepayer, reliability, 
community-cost, and financial-surety exposures they generate. Maven introduces the CBRS 
methodology to the Commission's record through these Comments. 
Maven Solutions is a named party of record in Docket No. RM26-4-000 (Large Loads Co-
Located at Generating Facilities and Other Considerations for the Efficient Use of the 
Transmission System). Maven's May 20, 2026 supplemental comment (Submission ID 
1755084) and June 8, 2026 filings (Accession Nos. 20260608-5025 and 20260608-5026) are on 
the federal record. On June 15, 2026, the Joint Consumer Advocates of Pennsylvania, 
Delaware, and New Jersey addressed Maven's filings alongside those of the Harvard 
Environmental & Energy Law Program, the Working Group for Investment in Reliable and 
Economic Electric Systems (WIRES), and the Transmission Access Policy Study Group (TAPS) 
in Accession No. 20260615-5141. Maven files these Comments in Docket No. EL26-67-000 in 
continuation of that record. 
Maven's interest in this proceeding is direct and specific. The Commission's June 18, 2026 
Show Cause Order in this docket directs PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) to demonstrate 
why its existing tariff provisions governing large-load interconnection are just and reasonable, or 
to propose reforms consistent with the five reform categories specified in that Order.1 The CBRS 
instrument was developed to score precisely the class of tariff and interconnection questions the 
Commission has placed before PJM. 
II.  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
HEADLINE FINDING 
PJM's current tariff posture on large-load interconnection scores CONDITIONAL under the Civic 
Bankability Rating Standard with two active keystone flags — Ratepayer Protection (FERC 
Category 2) and Financial Surety (FERC Category 4). Under CBRS scoring architecture, 
concurrent activation of two keystone flags warrants Commission-level intervention on the specific 
reform categories to which those flags attach. 
 
The PJM system is under greater documented ratepayer and reliability stress than any regional 
grid in the modern capacity-market era. The evidentiary record supporting the Commission's 
Show Cause Order has intensified in the twenty-one days between issuance and the 
intervention deadline for these Comments.

Maven's principal recommendations, developed in Section VII, are structured against each of 
the five reform categories established in the Show Cause Order. In summary, the Commission 
should require PJM to submit tariff revisions or, in the alternative, a detailed evidentiary showing 
addressing: (1) time-bounded transmission-service application and study commitments with 
public reporting; (2) a cost-causation methodology allocating transmission-upgrade costs 
caused by large-load interconnection to the responsible load class; (3) co-location and behind-
the-meter generation tariff provisions consistent with the Commission's parallel PJM co-location 
proceedings and including measurable reliability-hold-harmless mechanisms and financial 
surety instruments; (4) a new tariff class for flexible or curtailable large loads with contribution-
to-reliability compensation and coordination with state Virtual Power Plant and FERC Order 
2222 DER aggregation pathways; and (5) study-process reforms for generation serving 
electrically proximate or co-located loads that mirror the transparency and cost-causation 
provisions recommended for Category (2).

IV.  METHODOLOGY: THE CIVIC BANKABILITY RATING 
STANDARD 
A.  Overview of CBRS 
The Civic Bankability Rating Standard is a statute-grounded scoring methodology Maven 
Solutions has developed for evaluating large-load and data-center projects against ratepayer, 
reliability, community-cost, and financial-surety exposures. Maven introduces the methodology 
to the Commission's record through these Comments. The methodology consists of ten 
dimensions, each anchored to a specific enacted statutory or regulatory framework in United 
States federal or state law. Each dimension is scored on a 0-to-5 evidence-weighted scale. 
Aggregate scores map to four bands: Below Threshold (0–12), Conditional (13–25), Acceptable 
(26–37), and Community Asset (38–50). 
Three of the ten dimensions are designated Keystone dimensions: Ratepayer Protection, Water, 
and Financial Surety. Any keystone dimension scoring below the Acceptable threshold caps the 
aggregate band at Conditional regardless of raw score total. Two or more concurrent keystone 
flags warrant elevated scrutiny of the specific reform categories to which they attach. 
B.  Jurisdictional Application to Commission Proceedings 
Not all CBRS dimensions map to matters within the Commission's Section 205 and Section 206 
jurisdiction. Maven applies to Commission proceedings a jurisdiction-relevant subset consisting 
of four dimensions: Ratepayer Protection, Transparency, Co-Location and Interconnection 
Governance, and Financial Surety. The full ten-dimension methodology is maintained by Maven 
Solutions and available on request. Maven's application of the jurisdictional subset in 
Commission dockets does not waive the applicability of the full methodology in state-utility-
commission, county-ordinance, permit, or corporate-decision contexts.

V.  ANALYSIS AGAINST FERC'S FIVE REFORM CATEGORIES 
A.  Category (1) — Efficient Transmission Service Application and Study 
Processes 
Primary CBRS Dimension: Transparency     Score: 2 of 5 — Conditional. 
PJM's current transmission service application and study processes for large loads (>50 MW) 
proceed without a bounded time-to-service commitment, without public reporting on median or 
90th-percentile completion timelines, and without a customer-facing remedy mechanism for 
delayed studies. The Commission's Show Cause Order requires that reform in this category 
address efficiency of application and study processes. Efficiency without measurable customer-
facing commitments is not reform. 
PJM's current serial and cluster study procedures produce study timelines that vary materially 
across cluster cycles and across similarly situated interconnection customers. Aggregate 
outputs are reported in general terms but not disaggregated by service size, load class, or 
region. Under CBRS Transparency, this posture scores 2/5: partial disclosure of aggregate 
outcomes with no time-bounded customer commitment and no disaggregated public reporting. 
B.  Category (2) — Preventing Cost Shifting and Requiring Transparency 
Primary CBRS Dimension: Ratepayer Protection  —  KEYSTONE FLAG ACTIVE     Score: 1 
of 5 — below the CBRS Acceptable threshold. 
KEYSTONE FLAG — RATEPAYER PROTECTION 
Under CBRS scoring architecture, Ratepayer Protection is a pass/fail keystone dimension. A 
failing score caps the aggregate scoring band at Conditional regardless of other dimension 
performance. This is the first of two active keystone flags in Maven's assessment of PJM's current 
tariff posture. It warrants Commission-level intervention on Category (2).

PJM identified a 5,249.9 MW increase in forecasted load for the 2027/28 delivery year, largely 
due to additional Large Loads.4 The Independent Market Monitor has separately reported that 
recent actual and forecast load increases were almost entirely due to existing and projected 
large data-center additions, and that the inclusion of 17,071 MW of existing and forecast data-

estimated that data-center-driven load growth could impose up to $163 billion in additional PJM

The mismatch between cost causation and cost allocation in this category is not incidental. It is 
the direct product of a tariff structure that treats new large loads as if their marginal system 
impact is proportionate to their share of aggregate load, rather than proportionate to the 
transmission upgrades and capacity procurement they specifically require. Under CBRS 
Ratepayer Protection, the current PJM tariff posture fails to allocate the costs of transmission 
upgrades and capacity procurement attributable to large-load interconnection to the responsible 
load class. Score: 1/5 — below the CBRS Acceptable threshold, with KEYSTONE FLAG active. 
C.  Category (3) — Accommodating Co-Location and Behind-the-Meter 
Generation 
Primary CBRS Dimension: Co-Location & Interconnection Governance     Score: 2 of 5 — 
Conditional. 
As Commissioner Chang's concurrence in this docket notes, the co-location clarity issue is 
somewhat different for PJM than for the other RTOs addressed in the June 18 Show Cause 
Orders, because PJM already has a separate co-location-related proceeding before the 
Commission, in addition to a behind-the-meter generation (“BTMG”) compliance docket. Both 
parallel proceedings have compliance filings pending. Reform in Category (3) of this Show 
Cause docket should be tested for consistency with dispositions in those parallel proceedings 
before final Commission action.1 
Substantively, co-location and BTMG arrangements create the specific reliability risk illustrated 
by the July 10, 2024 Dominion Energy event, in which a failed lightning arrestor on a 230 kV 
facility produced voltage disturbances that led data-center-type load to transfer to backup power 
systems, resulting in an approximately 1,500 MW reduction of voltage-sensitive load on the 
transmission system.11 
Under CBRS Co-Location & Interconnection Governance, the current PJM posture scores 2/5. 
The posture recognizes co-location as a service class but does not yet include measurable 
reliability-hold-harmless mechanisms, financial surety instruments backing switching-event 
ratepayer exposure, or reciprocity provisions between co-located generation and the load it 
serves.

D.  Category (4) — New Transmission Services for Flexible Large Loads 
Primary CBRS Dimension: Financial Surety  —  KEYSTONE FLAG ACTIVE     Score: 2 of 5

KEYSTONE FLAG — FINANCIAL SURETY 
Under CBRS scoring architecture, Financial Surety is a pass/fail keystone dimension. This is the 
second of two active keystone flags in Maven's assessment of PJM's current tariff posture. 
Concurrent activation of two keystone flags on Categories (2) and (4) warrants coordinated 
Commission-level intervention on both categories. 
 
The Commission's identification of Category (4) as a reform lane recognizes that current firm-
service transmission tariffs are not the only option for large-load service. Curtailable, 
interruptible, or flexible-load tariff classes could reduce the cost-causation impact of large-load 
interconnection on the capacity market and on transmission upgrade needs. 
The state-level policy environment reinforces this reform lane. New Jersey Executive Order No. 
2, signed January 20, 2026, directs the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities to commence 
development of a Virtual Power Plant (“VPP”) program within 180 days of the order. The order 
directs that the VPP program aggregate behind-the-meter distributed energy resources and 
enable participation in PJM markets to the fullest extent possible.12 The 180-day mandate falls 
on or around July 19, 2026, thirteen days after the intervention deadline for these Comments. 
PJM has enabled DER aggregation participation in the capacity market beginning with the 
2028/29 Delivery Year BRA. Under PJM's implementation schedule, DER aggregations required 
notice of intent by June 12, 2026 for inclusion in the 2029/30 BRA, or by December 1, 2026 for 
inclusion in the 2030/31 BRA.13 This creates a structural timing gap between state VPP program 
launch and RTO capacity-market participation eligibility for aggregators in the PJM footprint. 
Under CBRS Financial Surety, the current PJM tariff posture does not provide financial surety 
instruments backing large-load commitments to their contracted service level, does not include 
compensation for contribution to reliability from flexible or curtailable loads, and does not yet 
coordinate operationally with state VPP or FERC Order 2222 pathways. Score: 2/5 — 
Conditional, with KEYSTONE FLAG active.

VII.  RECOMMENDATIONS TO THE COMMISSION 
Maven Solutions respectfully recommends that the Commission require PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. to submit tariff revisions or, in the alternative, a detailed evidentiary showing, addressing 
each of the five reform categories as set out below within the response period established in the 
Show Cause Order.

Recommendation 1 — Category (1): Time-Bounded Transmission Service Application and 
Study Commitments 
PJM should be required to file proposed tariff revisions that include: (i) a bounded time-to-
service commitment for large-load interconnection studies, disaggregated by requested service 
size class; (ii) quarterly public reporting of median and 90th-percentile completion times, 
disaggregated by service size and by region; and (iii) a customer-remedy mechanism for 
delayed studies. Compliance with (i) and (iii) should be tested annually against the reported 
metrics required by (ii). 
Recommendation 2 — Category (2): Cost-Causation Methodology (KEYSTONE) 
PJM should be required to file proposed tariff revisions establishing a cost-causation 
methodology that: (i) attributes transmission upgrade costs and capacity procurement costs 
caused by large-load interconnection to the responsible load class, not to Load Serving Entities 
or residential rate classes; (ii) provides for public disclosure of the allocation methodology on a 
per-cluster and per-upgrade basis; (iii) is coordinated with state utility commissions on retail rate 
design implications; and (iv) includes a mechanism for retroactive adjustment where actual load 
materialization differs from the load presented at interconnection. 
Recommendation 3 — Category (3): Co-Location and BTMG Reliability and Ratepayer-Hold-
Harmless 
Any co-location or behind-the-meter generation tariff provisions adopted in Category (3) should 
be consistent with the Commission's dispositions in PJM's parallel co-location and BTMG 
compliance proceedings, and should include: (i) measurable reliability-hold-harmless 
mechanisms addressing the specific switching-event risk illustrated by the July 10, 2024 
Dominion event; (ii) financial surety instruments backing switching-event ratepayer exposure; 
and (iii) explicit reciprocity provisions between the co-located generation and the load it serves. 
Consistency verification with the parallel proceedings should occur before final Commission 
action in Category (3) of this docket. 
Recommendation 4 — Category (4): Flexible Large-Load Tariff Class (KEYSTONE) 
PJM should be required to file proposed tariff revisions establishing one or more new large-load 
tariff classes that: (i) provide for curtailable or interruptible service with defined compensation; 
(ii) include contribution-to-reliability crediting; (iii) coordinate operationally with state Virtual 
Power Plant programs and with the FERC Order 2222 DER aggregation pathway, including 
scheduling coordination for the current 2029/30 and 2030/31 BRA participation windows; and 
(iv) include financial surety instruments backing service-class commitments. 
Recommendation 5 — Category (5): Study-Process Reciprocity for Proximate Generation 
The study process for generation serving electrically proximate or co-located loads should 
incorporate: (i) reciprocity with the study process for the load it serves; (ii) cost-transparency 
provisions matching those recommended for Category (2); and (iii) explicit treatment of the
