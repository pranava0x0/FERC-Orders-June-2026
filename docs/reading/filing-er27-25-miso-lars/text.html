I. 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
LARS responds to rapidly evolving reliability and resource adequacy needs in the MISO 
region.  MISO is proposing LARS to facilitate the cost effective, timely and reliable integration of 
new large loads into the system.  LARS supports the Commission’s call to action to meet these 
new challenges in short order.  LARS will provide a path for the interconnection of both large 
loads and supporting generation pairs in a parallel 120-day study process.  The proposal leverages 
the existing success in the MISO’s Expedited Project Review (“EPR”) process on the load side 
and lessons learned from Expedited Resource Additions Study (“ERAS”) process on the 
generation side.  
 
MISO is experiencing significant growth in electricity demand at the same time that 
additional resources are needed to reliably serve that demand.  MISO projects annual load growth 
of approximately 1% to 3% through 2044, driven in part by energy-intensive loads such as data 
centers and manufacturing expansion, and has identified the need to accelerate new resource 
additions as demand grows.6   
 
The need to accelerate the infrastructure necessary to reliably serve growing electricity 
demand is also reflected in the Department of Energy’s (“DOE”) Speed to Power initiative, which 
seeks to accelerate development timelines for large-scale transmission and generation projects to 
meet rising electricity demand.7  LARS advances that objective within the MISO region by 
providing a process through which significant new large loads and generation intended to support 
that load can be studied on an accelerated and coordinated basis.

OVERVIEW OF THE LARS PROCESS

Another important condition of the LARS process is the requirement that the load and 
generation pair be located in the same Local Resource Zone (“LRZ”).44  The Point of 
Interconnection(s) specified in a LARS Application must be located in the same LRZ as the Point 
of Withdrawal (“POW”) of the LARS Associated Load.  As discussed by Ms. Drake, the intent of 
the LARS process is to identify an optimized Network Upgrade solution for both the load and 
generation, and MISO retains that flexibility in the LRZ because it aligns with MISO’s Resource 
Adequacy construct.45  Specifically, MISO does not have a requirement that loads bring generation, 
but through the proposed LARS process, by restricting the Point of Interconnection(s) specified in 
a LARS Application to be located in the same LRZ as the Point of Withdrawal, MISO providing 
an incentive for Load to bring Generation in a manner that maximizes the positive impact this 
Generation has on Resource Adequacy.  MISO recognizes there are other ways it could have 
limited the pairing such as requiring the generation and LARS Associated Load to be located in 
the same Local Balancing Authority area or through requiring that the location of the Generating 
Facilities be within a specific number of substations of the LARS Associated Load.  However, 
limiting the pairing of Generating Facilities and LARS Associated Load to the same LRZ strikes 
an appropriate balance between accommodating large, landlocked LARS Associated Loads that 
may need additional POIs to propose sufficient Generating Facilities needed to serve that load.
