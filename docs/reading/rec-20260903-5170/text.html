SUPPORTIVE COMMENTS AND LIMITED PROTEST OF THE NEW JERSEY 
BOARD OF PUBLIC UTILITIES 
 
On August 13, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) proposed revisions to the 
Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) and the PJM Open 
Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”).1  
This proposed service would be implemented by PJM during periods of insufficient resources to 
reliably meet demand in real-time operations, enabling PJM to prioritize load reduction in 
zones/areas where New Large Loads have not brought their own new capacity or had their capacity 
needs covered through the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”).  The New Jersey Board of 
Public Utilities (“Board”) supports the Proposal with a limited protest, as it presents a largely 
workable solution to the region’s resource adequacy shortfall and appropriately incentivizes New 
Large Loads in the region to bring new capacity to the system.

II. 
Interim Resource Adequacy Service 
The Board appreciates PJM’s decision to propose a PJM-led resource adequacy service for 
New Large Loads that fail to offset their registered peak load with sufficient capacity arrangements 
through Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) or by being allocated a share of the RBP 
Unforced Capacity (“UCAP”) MW.  PJM’s Proposal is a departure from PJM staff’s Stage 4 CIFP 
RBP/C&M proposal, which envisioned a voluntary state-led program,11 and the Board appreciates 
PJM’s tenacity in advancing a PJM-led program instead.  The PJM-led approach addresses the

At a high level, this new service will enable the grid operator to direct load shed directives

at large; (2) provides teeth to incentivize New Large Loads to contribute new capacity to the grid; 
(3) respects the state and federal jurisdictional division outlined in the FPA; and (4) avoids the

Limited Protest - Already-Cleared Resources Should Not Qualify for BYONC 
While the Board generally supports the BYONC criteria that PJM lists in Attachment DD-
4 section VI,13 the Board is not supportive of PJM’s decision to include capacity that cleared in the 
BRAs for the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 DYs to qualify as BYONC-eligible resources.  The Board 
thinks new capacity to meet BYONC eligibility should be defined as capacity that has not yet 
cleared a Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) auction.  Additionally, the Board appreciates that the 
process for setting new capacity for the 2029/2030 BRA is underway.  To the extent new resources 
want to participate in the 2029/2030 BRA and serve as a BYONC resource, they should present 
PJM with a BYONC contract before the auction, as contemplated in Attachment DD-4 section 
VII(c), and commit to acting as a price taker – otherwise new resources clearing in the 2029/2030 
BRA should likewise not be eligible to serve as a BYONC resource.  
Principally, the Board does not think capacity resources that cleared for the first time in the 
BRAs for the 2027/2028, 2028/2029, and 2029/2030 (if they did not present a BYONC contract 
before the BRA, as noted above) DYs should qualify as BYONC because these resources have 
already been committed to contribute to our system’s reliability.  Frankly, these are not new 
resources that require additional price certainty to enter PJM’s uncertain investment environment, 
take on risks from federal regulatory uncertainty, and undergo longer project development 
timelines due to supply constraints and siting and permitting delays.  They are not resources that 
large loads brought to the system.  Rather, these are resources that have already committed to come

The Board thinks that BYONC should be transferable for New Large Loads and new supply 
resources.  PJM asserts that “[a]llowing BYONC to leave or be traded after entering into an 
arrangement with a New Large Load would dramatically increase the complexity of the program 
without commensurate benefit.”25  The Board disagrees.  Transferable BYONC obligations serve 
an important role.  Transferring BYONC can reduce long-term counterparty and performance risk 
and can allow a New Large Load to replace a resource that becomes uneconomic, unavailable, or 
otherwise unable to support the arrangement.  It can also allow a large load to transfer its BYONC 
to another large load if the two loads value reliable service differently from each other over time.  
The Board thinks there should be more flexibility for New Large Loads and new supply resources 
over the course of the ten-year BYONC term, and the Board suggests PJM and FERC sever this 
restrictive provision.   
 
The Board, however, thinks PJM provides a reasonable accommodation for Demand 
Resources and DER Capacity Aggregation Resources.  These resources must show an initial ten-
year commitment, but they also have the ability to replace locations and contracts, if needed.  
However, the Board suggests that PJM’s proposed Tariff language provides further clarity on the 
eligibility of Demand Resources and DER Capacity Aggregation Resources that may qualify as 
BYONC, because as drafted, the criteria limits the eligibility of these resources to only those 
locations that have not participated in a prior RPM auction, which may be inappropriate if the 
characteristics of the load at such location have changed over time.  The Board requests that PJM 
allow more flexibility and permit a location to participate as a new Demand Resource or DER if 
the characteristics of the load have changed since it last participated in an RPM auction.

Limited Protest - PJMs Proposed IRAS Compensation Mechanisms Should Be Removed 
The Board thinks PJM’s compensation structure is ambiguous, unclear, and unnecessary, 
and requests that Attachment DD-4 section X, which puts forth the conditions of compensation for 
IRAS, is severed.  PJM states that whether a particular Eligible New Large Load is entitled to 
compensation will be determined by state authorities under state law.26  Attachment DD-4 section 
X establishes a wholesale rate of 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate unless the Eligible 
New Large Load elects to accept less compensation or waive it altogether.  The interaction between 
the rate, state action, and the large load is unclear.  For instance, if a state directs the large load to 
waive the rate through its retail tariff, is the large load obligated to waive it?  The Board is 
concerned that this mechanism may be setting up future litigation and disputes about who has the 
right to certain rate treatments.   
The Board thinks that the easier path is to sever the provision.  The compensation scheme 
appears intended, in part, to redress loads that may be subject to capacity price obligations even if 
they do not get the same reliability as other loads on the system.  However, if states wish to 
compensate Eligible New Large Loads for accepting reduced reliability of service in lieu of 
bringing their own capacity, they can do so through their existing retail ratemaking authority, and 
the large loads are fully able to challenge those retail ratemaking decisions through the proper 
venues.  Accordingly, the Board recommends that Attachment DD-4 section X of PJM’s proposed 
Tariff revisions be severed.

IV. 
Removal of Demand from VRR Curve 
The Board strongly supports PJM’s decision to remove uncovered incremental New Large 
Load amounts from the VRR Curve.  The Board has advocated for this in many stakeholder 
forums, including direct letters to the PJM Board of Managers from the Board’s President,27 and a 
separate letter from the Board’s Executive Director on this matter.28   
The Board has advocated this position for multiple reasons.  First, if Eligible New Large 
Loads29 subject to IRAS were included in the VRR Curve, the market would signal to these 
customers that the system will continue to procure capacity for them.  This would undermine one 
of the core tenets for designating this service to Eligible New Large Loads in the first place, which 
is to provide an incentive for these customers to procure their own capacity.  Additionally, keeping

Large Loads will not be allowed into the VRR Curve so long as they have not fulfilled their

year regional transmission organization (“RTO”) Reliability Requirement criterion is met.

Energy Market Costs Should Be Monitored  
To the extent that a significant amount of New Large Load elects to interconnect without 
BYONC or Allocated RBP UCAP MW, and thus remain subject to IRAS, energy prices may 
increase substantially for ratepayers.  We have yet to see how many New Large Loads will choose 
to be subject to IRAS service over seeking bilateral contract arrangements.  However, if a 
substantial amount of New Large Load fails to offset its registered peak load with sufficient 
capacity arrangements through BYONC or Allocated RBP UCAP MW and are subject to IRAS, 
PJM may need to consider additional measures to protect ratepayers from consistently higher costs 
in the energy market.  While there can be financial benefits to having a higher grid utilization rate 
at large, the Board suggests PJM and FERC monitor whether IRAS loads are resulting in 
substantially higher energy market costs.  In the future, it may be worth considering further 
incentivizing or requiring New Large Loads to bring commensurate new supply resources with 
them.  
V. 
Conclusion 
The Board commends PJM for proposing an RTO-led curtailment program that applies to 
New Large Loads that have not met their capacity obligations.  The Board also appreciates PJM’s 
proposed removal of Eligible New Large Loads from the VRR Curve, as it will protect ratepayers 
from many of the costs and risks associated with New Large Loads in the capacity market and 
better incentivize New Large Loads to bring their own new capacity.  However, the Board’s support 
does not come without concerns, and the Board requests that certain elements of the Proposal are
