Protest of Community Collaboration on Climate Change, et al. re the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.’s filing proposing revisions to establish the Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement

Community Organizations and Clean Energy Advocates (Sierra Club, NRDC, Clean Grid Alliance and others) protest MISO's ZGIA filing, accepting the zero-injection design but objecting to missing penalties, transparency, scope limits and cost-shift safeguards.

Asks FERC to: Direct MISO to cure the listed deficiencies, through a deficiency letter or compliance in the show cause proceeding, before FERC accepts the ZGIA filing.

Parallel proceeding. Protests MISO's ZGIA filing in ER26-3552, MISO's co-location response to the E-8 order, and argues it falls short of the order's cost transparency and cost-shift protections.

E-8 MISO: Services for flexible / co-located loads (P 6(d), p. 7) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6) E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. The protesters accept the ZGIA design and object to the missing tariff mechanics.
    Our organizations do not oppose the intended design of MISO’s ZGIA Proposal PDF p. 2
  2. They ask FERC to send MISO a deficiency letter or order further compliance in the show cause docket.
    we urge the Commission to direct MISO to address these tariff and procedural defects through either a deficiency letter or direct further compliance via the show cause proceeding. PDF p. 2
  3. They want operational penalties for unauthorized injections, citing the E-8 order's concern about control and protection requirements.
    The Commission should require MISO to modify its ZGIA proposal to include robust operational penalties for ZGIA Generating Facilities that make unauthorized injections PDF p. 4
  4. They say MISO has not justified the 20-second Transient Tolerance Window.
    MISO has not justified why a maximum period of 20 seconds is either operationally attainable by a generator PDF p. 12
  5. They say MISO falls short of the E-8 order's cost transparency requirements.
    MISO has fallen well short of the Order’s clear requirements on transparency. PDF p. 14
  6. They ask for a cap on the ZGIA program's size or duration, citing the ERAS and SPP HILLGA precedents.
    To achieve the narrow tailoring required by the Commission’s open access precedents, a program that bypasses the traditional interconnection queue must have some limitation on how large it could get. PDF p. 17
  7. They say deferred upgrade costs could reach other customers if supporting generation fails.
    the Proposal does not require adequate financial security, cost-recovery agreements, or true-ups for the cost of deferred transmission upgrades if the ZG Generating Facility fails to materialize, retires early, or becomes permanently unavailable. PDF p. 21
  8. They call the five-year commitment an intent, not a firm term, and unsupported by evidence.
    Beyond this bald statement, MISO provides no evidentiary basis for choosing this inadequate formulation and therefore is arbitrary. PDF p. 22
  9. They conclude that MISO has the structure for a reasonable co-location program and should cure defects before acceptance.
    Since MISO has the structure for a reasonable co-location program, we urge the Commission to direct MISO to cure the deficiencies with filing before accepting it. PDF p. 30
