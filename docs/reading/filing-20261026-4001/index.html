EL26-67-000 · 2026-10-26 · NATIONAL CONGRESS OF AMERICAN INDIANS

Comments of the National Congress of American Indians urging FERC for a Moratorium on Hyperscale Data Center Approvals Pending Comprehensive Grid Reliability and Ratepayer Impact Assessments

The National Congress of American Indians transmits Resolution MEM-26-016 to Chairman Swett, asking FERC to impose a moratorium on approvals supporting hyperscale data centers until independent grid and ratepayer assessments are done, with cost protections and Tribal consultation.

Asks FERC to: Impose an immediate moratorium on certificates, transmission approvals and authorizations for hyperscale data centers pending an independent assessment, with binding cost allocation conditions and mandatory Tribal consultation.

Background. Letter filed in EL26-67 after the show cause order; its call for data center operators to bear upgrade costs echoes the cost-shifting concern in the E-7 cost recovery directive but answers no directive.

E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28)

Key points

  1. NCAI asks for a moratorium on related approvals until an independent impact assessment is completed.
    impose an immediate moratorium on the issuance of any related certificates, transmission approvals, or regulatory authorizations supporting hyperscale data centers PDF p. 2
  2. It wants binding conditions so data center upgrade costs fall on facility operators, not existing Tribal or residential ratepayers.
    all grid and infrastructure upgrade costs attributable to data center demand are borne entirely by the facility operators, not passed on to existing Tribal or residential ratepayers. PDF p. 2
  3. It asks for independent, conflict-free assessments of resource adequacy and congestion effects.
    rigorous, independent, and conflict-free assessments of how these facilities impact regional resource adequacy and transmission congestion PDF p. 2
  4. It asks for Tribal consultation at the outset of transmission planning or interconnection for these developments.
    engage Tribal Nations as sovereign partners at the outset of any transmission planning or interconnection process PDF p. 2
