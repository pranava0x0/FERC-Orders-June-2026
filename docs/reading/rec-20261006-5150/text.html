II. LIMITED ANSWER
In its September 3 Protest, PPL Electric explained that because of how the applicable tariff language was written, if accepted without modification the IRAS Filing would mark the first time in history that the Commission would be requiring the mandatory and involuntary designation of end-use retail customers (Eligible New Large Loads) to provide a FERC- jurisdictional wholesale rate service (IRAS), and the compulsory provision of that FERC- jurisdictional wholesale rate service to a Regional Transmission Organization (“RTO”) pursuant to FERC-approved rates, terms, and conditions in a FERC-approved tariff.11  PPL Electric explained at length that, while PJM correctly defers to the applicable Electric Distributor and state regulatory authorities in determining which specific individual Eligible New Large Loads within a given zone should actually provide IRAS when called upon, the fact remains that all Eligible New Large Loads (end-use, retail customers) within a zone are assigned responsibility by PJM to provide this wholesale rate service, pursuant to FERC- jurisdictional rates, terms, and conditions, and must provide this wholesale rate service to PJM when called upon. PJM’s answer is straightforward—the states and/or RERRAs, in coordination with Electric Distributors and their LSEs and retail customers served by those LSEs ultimately identify those retail customers. That— and the regulation of the terms and conditions of retail rates, and the regulation of retail customers—remains within the exclusive jurisdiction of the states. As noted previously, PJM’s load reduction directives will be informed by the degree to which load in a certain zone/area’s IRAS responsibility has been reduced through MW of BYONC or Allocated RBP UCAP MW. However, PJM will direct load reduction directives to Electric Distributors, while states/RERRAs may determine which retail customers will implement the load reduction directives.

by granting those Eligible New Large Loads the ability to “say no” under retail service terms and conditions is not at all clear from PJM’s proposed tariff language, but if true is critically significant.

Unfortunately, if this is indeed the intent of the IRAS Filing, PPL Electric’s policy and jurisdictional concerns have only deepened.  First, with respect to PPL Electric’s policy concerns, the implications of PJM’s clarification in its September 23 Answer are striking.  If state regulatory authorities can exempt Eligible New Large Loads from IRAS curtailment obligations simply by permitting them to “say no” under state law or regulation, then there is a real risk of disparate treatment and cost shifting in the application of IRAS within the PJM Region.  Specifically, if Eligible New Large Loads may ‘opt-out’ through localized arrangements with state regulatory authorities to limit (or even eliminate entirely) their individual IRAS exposure, the benefits of BYONC in reducing an Eligible New Large Loads’ individual IRAS curtailment exposure are, by definition, undermined.  More importantly, the ability for state regulatory authorities to use that authority to remove the exposure of an Eligible New Large Load to IRAS curtailment creates the potential for the shifting of cost and reliability burdens onto other parts of the PJM Region that do not grant such protection.  This is a recipe for undue discrimination in the implementation of IRAS, and constitutes the exact outcome that PJM is trying to avoid.

Second, with respect to PPL Electric’s jurisdictional concerns, PJM’s clarification in its September 23 Answer creates significant ambiguity regarding the legal foundation for IRAS under the FPA.  As PPL Electric noted in its September 3 Protest, IRAS is:

1. a FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service;
2. established under the FERC-jurisdictional PJM Tariff;

3. provided by a FERC-defined class of providers (Eligible New Large Loads); and
4. compensated pursuant to FERC-jurisdictional rates specified in the PJM Tariff. If the purpose of PJM’s clarification above is to emphasize that, notwithstanding all of these federal characteristics of IRAS, Eligible New Large Loads are not compelled under federal law to provide this FERC-jurisdictional wholesale service but instead are only compelled to do so if the applicable state regulatory authority forces them to, this simply reorients PPL Electric’s original jurisdictional concern.  Specifically, it is unclear under what legal authority state regulatory authorities in the PJM Region would be able to compel end-use retail customers (Eligible New Large Loads) to provide a FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service to PJM. State utility law generally focuses on the entities providing a service to end-use retail customers, such as distribution utilities, and even then, the service being regulated is one established under state law itself.  To the extent that PJM is imagining that a state would somehow use its regulatory authority to directly force end-use retail customers to provide this FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service to PJM, it would be an unprecedented use of state authority and raise bizarre issues of federalism.  This is especially true in light of the fact that the Commission’s jurisdiction under FPA section 201 is “plenary” and extends “to all wholesale sales in interstate commerce except those which Congress has made explicitly subject to regulation by the States.”13  The FPA does not grant states the ability to compel the provision of FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate services.  To the contrary, “[t]he FPA leaves no room either for direct state regulation of the prices of interstate wholesales or for regulation that would indirectly achieve the same result.”14

PPL Electric appreciates the fine line that PJM is attempting to walk under FPA section 20115 in this proceeding.  But given the importance of establishing a sound legal basis for Commission approval of IRAS, PJM must fully explain and support the actual intent and jurisdictional basis of its proposal.  Put simply:

1. What legal authority (federal or state/local) is being invoked to force the involuntary designation of end-use retail customers to provide a FERC-jurisdictional wholesale rate service (IRAS), and to compel those end-use retail customers to provide that service to PJM at rates, terms, and conditions established under the PJM Tariff?

a. If the answer is “federal,” then under what provision of the FPA does the Commission have that power?

b. Alternatively, if the answer is “state,” then again, under what legal basis do state regulatory authorities have this ability, given FERC’s plenary jurisdiction over wholesale rates, services, and markets?

PPL Electric encourages PJM and the Commission to consider the alternatives that PPL Electric provided in its September 3 Protest which, if adopted, would strengthen the jurisdictional foundation upon which IRAS is built.  Specifically:

Option 1 -
Have PJM’s Tariff language stop at the issuance of the

have already signed the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.  This
would avoid a scenario where federally imposed obligations are
colliding with state and local-jurisdictional law and tariffs,
including the statutory “obligation to serve,” and would provide
a much more jurisdictionally sound legal pathway to effectuate
connect and manage under FPA section 201.
