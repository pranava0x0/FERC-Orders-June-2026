PROTEST OF THE SOUTHERN REGULATORS

The Mississippi Public Service Commission and the Arkansas Public Service Commission (together, the Southern Regulators) protest MISO’s1 August 28, 2026 filing to establish interconnection reliability requirements for large loads.2  Southern Regulators support Entergy’s3 Limited Protest4 and request for relief and offer the following additional comments.5 First, the Filing provides only a general overview of interconnection requirements applicable to large loads. It lacks the specific detail needed to actually interconnect to the grid. Absent that detail, developers and the transmission owners are unable to design, purchase and install the correct equipment to operate large loads (e.g., refineries, data centers).  A general description is not enough.  And, in the absence of those requirements, developers and transmission owners run the risk and expense of installing equipment, consistent with good utility practices, only to then replace that equipment at great expense after MISO later publishes the actual requirements.  MISO’s approach is analogous to building a house based on a picture instead of technical drawings.

Second, when it finally develops the requirements, MISO unreasonably proposes to place them in Business Practice Manuals (BPMs).  MISO can easily change BPMs with minimal or no

Developers and transmission owners have no recourse to challenge a BPM other than by filing a complaint at FERC.  These requirements will impose significant costs on developers and transmission owners; changing the requirements will further increase those costs.  As Entergy explained, the Rule of Reason mandates that these requirements be placed in the Tariff where they are subject to FERC’s oversight to ensure they are just and reasonable.6

Finally, the remedy Entergy seeks – that MISO make a compliance filing that clearly identifies these requirements and places them in the tariff – is fully within FERC’s authority. Conclusion

Consistent with Entergy’s Limited Protest, the Southern Regulators ask that FERC condition acceptance of MISO’s Filing by requiring MISO to file the specific interconnection requirements in the Tariff, not in the BPMs.
