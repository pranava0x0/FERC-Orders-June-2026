PROTEST OF THE SOUTHERN REGULATORS 
 
The Mississippi Public Service Commission and the Arkansas Public Service Commission 
(together, the Southern Regulators) protest MISO’s1 August 28, 2026 filing to establish 
interconnection reliability requirements for large loads.2  Southern Regulators support Entergy’s3 
Limited Protest4 and request for relief and offer the following additional comments.5  
First, the Filing provides only a general overview of interconnection requirements 
applicable to large loads. It lacks the specific detail needed to actually interconnect to the grid.  
Absent that detail, developers and the transmission owners are unable to design, purchase and 
install the correct equipment to operate large loads (e.g., refineries, data centers).  A general 
description is not enough.  And, in the absence of those requirements, developers and transmission 
owners run the risk and expense of installing equipment, consistent with good utility practices, 
only to then replace that equipment at great expense after MISO later publishes the actual 
requirements.  MISO’s approach is analogous to building a house based on a picture instead of 
technical drawings. 
Second, when it finally develops the requirements, MISO unreasonably proposes to place 
them in Business Practice Manuals (BPMs).  MISO can easily change BPMs with minimal or no

Conclusion 
Consistent with Entergy’s Limited Protest, the Southern Regulators ask that FERC 
condition acceptance of MISO’s Filing by requiring MISO to file the specific interconnection 
requirements in the Tariff, not in the BPMs.
