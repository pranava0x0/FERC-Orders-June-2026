I. INTRODUCTION
In the Order, the Commission found that PJM’s Tariff “is unjust and unreasonable because it does not contain provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to generators serving Co-Located Load and Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-Located Load.”3  Specifically, the Commission found PJM’s Tariff “unjust and unreasonable because it does not include types of transmission service that reflect Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of Co-Located Loads that are willing and able to limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.”4

The Commission ordered PJM to offer three new forms of transmission service under its Tariff: (i) Firm Contract Demand transmission service; (ii) Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service and (iii) interim, non-firm transmission service.5  The Commission also established a paper hearing to determine the just and reasonable rates, terms, and conditions for these new transmission services.6

The PJM Transmission Owners are proposing to charge the same rate for Firm Contract Demand Service and NITS because the level of service provided to both customers is, for all relevant purposes, the same.  Both NITS and Firm Contract Demand Service are firm services for which network upgrades will be planned and constructed as necessary, with the only difference being that upgrades for NITS customers are identified based on existing and anticipated load growth while upgrades for Firm Contract Demand Service customers will be identified based on their contracted demand level.  Firm Contract Demand Service customers will be able to serve their contracted demand through capacity procured by PJM just like NITS customers and will not be required to designate specific points of receipt and delivery. There is no need or benefit to developing a separate Firm Contract Demand Service rate or utilizing the existing Firm Point-to-Point Service (“PTPS”) rates.19  Firm PTPS rates are generally based on the same revenue requirement as the NITS rate, and the nature of Firm Contract Demand Service, as contemplated in the Order, is essentially a variation of NITS with a specified contract demand level.  Consequently, PJM will need to treat and plan for Firm Contract Demand Service load in the same manner that it plans for load using NITS, thus providing service to the Firm Contract Demand Service load on a comparable basis with NITS load.  Because NITS and Firm Contract Demand customers receive comparable benefits, using the same rate for NITS and Firm Contract Demand Service is consistent with the Commission’s cost allocation principles, particularly the requirement that transmission customers be allocated costs roughly commensurate with the estimated benefits they receive.20  On the other hand, it would be unjust and unreasonable and unduly discriminatory to charge Firm Contract Demand

3. As noted above, a Co-Location Arrangement is synchronized to the transmission system.  Should the rate paid by an Eligible Customer on behalf of a Co-Located load taking only the new Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service that elects to not reserve any amount of that service for a period of time (e.g., one month) reflect not only charges for regulation and black start services, but also an additional charge given that regulation and black start services could not be provided without the transmission system?  If so, how should that additional charge be determined?

The PJM Transmission Owners are proposing to charge a Grid Reliance Charge to Eligible Customers taking the new Contract Demand services based on the difference between the amount of their firm contract demand for Firm Contract Demand Service or, in the case of Non-Firm Contract Demand Service customers, the amount of their reserved service, and their total (gross) on-site load.34  The proposed Grid Reliance Charge is designed to reflect the costs of using the transmission system to deliver black start and regulation services, as well as providing the inherent benefits that all customers, including Contract Demand customers, receive as a result of their synchronization to and reliance on the grid.35  The Grid Reliance Charge also recognizes the costs associated with the operations, maintenance, and other activities that PJM Transmission Owners will need to perform for the grid and to accommodate the increased number of protection schemes that will be required for Co-Located Load customers. As Commissioner Chang stated in her concurrence to the Order, a minimum charge such as the Grid Reliance Charge is “particularly essential to protecting customers against transmission cost shifts under the new framework established through [the Order].”37 Commissioner Chang went on to explain that all Co-Located Loads are synchronized with the grid through their generators, and rely on the PJM grid.38  The PJM Transmission Owners agree. The Grid Reliance Charge is necessary and appropriate to ensure that Co-Located Load customers pay their appropriate share of transmission system costs.  Co-Located Load and its associated generation is electrically connected to the transmission system (not islanded) and therefore benefits from the existence and reliable operation of the transmission system and should contribute to paying the costs of continuing to reliably operate the system regardless of the amount of energy withdrawn.  Even with a zero MW contract demand, a large Co‑Located Load and generation arrangement will still depend on the grid for the delivery of various ancillary services and a suite of synchronization support services, such as stable frequency, inertia, stability services (damping, angle stability), fault current capability, and ride-through and protection coordination.  In short, a Co-Located Load’s net withdrawals do not fully reflect its actual reliance on the grid.

The Grid Reliance Charge methodology proposed by the PJM Transmission Owners represents a just and reasonable means of estimating the grid reliance benefits to Co-Located Load with an appropriate level of precision, while ensuring that the Grid Reliance Charge can be fairly and efficiently implemented.42  Pursuant to this methodology, the Grid Reliance Charge will be determined on an annual basis by multiplying an average fixed percentage of the portion of transmission costs associated with providing grid reliance services by each PJM Transmission Owner’s Attachment H annual transmission revenue requirement.  Using annual depreciation and O&M expenses to establish the Grid Reliance Charge is appropriate because these expenses best capture the portions of a transmission owner’s revenue requirement relating to the ongoing reliable operation of the grid.

5. What is the appropriate penalty charge or other remedial action that PJM should take to address any unreserved use of the PJM transmission system for an Eligible Customer taking Firm Contract Demand transmission service on behalf of a Co- Located load?

Robust penalty provisions are a necessary component of the new services required by the Commission.  First, and most importantly, any penalties must be sufficient to meaningfully deter Co-Located Load customers from exceeding their contract demand and making unreserved use of the transmission system.  Because network upgrades are planned and built only to accommodate load covered by Firm Contract Demand Service or NITS, it is of the utmost importance that Co-Located Loads respect their contractual limits, including any related curtailment or other instructions from PJM.  This concern is particularly acute because many of these arrangements will likely involve substantial amounts of load—hundreds, if not thousands of MWs—being served by on-site generation but still connected to the grid.  If these limits are not respected, the reliability consequences to the system could be severe.  Therefore, operational penalties must be structured to meaningfully deter load customers that exceed reserved service amounts.  Financial penalties alone are insufficient.

The PJM Transmission Owners understand that PJM intends to propose operational penalties, and agree with PJM that the imposition of such penalties is appropriate.  The PJM Transmission Owners believe that any penalty regime should rely on both operational consequences and complementary financial penalties.  The PJM Transmission Owners understand that, at a high level, PJM’s proposal would be to refer to FERC enforcement or applicable reliability organizations any failure to comply with PJM operating instructions.  In addition, PJM is proposing a “two-strike” approach, pursuant to which if a customer’s protective systems fail to operate as required, PJM would first determine whether to impose a 120-day “pause” in service and, with the second failure, would terminate the customer’s Contract

The PJM Transmission Owners believe this proposal is a good starting point conceptually, but recognize that additional details will need to be developed as to how these operational consequences would be applied.  As such, the PJM Transmission Owners reserve the right to provide further input as part of this paper hearing process when further information becomes available.

The PJM Transmission Owners also believe that a financial penalty structure is appropriate to complement and supplement operational penalties implemented by PJM. Although the PJM Transmission Owners anticipate that operational penalties will provide a significant deterrent against Co-Located Load customers exceeding their reserved transmission service use, financial penalties could still play a valuable role in enhancing this deterrent as well as ensuring the market and other ratepayers are made whole if a Co-Located Load customer exceeds its contract demand. The PJM Transmission Owners intend to work with PJM in continuing to explore the penalty options and will, if appropriate, offer a proposal at a subsequent stage of this paper hearing process.

7. What are the appropriate mechanisms for an anti-toggling feature for the Firm Contract Demand transmission service, including the appropriate minimum

service period and minimum notice period for discontinuing or modifying the

The Order indicates that Firm Contract Demand Service should have a minimum term of one year.  However, as the Commission recognized, there are concerns regarding a customer’s ability to toggle between firm and non-firm service levels.49  One of the primary concerns with permitting Firm Contract Demand Service customers to take service for a single-year term is that PJM will be required to procure capacity for these customers, and one-year commitments will not encourage the appropriate price signals necessary to incent additional needed capacity. There also is a potential concern that Firm Contract Demand Service customers will cause the need for network upgrades to accommodate their Firm Contract Demand Service and then exit the system, thereby potentially shifting costs to other customers.50  To partially address these concerns, the PJM Transmission Owners propose a five-year minimum commitment period for Firm Contract Demand Service.  A five-year minimum commitment period is appropriate because, as noted by the Commission in the Order, it mirrors the commitment period for loads seeking to procure their own capacity.51
