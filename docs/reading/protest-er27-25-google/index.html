ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23 · Google

Limited Protest of Google LLC

Google files a limited protest of MISO's LARS filing. It supports studying large loads with paired generation but says LARS lacks cost-shifting protections and gives load-serving entities gatekeeper control, and asks FERC to reject it or accept and suspend it.

Asks FERC to: Reject LARS with guidance to refile alongside cost-shift protections, or accept and suspend it to review with MISO's pro forma cost shifting agreement; add direct access for independent generators.

Answers the order. Protests MISO's LARS response to E-8 because the cost shifting protections the order seeks, including a pro forma cost recovery agreement, were not filed with it.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6)

Key points

  1. Google supports the LARS concept but protests that MISO filed it without protections against cost shifting.
    MISO has filed LARS without accompanying and necessary protections against cost shifting, which prevents the Commission from finding that LARS is just and reasonable. PDF p. 2
  2. Upgrades for load-caused violations are classed as Baseline Reliability or Other projects, whose costs are recovered from all network customers in the pricing zone.
    the costs of those project types are recovered from all network customers in a Transmission Pricing Zone. PDF p. 2
  3. Overallocated headroom would be fixed through a future MTEP project, and residential customers could bear that cost.
    residential and other commercial customers could be left holding the bag for transmission projects needed to interconnect LARS generators PDF p. 6
  4. Google suggests more conservative study assumptions, such as counting DPP requests at Decision Point II, to avoid overallocation.
    LARS could be modified to use more conservative assumptions PDF p. 7
  5. MISO plans to file the pro forma cost shifting agreement by November 16, 2026, so the record lacks it today.
    the cost shifting agreement will be submitted in a separate section 205 filing by November 16, 2026 PDF p. 8
  6. Google offers two options: reject with direction to refile with protections, or accept, suspend and consolidate with the MISO show cause proceeding.
    the Commission could accept and suspend the proposal and consolidate it with the MISO Large Load Show Cause proceeding PDF p. 8
  7. Google says routing applications through load-serving entities risks undue discrimination against independent generators.
    LARS opens the door to undue discrimination and could undermine core open access principles by making load-serving entities ("LSEs") the gatekeepers of the LARS process. PDF p. 9
