I. 
Executive Summary 
MISO appreciates the support of the Commission to ensure efficient, cost-effective and 
reliable integration of large loads.  MISO continues to work diligently with the states both 
individually and through the Organization of MISO States (“OMS”), as well as its member 
utilities, to support resource adequacy for planned load growth in a manner that maintains customer 
affordability, efficiency and regional reliability.  MISO’s current framework is well-suited to meet 
the needs of traditional load growth, and MISO’s existing processes have enabled us to maintain 
resource adequacy.  The unique nature of certain new large loads and the need to help ensure speed 
to power, however, brings new challenges that require MISO to continue to work with its states 
and broader stakeholder community to develop new tools and processes to ensure resource 
adequacy is maintained going forward.  Conventional load forecasting was designed around 
gradual and geographically-dispersed growth.  The new large loads, which include data center 
developments, reflect a new type of load growth.  A single project may require more load than an 
entire city at one location and desire service within a short period.  This concentrated and 
discontinuous load growth requires new processes to most effectively meet this new demand and 
maintain resource adequacy.  Many of these new large loads are difficult to forecast with a high

The MISO Region Is Resource Adequate.

The role of the States in ensuring resource adequacy.

involves overseeing Load Serving Entities (“LSEs”) and ensuring LSEs plan for and secure enough 
power to meet their customers’ demand.  MISO supports resource adequacy by determining 
Planning Reserve Margin, administering the Planning Resource Auction and providing data for 
planning, but it is the responsibility of the States and LSEs to ensure sufficient  capacity is available

b. 
MISO’s Regional Resource Assessment 
MISO is confident that the MISO Region will continue to be resource adequate in both the 
near and longer term.  In addition to the OMS-MISO Survey, MISO had also developed Regional 
Resource Assessment (“RRA”) to assess the state of resource adequacy in its footprint in the past.  
The RRA10 was one of the periodic studies MISO used to forecast how the mix of electricity-
generating resources in the MISO Region could evolve going forward.  
While the RRA and the OMS-MISO Survey are similar in some ways, there are some key 
differences.  The RRA is a 20-year outlook based on publicly announced resource plans and state 
and policy goals, whereas the OMS-MISO Survey is a more near-term (5-year outlook) resource 
adequacy assessment based on member plans and resource addition trends.  Separately but related, 
MISO also develops MISO Futures which typically entails two or more scenarios, with 20-year 
outlook, and is used for transmission planning purposes.   
B. 
MISO’s Efforts to Support Resource Adequacy 
MISO works diligently to develop, implement, and refine programs to give states and our

Seasonal Construct and Reliability Based Demand Curve  
MISO’s recent shift to a seasonal capacity market with seasonal accredited capacity better 
reflects extreme weather conditions that have become more prevalent.  These weather conditions 
affect both the seasonal peak demand and the available seasonal supply.  In addition, the seasonal 
construct better reflects the seasonal planned & forced outage patterns of supply.  The recent 
implementation of the Reliability Based Demand Curve (“RBDC”) reflects the contributions to 
reliability incremental MWs can add to the system.  The RBDC helps with price signals by 
indicating if the MISO Region has sufficient capacity for the prompt Planning Year.  On the supply

2. 
Transition to the Direct Loss-of-Load Accreditation Methodology 
MISO is currently transitioning to a two-step accreditation approach (the “Direct Loss-of-
Load” or “DLOL”-based methodology) that accredits capacity based on marginal contribution to 
reliability during periods of highest system risk (“marginal effective load carrying capability” or 
“marginal ELCC”) on a Resource Class basis.  MISO’s DLOL-based methodology combines both 
probabilistic and deterministic elements into a single resource accreditation process.  Simply 
described, the DLOL-based methodology takes two steps by first determining the size of the pie, 
and second, divvying up the pie.  The DLOL-based accreditation methodology will provide both 
MISO members and regulators engaged in integrated resource planning the right investment and 
retirement signals to help ensure the availability of resources with the attributes necessary to 
maintain reliability into the future.

Expedited Resource Addition Study

Expedited Resource Addition Study (“ERAS”) proposal (“Revised ERAS Proposal”).  As noted 
in its filing, the ERAS proposal was driven by the fact that MISO was facing urgent near-term 
resource adequacy and reliability concerns due to load growth, generation retirement, and delays 
in the interconnection process11 and that it would experience a 4.7 gigawatt (GW) shortfall by 2028 
if currently planned generation facility retirements were to occur.12

Interconnection Procedures (“GIP”) and pro forma Generator Interconnection Agreement (“GIA”) 
to establish a ERAS process, which provides for accelerated study of interconnection requests that 
will address urgent resource adequacy and reliability needs in the near term (i.e., within the next 
five years).13  Specifically, under the Revised ERAS Proposal, MISO proposed to create a quarterly

5. 
Joint Targeted Interconnection Queue Study 
 
The MISO-SPP Joint Targeted Interconnection Queue Study (“JTIQ”) has sped the queue 
up considerably by providing an alternative to the backlogged ordinary affected systems study 
process with SPP while providing both more efficient solutions to the needs of multiple cycles of 
Interconnection Customers as well as greater up-front cost certainty regarding potentially 
significant affected systems costs to generators entering the Definitive Planning Phase or MISO’s 
ERAS processes.  This increased efficiency and cost certainty allows generators to reach GIA 
faster and develop their projects from a starting point of increased cost certainty.  
The JTIQ portfolio directly enables new generation to serve emerging load by proactively 
constructing the transmission backbone needed for interconnection, rather than waiting for 
incremental upgrades triggered by one project at a time.  By identifying and mitigating cross‑seam 
constraints in advance, JTIQ provides large, regionally optimized transmission solutions that 
unlock more than 28 GW of new interconnection capability along the MISO‑SPP seam.  Because 
these upgrades are included upfront in MISO and SPP’s planning and interconnection models 
inclusive of ERAS studies, DPP cycles, and Expedited Project Reviews; projects can interconnect 
faster and with far greater cost certainty.

2. 
Large Load Parallel Study Process 
Current load and generation study processes are dependent on each other, requiring one to 
be complete before the next can begin.  Some large load customers are seeking a more integrated, 
predictable experience that reflects the real-world pairing of large loads with the generation needed 
to serve it, without requiring co-location.  MISO is working to address these concerns through the 
proposed Large Load Parallel Study Process.  The Large Load Parallel Study Process will be used 
for studying load greater than or equal to 250MW and will allow MISO to integrate studies for 
planned load and generation to increase speed to approval and optimize network upgrades.   
Through the proposed Large Load Parallel Study Process, an LSE will submit a proposed 
generation and load combination.  The generation and load have separate eligibility requirements 
(e.g., load ≥250 MW, generation nameplate max 150% of identified load need).  The proposed 
Large Load Parallel Study Process will analyze generation and transmission using the same study 
assumptions applied in Generator Interconnection and MTEP.  Both studies will be run 
concurrently and in parallel, with the load/transmission model incorporating the planned 
generation and planned load and finish at approximately the same time—within 120 days.  The 
proposed process is intended to ensure faster and more transparent interconnection arrangements 
for large loads, while offering customers speed-to-power, optimized transmission solutions, and 
greater certainty to support siting, financing, and state coordination.  This solution aligns with the 
spirit of the Commission’s Show Cause Order for interconnection process arrangements to address 
the speed of adding generation to serve large load.  The outcome of the processes is MTEP 
approval of transmission required to reliably facilitate the withdrawal of the load and injection 
rights for the generation. 
MISO initiated the stakeholder process with a presentation of the Large Load Parallel Study 
Process at the July 16, 2026, Large Load Working Group and has requested stakeholder feedback 
on the proposed process.  MISO will continue stakeholder discussions on the Large Load Parallel

unique challenges associated with the new spot load growth occurring across the footprint.  These

effective manner that can meet the new emerging demand.
