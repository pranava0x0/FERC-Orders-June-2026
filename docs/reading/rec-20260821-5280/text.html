COMMENTS OF BUCKEYE POWER, INC. 
 
Buckeye Power, Inc. (“Buckeye”) submits these comments pursuant to the 
Commission’s Notice Requesting Post-Conference Comment of July 30, 2026.1  
Buckeye is an Ohio non-profit generation and transmission cooperative that 
produces, procures, and provides at wholesale all the electric capacity, energy, and 
transmission required by its member electric distribution cooperatives. Buckeye operates 
as a cooperative and is owned by its 25 member distribution cooperatives, which are in 
turn each owned by their member-consumers.2  Those member cooperatives serve more 
than 400,000 residential, commercial, and industrial customers in service territories 
encompassing 77 of Ohio’s 88 counties. Buckeye operates not for the benefit of 
shareholders, but for the benefit of its member-consumers.

Buckeye’s mission is to provide “to all member systems, stably and competitively 
priced, economical and highly reliable wholesale power, for the benefit of their members 
and their communities.”3 Buckeye provides this supply through wholesale power 
agreements under which it obtains generation resources and transmission service to meet 
the demand of its members, and its members pay a cost-based rate for these resources. 
Buckeye or its affiliates own or have the right to the output of approximately 2,445 
megawatts of generating capacity located in or delivered into PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. (“PJM”). Buckeye is both a taker of transmission service under the PJM Open 
Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and a participant in the PJM market. Buckeye 
participates in the PJM market both as a market seller (as a generation owner) and as a 
buyer (as a Load-Serving Entity). 
Buckeye has appended to these comments its responses to the questions posed in 
the appendix to the Notice.

7) 
In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the 
public? 
Y  
 
  
Comments: Buckeye supports allowing certain Board meetings to be open to the public, recognizing the value of 
transparency and stakeholder engagement. However, Buckeye does not support requiring all Board meetings to 
be open to the public, as non-public meetings can provide an important forum for candid, open discussion, 
deliberation, and the exchange of ideas that supports effective decision-making.
