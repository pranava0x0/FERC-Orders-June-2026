Pursuant to the Commission’s July 30, 2026 notice in the above-captioned proceeding,1 Constellation Energy Generation, LLC (Constellation) submits these comments addressing issues raised at the Commissioner-led technical conference held on July 23, 2026, and responding to the Commission’s issues list found in the appendix of the Notice.

I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY
Constellation commends the Commission for proactively addressing issues with the PJM stakeholder process.  While we caution against overhauling a governance process that, despite its challenges, has led to the successful implementation of many market enhancements over the years, resulting in a regional market that saves consumers approximately $5 billion annually,2 there are nonetheless a number of reforms that can be made to improve the efficiency of the existing stakeholder process.  As explained in responses to the Commission’s questions, Constellation does not support altering section 205 filing right authority or reforms that may result in undue political influence on market rules.  Most of the unrest in PJM over the last decade has involved issues related to resource adequacy, generator retirements and replacements, and capacity market rules changes—matters over which PJM already has section 205 filing rights and is able to take action

II. QUESTION RESPONSES
A. PJM Board Independence
1. Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?

Yes.  Board terms should be lengthened to five years with two full terms possible, and substantive criteria should be considered for removing Board members.  Terms exceeding five years may be too long of a commitment period for Board member candidates and could inadvertently limit Board member candidates.  Further discussion on this matter should be pursued.

2. Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 years)?

Yes.  See our response to Question 1.  Constellation generally agrees that a maximum number of years is reasonable and welcomes discussion on determining the most appropriate number of years any Board member may serve.  For example, a limit of two, five-year terms—for a ten-year cap—could make sense.3

3. Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region?

Yes.  The key focus on Board qualifications should remain (as it has in the past) on relevant experience and expertise.  If strictly applied, a residency requirement risks diminishing the number of otherwise qualified Board candidates.  Nonetheless, the deeper appreciation of a region that could be gained through living there (and being directly impacted by decisions made by the Board) could be beneficial.  Constellation recommends a requirement that one PJM Board member reside

7. In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the public?

The Members’ Retention of Section 205 Filing Rights Was Heavily Negotiated and Cannot Be Set Aside Because PJM Wants Control

Nor can the Members Committee section 205 filing rights be abrogated simply because PJM wants control.  The allocation of those rights, as set forth in Sections 8.8 and 10.4 of the Operating Agreement, is entitled to Mobile-Sierra protection,19 meaning that by operation of law the filing rights can only be abrogated upon a finding of “unequivocal public necessity” or “extraordinary circumstances.”20

Section 8.8 grants the Members Committee the authority to “amend any portion of this Agreement, including the Schedules hereto, or create new Schedules, and file any such amendments or new Schedules with FERC.”21  This includes Schedule 1, which governs the energy and ancillary services markets.  Section 10.4 sets forth PJM’s duties and responsibilities, including “[filing] on behalf of the Members any amendments to this Agreement or the Schedules hereto, any new Schedules hereto, and mak[ing] any other regulatory filings on behalf of the Members or the LLC necessary to implement this Agreement.”22  Thus, while the Members entered into an agreement that requires PJM to run the energy markets and to submit filings under section 205, PJM can only submit those filings on behalf of the Members, not on its own behalf. The allocation of section 205 filing rights falls squarely within “contract rates” afforded Mobile-Sierra protection.23  As Atlantic City Electric Company states, public utilities may only cede their section 205 filing rights “voluntarily,” that is “by contract” under the Mobile-Sierra

Investor Concerns Are Likely to Rise if PJM Authority Is Increased

The Commission should refrain from fundamentally overhauling filing rights in PJM,
especially at this critical time when significant investment is needed to meet tightening system conditions; simply put, a governance overhaul that includes additional PJM filing rights could undermine investor confidence in PJM at a time when their investment is most needed.  Investor confidence and willingness to invest in assets in PJM is shaped by (among other things) PJM market outcomes and expectations in the rules that shape those market outcomes into the future. Actions by PJM that are counter to competitive market results undermine investor confidence in the markets and the desire by competitors (and their owners) to invest.  The bottom line is that PJM has failed to appropriately consider investor impact in exercising the section 205 authority over resource adequacy matters it already has.  This is inconsistent with the Commission’s and Administration’s desire to instill investor confidence and incent new infrastructure in PJM.37  And, against this backdrop, it is particularly difficult to justify increasing PJM’s filing authority.

B. Stakeholder Process
16. Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-thirds sector-weighted voting?

Yes.  If the PJM Members Committee agrees to such a change, reducing the voting threshold to a majority vote could support the Commission’s goal of facilitating section 205 filings without upending PJM’s existing governance structure as some have suggested.

17. Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior standing committees, also apply to lower-level committees?

No.  Affiliate voting is an important component for balancing interests within the PJM voting structure.  Discussion at the lower level committees—where affiliate voting is relied on—are usually more technical and detailed in nature and having the diversity of interests makes for a more robust vetting of issues.  At the senior level committees (Markets and Reliability and Members Committees) each Member has only one vote, consolidating the interests to the primary voting member on matters that have already been largely shaped at the lower committee.

a. Board Seat Duration
