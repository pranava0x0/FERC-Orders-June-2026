I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Pursuant to Rules 206, 212, and 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s 
(FERC or Commission) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Southern California Edison Company; 
Pacific Gas and Electric Company; San Diego Gas & Electric Company; Citizens Sunrise 
Transmission LLC; Citizens Sycamore-Penasquitos Transmission LLC; Citizens S-Line 
Transmission LLC; SunZia Transmission, LLC; LS Power Grid California, LLC; DesertLink, 
LLC; GridLiance West LLC; Horizon West Transmission, LLC; Trans Bay Cable LLC; Viridon 
Path 15 LLC; Valley Electric Association, Inc.; and Startrans IO, LLC (together, the Joint PTOs) 
respectfully submit this Request for Abeyance in response to the Order Instituting Proceeding 
Under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act (the OSC) issued in the above-captioned proceeding 
on June 18, 2026.  The Joint PTOs seek a 90-day full abeyance of the current 60-day deadline to 
respond to all issues raised in the OSC so that the Joint PTOs may each have the additional time 
necessary to prepare Federal Power Act (FPA) section 205 filings revising their respective 
Transmission Owner (TO) Tariffs. 
The Joint PTOs appreciate the Commission’s attention and guidance on issues relating to 
the interconnection of large loads, including data centers, to the FERC-jurisdictional 
transmission system.  The Joint PTOs believe the potential tariff changes the Commission 
discusses in the OSC will provide opportunities for the California Independent System Operator

Corporation (CAISO) and the PTOs to bring large loads online efficiently and further protect 
ratepayers across the CAISO region from transmission-related costs associated with large loads.  
The Joint PTOs also agree that revising the interconnection process to facilitate the connection of 
large loads to the California grid should be a priority as the stakeholders work through this 
process.  The Joint PTOs seek an abeyance so that they can have further time to develop a robust,

differences among the California PTOs (some PTOs serve load while others do not, for example) 
such that the appropriate TO Tariff changes to implement the OSC will also likely vary from 
PTO to PTO such that each PTO must develop its proposed tariff changes to reflect its unique 
circumstances and to coordinate those unique circumstances with CAISO and the other PTOs. 
II. 
ARGUMENT 
In the OSC, the Commission states that it will consider requests for abeyances of up to 90 
days, which should include both (1) a robust description of the content of a potential future filing 
under FPA section 205 and (2) a reasoned and specific explanation of when such a filing is 
expected to be made.2 
The Joint PTOs each plan to develop an FPA section 205 filing to amend their respective 
TO Tariffs, as described below.  The Joint PTOs respectfully request the Commission hold the 
deadline to respond to the OSC in abeyance for a 90-day period.  Good cause exists to grant this 
motion because doing so will allow the Joint PTOs to address issues identified by the 
Commission in the OSC through FPA section 205 filings.  The issues the Commission has raised 
in the OSC are complex and substantial, requiring each of the PTOs to draft entirely new 
sections of their respective TO Tariffs, potentially including new pro forma contract(s), and 
incorporate study and application processes tailored to large loads interconnecting to the 
transmission system.  For those Joint PTOs with retail-load responsibilities, state-jurisdictional

In order to develop a comprehensive set of TO Tariff revisions addressing the myriad concerns

CAISO’s design, which the Joint PTOs cannot reasonably predict this early in the stakeholder 
process.  The requested 90-day abeyance will instead permit a coordinated set of filings.  
Accordingly, the Joint PTOs seek an abeyance until November 16, 2026, for each of the Joint 
PTOs to submit its response to the OSC, including its anticipated FPA section 205 filing.  
A. 
Proposed TO Tariff Revisions 
In the OSC, the Commission has identified five areas of potential deficiency in either the 
CAISO and/or TO Tariffs.  First, the Commission has stated that the CAISO and/or TO Tariffs 
may lack provisions that address, with sufficient clarity and consistency, how CAISO and/or the 
Participating TOs will timely study the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers 
on behalf of large loads.3  The OSC states that the CAISO and/or the TOs should explain 
whether their respective tariffs remain just and reasonable without provisions outlining an 
application process, study procedures, and ongoing operational requirements that address the 
unique characteristics and challenges of providing transmission service to Eligible Customers on 
behalf of large loads, or propose appropriate tariff revisions.  Second, the OSC indicates that the

CAISO and/or TO Tariffs may be unjust and unreasonable because they appear to lack (1) 
transparency regarding the assignment of network upgrades, and their associated costs, that are 
needed to provide transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads; and (2) a 
pro forma cost recovery agreement among CAISO, the relevant transmission owner, and the 
Eligible Customer taking transmission service on behalf of large loads that helps ensure that 
Eligible Customers bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide 
transmission service.4  Third, the Commission has identified the CAISO Tariff as potentially 
deficient without clear and consistent provisions addressing the rates, terms, and conditions 
applicable to co-location arrangements and for transmission services that reflect an Eligible 
Customer taking transmission service on behalf of Eligible Load that is willing and able to limit 
its energy withdrawals from the transmission system under certain conditions.5  Fourth, the OSC 
states that the CAISO Tariff appears unjust and unreasonable because it does not include 
transmission services that reflect Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of 
flexible large loads that are willing and able to limit their use of the transmission system under 
certain conditions.6  Fifth, the Commission preliminarily finds that the CAISO Tariff appears to 
be unjust and unreasonable because it does not contain provisions allowing an interconnection 
customer to seek the generator interconnection service(s) reflecting the operational dynamics of 
serving electrically proximate or co-located load with a high peak load.7 
The OSC does not suggest that the TO Tariffs are unjust and unreasonable with respect to 
all Commission concerns.  For instance, the third, fourth, and fifth issues – co-located load, 
flexible services, and proximate load – are primarily directed at the CAISO Tariff, where these

B. 
Briefing Questions 
The Commission also identifies several issues for further briefing.12  The Joint PTOs also 
request to toll the deadline to respond to the briefing questions for 90 days, consistent with the 
OSC’s explanation of the effect of an abeyance.13  The Joint PTOs believe an abeyance is 
appropriate as to the briefing questions as they are wholly interrelated with the issues to be 
addressed in the Joint PTOs’ Section 205 filings, as described above.  
III. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons described above, the Joint PTOs respectfully request that the Commission 
grant the Request for Abeyance and extend each of the Joint PTOs’ deadlines to respond in full 
to the OSC to November 16, 2026.
