I. PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
1. Movants do not ask the Commission to reject the RBP Filing. Movants do not allege 
that PJM has acted in bad faith. Movants do not contend that the Reliability Backstop 
Procurement ("RBP") is unnecessary, or that PJM's resource adequacy concerns are unfounded. 
2. Movants' objection is narrow. PJM asks the Commission to authorize a novel, one-time 
procurement of capacity commitments of up to fifteen years, extending through the 2042/2043 
Delivery Year, in a quantity defined by the output of PJM's ELCC/Reserve Requirement Study 
model. Yet the RBP Filing does not disclose to the Commission the independent evaluation of 
that model which PJM itself commissioned and received in December 2025, nor PJM's own

PDF page 2

published sensitivity analyses quantifying how far the model's output moves when identified 
modeling questions are resolved one way or another. 
3. Those analyses are material because they move the answer in both directions, and by 
amounts that are not small relative to the procurement PJM proposes. Movants candidly disclose 
below that the single most-discussed correction — crediting increased thermal generating 
capability in cold weather — is, on PJM's own analysis, outweighed by a separate correction that 
increases the reserve requirement. That is precisely Movants' point. The relevant fact is not that 
PJM's number is too high. The relevant fact is that PJM's own work product shows the number to 
be materially unstable across unresolved modeling choices, and PJM proposes to commit 
customers to fifteen-year obligations sized by it without placing that instability before the 
Commission. 
4. This concern is not hypothetical. In the 2024/2025 Base Residual Auction, PJM 
produced an erroneous Locational Deliverability Area Reliability Requirement for the DPL 
South Zone — a sub-area of Movants' own distribution zone. The consequence, after the auction 
was re-run, was an additional $182.8 million in cost for 1.9 percent more capacity. The resulting 
litigation — brought by, among others, the Delaware Public Service Commission and the 
Delaware Division of the Public Advocate — was remanded to this Commission by the United 
States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit on January 13, 2026, and remains 
unresolved. 
5. The relief Movants request is modest and lies comfortably within the Commission's 
authority. PJM has itself consented, "to facilitate Commission approval," to the Commission's 
exercise of authority to modify the proposed Tariff and RAA language under NRG Power

PDF page 3

Marketing, LLC v. FERC, where such modification is consistent with the filing's overarching 
objectives. RBP Filing, transmittal § V, at 130–31. Movants ask the Commission to condition 
acceptance on PJM demonstrating what the procurement target should be once the modeling 
questions identified by PJM's own consultant are applied — the substitution of a substantiated

II. MOTION TO INTERVENE

("DPL"), taking service in the State of Delaware within the DPL transmission zone of the PJM 
Region under DPL Service Classification MGS-S (Medium General Service — Secondary). The

these facts precisely because the accuracy of a party's asserted interest is the foundation of its 
right to be heard.

B. Movants' Interest 
9. Rule 214(b)(2) requires that a movant show that it "has or represents an interest which 
may be directly affected by the outcome of the proceeding," or that "the movant's participation is 
in the public interest." 18 C.F.R. § 385.214(b)(2)(ii), (iii). The Customer satisfies the former

the alternative, his participation is in the public interest under Rule 214(b)(2)(iii), as the person

proceedings at all. 
10. The Customer pays a stated, demand-denominated supply charge for capacity. DPL's 
MGS-S schedule states the Standard Offer Service supply charge as "Supply Capacity, Energy 
and Ancillary," billed at stated Demand Rates of $15.397739/kW and $9.413973/kW, together 
with stated energy rates. DPL's Rider "SOS" § B provides that the Standard Offer Service Rate 
"will include the costs for energy, capacity and ancillary services as reflected in the awarded 
electric supply prices for specific services in each year." 
11. The mechanism by which PJM capacity prices enter that charge is documented by the 
Delaware Public Service Commission's own technical consultant. In its report on DPL's 2026 
SOS procurement, Liberty Consulting Group explained that "[b]idders use $/MW-day capacity 
auction prices to create a capacity component (in $/MWh) of their SOS bid prices," and that 
"[l]ower load factor customer classes ... feel a greater impact from capacity prices than high load 
factor ... customers since they have fewer MWh over which to spread their demand-based 
capacity charges."

III. BACKGROUND 
A. The RBP Filing 
17. On July 31, 2026, PJM submitted a filing under section 205 of the Federal Power Act 
captioned Reliability Backstop Procurement to Address Resource Adequacy Concerns Stemming 
from Large Loads, requesting an effective date of September 29, 2026. 
18. PJM proposes a one-time procurement of new capacity, with commitments "for a 
fixed term of up to 15 years, starting with the 2028/2029 Delivery Year and ending with the 
2042/2043 Delivery Year." 
19. PJM proposes to procure a quantity equal to the "Final RBP Target," which "will 
initially be based on the observed MW UCAP shortfall in the BRA for PJM's 2028/2029 
Delivery Year, i.e., the Initial RBP Target." The Final RBP Target is derived from the Initial 
RBP Target by subtracting verified Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW and specified opt-outs. 
RBP Filing, transmittal at 31–33; proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, §§ 18.1, 18.2.

PDF page 7

20. The Initial RBP Target is 6,831.3 MW. That figure is the amount by which the 
2028/2029 Base Residual Auction cleared short of the PJM Region Reliability Requirement — 
defined in PJM's Tariff as the Forecast Pool Requirement multiplied by the Preliminary PJM 
Region Peak Load Forecast, less the Unforced Capacity Obligations of Fixed Resource 
Requirement Entities. The auction cleared at the market price cap of $325/MW-day. PJM states 
that the 2027/2028 Base Residual Auction likewise cleared 6,623 MW short, at a cap of 
$333.44/MW-day. 
21. The procurement quantity is therefore not merely informed by PJM's reliability 
model. It is defined by it. The Forecast Pool Requirement and the Reliability Requirement are 
outputs of PJM's ELCC/Reserve Requirement Study model. On these facts — where both 
auctions cleared short at the administrative price cap — an error of X MW in the Reliability 
Requirement produces an error of approximately X MW in the Initial RBP Target, and a 
corresponding error in the quantity of fifteen-year commitments PJM would enter. 
B. PJM's independent evaluation of that model 
22. In December 2025, Energy and Environmental Economics, Inc. ("E3") delivered to 
PJM a report titled PJM ELCC / RRS Model Evaluation. E3 states that it "was retained by PJM to 
independently assess PJM's Effective Load Carrying Capability / Reserve Requirement Study 
Model ... for alignment with industry best practices in loss-of-load-probability ... modeling," and 
notes that "[t]he outputs of the ELCC/RRS model are used directly in PJM's capacity market." 
The report is provided as Attachment A hereto. 
23. E3 identified fourteen "Considerations for Improvement," presented "[i]n order of 
approximate prioritization." Three are directly pertinent.

PDF page 8

24. Consideration 4 — "Use more temporal granularity to model capability and 
deliverability of unlimited resources." E3's evaluation states that this "[r]eflects resource 
capability changing with temperature conditions, increasing model accuracy," and assigns it an 
Impact Level of: "High impact. Approximately ~8 GW of additional unlimited resource 
capability in the winter months, ~1% IRM reduction." E3 records the implementation difficulty 
as "Low difficulty to implement a seasonal approach (which PJM has already done)." 
25. Consideration 6 — "De-weight historical resource performance events as new 
resource performance events under similar weather conditions occur." E3 explains that "[t]he 
current PJM model methodology draws resource performance profiles from historical 
performance during the years 2012-2024," that "[a]ny resource performance day within that time 
period is selectable by the model with equal probability," and that consequently a resource "that 
performs poorly but implements weatherization, better fuel security, or other enhancements 
would still be modeled with its pre-enhancement performance." 
26. Consideration 3 — "Update the scrambling process to draw resource performance 
from the same weather conditions used to synthesize load." E3 assigns this a "[m]odest to high 
impact on IRM and ELCC values as indicated by PJM sensitivity analysis," and notes "[l]ow 
implementation effort given that PJM has already built this functionality to test impacts as part of 
Package C." 
27. Movants disclose, as they must, E3's overall qualification: "E3 does not view 
implementation of any of these considerations as strictly necessary for the continued use of the 
current model in the administration of the capacity market. Instead, they are intended to support 
continued improvement of PJM's reliability modeling framework over time."

PDF page 9

28. That qualification is important but it does not answer the question presented here. E3 
was engaged to assess whether PJM's model remains fit for continued use in administering the 
capacity market — an annual, self-correcting process in which each Delivery Year's requirement 
is recalculated and each auction reprices. E3 was not asked, and did not opine, whether the 
model's output is a sufficient basis for a novel, one-time procurement of commitments extending 
fifteen years into the future, which by design cannot be recalculated annually. The distinction is 
the entire subject of this protest.

D. What the RBP Filing says about any of this 
33. The RBP Filing does not engage any of it. Movants have searched the complete 
filing, including its attachments and testimony. The filing refers to "ELCC" approximately 200 
times, and refers to the "Installed Reserve Margin" in definitional tariff provisions and as an 
observed benchmark — the transmittal reports that both recent auctions "cleared 5.6% below the 
PJM Region Installed Reserve Margin of 20%." RBP Filing, transmittal at 124. It contains no 
reference to E3 or to Energy and Environmental Economics; no reference to "WICAP"; no 
reference to "winter capability"; and no reference to the "Reserve Requirement Study." Its seven 
references to "sensitivity" or "sensitivities" concern Optional Interconnection Studies and the 
calculation of RBP settlements, not reliability modeling. Nowhere does the filing address how 
the Reliability Requirement was derived, the independent evaluation PJM commissioned of the

model that derives it, or PJM's own analyses of how far the derivation moves under identified 
corrections. 
IV. THE COMMISSION SHOULD NOT FIND THE PROCUREMENT 
QUANTITY JUST AND REASONABLE ON THIS RECORD 
34. Under section 205, PJM bears the burden of showing that its proposal is just and 
reasonable. 16 U.S.C. § 824d(e). Where the central quantitative term of a proposal — here, how 
much capacity to buy, and for how long — is the output of a model, PJM's burden includes some 
demonstration that the output is a reasonable basis for the commitment proposed. 
35. PJM has not made that demonstration. It has described the shortfall as an observed 
auction outcome, which it is, without addressing the fact that the outcome is measured against a 
modeled requirement whose known sensitivities PJM has studied and not disclosed here. 
36. The fifteen-year term is what makes this dispositive. In the ordinary Base Residual 
Auction, an error in the reliability requirement is bounded: the Delivery Year ends, the model is 
re-run, and the market reprices. PJM's own conduct illustrates the point — PJM revised its peak 
load forecast for 2030 downward by 875 MW in its 2026 Long-Term Load Forecast "due to 
forecasting improvements." PJM corrects its inputs when its methods improve, and customers 
receive the benefit at the next auction. 
37. The RBP has no such self-correction. Commitments run to the 2042/2043 Delivery 
Year. If the Initial RBP Target is materially overstated because of a modeling question that 
PJM's consultant identified in December 2025 and PJM resolves in 2027 or 2028, customers in 
the zones allocated RBP Charges will nonetheless pay for the resulting commitments for as long 
as fifteen years — and every customer in the PJM Region, Movants included, will continue to

PDF page 12

bear the effects of the modeled requirement through each annual auction and the supply prices 
built upon it. The asymmetry between a correctable annual process and an uncorrectable long-
term commitment is the reason the modeling record should be before the Commission now. 
38. That asymmetry is not theoretical. In the 2024/2025 Base Residual Auction, PJM's 
LDA Reliability Requirement for the DPL South Zone — a Locational Deliverability Area 
within Movants' distribution zone — rested on the assumption that certain suppliers would bid; 
they did not, and the requirement was not corrected. On re-run, the auction cleared at the price 
cap and PJM procured 1.9 percent more capacity at an additional cost of $182.8 million. See Md. 
Office of People's Counsel v. FERC, No. 24-1353 (D.C. Cir. Jan. 13, 2026), granting pet. for 
review of PJM Load Parties v. PJM Interconnection, LLC, 189 FERC ¶ 61,199 (2024). The 
Delaware Public Service Commission and the Delaware Division of the Public Advocate were 
among the complainants. The matter is on remand and unresolved. 
39. Movants do not contend that the DPL South episode governs this proceeding, and 
recognize that the D.C. Circuit expressly declined to hold that any refund is due. Movants cite it 
for a narrower proposition: PJM's reliability requirement calculations have previously proved 
materially erroneous, at substantial cost, in a sub-zone of Movants' own distribution zone, and 
the Commission has recent and direct experience of how difficult such an error is to remedy after 
the fact. The prudent course is verification before commitment, not litigation afterward. 
40. The Independent Market Monitor's findings reinforce the need for verification. In its 
analysis of the 2027/2028 Base Residual Auction — the first of the two shortfall auctions PJM 
invokes — the Monitor concluded that the auction's results "were not competitive as a result of 
the inclusion of large new data center loads in the load forecast, the lack of a queue for the

PDF page 13

addition of large new data center loads, the performance assessment interval (PAI) penalties that 
are part of the CP design, PJM's ELCC approach, the definition of market seller offer caps, the 
failure to extend the RPM must offer requirement to demand resources, and the product 
definition and lack of market power mitigation for demand resources." Monitoring Analytics, 
Analysis of the 2027/2028 RPM Base Residual Auction, Part B, at 2 (July 9, 2026). The Monitor 
further concluded that "[t]he BRA prices do not reflect supply and demand fundamentals but 
reflect, in significant part, PJM decisions about the definition of supply and demand," and that 
"capacity market prices can be significantly affected by forecast data center load that bears little 
known resemblance to reality." Id. at 2, 4. 
41. Movants take no position on the Monitor's proposed remedies, and note that the 
Monitor's report addresses the 2027/2028 auction rather than the 2028/2029 auction from which 
the Initial RBP Target is drawn — though the Monitor warns the identified problems "will 
continue to result in even higher prices unless the related issues are addressed." Id. at 8. Movants 
cite the report for one proposition: the "observed" shortfall that defines the Initial RBP Target is 
not a pure market datum. By the assessment of PJM's own independent Market Monitor, it 
reflects, in significant part, administrative determinations — including the ELCC approach and a 
load forecast incorporating large-load projections of uncertain reliability. A figure of that 
character should be substantiated before it fixes fifteen years of procurement obligations. 
V. PJM SIMULTANEOUSLY PROPOSES TO DEFER THE TARIFF'S OWN 
MANDATED INVESTIGATION OF THE SHORTFALL'S CAUSES 
42. PJM's existing Tariff already requires the inquiry Movants seek. As PJM's own 
transmittal acknowledges, the Tariff contains an "existing requirement for PJM to investigate the 
cause for clearing short of the reliability requirement and to recommend corrective action." RBP

PDF page 14

Filing, transmittal at 124. And PJM's own footnote quotes the 2027/2028 BRA Report: the 
shortfall "constitutes a triggering event as defined in the PJM Tariff, mandating an investigation 
to determine the root causes of the region's failure to secure sufficient resource adequacy." Id. at 
124 n.308. 
43. Yet the RBP Filing proposes to reset that investigation and corrective-action 
obligation to the 2032/2033 Delivery Year, on the stated rationale that doing so "avoids posting 
substantively repetitive reports should the BRAs continue to clear short in the intervening 
Delivery Years." Id. at 124. The filing does not state whether the root-cause investigation already 
mandated by the 2027/2028 shortfall has been completed, and no such analysis appears in the 
filing. 
44. The juxtaposition is the problem. PJM proposes to (a) procure up to fifteen years of 
capacity commitments sized by the shortfall, while (b) deferring until 2032/2033 the Tariff's own 
mandated investigation into the causes of that shortfall. Whatever the administrative convenience 
of avoiding repetitive reports, the sequence is backwards: the root-cause analysis the Tariff 
already requires should be completed and placed on the record before long-duration 
commitments are fixed by the number it examines — not five Delivery Years after. 
VI. THE RELIEF REQUESTED IS AVAILABLE UNDER SECTION 205 
45. Movants recognize that in a section 205 proceeding the Commission's role is to 
determine whether the rate the utility has filed is just and reasonable, and that the Commission 
may not impose modifications resulting in "an entirely different rate design" from the utility's 
proposal. NRG Power Mktg., LLC v. FERC, 862 F.3d 108, 114–16 (D.C. Cir. 2017).

PDF page 15

46. No such limit is implicated here, for two independent reasons. First, PJM has 
consented. The RBP Filing states that "to facilitate Commission approval and prompt opening of 
the RBP procurement window, PJM consents to the Commission's exercise of authority to 
modify and/or sever the proposed Tariff and RAA language, as needed and permitted under the 
FPA and NRG Power Marketing, where such modification is consistent with PJM's overarching 
objectives in this filing," and asks only that the Commission "expressly prescribe the revised rate 
or rule in its order." RBP Filing, transmittal § V, at 130–31. Conditioning acceptance on 
substantiation of the procurement target is consistent with PJM's stated overarching objective — 
procuring capacity to meet the Region's actual resource adequacy need — and alters no element 
of the RBP's design. 
47. Second, the condition sought substitutes one number for another — the precise 
operation the D.C. Circuit has approved. In Aera Energy LLC v. FERC, 789 F.3d 184, 190–92 
(D.C. Cir. 2015) — quoted by PJM's own filing at note 318 — the court affirmed a conditional 
acceptance that "simply required [the utility] to substitute one number for another." A Final RBP 
Target substantiated against the modeling questions identified by PJM's own consultant leaves 
every design element of the RBP untouched: the same procurement mechanism, the same 
eligibility rules, the same commitment term, the same allocation methodology. Only the quantity 
changes, and only if the substantiation shows it should. 
48. In the alternative, and independent of consent: requiring an applicant to substantiate 
the central quantitative term of its own filing is not redesign at all — it is the application of the 
applicant's burden of proof under section 205(e). The Commission may also accept and suspend 
the filing for a nominal period and set the procurement quantity for further proceedings, or

PDF page 16

accept the mechanism while directing that the Final RBP Target be established in a compliance 
filing supported by the analyses identified herein. 
VII. COST ALLOCATION: THE PROPOSAL RESTS ON STATE ACTION 
THAT HAS NOT OCCURRED 
49. PJM proposes to allocate the Final RBP Target first to Electric Distributor zones by 
forecasted load growth, and then to Load Serving Entities in a manner that "permits states to 
determine the appropriate allocation of RBP UCAP MW to specific LSEs and/or end-use 
customers, thus allowing Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authorities ('RERRA') to 
appropriately assign costs to their respective retail customers, including data centers." 
50. Movants state candidly that PJM's zonal step does not, on PJM's illustrative example, 
allocate RBP UCAP MW to the DPL zone, and that PJM's 2026 Load Forecast Table B9b shows 
no load adjustment for the DPL zone in any year from 2026 through 2046. To that extent PJM's 
allocation directs cost toward the zones whose forecast load growth gives rise to the shortfall, 
and Movants do not criticize that design. 
51. Movants' concern is with what happens when the design's premise fails. PJM states 
that "[s]hould the relevant RERRA(s) decline to communicate RBO MW to the relevant Electric 
Distributors ..., PJM has proposed to apply a backup default cost allocation." The protection PJM 
offers to existing customers thus depends on affirmative, discretionary action by state regulators 
that has not yet occurred, over a term of up to fifteen years. 
52. PJM further relies on the Statement of Principles Regarding PJM issued January 16, 
2026, in which the governors of the PJM states "agreed to ... [u]se their Authorities to Allocate 
Costs to Data Centers and Protect Residential Customers." Movants do not question the sincerity

PDF page 17

of that commitment. It is, however, a statement of intent by persons who are not parties to this 
proceeding and who cannot be bound by any order the Commission issues here. A section 205 
filing must be shown just and reasonable on its own terms. 
53. Delaware illustrates the gap. The Delaware Public Service Commission opened its 
large load tariff proceeding, Docket No. 25-0826, in October 2025 — announcing the effort as 
one to ensure that "large load facilities, like data centers, do not shift costs to other ratepayers." 
Del. PSC, Delaware PSC Opens Docket for Large Load Tariff, Pauses Interconnections (Oct. 
14, 2025). That proceeding remains pending. Delaware has not yet adopted the rate structures on 
which PJM's allocation design depends. 
54. Movants therefore request that the Commission require PJM to specify the backup 
default cost allocation with particularity and to demonstrate that it is just and reasonable as 
applied to zones and customer classes that neither caused nor benefit from the shortfall, 
including in the event a RERRA does not act within the RBP timeline.
