COMMENTS OF ADVANCED ENERGY UNITED

Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedures,1 Advanced Energy United2 (“United”) submits these comments in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”).3 United generally supports PJM’s July 31, 2026, filing as the best available solution to address the pressing challenge of rapid load growth and slow resource entry in a manner that avoids putting even greater cost burden on existing customers not contributing to the resource adequacy shortfall. However, RBP is far from an ideal outcome, and must be implemented in a manner that avoids perverse and distortionary outcomes that could undermine the investment landscape in PJM. Advanced Energy United’s comments below point to areas that need clarification and/or commitment from PJM, both in the context of RBP itself and to improve the region’s resource adequacy outlook moving forward.

I. INTRODUCTION
PJM is navigating a genuinely difficult situation, and the RBP reflects real effort to design a workable response under significant time pressure. United and its members have been active participants throughout the stakeholder process leading to this filing, and the RBP framework and related Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) proposal as proposed reflect meaningful refinement from the concepts PJM initially floated through the Critical Issue Fast Path processes earlier this year and in 2025. For these reasons and because of the urgency of taking action, United does not oppose the RBP mechanism PJM has proposed.

United’s support, however, is not without reservation. The comments below identify specific areas where PJM’s proposal falls short of its own stated objectives, or where PJM’s own reasoning for excluding otherwise-qualified resources does not withstand scrutiny. United urges the Commission to direct PJM to act on these issues promptly, for two related reasons. First, several of the barriers discussed below—particularly with respect to Surplus Interconnection Service and demand-side resources—unnecessarily constrain the pool of fast-to-deploy capacity available to the RBP, and addressing them would materially improve PJM’s chances of actually filling the shortfall the RBP is designed to address. Second, and more broadly, these same barriers bear directly on the success of PJM’s separate Build Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) initiative and on the durability of investment confidence in PJM generally; resolving them now will pay dividends well beyond this single procurement. United emphasizes that RBP was conceived, and must remain, a one-time, bounded response to the specific shortfall identified in the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction, not a template PJM may invoke unilaterally

II. COMMENTS
A. RBP Includes Important Elements that will Contribute to its Success
PJM should be commended for several elements of the RBP design that will support full participation by qualified resources and promote fair outcomes for developers navigating the procurement’s compressed timeline.

First, United supports the shortfall-charge exception for resources unable to meet their expected commercial operation date solely due to Network Upgrades, where the resource demonstrates readiness through test energy and seeks interim deliverability.4 Network Upgrade costs and schedules represent one of the largest sources of risk facing new capacity development in PJM today. A meaningful share of the resources best positioned to participate in the RBP are currently at some stage of the interconnection process, and without this exception, those resources would bear shortfall-charge risk for delays entirely outside their control, a result that would needlessly shrink the pool of supply available to the RBP at the exact moment PJM most needs it to be as large as possible.

United cautions, however, that this exception addresses only one element of the risk that projects still in the queue face: it excuses the shortfall charge itself, but developers must still price uncertain Network Upgrade costs and schedules into their RBP bids, and continue to bear real cost and schedule risk for as long as their projects remain queued. This exception is a necessary and well-designed accommodation, but it is not a substitute for PJM continuing to prioritize a faster, more predictable interconnection process and to resolve Network Upgrade cost

i. Peak Shaving Adjustment Opt-Outs Should be Clarified
As noted above, United supports PJM’s proposal to allow Electric Distributor zones/areas with a qualifying peak shaving adjustment to opt out of a portion of their RBP obligation.10 This opt-out recognizes that load which has already taken concrete steps to reduce its own contribution to peak demand should not be treated the same as load that has done nothing to address the shortfall it is helping to cause, and it gives Electric Distributors a meaningful incentive to pursue peak shaving arrangements going forward.

That incentive, however, depends entirely on the opt-out actually relieving the load in question of backstop-related obligations, and PJM’s filing leaves that question unresolved. C. Unpredictable ELCC Values Carry Excessive Risk for Developers
ELCC values for solar, storage, and other accredited resources are set by PJM based on system-wide fleet mix and grid conditions that no individual developer can control, predict, or hedge over a fifteen-year commitment. PJM’s proposal only partially addresses the risk this uncertainty produces in the context of the RBP. Specifically, PJM allows developers to bid into

PDF page 8

8
the RBP based on their own expectations of a resource’s future Effective Load Carrying Capability (“ELCC”), including the ability to offer above the forecasted class rating, up to a defined cap, where a developer believes its resource’s accreditation value is likely to improve over the term.13 PJM also avoids applying shortfall charges to resources whose ELCC declines below their committed RBP UCAP once they achieve commercial operation.14 This treatment is a reasonable accommodation as far as it goes, but it does not go far enough: RBP resources remain subject to unhedgeable ELCC risk in the revenue settlement for supply.

Because that risk is effectively unhedgeable at the individual resource level, rational developers will either choose not to participate in RBP or price a substantial risk premium into their RBP offers to protect against it. This premium will exceed the actual expected cost of ELCC variability and raise the cost of the RBP regardless of whether the risk materializes. A better approach, consistent with proposals other stakeholders have put forward, would separate this risk into its two component parts rather than assigning both to the developer.15 The portion of ELCC decline that PJM can already forecast, based on known and projected fleet buildout, should be published as an official long-term forecast covering the full RBP term, so that developers can price that expected trajectory into their bids at face value, without needing to guess at it or pad for it. The portion that cannot be forecast, meaning the variance above or below that expected trajectory driven by factors outside any single developer’s control, should instead

D. Exclusion of Surplus Interconnection Service from RBP is a missed opportunity

PJM recognized SIS as a tool to address “the need for more Capacity on the PJM Transmission System from resources that can be brought online quickly” when it proposed reforms to its SIS process in 2024, approved by FERC in 2025, to “encourage and facilitate the use of Surplus Interconnection Service from existing and planned generating units on PJM’s system.”17 Despite significant progress, barriers to full utilization of SIS remain, and this filing misses an opportunity to capitalize on SIS as a pathway to accelerate capacity additions. PJM’s proposal draws a sharp line between the criteria for capacity that may offset a load’s RBP obligation and the criteria for capacity eligible to be sold into the RBP as an RBP Resource, and Surplus Interconnection Service capacity falls on the favorable side of that line for one purpose and the unfavorable side for the other: new load may use SIS-derived capacity to reduce its own RBP obligation,18 but a resource relying on SIS is expressly barred from selling into the RBP as an RBP Resource.19 PJM’s stated rationale for the exclusion turns principally on the difficulty of cleanly attributing UCAP between an existing host resource and a surplus addition, which PJM states “creat[es] uncertainty and the need for sensitivities or estimates” in both the incremental capacity determination and RBP settlement.20 PJM’s reasoning treats SIS- based capacity as real and valuable enough to net against a load’s RBP obligation, yet not reliable enough to sell into the RBP, even though the underlying physical capacity is identical

Unnecessary Barriers to Entry

III. PJM MUST BE HELD ACCOUNTABLE FOR ENSURING THAT RBP
REMAINS A ONE-TIME MECHANISM
A. RBP is Specifically Designed as a One-Time Mechanism
PJM’s own filing provides the clearest evidence that RBP is meant to be a bounded, non- recurring response to the shortfall identified in the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction, and PJM should be held to that characterization going forward. United understands that PJM has represented RBP as a one-time mechanism and takes PJM at its word on that point. United nonetheless requests assurance that steps will be taken to ensure this characterization holds in practice, so that RBP does not become a recurring tool PJM invokes each time a future auction reveals a shortfall.

Most notably, PJM defends its choice of a pay-as-bid clearing structure, rather than the uniform clearing price design PJM otherwise favors across its markets,45 specifically on the ground that the RBP “is not designed to be a durable or repeatable procurement,” such that “its pricing outcomes are less likely to serve as incentive market signals.”46 PJM further explains that because RBP resources will differ in contract duration, commercial operation date, and ELCC accreditation over the term, they do not constitute a single homogeneous product suited to uniform pricing, a rationale that only holds together if RBP is understood as a one-time, purpose-

IV. RETAIL COST ALLOCATION - SUPPORTING STATE/LARGE LOAD
COORDINATION

The fairness premise underlying PJM’s proposed cost allocation framework, that large loads should bear the cost of the capacity procured on their account, depends on state and Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authority (“RERRA”) action that PJM’s proposal cannot itself guarantee.52 PJM’s allocation stops at the LSE level, using a state or RERRA-determined RBP Obligation where one is reported, or a default Peak Load Contribution ratio share where it is not.53 Whether the cost that lands on an LSE is then actually passed through to the large load causing it, or spread across an LSE’s broader retail base, is a retail ratemaking question entirely outside PJM’s proposal and, as PJM itself acknowledges, outside the Commission’s jurisdiction.54
