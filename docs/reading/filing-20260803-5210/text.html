I. BACKGROUND
A. The Show Cause Order
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, instituting a proceeding under FPA section 206 based on the Commission’s preliminary finding that the NYISO’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“OATT” or “Tariff”)9 appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential.10  The Commission issued the Show Cause Order as part of a suite of six coordinated orders directed to each of the RTO/ISO regions subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction.

The Show Cause Order includes preliminary findings that the NYISO’s Tariff appears unjust and unreasonable in five principal respects:

1.  Transmission Service Study Process for Large Loads.  The Commission
preliminarily found that the NYISO’s Tariff lacks sufficiently clear and consistent provisions addressing: (a) a definition of large load as a distinct category of load; (b) a mandatory application process, readiness requirements, and study procedures for Eligible Customers seeking transmission service on behalf of large loads; (c) study timelines of no more than 60-90 calendar days; (d) ongoing operational requirements – including telemetry, data reporting, remote disconnect capability, ramp rate and ride-through requirements, and protection systems – for transmission customers serving large loads; (e) evaluation of alternative transmission technologies

A. Cost Recovery and Transmission Service Charges
1. Network Upgrade Cost Recovery
The Commission preliminarily found in the Show Cause Order that the NYISO Tariff appears unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among transmission customers.  First, the Commission noted the lack of Tariff provisions requiring NYISO to post in a single location on the NYISO website certain Network Upgrade and cost data and other related information.28  Second, the Commission pointed to the absence of a pro forma cost recovery agreement between NYISO, the relevant Transmission Owner, and the Eligible Customer taking transmission service on behalf of large loads that helps ensure that Eligible Customers bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide transmission service, including Network Upgrade costs.29  The Commission also contemplated the possibility of Transmission Owners entering into cost recovery agreements with large load customers themselves rather than the corresponding Eligible Customers.30

The NYTOs plan to coordinate with NYISO, NYPSC, the New York Department of Public Service (“DPS”),31 and other interested stakeholders to develop a process for the Transmission Owner to recover from the Eligible Customer or large load customer, as applicable, an allocable share of the cost of Transmission Owner Interconnection Facilities and Network Upgrades. In a single-state ISO like the NYISO, a state public utility commission is in a good position to address cost allocation issues associated with upgrades to accommodate large loads. 2. Pro Forma Interconnection Agreement
The Commission observed that the large load interconnection agreement is not included in the NYISO Tariff and is not subject to sufficiently clear or consistent pro forma provisions.54 As the Commission has recognized,55 the absence of pro forma provisions in the interconnection agreement between the Transmission Owner and the Eligible Customer undermines the Transmission Owner’s ability to enforce the operational and cost-recovery requirements that arise from the study process.  The Transmission Owner – not NYISO – is the counterparty to the load interconnection agreement, and it is the Transmission Owner and its customers that bear the direct financial exposure to cost-shifting risk if the terms of that agreement are not standardized and clearly specified.

As noted, the NYPSC commenced a proceeding to review large load interconnection and related service requirements and cost allocation.  Ideally, the NYPSC-developed requirements will be acceptable to the Commission for implementation without having to address any conflicts and jurisdictional or preemption issues. C. Operating Terms Applicable to Non-Firm Transmission Withdrawal Service

The NYISO is responsible for the reliability of the New York bulk power system.56  The NYTOs are responsible for the reliable operation of their systems, including local (non-bulk) transmission and distribution systems.57  The NYTOs also have certain obligations under New York State law concerning the reliable operation of their systems.  With potentially large increases in the amount of new large loads on the system, and new non-firm or contract demand services, new operating requirements will be needed, as the Commission recognizes.58  The NYTOs will coordinate with the NYISO to develop operating requirements for loads that have chosen Non- Firm Transmission Withdrawal Service where the service level is less than the maximum potential withdrawal of the load and for supply paired with load, or that is behind-the-meter generation and requests Energy Resource Interconnection Service (“ERIS”) and/or Capacity Resource Interconnection Service (“CRIS”) at a level below the maximum potential injection.  Associated large load customers and suppliers will be required to satisfy operating requirements allowing for dispatch, ramping, curtailment, interruption, and/or disconnection on an automated, NYISO- controlled or Connecting Transmission Owner/Affected Transmission Owner-controlled, and remote basis, depending on the circumstances.59
