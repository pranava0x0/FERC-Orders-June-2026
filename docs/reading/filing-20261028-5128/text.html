II. 
ANSWER 
A. 
Further clarity is needed to ensure that MISO’s proposed 
Interconnection Reliability Requirements are limited to 
Transmission System-connected Large Loads.  
Midwest TDUs appreciate MISO’s apparent confirmation that it intends for the 
proposed Interconnection Reliability Requirements to apply only “to Transmission 
Customers taking Transmission Service on behalf of Large Loads connected to the MISO 
Transmission System.”9 This straightforward statement of the applicability of these 
requirements is consistent with testimony that its Answer cites: “This filing proposes 
reliability requirements and a reliability-based framework applicable to Transmission 
Customers taking Transmission Service on behalf of Large Load interconnected to the 
MISO Transmission System.”10 The brief discussion of this critical threshold point in 
MISO’s Answer, however, fails to resolve the potential for ambiguity in the proposed 
Tariff language.  
As noted in our Comments at 6-7, MISO defines a Large Load as one “located at 
a single substation behind one or more Point(s) of Withdrawal from the Transmission 
System,”11 with “Point(s) of Withdrawal” defined as “[t]he point(s) where a non-
transmission facility that is used to withdraw Energy from the Transmission System to 
serve Load connects to the Transmission System.”12 MISO’s definition, unlike the one in 
the Commission’s Show Cause Order, does not specify that a Large Load “interconnects

B. 
There remains uncertainty as to how Transmission Customers 
can rely on their agreements with Large Loads to effectuate 
compliance. 
MISO reiterates that “[t]he proposed Tariff expressly accommodates contractual 
arrangements between the Transmission Customer and the Large Load,”18 but its Answer 
fails to provide more details as to how those contractual arrangements will be 
accommodated in practice. Clarification of these implementation details are important to 
ensure that Transmission Customers are not unduly burdened by provisions intended to 
address reliability concerns caused by Large Loads. Midwest TDUs do not repeat all of 
clarification issues raised in our earlier Comments that should be addressed,19 but we 
highlight two specific shortcomings in MISO’s Answer.  
First, although MISO’s Answer acknowledges that “an Agreement assigning 
relevant performance and information duties to the Large Load may be part of a sufficient 
compliance showing,”20 it appears to reject the idea that agreements between a Large 
Load and the Transmission Customer can enable the Large Load to provide information 
directly to MISO. MISO states that it “must be able to obtain, through the Transmission 
Customer, all information and operational access necessary” and that agreements can 
“establish[] the Transmission Customer’s access to the information needed for

compliance.”21 MISO does not explain why it could not accept relevant information 
directly from the Large Load where a Large Load-Transmission Customer agreement 
expressly assigns compliance responsibility to the Large Load.  
To the extent a Large Load enters into an agreement with the Transmission 
Customer to expressly take on identified responsibilities for complying with 
Interconnection Reliability Requirements, MISO should not encumber that compliance 
by insisting that the Transmission Customer act as a middleman. While MISO says it 
“will continue working with Transmission Customers and Large Loads through 
implementation to facilitate efficient compliance and provide education regarding the 
new requirements,”22 the Commission should not permit MISO’s apparent insistence that 
information be needlessly funneled through the Transmission Customer. Where a Large 
Load agrees to take on responsibility for compliance with an Interconnection Reliability 
Requirement, requiring the Transmission Customer to pass information from the Large 
Load to MISO serves as a barrier to implementing agreements that facilitate efficient and 
effective compliance.  
Second, MISO’s Answer stops short of confirming that the list of potential 
consequences in proposed section 37A is the exclusive list of consequences for 
noncompliance with the proposed Interconnection Reliability Requirements.23 And while 
MISO states that this list is “not an automatic penalty schedule,”24 MISO does not state

whether, and in what circumstances, it might seek to impose penalties under section 37A 
despite none being explicitly identified in that provision. Nor does it clarify (either in its 
Answer or with revised proposed Tariff language) the scope of the potentially broad 
catchall for “other remediation action(s)” in the provision of section 37A. As explained in 
Midwest TDUs’ Comments (at 13), it would be inappropriate to impose punitive 
consequences such as financial penalties on the Transmission Customer when an 
Interconnection Reliably Requirement is not met by the Large Load assigned compliance 
responsibility under an agreement. In these circumstances, the Tariff language in section 
37A should be clear that consequences of non-compliance should be tailored to address 
and prevent the reliability risks posed by Large Loads. 
Requiring additional clarity on these issues does not require MISO to establish 
direct relationships with all Large Loads, nor does it alter that the Transmission Customer 
is the entity taking Transmission Service of behalf of the Large Load. Rather, Midwest 
TDUs’ Comments raised narrow questions about how MISO will effectuate agreements 
between a Large Load and the Transmission Customer where the Large Load has agreed 
to take on responsibilities for compliance with one or more of the Interconnection 
Reliability Requirements. Given that the Large Loads are the source of the reliability 
concerns driving the need for the Interconnection Reliability Requirements, it is 
appropriate for MISO to engage with those Large Loads that enter into agreements with a 
Transmission Customer to take on responsibility for compliance with those 
Requirements. Doing so will result in more efficient and effective implementation of 
these important reliability requirements, without requiring a direct contractual
