II. ANSWER
A. Further clarity is needed to ensure that MISO’s proposed
Interconnection Reliability Requirements are limited to
Transmission System-connected Large Loads.
Midwest TDUs appreciate MISO’s apparent confirmation that it intends for the proposed Interconnection Reliability Requirements to apply only “to Transmission Customers taking Transmission Service on behalf of Large Loads connected to the MISO Transmission System.”9 This straightforward statement of the applicability of these requirements is consistent with testimony that its Answer cites: “This filing proposes reliability requirements and a reliability-based framework applicable to Transmission Customers taking Transmission Service on behalf of Large Load interconnected to the MISO Transmission System.”10 The brief discussion of this critical threshold point in MISO’s Answer, however, fails to resolve the potential for ambiguity in the proposed Tariff language.

As noted in our Comments at 6-7, MISO defines a Large Load as one “located at a single substation behind one or more Point(s) of Withdrawal from the Transmission System,”11 with “Point(s) of Withdrawal” defined as “[t]he point(s) where a non- transmission facility that is used to withdraw Energy from the Transmission System to serve Load connects to the Transmission System.”12 MISO’s definition, unlike the one in the Commission’s Show Cause Order, does not specify that a Large Load “interconnects

B. There remains uncertainty as to how Transmission Customers
can rely on their agreements with Large Loads to effectuate
compliance.
MISO reiterates that “[t]he proposed Tariff expressly accommodates contractual arrangements between the Transmission Customer and the Large Load,”18 but its Answer fails to provide more details as to how those contractual arrangements will be accommodated in practice. Clarification of these implementation details are important to ensure that Transmission Customers are not unduly burdened by provisions intended to address reliability concerns caused by Large Loads. Midwest TDUs do not repeat all of clarification issues raised in our earlier Comments that should be addressed,19 but we highlight two specific shortcomings in MISO’s Answer.

First, although MISO’s Answer acknowledges that “an Agreement assigning relevant performance and information duties to the Large Load may be part of a sufficient compliance showing,”20 it appears to reject the idea that agreements between a Large Load and the Transmission Customer can enable the Large Load to provide information directly to MISO. MISO states that it “must be able to obtain, through the Transmission Customer, all information and operational access necessary” and that agreements can “establish[] the Transmission Customer’s access to the information needed for

compliance.”21 MISO does not explain why it could not accept relevant information directly from the Large Load where a Large Load-Transmission Customer agreement expressly assigns compliance responsibility to the Large Load.

To the extent a Large Load enters into an agreement with the Transmission Customer to expressly take on identified responsibilities for complying with Interconnection Reliability Requirements, MISO should not encumber that compliance by insisting that the Transmission Customer act as a middleman. While MISO says it “will continue working with Transmission Customers and Large Loads through implementation to facilitate efficient compliance and provide education regarding the new requirements,”22 the Commission should not permit MISO’s apparent insistence that information be needlessly funneled through the Transmission Customer. Where a Large Load agrees to take on responsibility for compliance with an Interconnection Reliability Requirement, requiring the Transmission Customer to pass information from the Large Load to MISO serves as a barrier to implementing agreements that facilitate efficient and effective compliance.

Second, MISO’s Answer stops short of confirming that the list of potential consequences in proposed section 37A is the exclusive list of consequences for noncompliance with the proposed Interconnection Reliability Requirements.23 And while MISO states that this list is “not an automatic penalty schedule,”24 MISO does not state

whether, and in what circumstances, it might seek to impose penalties under section 37A despite none being explicitly identified in that provision. Nor does it clarify (either in its Answer or with revised proposed Tariff language) the scope of the potentially broad catchall for “other remediation action(s)” in the provision of section 37A. As explained in Midwest TDUs’ Comments (at 13), it would be inappropriate to impose punitive consequences such as financial penalties on the Transmission Customer when an Interconnection Reliably Requirement is not met by the Large Load assigned compliance responsibility under an agreement. In these circumstances, the Tariff language in section 37A should be clear that consequences of non-compliance should be tailored to address and prevent the reliability risks posed by Large Loads.

Requiring additional clarity on these issues does not require MISO to establish direct relationships with all Large Loads, nor does it alter that the Transmission Customer is the entity taking Transmission Service of behalf of the Large Load. Rather, Midwest TDUs’ Comments raised narrow questions about how MISO will effectuate agreements between a Large Load and the Transmission Customer where the Large Load has agreed to take on responsibilities for compliance with one or more of the Interconnection Reliability Requirements. Given that the Large Loads are the source of the reliability concerns driving the need for the Interconnection Reliability Requirements, it is appropriate for MISO to engage with those Large Loads that enter into agreements with a Transmission Customer to take on responsibility for compliance with those Requirements. Doing so will result in more efficient and effective implementation of these important reliability requirements, without requiring a direct contractual
