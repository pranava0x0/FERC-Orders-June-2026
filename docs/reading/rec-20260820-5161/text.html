POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF  
INDIANA MUNICIPAL POWER AGENCY 
 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Indiana Municipal Power Agency (“IMPA”) respectfully submits these post-technical 
conference comments in response to the Commission’s examination of PJM Interconnection, 
LLC (“PJM”) governance and stakeholder processes in Docket No. AD26-7-000. IMPA is a 
not-for-profit utility providing 61 communities with a wholesale power supply that is low-
cost, reliable, and environmentally responsible. IMPA is registered as an Electric Distributor 
in PJM. IMPA submits the following direct answers to the Commission’s Notice Requesting 
Post-Conference Comment dated July 30, 2026. Responses that do not have a direct ‘Yes’ or 
‘No’ answer indicate IMPA’s indifference on the matter’s outcome or lack of information to 
provide a response that is worthwhile to the Commission. IMPA only addresses the posed 
governance and stakeholder process principles and does not address the merits of any 
specific contested proceeding pending before the Commission. 
 
II. 
PJM BOARD INDEPENDENCE 
 
 
Questions/Comments 
 
Yes No 
1) 
Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years? 
 
X 
 
 
Given the insight provided by David Mills and other participants at the technical 
conference regarding the limitations of current term duration, the terms of Board 
members should be extended. Some protection measures should be added, such as a 
mechanism that would allow a Board member that is failing in their duties or their 
ability to bring value to the Board to be removed. 
 
2) 
Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may 
serve (e.g., 10 years?) 
 
X 
 
 
Yes, as part of the same protections as described in Question 1.

3)  
Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a 
preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region? 
 
X 
 
 
Updating qualification rules to provide a preference for Board member candidates that 
reside in the PJM region would be beneficial and potentially provide a greater incentive 
for success. However, having some members who reside outside of PJM could provide 
diverse ideas or perspectives that would bring value to the Board. A well-qualified 
candidate from another ISO/RTO footprint that may bring great value to the Board 
should not have a disadvantage during the nomination process. Any updates to the rules 
should be crafted with that principle in mind.  
 
4)  
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off 
period for Board members from two years to one year? 
 
X* 
 
 
*If reducing the cooling off period improves the ability to add qualified Board members 
that can work towards completing PJM’s goal in an independent manner, then the period 
should be reduced. But if changing the cooling off period would be detrimental to the 
PJM Board, then the period should remain two years or be altered in a different way. 
 
5)  
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more 
communication between the Board and stakeholders? 
 
X 
 
 
The Board should have greater communication with stakeholders to improve the 
Board’s ability to understand the issues and take necessary action that serves the public 
interest in its’ entirety. However, communication should be open & public to eliminate 
any real or perceived risk of undue influence that might be argued to stem from less 
stringent Board isolation. This open & public communication would boost confidence in 
the Board’s integrity and independence in their actions. One possible method is 
described in question 7. 
 
6) 
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the 
board be required to explain its reasoning? 
 
X 
 
 
The Board’s individual member votes should not be made public to prevent any 
potential targeting of individual Board members or otherwise cause trepidation for the 
Board’s independence. But the overall voting results should be made public. Any 
commentary from the Board, should any Board member wish to include it, should be 
included with the overall voting results. 
 
7) 
In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or 
available to the public? 
 
X 
 
 
At minimum, PJM should consider offering the same level of public meetings that 
MISO offers to achieve increased transparency. MISO regularly holds open, public

meetings of its Board colloquially called “MISO Board Week” and PJM should consider 
mirroring that structure. 
 
8) 
Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the 
responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest? 
 
X 
 
 
The governing documents should explicitly state that the Board serves the public 
interest of PJM in its’ entirety, not a specific state, stakeholder, or any other individual 
entity. The public interest would be served by PJM having the ability to take necessary 
action to run a fair market and focus on reliability without needing to placate specific 
entity interests. This should be treated as PJM’s new goal, and the Board should take 
action to effectuate that goal. 
 
9) 
Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the 
Nominating Committee? 
 
 
X* 
 
*Any state representatives on the Nominating Committee should not have any political 
ties. Maintaining the independence of the Board is paramount, and political ties could 
create a conflict of interest with the Board serving the public interest. If a state 
representative will be required to be on the Nominating Committee, they should not 
have political ties as a protective measure against politics being a controlling influence 
on PJM. A potential option for a state representative that may not have political ties is a 
member of OPSI. OPSI has a longstanding history of involvement in PJM on complex 
issues that may better match PJM’s potential explicit purpose of serving the public 
interest in its’ entirety.  
 
10) 
Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management 
compensation to their achievement? 
 
X 
 
 
Yes, but the metrics need to be well crafted to avoid any perverse incentives while 
simultaneously providing appropriate impetus for success. Poorly crafted performance 
metrics that do not offer proper incentives for the PJM Board to succeed may lead to 
continued issues in PJM. 
 
 
III. 
SECTION 205 FILING RIGHTS 
 
11) 
Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only 
inform the Board? 
 
X 
 
 
PJM should review the stakeholder process in MISO for potential improvements. 
MISO’s stakeholder process is advisory, and MISO takes appropriate action when 
needed to address issues. While stakeholders’ perspective and expertise during the 
process are invaluable, the process in PJM has caused difficulties. During the technical

PDF page 4

4 
 
conference, the Commission and participants highlighted the PJM Board’s hesitance to 
take action that conflicted with the PJM members, while the voting and stakeholder 
process in its current format was described as slow. Commissioner LaCerte pointed to 
the recent CIFP that, after a long and arduous process, resulted in all 12 packages being 
voted down by members as an example of the slow process that ultimately failed to give 
the PJM Board direction. Changing the role of the stakeholder process to advisory 
would preserve the stakeholders’ input while allowing PJM to take necessary action 
without potential impediment by the stakeholder process. 
 
12) 
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and 
ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of a 
two-thirds of the Members Committee? 
 
X 
 
 
PJM should have the ability to take necessary action to run a fair market and focus on 
reliability without needing to placate stakeholder interests, which may conflict with 
PJM’s new explicit interest to serve the public interest as described in these responses. 
Section 205 rights would assist PJM in their mission. FERC, stakeholder intervention, 
and the IMM will continue to ensure PJM has appropriate checks and balances.  
 
13) 
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission 
Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain 
approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee? 
 
X 
 
 
Same answer as Question 12. 
  
14) 
Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights 
over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a 
supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal? 
 
 
X 
 
In the interest of consistency with providing PJM the greatest ability to take unimpeded 
action, OPSI should not have “jump ball” rights because it could impede PJM’s ability 
to take necessary action. However, it would be best practice for PJM to collaborate with 
OPSI on proposals during PJM’s design process to lessen the need for “jump ball” rights 
at the end. 
 
15) 
Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the 
portions of the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority 
of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal? 
 
 
X 
 
Same answer as Question 15, but the stakeholders will be involved in the normal 
stakeholder process and PJM can choose to update any tariff revisions to incorporate an 
alternative proposal if it is in the public interest.

V. 
STATE PARTICIPATION 
 
18) 
In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group 
consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ 
representatives? 
 
 
X 
 
OPSI should be the only state representative group. OMS represents state interests in 
MISO, and the RSC represents states’ interests in SPP. Those groups have a history of 
an active, day-to-day handle on issues that regularly provide input to their respective 
RTO. Any governors or state legislator group would potentially have inconsistent 
participation and political ties that may conflict with the goal of ensuring PJM acts in 
the best interest of the entire PJM region and not just in the interest of one stakeholder 
or state. The PJM Board, if pressured by politics and state representatives too 
extensively, may have difficulty executing that goal. 
 
19)

process (limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional
