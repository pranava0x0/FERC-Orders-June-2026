ER27-512-000 · 2026-11-16 · DCR Transmission, L.L.C.

Proposed TO Tariff Revisions

DCR Transmission, L.L.C. files tariff revisions under section 205 on November 16, 2026, in ER27-512-000, answering the CAISO show cause order. It owns the Ten West Link, a 125-mile 500 kV line, and adds a new Appendix IV with large load interconnection procedures, a pro forma agreement modeled on CAISO's generator agreement, and an interim service. It adds no new transmission service.

Asks FERC to: Accept the revisions effective February 15, 2027.

Filed under section 205 to answer the E-10 show cause order. It adds a study process, an interconnection agreement and an interim service, handles cost responsibility through reimbursement terms, and leaves new transmission services to CAISO.

E-10 CAISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-10 CAISO: Application / study process (P 5, p. 25) E-10 CAISO: Cost-recovery agreement (§ III.B.3, p. 25) E-10 CAISO: Service for flexible loads (§ III.D, p. 62) E-10 CAISO: BTMG netting (pp. 60-61, p. 60)

Key points

  1. DCR is a single-asset owner whose line crosses the California-Arizona border.
    DCRT owns and operates the 125-mile, 500 kilovolt Ten West Link Transmission Line PDF p. 2
  2. It notes the other parties held other views but tailors its filing to its position as a single-asset owner.
    tailored its proposed tariff revisions to its unique situation as a single-asset PTO whose transmission line traverses the California-Arizona border PDF p. 6
  3. It proposes no new transmission service, deferring to CAISO.
    DCRT has not proposed additional or revised transmission services in this filing PDF p. 6
  4. Its study steps average about 85 days, within the 60 to 90 day range FERC set.
    these steps will average roughly 85 calendar days from start to finish, meeting the OSC Order’s target of 60–90 days PDF p. 11
  5. Its pro forma large load interconnection agreement is modeled on CAISO's generator agreement.
    generally modeled on the CAISO’s pro forma Large Generator Interconnection Agreement (“LGIA”) PDF p. 11
  6. Co-located generation that exports must still go through CAISO's generator process.
    Any generating facility that will export energy to the CAISO Controlled Grid, however, must interconnect through the CAISO’s GIDAP and execute a Commission-accepted LGIA PDF p. 16
  7. The interim service lasts until the identified upgrades reach commercial operation.
    a Large Load Interconnection Customer may elect to take FILI Service until the Network Upgrades identified in Appendix A have achieved Commercial Operation PDF p. 19
  8. Large load facilities are reimbursed for upgrades over $10 million once they reach commercial operation.
    Large Load Facilities will be reimbursed for Reimbursable Network Upgrades costing more than $10 million if the Large Load Facility reaches commercial operation PDF p. 20
