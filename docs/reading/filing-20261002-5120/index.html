Motion for Leave to Answer and Answer of Coalition of MISO Transmission Customers et al. to the Joint Protest of the Sierra Club et al

Six large industrial customer groups answer the protests of MISO's large-load reliability filing and defend its grandfathering provisions as protecting existing manufacturers, with cost-shift concerns left to MISO's later filing.

Asks FERC to: Accept MISO's grandfathering and transition provisions and treat cost-shifting objections as belonging to MISO's forthcoming cost-shift filing.

Answers the order. Answers protests of MISO's first post-E-8 filing, citing the order's instruction to protect existing commercial arrangements and placing cost-shift protections in MISO's separate filing due under the abeyance deadline.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6)

Key points

  1. The Large Energy Users say the grandfathering terms balance reliability against commercial certainty for existing manufacturers.
    strike a balance between managing reliability issues caused by new Large Loads, while protecting commercial certainty for existing manufacturers and industrial customers. PDF p. 2
  2. They point to the E-8 order's statement that the proceeding aims at prospective reforms, not disruption of existing arrangements.
    This proceeding is not intended to disrupt existing commercial arrangements, but rather to establish prospective reforms. PDF p. 3
  3. They describe MISO's two exempt categories: service commencement by December 4, 2026, and MTEP 2026 or EPR-linked projects.
    Large Loads with a Service Commencement Date on or prior to December 4, 2026 PDF p. 4
  4. They say the protestors have not shown that long-standing industrial loads cause the reliability concerns.
    The Show Cause Order is aimed at recent and exponential growth of Large Loads, not at large customers who have been reliably participating in MISO for decades. PDF p. 5
  5. They say cost-shift concerns belong in MISO's separate cost-shift filing, due November 16, 2026.
    these concerns are more appropriately addressed in a future filing specifically focused on cost shifting risk among transmission customers. PDF p. 6
  6. They argue the proposal reflects the stakeholder process and deserves deference.
    The Commission affords an appropriate amount of deference to consensus-based proposals developed during the stakeholder process. PDF p. 7
