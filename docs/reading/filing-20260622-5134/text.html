REQUEST FOR ISSUANCE OF AN ERRATA

To the Commission:

On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued its “Order Instituting Proceeding Under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act” in this above captioned proceeding.1  In its Order, the Commission found that ISO New England’s (“ISO-NE”) existing Transmission, Markets and Services Tariff (“Tariff”) appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential and thereafter directed ISO-NE and sixteen (16) Participating

Transmission Owners (“PTOs”) within ISO-NE to show cause with respect to a variety of concerns related to large load or co-located load transmission and interconnection within sixty (60) days of the date of its Order.2  Vermont Electric Cooperative, Inc. (“VEC”) is a member owned electric distribution utility that provides safe, affordable, and reliable energy services to over 33,000 members in 75 communities.  VEC has identified an issue with the Order and respectfully requests that the Commission issue an errata as soon as practicable at this early stage of the proceeding.

At the onset, VEC desires to make crystal clear that it does not challenge the need for the Commission to boldly address the important national issue of service to large load and co-located load within organized markets and elsewhere.  VEC further acknowledges Commission precedent with respect to non-jurisdictional entities with certain rate schedules within a jurisdictional RTO or ISO.  VEC’s request is purely procedural. The Commission lists VEC as one of the PTOs in the caption of the Order.  But at footnote 10 of the Order, describing which PTOs it intended to include in the caption, the Commission made clear:

Like each of the other PTO entities listed in footnote 10, VEC is a not a public utility pursuant to section 201(f) of the FPA.  In 2006, VEC informed the Commission that as a result of the passage of the Energy Policy Act of 2005, it ceased to be a public utility on August 8, 2005 because the changes to the Federal Power Act effective as of that date exempted electric cooperatives that sell less than four (4) million megawatt hours annually. At the time of the change in law, VEC sold approximately 500,000 megawatt hours.  The Commission subsequently acknowledged VEC’s acceptance of its withdrawal of jurisdictional rate schedules based on the change in law.4

Twenty years later, VEC is still well under the established statutory four (4) million megawatt hour threshold.  To be sure, VEC is a PTO within ISO-NE but it is a PTO that is similarly situated to all of the other of the non-jurisdictional entities listed in footnote 10 of the Order.  VEC respectfully submits that it is unaware of any basis, stated within the Order or any facts external to the Order, to treat VEC any different. VEC believes that the Order inadvertently identified it based on its legacy status as a public utility. Further. VEC believes the Commission did not intend to include it as a named respondent because in the Show Cause Order issued to the California Independent System Operator, Inc. and its transmission owners in Docket No. EL26-71-000, non-jurisdictional entities were purposefully included as respondents.5  However, in that order, the Commission made clear in footnote 9 that it included each of the non-jurisdictional entities as respondents “because their Transmission Owner Tariffs on file with the Commission detail the wholesale load interconnection process in CAISO.”6 The Commission continued, “[i]f a non-public utility wishes to be removed from this proceeding, we will rescind the show cause order against them at their request, and we expect CAISO to respond to this order regarding the non-public utility’s CAISO Tariff provisions on file with the Commission.”7  The Commission did not express a similar concern with respect to ISO- NE’s non-jurisdictional transmission owners.

An errata to the Order affecting ISO-NE would not impact or diminish the Commission’s stated show cause goals whatsoever; the Commission’s interests are in modification of the terms and conditions of the ISO-NE Tariff. Based on these facts, VEC respectfully requests that the Commission issue an errata to the Order that

(1) removes VEC from the caption of Docket No. EL26-72-000, which intended to identify only ISO-NE and jurisdictional PTOs; and

(2)  adds VEC in the list of non-jurisdictional PTO entities in footnote 10.

To the extent the Commission determines the requested correction exceeds the scope of an errata, VEC respectfully requests in the alternative that the Commission issue a clarification that rescinds the Order with respect to VEC as described in the CAISO Order precedent set out above.
