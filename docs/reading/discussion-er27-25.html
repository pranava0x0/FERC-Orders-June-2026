ER27-25: the filed comments

Positions read for this site

Mississippi and Louisiana Public Service Commissions

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The commissions backed a 120-day joint study. They said studying the load and its generator together could avoid duplicating network upgrades; the limit of ten pairs per period made it manageable. They wanted a permanent process so projects could plan and finance construction.

Arkansas Public Service Commission

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

Arkansas expressly agreed with the Mississippi and Louisiana commissions’ comments and asked FERC to accept MISO’s tariff changes. It did not offer its own numerical estimate of costs or queue delays.

Iowa Office of Consumer Advocate, Illinois and Michigan attorneys general, Minnesota Department of Commerce and co-protesters

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The joint protesters asked how the study would prevent costs shifting to other customers. They warned that related MISO proposals could exempt about 30 GW of existing load from some reliability requirements; 30 GW was not the size of the proposed LARS study queue.

Google

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

Google supported studying loads and generators together but questioned the separate assumptions used in the ordinary queue. It wanted cost protections and a way for independent generators to participate without discrimination by the serving utility.

Bloom Energy

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

Bloom backed the study but challenged its 200 MW entry threshold. It proposed letting transmission-connected loads of at least 50 MW participate and asked FERC not to delay the requested start date.

American Clean Power Association and Southern Renewable Energy Association

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The trade associations said studying accelerated projects without other generators already waiting could leave later studies with fewer feasible connections and higher costs. They objected to a permanent parallel route without a cap on total capacity.

Solar Energy Industries Association and Advanced Energy United

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The protesters asked FERC to reject the proposal or clarify that an applicant meeting all published requirements could not lose access at the transmission customer’s discretion.

Clean Energy Advocates

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The advocates warned that two study tracks using different assumptions could count the same unused transmission capacity twice. They challenged assigning upgrade costs to existing customers based on agreements whose terms FERC had not seen.

Alliant Energy and Consumers Energy

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The utilities backed the readiness requirements because a speculative project could occupy one of a limited number of study slots. They argued the joint model could identify cheaper combined upgrades.

Entergy and Cleco

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The utilities said some ordinary MISO study cycles had taken four years or more. They supported the 120-day path and argued that its cap of ten load-generator pairs per study period would keep it workable.

DTE Electric

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

DTE supported the pairing rules and minimum commitment as evidence of a lasting commercial relationship. It also said the tariff allowed the interconnection customer to participate even though the transmission customer submits the application.

Midcontinent Independent System Operator

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-02

MISO proposed LARS in a separate section 205 docket. It argued that modeling a matched pair could identify upgrades for the combination instead of each project separately. This was one proposed response to the June concerns. The full show cause case is still open.

Midcontinent Independent System Operator

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-11-09

MISO said the transmission customer submits an application for an already matched pair. It argued that an independent generator remains free to bring a proposal to that customer before the application, and that the eligibility rules are nondiscretionary.

Midcontinent Independent System Operator

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-11-09

MISO defended 200 MW based on its experience with large-load studies. It said modeling the load and several associated plants together offered the largest potential savings where loads and upgrades were especially large.

Midcontinent Independent System Operator

· ER27-25-000 · 2026-11-09

MISO said the study should go ahead before related cost-recovery reforms were complete. It promised to incorporate those protections once FERC accepted them and cited other existing tariff mechanisms.

MISO Transmission Owners

Supports · ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The transmission owners asked FERC to accept LARS effective December 2, 2026. They said it would yield an agreement in an estimated 120 days, is limited to three study cycles a year with ten applications per cycle, and keeps LARS requests out of the standard queue studies.

the LARS proposal will provide for a new Large Load Generator Interconnection Agreement in an estimated 120 days PDF p. 5

Data Center Coalition

Supports with changes · ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The coalition supported approving LARS but objected that every application must come through the load-serving entity, which keeps third-party generators from applying directly. It asked FERC to encourage MISO to let generators with demonstrated commercial pairings apply directly, or to add transparency on how utilities select applications.

DCC supports approval of the proposed LARS framework, but requests FERC encourage MISO to consider improvements for independent generator access PDF p. 7

Southern Minnesota Municipal Power Agency and WPPI Energy

Opposes in part · ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

The two municipal power suppliers protested only the rule that any generator and load in the same Local Resource Zone can pair. They said it departs from the Show Cause Order’s two-substation example and excludes close pairs in different zones. They asked FERC to reject that scope and to condition acceptance on a later finding that MISO addressed cost allocation and transparency.

MISO’s Filing does not come close to justifying a departing from the Show Cause Order’s approach by substituting a geographic scope that is both overinclusive and underinclusive PDF p. 5

Geronimo Power

Asks FERC to reject · ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

Geronimo supported coordinated study in principle but said a generator cannot enter LARS without the transmission owner’s sign-off on its point of interconnection and a utility sponsor for the application. It also said virtual power purchase agreements and co-located projects may be shut out. It asked FERC to reject LARS without prejudice to a revised filing.

Geronimo Power urges the Commission to reject LARS on the basis that it has not been shown to be just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential PDF p. 5

Invenergy Transmission

Supports with changes · ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23

Invenergy said LARS fits FERC and DOE goals but that its definitions appear to admit only generating facilities. It asked FERC to direct MISO, on compliance, to clarify that merchant high voltage direct current lines and generation connected to them can participate, and attached a tariff redline.

Invenergy therefore respectfully requests that the Commission direct MISO, on compliance, to revise the proposed Tariff to clarify that MHVDC transmission lines are eligible to participate in the LARS process PDF p. 5

Geronimo Power

Asks FERC to reject · ER27-25-000 · 2026-11-18

Geronimo said MISO’s answer gave no response on virtual power purchase agreements or co-location and did not justify the roles of load-serving entities and transmission owners. It said a transmission owner could also remove a project by changing the point of interconnection. It again asked FERC to reject LARS without prejudice to a revised filing.

A long-term virtual PPA provides the same level of commercial certainty as a long-term physical PPA and allowing this arrangement to qualify for LARS would in no way lower the bar MISO has set. PDF p. 3

They said cost-shifting protections rest on future filings, and repeated that FERC should reject the geographic scope and condition acceptance rather than delay LARS.
