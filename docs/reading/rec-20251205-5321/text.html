23. First, limiting the amount of multi-year price-cap supply cleared to the amount needed to increase total cleared supply to the Procurement Threshold, but no more, is a key feature that reduces the distortive impact of the Enhanced Backstop on competitive markets in several important ways.

24. Thus, the Enhanced Backstop will not affect market clearing outcomes in years in which the RPM clears below the price cap (with the exception that multi-year supply cleared in prior years may have an ongoing effect in subsequent years).  The Enhanced Backstop will also not have an effect on the clearing price in years in which it is triggered and any multi-year price cap offers cleared will be paid the same price as other resources cleared in that year because the amount of multi-year supply cleared will not push the total cleared quantity past the point where the demand curve begins sloping downward at 99% of the Reliability Requirement.  Rather, as Figure 1 shows, the operation of the Enhanced Backstop will simply move the market clearing outcome along the flat portion of the demand curve at the administrative price cap.

25. The Enhanced Backstop thus creates an additional supplemental pool of supply that kicks in to improve reliability only under very tight market conditions without suppressing the capacity price signal below where it would have otherwise cleared.  Setting the Procurement Threshold at 98% of the Reliability Requirement strikes a balance between ensuring that the market is able to achieve a reasonable minimum level of reliability and also providing a cushion for clearing lumpy offers while still staying below the point on the demand curve (99% of the Reliability Requirement) where the backstop would have a suppressive impact on the clearing price.

IV. CERTAIN FEATURES OF THE ENHANCED BACKSTOP COULD BE VARIED TO ACHIEVE A
DIFFERENT BALANCING OF MARKET CONFIDENCE AND HARM LIMITATION
30. In my opinion, the various features of the Enhanced Backstop discussed above strike the best balance between increasing confidence in the ability of the RPM to bring forth supply when needed on one hand while also limiting harm to the market on the other.  That said, there are several features and parameters of the Enhanced Backstop that could be varied to the extent that the Commission or other stakeholders wish to recalibrate the balance between these two goals.

31. First, in my view the Procurement Threshold of 98% of the Reliability Requirement is the most important parameter in balancing between the two goals.  While setting the Procurement Threshold at 98% of the Reliability Requirement strikes the best balance in my opinion, setting it to a higher or lower number, within certain bounds, would still achieve a workable mechanism, albeit one with a different tradeoff between reliability on one hand and potential harm to the market on the other.  Specifically, reducing the Procurement Threshold below 98% of the Reliability Requirement would result in the market procuring less supplemental supply when the Enhanced Backstop is triggered, which would result in a lower level of reliability but also less potential price distortion over time, while procuring more than 98% would have the opposite effect.

33. In my opinion, a Procurement Threshold of 98% of the Reliability Requirement sets a reasonable balance between reliability assurance on one hand and limitation of market distortions on the other.  Setting the threshold at or close to 99% increases the risk that the Enhanced Backstop could affect market prices in the year in which it is triggered due to lumpy offers, and further results in a higher volume of multi-year commitments that could potentially distort prices in subsequent years.  Conversely, setting the procurement threshold at less than 98% would result in loss of load expectations more than doubling relative to the long-term reliability target in years in which the Enhanced Backstop is triggered, which is likely to be an unacceptably low minimum level of reliability for consumers.  However, a range of Procurement Threshold values between 97% and approximately 98.5% (to allow for lumpy offers) is likely workable.

34. Another potential lever that could be varied would be to increase the maximum allowable multi-year offer term from seven years to potentially as high as ten years.  Increasing the maximum allowable term expands the universe of potential supply that may be willing to submit multi-year price-cap offers because it increases the level of assured capacity revenue over time that resources are able to express in their offers.  This decreases the likelihood that PJM may end up in a situation where there are insufficient multi-year, price cap offers to reach the Procurement Threshold, with a resulting degradation in reliability.  Conversely, as discussed previously, increasing the maximum allowed term increases the potential for market distortions over the long term.  In my opinion, the proposed seven-year term limitation is reasonable but raising it to as high as ten years would still be workable.

Finally, another lever that could further reduce any potential harm on markets would be to require that PJM support the continuation of the Enhanced Backstop for delivery years after 2028/29 with an analysis of projected capacity supply and demand for the upcoming year prior to the BRA.
