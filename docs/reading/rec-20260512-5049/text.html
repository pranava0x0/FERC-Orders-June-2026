A. PJM TOs Ignore Operational Realities of Industrial BTMG Facilities
First, the PJM TOs Answer inappropriately lumps together all industrial BTMG 
facilities, ignoring explicit statement that not all loads using BTMG create the reliability 
and resource adequacy risks.3 The blanket endorsement of a 50 MW threshold4 does 
not provide consideration for the realities of individual facilities, including the Eastman 
facility which, despite the 194 MW BTMG capacity of its facilities, could not operate 
solely on electrical power delivered from the grid.5 
Nameplate capacity is not inherently indicative of the actual capability of BTMG 
at a facility. As Eastman explained in its Protest, the Kingsport facility is a co-
generation facility, and its electric generation is coupled with steam production which 
is necessary for Eastman’s industrial processes.6 When the facility's generators are 
constrained, both electricity output and steam production fall, reducing the facility's 
own industrial load and with it, demand on the grid.7 The facility naturally reduces grid 
demand precisely when its generation output is limited, the opposite dynamic from the 
large loads that motivated the Commission's concern.8

C. The PJM TOs Justification of 50 MW Threshold is Arbitrary
Third, the PJM TOs provide no operational justification for the 50 MW 
threshold. The PJM TOs sole justification for the 50 MW threshold is a definition 
developed in an entirely different regulatory context, which was not intended to address 
the reliability or resource adequacy risks that the Commission raised in relation to 
BTMG netting.18 The PJM TOs provide no evidence that BTMG facilities at or above 
50 MW have historically caused the resource adequacy or reliability problems the 
Commission identified as a concern.19  
Additionally, PJM TOs administrability argument is unpersuasive. While a 
static nameplate-based threshold may be easy to administer, administrative 
convenience does not establish that a proposal is just and reasonable, particularly where 
the result is the arbitrary elimination of netting rights for facilities that pose no grid 
reliability or resources adequacy risk. Nameplate capacity measures the maximum 
potential output of a generator, not the actual impact that a BTMG facility has on the 
grid. For industrial co-generation facilities like the Kingsport facility, the relevant 
metric is not how much electricity the facility could generate, but how that generation 
interacts with the facility's load under real operating conditions. Further, the
