1) 
Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years? 
X 
 
 
Comments: Rockland Electric Company supports extending Board member terms to 5 
years. The intended result of longer Board terms should be to empower the Board to 
make decisions that improve reliability and resource adequacy within the PJM 
footprint.  
2) 
Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member 
may serve (e.g., 10 years)? 
 
X 
 
Comments: Rockland Electric Company finds that 5-year Board terms strike a balance 
that allows the PJM membership to vote for or against Board members on an 
appropriate cadence.

Comments:  Rockland Electric Company does not believe that individual Board votes 
need to be public. However, the PJM membership and the public would benefit from 
greater explanation of how the Board comes to its final decision on matters where it 
possesses Federal Power Act (FPA) section 205 filing rights. This could manifest in 
PJM management staff providing greater education to PJM members and the public.  
7) 
In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open 
or available to the public? 
 
X 
 
Comments:  The Board has taken efforts to increase its availability and visibility to the 
public through appearances at monthly Members Committee meetings and other PJM 
events. Rockland Electric Company encourages PJM to identify other informal venues 
in which members and the public can interact directly with Board members.  
8) 
Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that 
the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest? 
 
X 
 
Comments:  Rockland Electric Company is not opposed to recognizing the 
responsibility of PJM and its Board to serve the public interest. However, it is not 
apparent how incorporating this new language would provide clear direction to PJM 
and its Board in carrying out that responsibility.  
9) 
Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the 
Nominating Committee? 
X 
 
 
Comments:  Rockland Electric Company supports the states selecting one 
representative to serve as a voting member on the nominating committee to provide the 
states with the same amount of representation as the five existing PJM member sectors.

Stakeholder Process 
 
 
Questions/Comments 
Yes 
No 
16) 
Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered 
from two-thirds sector-weighted voting? 
 
X 
 
Comments:  Rockland Electric Company supports maintaining the current two-
thirds sector-weighted voting threshold, which encourages parties to seek 
consensus and compromise when developing packages through the PJM 
stakeholder process. However, if the PJM stakeholder process moves more to an 
advisory role, Rockland recognizes that the need for a two-thirds voting threshold 
at the Members Committee would be unnecessary for the purpose of approving 
proposals.  
17) 
Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior 
standing committees, also apply to lower-level committees? 
X 
 
 
Comments:  Rockland Electric Company supports implementing sector-weighted 
voting at the lower-level committees to promote stakeholder consensus and to 
improve efficiency in developing consensus of proposals as they move through the 
consensus-based issue resolution process.

20) 
Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making an FPA 
section 205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost 
allocation? 
 
X 
 
Comments:  Rockland Electric Company supports the Board using the stakeholder 
process in an advisory manner to inform the development of the Board’s FPA 
section 205 filings. Rockland Electric Company also supports more formal state 
involvement in the PJM Members Committee that would grant OPSI or the other 
state entities greater opportunity to engage with FPA section 205 filings developed 
pursuant to the Board’s authority. Incorporating OPSI directly into the stakeholder 
process would allow for greater representation at the Members Committee while 
still allowing the Board to make FPA section 205 filings on reasonable timelines.
