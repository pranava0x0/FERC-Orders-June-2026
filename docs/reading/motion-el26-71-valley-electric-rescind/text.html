II. DESCRIPTION OF VEA
VEA is a rural electric distribution cooperative based in Pahrump, Nevada, with a service territory in southern Nevada and a small portion in California.  VEA is generally not regulated by the Commission because it is a rural electric cooperative that sells fewer than 4 million megawatt hours of electricity per year.   In 2011, the Commission accepted a Transition Agreement between VEA and the CAISO that established the process for VEA to join the CAISO as a Participating TO.6

VEA became a Participating TO and a signatory to the Transmission Control Agreement (“TCA”) in 2013, placing its 230 kV and 138 kV facilities under the CAISO’s Operational Control. In 2017, the Commission approved the acquisition of VEA’s 230 kV system by GLW, a Non- Load-Serving Participating TO and a respondent in this proceeding.7 VEA’s only remaining facilities under CAISO Operational Control are its 138 kV facilities, which are Low Voltage Transmission Facilities whose costs are recovered from VEA’s own members.  Interest from large loads in VEA’s territory has focused on GLW’s 230 kV system.

IV. JUSTIFICATION FOR RESCISSION
A. VEA Is Exempt From the Commission’s Jurisdiction Under FPA Section 201(f)
Section 201(f) excludes from Part II of the FPA “an electric cooperative that receives financing under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936 . . . or that sells less than 4,000,000 megawatt hours of electricity per year.”13 VEA sells approximately 577,130 MWh’s of electricity per year; therefore, it is exempt from Part II of the FPA pursuant to Section 201(f). As the Six Cities did, VEA acknowledges the Commission’s authority to review VEA’s Transmission Revenue Requirement to ensure that the CAISO’s rates remain just and reasonable.14

VEA stands in the same position as the Six Cities and WAPA. Each is a non-public utility Participating TO with a TO Tariff on file with the Commission, each asked to be removed from this proceeding while committing to continue coordinating with the CAISO, and the Commission granted each request.15 The same result is appropriate for VEA. The timing of this motion is also no obstacle. The Show Cause Order places no deadline on a non-public utility’s request; it provides that the Commission will rescind the order “[i]f a non-public utility wishes to be removed from this proceeding.”16

C. VEA’s Participation in the Joint PTOs’ Request for Abeyance Does Not Alter Its Section 201(f) Status

VEA joined the Joint PTOs’ Request for Abeyance to preserve its ability to coordinate with the CAISO and the other Participating TOs and to participate in the CAISO large load stakeholder process to develop a common approach to responding to the Show Cause Order. The Joint PTOs explained that many of the Commission’s concerns “can only be resolved with complementary or, at a minimum, non-conflicting tariff provisions among the CAISO Tariff and the various TO Tariffs,” and that their requests “reflect a coordinated regional effort consistent with Commission direction.”17 VEA supported that effort and continues to support it.

D. Large Loads in VEA’s Territory Would Interconnect to GLW’s 230 kV System, Which Remains Before the Commission

The Show Cause Order addresses large loads, which the Commission described as new commercial or industrial customers with a peak load of 50 MW or greater that interconnect to the transmission system at a voltage above 69 kV.21 Large loads seeking service in VEA’s territory likely would not interconnect to VEA’s 138 kV facilities, given the option to interconnect with GLW’s more robust 230 kV system.

Service over those 230 kV facilities is governed by GLW’s TO Tariff, not VEA’s. GLW is a public utility, and remains a respondent in this proceeding, The rates, terms, and conditions the Show Cause Order addresses for large loads in VEA’s territory will therefore remain before the Commission through GLW and the CAISO. Rescinding the Show Cause Order as to VEA leaves no gap in the Commission’s review, and the Commission has already stated that it expects the CAISO to respond regarding a non-public utility’s CAISO Tariff provisions on file with the Commission.22
