A. The changing nature of load growth in the MISO region.

The MISO region is experiencing a change in the scale, concentration, and pace of new electricity demand.  Large Load additions, including data centers and advanced manufacturing facilities, can introduce substantial demand at a single location and seek service on schedules that are shorter than those traditionally required to develop supporting generation and transmission infrastructure.  These developments require MISO, the Transmission Owners, and the entities serving those Loads to coordinate planning, investment, and operational requirements early in the development process to address the unique needs associated with such Large Load additions to the grid.  As MISO explained in its July 20, 2026, Informational Report, conventional forecasting methods were developed for more gradual and geographically dispersed growth; Large Load projects present additional uncertainty concerning location, timing, and the demand that ultimately materializes.12

The resulting challenge extends beyond identifying a physical point of connection.  MISO must assess the transmission facilities needed to serve new demand, understand how the Load will behave during normal operations and system disturbances, and coordinate the addition of resources needed to support resource adequacy.  At the same time, the uncertainty surrounding Large Load development makes it necessary to distinguish committed projects from speculative requests and to address the risk that investments made to serve a particular load may remain unrecovered if that Load is delayed, reduced, or does not materialize.  MISO’s Large Load initiatives were developed to address these related planning, reliability, and cost recovery concerns.13

MISO has also recognized that certain Large Loads may be willing and able to limit their withdrawals from the Transmission System.  That flexibility can support service before all facilities needed for full firm service have been completed, or an ongoing arrangement under which only a portion of the Load receives firm service.  Accommodating those different needs requires clear service rights, enforceable operating limits, and appropriate treatment of the customer’s planning and financial obligations.  The development of additional Transmission Service options therefore became a component of MISO’s broader Large Load work.

K. The Proposed Tariff Revisions are Just and Reasonable.

Under section 205 of the Federal Power Act, MISO must show that its proposed Tariff revisions are just and reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential. “For filings made under FPA section 205, the Commission evaluates whether the proposal in the filing is just and reasonable, and not whether alternative proposals are more or less reasonable than the filing party’s proposal.”117  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found that MISO’s Tariff lacked clear and consistent provisions addressing large load integration. The proposed LLPR revisions directly address these concerns.

MISO developed a dedicated framework for Large Loads and proposes to add the LLPR process to Attachment FF of the Tariff as well as new pro forma Attachments LLL and MMM. V. TRANSMISSION SERVICE OFFERINGS

A. Overview and service selection.

MISO proposes transmission service options that distinguish a customer seeking temporary non-firm transmission service while progressing toward full NITS from a customer electing an ongoing permanent firm and non-firm service mix.  Firm Service Step Up (“FSSU”) permits NITS service to increase as the required capability becomes available.  Interim NITS permits transmission service within approved non-firm limits while the remaining upgrades needed for full NITS are completed.

The transmission service election determines the customer’s rights and obligations. Firmness addresses the availability and priority of Transmission Service; Resource Adequacy addresses the capacity needed to support demand.  The proposal treats those questions separately. Interim NITS remains in the capacity construct because it is a transition to full NITS service. Neither election authorizes withdrawals beyond the quantities and operating conditions established for that service.

B. Firm Service Step Up.

The FSSU process is designed to allow a Transmission Customer to obtain NITS in increments before all upgrades supporting its ultimate NITS requirement have been completed. As the relevant capability becomes available, a portion of the customer’s Interim NITS, described further below,  may convert to NITS while the remaining portion continues under the applicable interim restrictions.  The customer therefore can use available firm capability without assuming a firm entitlement to the full quantity before the system can support that service.

The FSSU process is optional and allows NITS to be combined with Interim NITS.  At each step, the applicable firm NITS quantity remains tied to the capability supporting that step; any remaining interim quantity continues to be subject to its non-firm, Interim NITS, limits.  This distinction preserves the integrity of the service actually available while accommodating a customer’s phased development toward full NITS.171

FSSU provides speed to power because it enables faster integration of incremental amounts of Large Load using firm service or Interim NITS.  Ms. Xie explains that an FSSU request may include a firm service component and Interim NITS component, allowing a portion of the customer’s non-firm service to convert to firm service before all required Upgrades are complete, with the remaining non-firm service continuing under the applicable Interim NITS operating limits.172  The FSSU proposal thus provides an expeditious option to obtain firm service, at least in part.

C. Interim NITS.

The interim service developed through the Flexible Large-Load Interconnections and Transmission Service (“FLITS”) stakeholder work is referred to here as Interim NITS.  It allows a Transmission Customer taking NITS on behalf of a Large Load to begin receiving an approved quantity of non-firm service while the Network Upgrades required for full NITS are completed. The customer remains on a path to full NITS rather than selecting a permanent substitute for NITS.  When the required upgrades are completed and the conditions for full NITS are satisfied, full NITS begins.  Where FSSU is used, the interim agreement continues to govern the portion that has not yet converted to firm service.

Charges for Interim NITS are determined under Schedule 9, and Interim NITS load is treated as Network Load in almost all cases under the proposed design.  An increase in the interim load quantity is reflected in the applicable Attachment F exhibit, but updating the exhibit does not establish additional system capability or authorize operation beyond the approved limit. Cumulative load growth of at least 25 MW requires the applicable LLPR study.174

Interim NITS is subject to non-emergency operating restrictions and curtailment when required by transmission conditions.  During a Transmission Emergency, Interim NITS is curtailed after non-firm Point-to-Point service but before NITS and Firm Point-to-Point Transmission Service, and Firm GRITS.  Interim NITS is not curtailed solely to mitigate economic congestion. Its non-firm limits are tied to the transmission capability available before completion of the Upgrades supporting full NITS.175
