ER27-526-000 · 2026-11-16 · Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. and the MISO Transmission Owners

MISO and its transmission owners file large-load review, cost recovery and transmission service revisions under section 205

MISO and its transmission owners file tariff revisions under section 205 on November 16, 2026, in a new docket, ER27-526-000. The filing adds a Large Load Project Review with a $250,000 application fee, two pro forma cost recovery agreements, a public dashboard, and two new services, Firm Service Step Up and Interim NITS.

Asks FERC to: Accept the revisions effective February 14, 2027, 90 days after filing, with a 30-day comment period.

Filed under section 205 by MISO and its transmission owners to answer the E-8 show cause order for MISO, alongside the companion GRITS filing and MISO's earlier large-load filings.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Application / study / operating requirements (P 6(a), p. 6) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6) E-8 MISO: Minimum cost recovery / security (P 119, p. 74) E-8 MISO: Alternative transmission technologies (P 119, p. 74)

Key points

  1. A new Large Load Project Review would study every large-load service request, with deposits and milestones to deter speculative ones.
    The LLPR also includes deposits, project milestones, and other safeguards designed to discourage speculative Large Load requests. PDF p. 5
  2. Applicants pay a $250,000 non-refundable fee.
    a non-refundable application fee of $250,000 PDF p. 14
  3. Large-load customers would bear the cost of the upgrades built for them through cost recovery agreements.
    designed to ensure that the costs incurred to provide Transmission Service to Large Load Transmission Customers, including the costs of Large Load Upgrades (defined herein), are born by such customers PDF p. 5
  4. MISO would publish the location and estimated costs of large loads on a dashboard.
    The results of the LLPR periodically will be published to provide transparency into the location and estimated costs associated with Large Load facilities in the MISO region PDF p. 5
  5. Two new services, Firm Service Step Up and Interim NITS, would let customers take service in stages as they build out.
    The new Transmission Services available to Large Loads include Firm Service Step Up and Interim Non-Firm NITS service. PDF p. 5
  6. Loads of 200 MW or more partnered with a generator for at least 15 years go through the resource study instead.
    If the Large Load is greater than or equal to 200 MW and has formally partnered with a generator for a minimum of fifteen years, then the applicants will go through the LARS study process. PDF p. 15
  7. Transmission owners' study reports filed with a large-load application must say which alternative transmission technologies were considered and what was decided.
    requires Large Load Transmission Customers to include with their LLLPR Application the applicable Transmission Owner’s Interconnection Study Report, specifically identifying what ATTs and GETs were considered PDF p. 44
  8. The application also carries the owner's cost estimate, which MISO says gives the cost transparency the order sought.
    This information will enable MISO to verify any analyses performed by the Transmission Owner and provide the cost transparency sought by the Show Cause Order. PDF p. 16
  9. A load that wants to co-locate with generation must first have an interconnection agreement, such as a zero-injection agreement, in place for the generator.
    if the applicants are looking to co-locate the Load with the generation, then they will need to ensure that an appropriate Interconnection Agreement e.g. “ZGIA” has already been executed for the generator PDF p. 15
  10. Paired generation can ask for up to 150 percent of the load's peak demand and must sit in the same resource zone.
    limits the requested interconnection service to 150 percent of the paired load’s peak demand, and requires the load and generation to be located in the same Local Resource Zone PDF p. 45
