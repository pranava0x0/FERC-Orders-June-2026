POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF ILLINOIS MUNICIPAL ELECTRIC AGENCY

Illinois Municipal Electric Agency (“IMEA”) respectfully submits these comments in response to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC’s”) Notice Requesting Post- Conference Comment (“Notice”).1  IMEA is an intervenor in the instant proceeding.2 IMEA is a municipal joint action agency organized under Illinois law as a municipal corporation and unit of local government of the State of Illinois.3  IMEA is comprised of thirty- two municipal members, all of which own and operate their own municipal electric systems in the State of Illinois and provide electricity to their end-use customers.  IMEA load is split roughly evenly between PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) and Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.  IMEA provides a fully bundled and delivered, full-requirements service to each of its members and as such is the Load Serving Entity responsible for meeting certain obligations imposed by the Regional Transmission Organizations (“RTOs”) such as PJM.

IMEA is a member of PJM’s  Electric Distributor sector.  In an effort to best facilitate reforms to PJM’s governance structure and stakeholder processes as a member thereof, IMEA submits answers to the questions found in the Appendix to the Commission’s Notice,4 and provides clarifying comments in each applicable question below.

I. PJM BOARD OF INDEPENDENCE

Questions/Comments
Yes No
1) Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?
X

Comments: Three years is too short, but five or more years is too long.  Four is appropriate.

2) Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 years)?

X

Comments: A Board member should serve, as long as they are elected by stakeholders, no longer than two terms of four years each (i.e., maximum of eight years of service).

3) Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region?

X

Comments: It is not necessary that a Board member reside in the PJM region, although maintaining a residence in the PJM region may be prudent.  In-person attendance at meetings with an in-person option should be encouraged.

4) Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for Board members from two years to one year?

X

Comments: So long as the restrictions on financial interests are maintained or strengthened, a one-year cool off period is sufficient and would aid in expanding the candidate pool.

5) Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more communication between the Board and stakeholders?

X

Comments: Yes, Board communication with stakeholders is essential.
6) In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be required to explain its reasoning?

X

Comments: The Board should be required to explain its reasoning, especially in the instances in which it proceeds with a proposal that differs in substance from what passes through the stakeholder process.

7) In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the public?

X

Comments: Though Board Decisional Letters are a useful tool in understanding the Board’s thought process, these come only after the decision has been made.  Opening some Board meetings to the public would increase transparency and strengthen communication.

8) Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest?

X

Comments: PJM’s primary purpose is to maintain reliability through its markets and planning processes.  Expanding its scope only risks further complicating its current outlook at a time when operations need to be streamlined.

Questions/Comments

9) Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the
Nominating Committee?

X

Comments: PJM covers a large service territory comprised of states each with their own resource adequacy, transmission, and energy outlooks.  The inclusion of a state representative on the Nominating Committee risks politicizing a process which needs to be geared towards PJM’s north star – maintaining reliability.  That would be more difficult if a process already as challenging as finding qualified Board members was made more so by expanding the criteria for inclusion on the Nominating Committee.

10) Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management
compensation to their achievement?

X

Comments: Public performance metrics tied to compensation can lead to compensation- oriented thinking in a way that sacrifices quality of work product.  For example, if “Target A” is tied to compensation, an organization may priority “Target A” at the expense of other meaningful “Targets” which are not tied to compensation.  PJM should work with stakeholders and the Board to prioritize a work plan, and all parties involved should stick to it.

II. SECTION 205 FILING RIGHTS

Questions/Comments
Yes No
11) Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board?

X

Comments: As an electric distributor and load serving entity which participates in an RTO with an advisory stakeholder process, MISO, IMEA strongly disagrees with the idea of making the PJM stakeholder process advisory.  The tasks that PJM is faced with on a daily basis – keeping the lights on for the 67,000,000 Americans within its service territory – is enormous.  As the bulk electric system becomes more complex, that job only becomes more difficult.  The stakeholder process in PJM offers enormous benefit through the communication and collaboration it enables by allowing both PJM and stakeholders to thoughtfully work through issues that are too demanding, too important, and too complex to be solved by any one party individually.  While it is clear that reforms to the stakeholder process are necessary for the sake of efficiency, these changes can be found within the process itself, not the rights held by stakeholders.  A purely advisory process would erode the checks and balances which exist today and greatly diminish the ability for stakeholders to contribute to the decisions which will ultimately affect their interests, and the downstream effect would be lessened transparency, trust, and balance in PJM’s day-to-day proceedings.

III. STAKEHOLDER PROCESS

Questions/Comments
Yes No
16) Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-thirds sector-weighted voting?

X

Comments: Interests are too diverse today to expect a two-thirds majority with the frequency necessary to move important items along.  For that reason, yes, the approval threshold in the Members Committee should be lowered to a simple majority.

Questions/Comments
Yes
No
12) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

X

Comments: The two-thirds majority is a threshold which promotes consensus building amongst stakeholders and ultimately results in proposals which have been subjected to increased scrutiny for the betterment of the RTO as a whole.  Nonetheless, two-thirds is a demanding statute in today’s environment and lowering it to a simple majority would foster efficiency gains without sacrificing needed rigor.  Further, PJM already has 205 filing rights over the energy and ancillary services market, which are not the subject of current reforms.  Current reform efforts are aimed at the capacity market.

13) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain

approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

X

Comments: Just as with the energy and ancillary service markets, the need to obtain a two- thirds supermajority is a requirement which promotes collaboration amongst stakeholders and PJM.  With that said, two-thirds is a high mark, and lowering it to a simple majority would promote collaboration while facilitating greater efficiency.

14) Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal?

X

Comments: While OPSI should have an augmented role in PJM’s stakeholder process, it should not have “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings, given the outsized influence this would provide, which would diminish the need for collaboration.

15) Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the portions of the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal?

X

Comments: The need for this can be best illustrated using PJM’s recent Reliability Backstop Auction filing (ER26-3380).  PJM’s filing did not incorporate many of the key elements that were approved by stakeholders.  The ability for a supermajority of stakeholders to have “jump ball” filing rights would allow for the Commission to consider a more fulsome panorama of opinions, at no detriment to the Commission.

STATE PARTICIPATION

Questions/Comments
Yes No
18) In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’

representatives?

X

Comments: The states have funding and representation through the Organization of PJM States, Inc. (OPSI).  If PJM wishes to emulate the method of state participation in MISO, it might consider augmenting the role of OPSI to mirror that of the Organization of MISO States (OMS), in lieu of splintering and diluting the states' voice through the creation of another new stakeholder group.  OMS is highly regarded and operates in conjunction with MISO, the MISO Board, and stakeholders, on many issues.  It is important for states to have their voices heard, but a second state organization may actually have the unintended consequences of complicating the conversation and adding to the confusion.  PJM needs to be streamlining its process to make it more efficient if it wants to be more effective.  A second entity could have the opposite effect, and in fact, PJM already has the tools today to bolster the states through OPSI.  Doing so would not necessitate the creation of a new stakeholder body nor the funding thereto.  Additionally, as PJM stated at the Technical Conference, within the same state PJM tends to hear differing viewpoints on the same issue. Maintaining OPSI as the states' vector of communication and participation, perhaps in an enhanced role, would solidify and amplify the state's voice through the usage of an already existing, funded, and respected entity.  An augmented OPSI could, for example, be adjusted to reflect a broader group of government/state representatives.

19) Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the consumer advocate votes?

X

Comments: State jurisdiction lies at the retail, not wholesale level.  Communication between states and PJM, as exists with OPSI, is important.  But voting rights on FERC- jurisdictional issues would be inappropriate.

20) Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation?

X

Comments: In light of diversifying needs across the region, consultation with OPSI is a requirement that would give both PJM and OPSI a beneficial opportunity for communication.
