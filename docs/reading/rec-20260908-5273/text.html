On August 13, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C., (“PJM”) proposed revisions to the Open Access Transmission Tariff and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) and a New Large Load (“NLL”) Registry.1 Among other things, IRAS would identify NLLs that have not brought capacity to meet their needs in an NLL registry and permit PJM to send a wholesale curtailment signal, equivalent to the NLL quantity that does not have associated capacity, prior to entering into pre-emergency procedures. Those NLLs that have not brought sufficient new capacity could be required to reduce load before PJM calls on Pre-Emergency Load Management resources. PJM also proposes that the NLLs be eligible for compensation when load is reduced.

Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,2 the Virginia State Corporation Commission (“VSCC”) submits this Motion

I. MOTION FOR ACCEPTANCE OF LATE-FILED COMMENTS
Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure, the VSCC respectfully requests that the Commission accept these late-filed Comments. On August 25, 2026, the VSCC timely filed a doc-less Notice of Intervention in this proceeding. In an attempt to coordinate positions with other PJM states, the VSCC was unable to provide these comments in the designated time permitted by the Commission. The VSCC offers the following Comments to aid the Commission in its decision-making process and avers that these Comments are not intended to delay or disrupt this proceeding. Therefore, the VSCC respectfully requests that the Commission grant this Motion and accept these Comments.

II. COMMENTS

As the Commission is aware, on June 30, 2026, the VSCC and the Data Center Coalition (“DCC”) announced their intention to work together on just the sort of issues IRAS is intended to address, calling on other state regulators and interested parties to develop model retail interruptible tariffs and emergency load reduction programs that

would allow large loads to take interruptible service, establish standardized curtailment obligations and operating rules, and allow state-jurisdictional utilities to curtail participating large commercial and industrial customers during system emergencies to protect existing customers.3

In filing its Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal in Docket No. ER26-3380- 000, PJM went to great lengths to describe these efforts, which PJM characterized as part of the cooperative federalism approach underpinning that proposal.4  In the instant proceeding, PJM again described these efforts and stated that it “stands ready to assist in those efforts and other related efforts and engagement.”5  The Commission should act here in a way that allows PJM, the states and all other interested parties to follow through on these commitments.  Approving the IRAS as filed would have the opposite effect. PJM characterizes IRAS as a wholesale service and the IRAS compensation rate as a wholesale rate.  But the customers proposed to be curtailed under IRAS are retail customers, and the compensation included in IRAS will be collected from, and paid to, retail customers.  The Federal Power Act (“FPA”) is clear that it is states, and not PJM or FERC, who have exclusive authority over the rates, terms, and conditions of retail

To be clear, the VSCC supports initiatives to encourage large load flexibility. The VSCC’s efforts in this area predate PJM’s IRAS proposal,6 and through state-level proceedings and the other initiatives discussed above, the VSCC continues to lead on this issue. Other state commissions within the PJM footprint are taking similar action. Rather than permitting PJM to move forward with a proposal based on questionable jurisdictional authority, the Commission should empower state efforts to craft tariffs and curtailment procedures based on clear state jurisdiction.

If, however, the Commission elects to move forward with a wholesale approach to retail NLL curtailment, it should do so without PJM’s proposed compensation rate, which rests upon an even more dubious jurisdictional foundation.
