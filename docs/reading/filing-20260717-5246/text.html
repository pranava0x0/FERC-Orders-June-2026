that wholesale cost shifting will occur in a manner that FERC’s proposed cost 
recovery agreements do not fully address because FERC’s proposed agreements 
will simply guarantee collection of revenue contributions under existing cost

III. 
Request for Clarification 
A. 
FERC should clarify that PJM’s existing regional cost allocation results in 
unjust and unreasonable transmission cost shifting for Network Upgrades 
caused by service to large loads and that FERC’s proposed cost recovery 
agreements address a different aspect of unjust and unreasonable cost shifting. 
The PA OCA requests FERC’s clarification that the Order’s pro forma cost recovery 
agreements will help solve some—but not all—of the Order’s stated unjust and unreasonable 
transmission cost shifting caused by the influx of large loads to the grid. Specifically, FERC’s 
proposed cost recovery agreements secure the collection of revenue contributions from Eligible 
Customers serving large loads under existing regional cost allocation methods. FERC’s 
clarification should specify an additional preliminary conclusion that existing regional cost 
allocation methods may result in unjust and unreasonable wholesale transmission cost shifting 
between Eligible Customers serving large loads and other transmission customers, because—under 
existing methods—other transmission customers pay for Network Upgrade costs that they do not 
cause or commensurately benefit from. FERC made a preliminary finding “that PJM’s Tariff 
appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk 
of cost shifting among transmission customers, which may result in unjust and unreasonable rates 
for transmission service.”10 FERC further identified that these unjust and unreasonable rates burden

As Commissioner Chang identified in her concurrence, “bilateral agreements that simply

Unjust and unreasonable wholesale transmission cost shifts from large loads in PJM can 
manifest through unjust and unreasonable cost allocation of both RTEP projects and Supplemental 
Projects, not just by speculative large loads failing to pay their existing share under existing cost 
allocation methods. Indeed, wholesale Network Upgrade cost allocation happens at the regional 
level, under PJM’s Tariff Schedule 12 and Appendix A, and PJM’s Tariff, Section 34.1 and 
Attachment H, as explained below. These PJM Tariff provisions establish cost responsibility 
methods for Network Upgrades for RTEP projects and Supplemental Projects, respectively. 
Accordingly, FERC should examine regional cost allocation for both RTEP projects and 
Supplemental Projects in this proceeding.  
For Network Upgrades designated as Supplemental Projects, PJM’s Tariff, Section 34.1, 
and Attachment H establish rates and cost responsibility for Network Integration Transmission 
Service (NITS). Supplemental Project costs are allocated or assigned within the PJM zone in which 
they are built. Pursuant to the FERC formula rate, the eligible costs of Supplemental Projects are 
included in the transmission owner’s revenue requirement. Attachment H calculates the NITS rate 
(expressed in dollars per megawatt), which is charged to all transmission customers within the

A state cannot reach beyond its borders to sub-allocate charges that the PJM Tariff assigns 
to transmission customers in other states.14 Similarly, a state cannot protect ratepayers from costs

more than one state. It appears that the following seven zones include substantial territories in at 
least two states: American Electric Power (AEP), Allegheny Power Systems (AP), American

Ohio and Kentucky (DEO&K), and Potomac Electric Power (PEPCO). These seven multi-state 
Zones comprise 52 percent of PJM’s peak load.15 Two other Zones (EKPC and JCP&L) have small 
protrusions into a second state. Because large loads occupy small geographic footprints, even these 
modest extensions into a second state could enable significant cost shifts that a state commission 
would be incapable of remedying. Thus, across more than half of PJM, retail sub-allocation cannot 
undo cost shifts that have already occurred among transmission customers through the PJM Tariff.

Upgrades caused by large loads—in addition to the cost shifting protections addressed in the 
Commission’s Order. As explained above, wholesale Network Upgrade cost allocation happens at 
the regional, PJM level.
