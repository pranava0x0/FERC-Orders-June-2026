As Commissioner Chang identified in her concurrence, “bilateral agreements that simply provide transmission revenue contributions untethered from any assessment of the actual cost of providing transmission service induced by individual large loads may be insufficient to adequately protect other customers against unjustified cost shifts.”12

Unjust and unreasonable wholesale transmission cost shifts from large loads in PJM can manifest through unjust and unreasonable cost allocation of both RTEP projects and Supplemental Projects, not just by speculative large loads failing to pay their existing share under existing cost allocation methods. Indeed, wholesale Network Upgrade cost allocation happens at the regional level, under PJM’s Tariff Schedule 12 and Appendix A, and PJM’s Tariff, Section 34.1 and Attachment H, as explained below. These PJM Tariff provisions establish cost responsibility methods for Network Upgrades for RTEP projects and Supplemental Projects, respectively. Accordingly, FERC should examine regional cost allocation for both RTEP projects and Supplemental Projects in this proceeding.

For Network Upgrades designated as Supplemental Projects, PJM’s Tariff, Section 34.1, and Attachment H establish rates and cost responsibility for Network Integration Transmission Service (NITS). Supplemental Project costs are allocated or assigned within the PJM zone in which they are built. Pursuant to the FERC formula rate, the eligible costs of Supplemental Projects are included in the transmission owner’s revenue requirement. Attachment H calculates the NITS rate (expressed in dollars per megawatt), which is charged to all transmission customers within the

A. FERC’s Order violates the Federal Power Act and Administrative Procedure
Act to the extent FERC interprets the Order to sufficiently address the
wholesale transmission cost-shifting problem caused by large loads.
The Order’s pro forma cost recovery agreements will help solve some—but not all—of the Order’s stated transmission cost shifting problem caused by large loads. Other aspects of wholesale transmission cost shifting are, or may be, caused by large loads. FERC made a preliminary finding “that PJM’s Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among transmission customers, which may result in unjust and unreasonable rates for transmission service.”46 FERC further identified that these unjust and unreasonable rates burden transmission customers that “neither caused the need for nor benefit commensurately from” Network Upgrades for large loads.47 The cost recovery agreements address
