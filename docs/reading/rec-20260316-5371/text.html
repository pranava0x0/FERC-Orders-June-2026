Pursuant to Rules 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 the Maryland Office of People’s Counsel (“MdOPC”) respectfully submits the following protest regarding PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) compliance filing submitted on February 23, 2026 in the above captioned proceeding (hereinafter, “February 23 Compliance Filing”) that proposes tariff revisions with respect to behind-the-meter generation, and services available for co-located load, among other proposed revisions, to implement the Commission’s December 18, 2025 compliance directives in the co-located load show-cause proceeding (“Show Cause Order”).2

INTRODUCTION
PJM’s February 23 Compliance Filing proposes tariff revisions in compliance with the Commission’s Show Cause Order, that attempt to create clarity and consistency with regard to the rates, terms, and conditions applicable to co-location arrangements. Particularly, the February 23 Compliance Filing proposes to clarify how both new and existing generation can interconnect co-location arrangements.

However, PJM’s proposed revisions are incompatible with the Statement of Principles agreed by the White House-Governors, dated January 15, 2026 (hereinafter,

Show Cause Proceeding
On February 20, 2025, the Commission initiated a show cause proceeding under section 206 of the Federal Power Act to determine whether PJM’s tariff is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential without provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to Co-Location Arrangements.5 On December 18, 2025, the Commission determined that PJM’s tariff is unjust and unreasonable, explaining that the tariff:

does not contain provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to Interconnection Customers serving Co-Located Load, including generators serving Co-

Located Load in a configuration with special protection schemes to prevent the Co-Located Load from taking energy from the transmission system.6

The Commission found that:

February 23 Compliance Filing
PJM’s February 23 Compliance Filing generally sets forth the relative obligations of project developers seeking to serve co-located load, and PJM. The proposal defines “Co-Located Generating Facility” as “an existing or planned Generating Facility that is physically connected to a Co-Located Load,” and “Co-Located Load” as “a configuration that refers to end-use customer load that is physically connected to the facilities of an existing or planned Generating Facility on the Project Developer’s side of the Point of Change in Ownership to the Transmission Provider’s Transmission System.”12 Importantly, these definitions appear to accommodate not only new generators, but existing generators seeking to serve co-located load.

White House and PJM Governors Statement of Principles and White House Pledge On January 15, 2026, the National Energy Dominance Council within the White House and thirteen governors of states within PJM signed the Statement of Principles urging PJM to expeditiously file proposed tariff revisions with the Commission that reflect a set of principles for how generation capacity for data centers should be procured, and how costs should be allocated to data centers to protect residential customers.17 Specifically, under these principles, PJM should hold a RBA to procure new capacity resources, with the costs of such new capacity going primarily to load serving entities with new data centers that have not self-procured new capacity or agreed to be curtailable.18

On March 4, 2026, the White House issued the Ratepayer Protection Pledge that companies will build, bring, or buy the new generation resources and electricity needed to satisfy their new energy demands, paying the full cost of those resources whether by building, or buying from, new or otherwise additive power plants.19 The pledge also provides that where possible, these companies will also add more capacity that serves the broader public by increasing supply.20

PROTEST
I. PJM’s proposed tariff revisions are unjust and unreasonable because it is unclear how they will interact with PJM’s implementation of the RBA.

As explained by the Commission in the Show Cause Order, PJM’s tariff is unjust and unreasonable because it does not contain provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to generators serving Co-Located Load and Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co- Located Load.21 However, in light of the Statement of Principles issued jointly by the White House and thirteen governors of states within PJM, the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, and PJM’s subsequent responsive work toward instituting a RBA to be held toward the end of 2026, PJM’s tariff remains without sufficient clarity and consistency. The Statement of Principles directed PJM to hold an RBA to procure new capacity resources.22 The Statement of Principles further urges that costs of such new capacity should go primarily to load serving entities with new data centers that have not self- procured new capacity or agreed to be curtailable.23 The Ratepayer Protection Pledge also commits the major data center developers to procure new capacity resources to serve their needs. Thus, the thrust of the Statement of Principles and the Ratepayer Protection Pledge is that generally new data centers should pay for new capacity.

Under this framework, new data centers that are not served by self-procured new co-located generation could be subject to higher costs from the load serving entity as

result of the RBA. But PJM’s proposed tariff revisions do not explain how existing capacity resources that shift to co-located arrangements with new data centers would be treated relative to the RBA. For example, it is unclear whether the capacity of existing generators that enter co-location arrangements with new data center load would be added to the capacity required to be procured in the RBA, or to the capacity procured in the regular base residual auction (“BRA”).

If the capacity of existing generation that shifts to serve new co-located data center load is added to the BRA, ratepayers would be saddled with enormous costs that should be assigned to the co-located load, in contravention of the Statement of Principles and the Ratepayer Protection Pledge. For example, recent analysis by PJM’s Independent Market Monitor concluded that if 1,000 UCAP MW of nuclear capacity in Maryland was removed—due to shifting to serving a co-located load for example—total market revenues for the 2025/2026 BRA would increase by more than $3.6 billion.24 Yet PJM’s compliance filing provides no explanation as to how such costs would be paid by new co- located data center load rather than being shifted to existing ratepayers. PJM’s compliance filing thus leaves significant and fundamental questions unanswered with respect to how existing generation can serve new co-located data center load. Without providing more details regarding how the proposal will align with PJM’s plans for the RBA, PJM’s proposal remains unclear and inconsistent with the Statement

CONCLUSION
The issuance of the Statement of Principles by the White House and thirteen governors of states within PJM calling for an RBA to serve new data center load and the Ratepayer Protection Pledge are salient intervening events that have occurred since the Show Cause Order issued. However, despite the significance of PJM’s work toward implementing an RBA, fundamental issues surrounding how existing generators that shift to serve co-located load under PJM’s proposed tariff revisions will be treated relative to the RBA remain unaddressed. As a result, PJM’s proposed revisions do not provide the clarity and consistency required by the Show Cause Order. PJM’s proposed tariff revisions thus are unjust and unreasonable without a complete picture of how they will interact with the RBA. At the very least, the Commission should defer action on the proposed revisions until PJM proposes tariff revisions to implement the RBA.
