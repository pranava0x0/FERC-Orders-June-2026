Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (the “Commission”)1 and in accordance with the Commission’s Combined Notice of Filings notice issued August 13, 2026, Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. (“SMECO”)2 respectfully submits this Comments and Limited Protest regarding the filing that PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) submitted in the above-captioned docket on August 13, 2026 (the “IRAS Filing”).3

PJM proposes revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish a new “Interim Resource Adequacy Service” (“IRAS”) and an associated “Large Load Registry.”  As proposed by PJM, IRAS is a supplemental capacity product through which Eligible New Large Loads may be directed by the Electric Distributor to reduce their load in response to an Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action called by PJM.4

Specifically, beginning with the 2027/2028 Delivery Year, IRAS would allow New Large Loads to interconnect without first securing capacity to cover their full demand, subject to a condition: PJM may direct the Electric Distributor serving such a load to reduce an aggregate quantity of that load -- ahead of PJM’s existing Pre-Emergency Load Management Reductions -- when PJM determines that available real-time supply may be insufficient to maintain required reserves.5

SMECO’s comments and limited protest address certain aspects of the IRAS Filing, including whether the real-time Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action mechanism that Mr. Christopher Pilong’s affidavit describes will operate as PJM intends without exposing Electric Distributors’ core native load to curtailment, and further aspects in which Electric Distributors are assigned new operational, data-reporting, compensation-administration responsibilities and exposure to payments and default allocations for payments owed by other entities under PJM’s proposed billing indemnification provision.

I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY
PJM’s IRAS proposal responds to the same capacity shortfall underlying PJM’s separate Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) filing in Docket No. ER26-3380 – namely, that the 2028/2029 Delivery Year Base Residual Auction cleared 6,831.3 MW short of the PJM Region Reliability Requirement, a shortfall PJM attributes in substantial part to the rapid interconnection of large loads, including data centers.6 Where the RBP is a one-time capacity procurement, IRAS is a standing framework – i.e., it permits New Large Loads to connect to the PJM system ahead of

PJM proposes to define “Large Load” as end-use customer load with a cumulative peak of 50 MW or greater at a single electrical site, including affiliated load facilities within a one-mile radius under specified configurations.10 A “New Large Load” is a Large Load (or incremental load at an existing site) that enters service after June 1, 2027,11 and an “Eligible New Large Load” is a New Large Load that has not entered into a “Bring Your Own New Capacity” (“BYONC”) arrangement or has not been allocated UCAP MW through the RBP.12

IRAS is triggered when PJM determines that available economic real-time supply may not be sufficient to maintain required reserves or applicable Interconnection Reliability Operating Limits.13 In that circumstance, PJM may issue an “Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action” directing the affected Electric Distributor to reduce an aggregate MW quantity of load -- based on the zone/area’s pre-Delivery Year IRAS obligation -- before calling on existing Pre-Emergency Load Management Reduction Actions.14

III. COMMENTS.
A. PJM’s IRAS Proposal Assigns Significant New Electric Distributor Responsibilities. In its IRAS Filing, Proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section III requires that Electric Distributors “shall” fulfill four tasks:

1. Execute IRAS Load Reductions. An Electric Distributor must “execute the Office of the Interconnection’s directives to implement Interim Resource Adequacy Service Actions.”22 SMECO does not object to this task, which is a natural extension of the Electric Distributor’s existing Emergency-response role.

2. Build and validate the Large Load Registry. An Electric Distributor must “provide PJM with any information or data the Electric Distributor believes appropriate to facilitate an accurate Large Load Registry, including applicable state or RERRA retail tariff or contractual obligations,” and coordinate with PJM and the applicable Transmission Owner to “check the validity of the information provided.”23

This task goes beyond OA section 11.3.3(h)’s data-reporting obligation, which speaks to data “necessary or appropriate to implement or administer” the OA and RAA themselves -- not to serving as a validation source for a registry whose contents is built on information originating with LSEs or Large Loads that the Electric Distributor does not necessarily control.24

3. Identify which retail customers to curtail. An Electric Distributor must “coordinate, as appropriate, with the relevant state(s) or RERRA(s), individual [LSEs], and customers served by the [LSEs] in its zone/area to support transparent, effective, and orderly deployment” of IRAS.25 PJM characterizes this function as “memorializing” existing practice, under which Electric Distributors already determine, consistent with state programs, which loads to reduce in response to a PJM directive.26

While PJM OA sections 11.3.3(c) and (e) require an Electric Distributor to maintain load- shedding capability and to “[s]hed connected load … and take such other coordination actions as may be necessary in accordance with the directions of the Office of the Interconnection in Emergencies” -- an operational, physical-curtailment obligation27, PJM’s description of the coordination duty as a “memorialization” of past practice does not change that Electric

IV. LIMITED PROTEST.
B. PJM’s Real-Time Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action Mechanism Does Not Confirm That the New Large Load Underlying an Electric Distributor’s Assigned Obligation Is Actually Energized or Online, Risking Curtailment of Core Native Load. The Affidavit of Christopher Pilong, submitted in support of the IRAS Filing, confirms that the “[i]dentification of an IRAS requirement for each Electric Distributor’s zone/area establishes the reliability obligation applicable to affected load,”29 and that “the IRAS requirement establishes an operational reliability resource available to PJM, which can be called upon when actual system conditions indicate that the reliability need addressed by the IRAS requirement is materializing.”30

Nothing in Mr. Pilong’s affidavit, however, ties PJM’s actual exercise of that authority in real time to whether the New Large Load generating the obligation, in fact, is energized and taking service. To the contrary, Mr. Pilong describes an Interim Resource Adequacy Service Action as a directive to reduce a fixed, pre-set quantity of load at the Electric Distributor zone/area level, determined without regard to real-time large-load operating status.

Specifically, the MW quantity of IRAS assigned to a given zone/area is fixed “prior to the start of each Delivery Year,” and thereafter is simply the figure “utilized by PJM Dispatch in their

C. The IRAS Filing’s Billing Indemnification Provision Shifts Compensation Default Risk to Electric Distributors, Including for Payments Owed by Other Entities.

The PJM Filing proposes “IRAS Compensation”, in which “Electric Distributors bear the responsibility to implement the compensation scheme for New Large Loads that provide the IRAS load reduction service.”37   As noted above, PJM is proposing a rate for IRAS that is equal to 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate. Given that the current RTO-wide Non-Performance Charge Rate is approximately $2,278/MWh (for the 2027/2028 Delivery Year), a new large load that did not arrange for new capacity would be compensated for curtailing at a rate of over $1,100/MWh. However, proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section X(b)(iii) provides that “neither PJM nor the PJM membership, generally, is financially responsible for any potential defaults that may arise in association with the billing of” IRAS compensation.”38 Under this provision, if a state-approved rate requires collection of IRAS compensation from other LSEs, and an LSE does not pay as required -- “whether billed directly by the Electric Distributor or by PJM upon request of the Electric Distributor” -- “the default will not pass to the PJM membership,” and instead “any default shall be assessed to the Electric Distributor.”39

PJM acknowledges that “in restructured states, the LSE may be an independent entity, not affiliated with the Electric Distributor.”40 Where that is so, an Electric Distributor directed to bill (or to request that PJM bill) another LSE for IRAS compensation would bear the full risk of that unaffiliated LSE’s non-payment, notwithstanding that the Electric Distributor has no ownership relationship, and potentially no pre-existing commercial or credit relationship, with the paying LSE. PJM’s proposal includes no mechanism -- no collateral requirement or no PJM-administered mutualization -- to protect the Electric Distributor against that exposure. SMECO respectfully requests that the Commission direct PJM to revise section X(b)(iii) to limit an Electric Distributor’s exposure to such circumstances.
