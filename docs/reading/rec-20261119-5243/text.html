With this filing, SPP is proposing the addition of PALS to the Tariff to address the Commission’s specific concern that the Tariff “does not include transmission services that reflect Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of flexible large loads that are willing and able to limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.” PALS is a flexible transmission service that allows Transmission Customers to limit their use of the Transmission System. Under PALS, a PAL’s withdrawals are governed by the price-sensitive PAL Energy Bids11 its Market Participant submits, which are subject to a uniform $25/MWh cap that protects other customers from substantially high energy prices and from utilizing resources for PALS loads that are otherwise intended to serve firm load. PALS is available for renewable terms of one to ten years and is not tied to the construction of Network Upgrades. PALS is SPP’s regional variation on the Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service described in the PJM Co-Location Order, tailored to SPP’s Integrated Marketplace and SPP’s transmission services. Like the Commission’s Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service, PALS limits service to a specified MW quantity (the PALS Capacity). It is curtailed before firm service, imposes penalties for unreserved use, carries no resource adequacy obligation, and requires control technologies or protection systems that keep withdrawals within approved levels.
