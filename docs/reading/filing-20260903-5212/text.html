SUMMARY
The instant proposal and PJM’s separate RBP initiative address the same reliability problem: unprecedented growth in new large loads—chiefly, data centers—that has

outpaced new generation and transmission development and produced consecutive years of capacity shortfalls in PJM’s BRAs.9 RBP seeks to cover the immediate shortfall, though does so by means that are unjust and unreasonable in myriad ways.10 IRAS also seeks to preserve regional reliability in the face of an unprecedented resource adequacy challenge. The mechanism facilitates the interconnection of new large loads but requires (or should require) that they make a choice: either bring new capacity sufficient to meet their needs or (assuming appropriate state action) face curtailment when circumstances necessitate load reductions.

Joint Consumer Advocates recognize and appreciate that PJM’s IRAS proposal
seeks to navigate a difficult course, and we support the general thrust of the proposal. If implemented correctly, the approach could go far toward maintaining reliability without either stopping the interconnection of non-capacity backed new large loads or socializing the reliability consequences of non-capacity backed large-load growth. We submit that neither of those options was likely to gain regulatory traction or, even more importantly, to be consistent with PJM’s obligation to ensure resource adequacy.11 IRAS rightly strikes a

PLAGUING THE BRA.

In a supply-constrained market, new entry cannot perform its function of disciplining the market power of incumbent resources:42

continued shortfalls, increased reliability issues, continued
