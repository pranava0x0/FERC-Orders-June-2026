Protest and Comments of Joint Consumer Advocates on the 08/13/2026 filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C

Six state consumer advocates (the Joint Consumer Advocates) protest PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service and Large Load Registry filing. They support the concept but ask FERC to require non-firm transmission service, tighten bring-your-own-capacity eligibility, and reject the separate backstop procurement.

Asks FERC to: Find IRAS unjust and unreasonable as filed and direct an amended filing with non-firm service for non-capacity-backed loads, new-only self-supply, a validated registry, and rejection of the backstop procurement.

Parallel proceeding. Invokes the June 2026 PJM show cause order and urges FERC to use Section 206 to require non-firm service for non-capacity-backed loads, the type of flexible-load service E-7 directs PJM to address.

E-7 PJM: Service for flexible loads (P 85, p. 56) E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. The advocates cite the June 2026 show cause order's finding on PJM's tariff and ask FERC to require non-firm service for new large loads that do not self-supply.
    new large loads that do not self-supply must take non-firm transmission service. PDF p. 2
  2. They want only resources that have not cleared a prior Base Residual Auction to count as new capacity.
    only resources that have not previously cleared a prior Base Residual Auction (BRA) are eligible. PDF p. 2
  3. PJM's design lets a load reduce curtailment risk with capacity that already cleared, adding load without adding supply.
    This would increase the amount of load on the system without either increasing system supply or making the new load an IRAS resource subject to curtailment. PDF p. 4
  4. Compensating curtailed loads leaves states to decide who pays, so other customers may fund payments to data centers.
    which leaves open the possibility that non-large load customers may yet end up paying resource adequacy costs to the new large data center loads that are driving PJM’s reliability concerns. PDF p. 5
  5. The Market Monitor figures show data center demand raised costs to other customers by $23.1 billion over three auctions.
    The total increase in costs to other PJM customers as a result of data center load in the last three BRAs was $23,100,955,341. PDF p. 8
  6. FERC has called large loads a separate category and directed every RTO and ISO to define them.
    a separate category of load PDF p. 9
  7. IRAS could meet the roughly 6.8 GW need the backstop targets, sooner and more durably, making the backstop unnecessary.
    IRAS can satisfy the capacity need at which the RBP is aimed sooner, more durably, and, if correctly modified as non-firm transmission service, at less cost. PDF p. 38
