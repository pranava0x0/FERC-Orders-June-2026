INTRODUCTION

The unprecedented number of new and forecasted large load additions, mostly data centers, in the PJM footprint has outstripped new supply, and PJM’s capacity auctions have cleared short of the region’s reliability requirement for two consecutive auctions despite hitting auction price caps in both auctions.3 These shortfalls increase the risk of loss-of-load events and sustained high-capacity prices for existing ratepayers who are not responsible for these new shortfalls. Moreover, PJM expects that capacity shortfalls may continue due to the rapid growth of data centers and other large loads that PJM cannot currently serve reliably. The IRAS Filing is an attempt by PJM to ensure that future shortfalls do not create reliability risks and to avoid shifting costs to residential or other existing ratepayers. PJM proposes four changes to its governing documents. First, PJM will require that a Load Serving Entity (“LSE”) must bring new capacity to offset or exceed the amount of peak load from new large loads.4 If an LSE fails to bring such new capacity, it will be subject to load reduction requirements prior to Pre-Emergency Load Management Response.5 Second, PJM will establish a Large Load Registry, which will allow PJM to identify how much new capacity an LSE is required to bring to offset new large loads.6 Third, PJM will define Large Loads, New Large Loads, and Eligible New Large Loads.7 Fourth, PJM will exclude New Large Loads that do not Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) from the Variable Resource Requirement (“VRR”)

DISCUSSION

I. The IRAS Framework is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory.
Overall, the IRAS proposal presents a just and reasonable approach to protect reliability and fairly allocate costs among PJM customers and zones. Without the IRAS proposal, PJM customers will be doomed to bear ever-rising costs and degrading reliability as large loads consistently outpace new supply. This reality warrants new rules. The IRAS framework is an essential course-correction that will help rectify years of public subsidization of large load

As described above, the IRAS Filing has distinct components which all must operate together to form a comprehensive solution. PJM proposed to establish a Large Load Registry, require LSEs to ensure that New Large Loads BYONC, implement IRAS load reduction requests prior to Pre-Emergency Load Management Response, and remove New Large Loads that do not BYONC from the VRR Curve in the capacity auction. These comments will refer to New Large Loads that do not BYONC as “unmatched large load.” The interplay between these components is essential to the function of the IRAS proposal. Together, these components can be referred to as the “IRAS Framework.”

The IRAS Framework is not unduly discriminatory because it recognizes that large load customers are differently situated from other customers due to their outsized impacts on other customers’ costs, resource adequacy, and reliability risks. Similarly, the IRAS Framework is jurisdictionally sound because it respects the roles of PJM, states, end-use customers, and Electric Distributors (“EDs”) outlined in the Federal Power Act. Finally, the IRAS Framework ensures just and reasonable rates by removing unmatched large loads from the capacity auction, ensuring that new large loads are responsible for their own capacity costs.

Large load customers are differently situated than traditional residential and commercial customers and warrant new rules and obligations. The IRAS Filing correctly recognizes this distinction and applies new responsibilities to large loads, through their EDs, that will protect other customers from unjust costs and reliability risks.

VI. Modification 4: The Commission should require BYONC supply to be fully
deliverable to new large loads by considering transmission limits between zones.

PJM’s proposed BYONC requirements do not consider the location of the New Large Load nor the associated supply. This jeopardizes reliability and risks socializing costs to other customers in violation of the objectives of the IRAS Framework. Without a locational requirement, PJM risks relying on new capacity that will not actually be able to serve the zone in which the sponsoring new large load resides, thus failing to achieve reliability objectives.149 Similarly, allowing distant load and supply arrangements could result in significant regional transmission needs, which shift costs to other ratepayers, failing objective 2. Because PJM’s capacity and energy markets recognize locational constraints, new supply that is transmission constrained will not deliver cost relief. The Commission should direct PJM to require BYONC to be located only in zones that are not constrained with respect to the zone in which the new large load resides.

CONCLUSION

PJM’s IRAS Filing generally presents a just and reasonable mechanism to ensure large loads are responsible for their own new capacity and protect other customers from unintended reliability consequences while respecting state jurisdiction over retail rates. However, to ensure that the IRAS Filing achieves its objectives, the Commission must make the modifications described above to: (1) ensure IRAS and BYONC requirements are permanent for all new large loads or else clarify that the trigger for turning IRAS off requires the capacity market to clear for three consecutive years with sufficient supply to meet all demand including all large loads subject to IRAS; (2) require penalties for LSEs that fail to comply with IRAS Actions; (3) either eliminate compensation for IRAS resource curtailment or require PJM to cap the compensation awarded to IRAS loads at no more than the amount the new large load paid in capacity charges for the relevant delivery year; and (4) require BYONC supply to be fully deliverable to new large loads by considering transmission limits between zones.
