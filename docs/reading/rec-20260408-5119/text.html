MOTION FOR LEAVE AND SUPPLEMENTAL PROTEST OF 
CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC  
Constellation Energy Generation, LLC moves for leave1 to submit this supplemental 
protest to advise the Commission of an important factual development underscoring why the 
Commission must reject PJM’s proposal to define Co-Located Load in relation to the “Point of 
Change in Ownership” which PJM proposed to do in both its 30-day and 60-day compliance 
filings.2   
The Commission’s December 18, 2025 Order3 was explicit in defining Co-Located Load 
as “a ‘configuration [that] refers to end-use customer load that is physically connected to the 
facilities or planned Customer Facility on the Interconnection Customer’s side of the Point of 
Interconnection to the PJM Transmission System.’”4 The Commission will recall that PJM’s

In its protest of both PJM’s 30-day and 60-day compliance filings, Constellation explained 
that PJM’s deviation from the Commission’s order would allow Transmission Owners to 
effectively block co-location arrangements. Specifically, a Transmission Owner could refuse to 
interconnect load located on the generator’s side of the Point of Interconnection unless the 
generator acquiesced to the Transmission Owner’s ownership of all facilities connecting that load 
to the Point of Interconnection. While such load would still be Co-located Load under the 
December 18th Order’s definition—it is still on the generator’s side of the Point of 
Interconnection, notwithstanding the Transmission Owner’s ownership of facilities on that side—
it would no longer count as Co-located Load under PJM’s alternative definition focused on the 
Point of Change in Ownership. 
Unfortunately, recent experience has borne out Constellation’s concern. As part of a load 
interconnection application for NITS, Constellation submitted to a PJM Transmission Owner a 
ring bus design to co-locate load at one of its generating stations. The interconnection application 
reflected a ring bus located on the generator’s side of the Point of Interconnection that would allow 
the load to access supply from the generation facility and the grid. Constellation’s protest to PJM’s 
60-day compliance filing depicted this as follows:

During the week of March 30, 2026, the Transmission Owner informed Constellation that 
it would not study Constellation’s proposed design unless Constellation agreed to transfer 
ownership of the ring bus facilities to the Transmission Owner. Under the December 18th Order’s 
definition, Constellation could do so, and the load—located on the generator’s side of the Point of 
Interconnection—would still qualify as Co-Located Load. But under PJM’s definition, transferring 
ownership of the ring bus facilities would mean that the load no longer would be on the generator’s 
side of the Point of Change in Ownership. By conditioning the load’s interconnection on the 
Transmission Owner’s ownership of those facilities, the Transmission Owner will have effectively 
blocked Constellation’s ability to host Co-Located Load at that site and eliminated the co-located 
load’s ability to use the new transmission services adopted in the December 18th Order. 
The fact that Transmission Owners are leveraging their monopoly position to stymie Co-
Location should come as no surprise. After all, the Transmission Owners’ joint comments to PJM’s 
60-day filing are unabashed in restating that “some” of those Transmission Owners “strongly 
disagree” that “the new transmission services” adopted in the December 18th Order should

“ultimately [be] required to be implemented.”5 Such statements from Transmission Owners—and 
the actions described herein—run exactly counter to the principal motivation of the Commission’s 
December Order to accommodate new ways for large loads to site more quickly.6 Given the depth 
of Transmission Owner resistance to the Commission’s December 18th Order, the Commission 
must be on guard for attempts by the Transmission Owners to effectively block access to these 
new transmission services, even while purporting to facially comply with the Order. PJM’s 
proposal to define Co-Located Load with reference to the Point of Change in Ownership, rather 
than the Point of Interconnection, enables exactly that kind of gamesmanship and risks rendering 
the Commission’s December 18th Order a dead letter. The Commission should reject PJM’s 
proposal in both the 30-day and 60-day compliance dockets.
