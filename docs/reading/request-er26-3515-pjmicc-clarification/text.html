OF THE PJM INDUSTRIAL CUSTOMER COALITION AND STEEL PRODUCERS 
 
Pursuant to Rule 713 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission” or 
“FERC”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 the PJM Industrial Customer Coalition (“PJMICC”) 
and Steel Producers respectfully request clarification or, in the alternative, rehearing of portions 
of the Commission’s October 9, 2026, Order Accepting Tariff Revisions in Part and Rejecting 
Tariff Revisions in Part (“October 9 Order” or “Order”) pertaining to the applicability of the Large 
Load Registry (“Registry”) to industrial and manufacturing loads and the Registry’s confidentiality 
rules.2 PJMICC is authorized to state that the Industrial Energy Consumers of America (“IECA”) 
strongly supports this Request.3    
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
The October 9 Order rejected PJM’s proposed Interim Resource Adequacy Service 
(“IRAS”) and Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) frameworks, which were the core of 
PJM’s Section 205 filing on August 13, 2026.4 However, the Commission accepted PJM’s 
proposal to create a Registry and accepted PJM’s proposed definition of Large Load.

PJMICC and Steel Producers appreciate the Commission’s statements affirming that 
existing protections in the PJM Operating Agreement (“OA”) will apply to retail customers listed 
in the Registry.5 However, the Registry will compile some of the most commercially sensitive 
information that energy-intensive manufacturers and institutions possess—data concerning who 
they are, where they operate, how much power they use, how they back up their operations, and 
with whom they contract for supply and at what rates. Without certain clarifications regarding who 
may see that information and for what purpose, the Registry risks becoming a source of 
competitive intelligence rather than a reliability tool.  Absent the clarifications requested herein, 
the October 9 Order would be arbitrary and capricious because it does not address how Registry 
data will be protected from uses that could confer market power in PJM’s electric markets or 
undermine competition in critical American industries.   
Clarification is necessary because OA protections must be applied to retail customers, 
including to customers who are not signatories to the OA and do not have the privileges of PJM 
Membership. PJMICC and Steel Producers therefore respectfully request that the Commission 
clarify the scope of Registry data and the scope of access to Registry data in order to ensure 
protection of competitively sensitive retail customer information while enabling targeted access to 
the information necessary to support reliable operations and planning. PJMICC and Steel 
Producers also submit an alternative Request for Rehearing, in the event the Commission does not 
grant the requested clarifications. 
II. 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
PJMICC is a coalition of energy-intensive industrial and institutional customers in the PJM 
region, including manufacturers of paper, cement, chemicals, metals, pharmaceutical products, and

consumer goods, as well as hospitals and universities.6 Steel Producers consists of the steel mills 
owned by (i) Steel Dynamics, Inc., and (ii) Nucor Corporation that are located in the PJM footprint. 
IECA is a nonpartisan association of leading manufacturing companies with $1.3 trillion in annual 
sales, over 12,000 facilities nationwide, and with more than 1.9 million employees—many of them 
in PJM’s territory. Together, PJMICC, Steel Producers, and IECA represent many retail customers 
whose competitiveness is largely determined by the cost and reliability of natural gas and 
electricity. These retail customers have traditionally been thought of as “large” users but are, in 
most cases, much smaller than the large data center projects that are being added to the grid. 
The October 9 Order accepts PJM’s Registry, which will gather into a single location, for 
each Large Load of 50 MW or more, information such as the customer’s identity, location, in-
service dates, peak load and ramp schedule, telemetry specifications, backup generation (including 
fuel type and permitting limitations), and contracts or service agreements with its Load Serving 
Entity (“LSE”), which may include power supply rate information.7 Because the Commission 
rejected IRAS, all retail customers that satisfy the definition of Large Load, including some 
existing PJMICC and Steel Producers members, will be included in the Registry for “informational 
purposes” only.8 
The Order finds that PJM’s existing confidentiality rules under section 18.17 of the PJM 
Operating Agreement (“OA”) will adequately safeguard Registry data.9 This holding is significant, 
but section 18.17 of the OA was not expressly designed for retail end-use customer data, and the 
Order does not specify how access will be limited among the many entities eligible to receive it.

The October 9 Order therefore fails “to consider an important aspect of the problem”10 and does 
not address the significant competitive and security implications of disclosure. Accordingly, 
PJMICC and Steel Producers request that the Commission clarify that: 
1. PJM must segment access to Registry data on a “need-to-know” basis, so that each 
recipient receives only the data relevant to its own planning and operational 
responsibilities, consistent with the scope limits in OA section 18.17.7, which provides 
for Transmission Owners to receive only confidential generator data that impacts that 
Transmission Owner’s planning. 
 
2. Confidentiality protections for retail customer data in the Registry will be no less 
restrictive than those for generator data, including limiting access to operational 
employees and prohibiting disclosure to marketing employees. 
 
3. An LSE may access Registry data only for the Large Loads that it serves, not as a broad-
based access to potential customers, consistent with OA Section 18.17.7. 
 
4. Retail customers listed in the Registry will have the same rights as PJM Members under 
OA section 18.17 to designate data as confidential and prevent it from being disclosed 
to other PJM Members or others. 
 
5. Information on retail customer rates or pricing will be excluded from the Registry and 
redacted from contracts that are added to the Registry. 
 
These clarifications are consistent with the Commission’s October 9 Order, with PJM’s 
commitment to limit Registry access to “as small a universe of entities as possible,” and with the 
protections the OA already affords generator data.11  
However, if the Commission does not grant the requested clarifications, PJMICC and Steel 
Producers respectfully request rehearing, as set forth below.

V. 
REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION 
PJMICC and Steel Producers respectfully request clarification regarding five issues: (1) 
segmentation of Registry data on a “need-to-know” basis; (2) confidentiality protections for retail 
customer data that are no less restrictive than those for generator data; (3) limitation of each LSE’s 
access to data for its own customers; (4) confirmation that retail customers listed in the Registry 
will have the same rights as PJM Members under OA section 18.17; and (5) exclusion or redaction 
of information on retail customers’ rates or pricing. 
As a threshold point, competition is the foundation of PJM markets. The emphasis in this 
Request for Clarification on the need for specific confidentiality clarifications is directly related to 
the importance confidentiality plays in competitive markets—both electric markets and the 
industries in which Large Load customers serve. Without confidentiality regarding key terms such 
as supplier relationships, contracts, load information, and more, competition will be undermined, 
and the risks of market power and antitrust concerns will increase.  Further, there is a direct line 
from confidentiality to competition and from competition to affordability: competitive markets 
discipline prices only when market participants cannot exploit non-public information about their 
customers and rivals. 
Additionally, in restructured states, a customer’s supplier relationship is typically 
confidential, and disclosure of retail customers’ LSEs could affect the functioning of retail 
markets.19 Further, confidentiality has antitrust considerations: Customer-specific load, contract, 
and supplier data shared among LSEs and competitive suppliers could facilitate coordination, 
targeting, or segmenting of customers or customer bases.

The Commission and PJM both recognize that PJM’s existing rules contemplate these 
concerns. The rules already mask resource names, load identity, and submitter identity to protect 
market-sensitive information.20 The rules also provide for data segmentation on a “need-to-know” 
basis. Registry data warrants at least equivalent protection. The FERC should affirm that these 
protections apply on an equivalent basis to load, as set forth below. 
A. The Commission should clarify that PJM must collect and segment access to 
Registry data on a “need-to-know” basis. 
 
The Order accepts PJM’s proposal to make Registry information available to Authorized 
Commissions, Transmission Owners, Electric Distributors, LSEs, the Independent Market 
Monitor (“IMM”), the Commission, and other entities, “subject to appropriate confidentiality and 
data protection safeguards.”21 PJM itself acknowledged that the Registry “is expected to collect 
information that may be confidential or commercially sensitive, or that may implicate 
infrastructure security concerns,” and that it therefore must permit access to “as small a universe 
of entities as possible.”22 In rejecting requests for broader access, the Commission explained that 
“the Large Load Registry will be available only to entities that already have access to this sort of 
information and consistent with existing confidentiality rules,” rules “that currently do not allow 
state consumer advocates or similar entities to have access to this type of information.”23 However, 
beyond this, the Order does not specify how that universe will be limited in practice, or whether 
each recipient will receive the entire Registry or only the portion relevant to its responsibilities. 
The OA already provides a model. When PJM shares confidential generator information 
with Transmission Owners, it does so only for generators that are or will be “modeled within the

Transmission Owner’s energy management system” or “identified in a Transmission Owner’s 
restoration plan.”24 The same principle should govern the Registry: a Transmission Owner or 
Electric Distributor should receive data only for Large Loads located on or directly affecting its 
own system, and no recipient should receive Registry data beyond what it needs to perform its 
planning and operational responsibilities. The Commission should clarify that this principle of the 
OA applies to retail customers’ data and that PJM must implement this need-to-know segmentation 
in its compliance filing or the PJM Manuals. 
Further, because the Commission rejected IRAS, the core reason for developing the 
Registry is no longer present. The Commission did affirm in the October 9 Order that the Registry 
will “provide PJM with the information necessary to address operational impacts” of Large Loads 
and “enable PJM to better forecast load growth and to plan and manage its transmission system.”25 
In addition to segmenting data PJM does collect, the Commission should clarify that PJM’s data 
collection must be limited to data that relates to the operational and planning purposes set forth in 
the Order. 
B. The Commission should clarify that confidentiality protections for retail 
customer data in the Registry will be no less restrictive than the protections for 
generator data. 
 
Generator data is provided substantial protection in the OA, and for good reason: in PJM, 
generators operate in a competitive marketplace. Per the OA, confidential generator data provided 
to Transmission Owners may be provided “only to transmission function employees,” may be used 
only for the Transmission Owner’s reliability and transmission functions, and may not be disclosed 
“to any person, including marketing function employees,” except as expressly permitted.26

Similarly, data available on PJM’s Energy Management System (“EMS”) terminals must be held 
in the Transmission Owner’s secure control room and may not be disclosed to “marketing 
organizations, energy affiliates or independent third parties.”27 
Retail customer data in the Registry has similar competitive and security sensitivities as 
generator data. PJM may not know or understand retail customers’ competitive sensitivities, as 
retail customers operate within particular industries. As PJMICC explained in its Comments, 
however, knowledge of a manufacturer’s load size, location, demand response participation, and 
LSE relationships enables competitors, including international competitors, to infer critical 
operational intelligence.28 The Registry will also contain backup generation details, permitting 
limitations, and contracts or service agreements between the Large Load and its LSE, which may 
include power supply rate information.29 A large manufacturer’s load profile and supply contracts 
should be viewed as at least as sensitive as a generator’s real-time output. The Commission should 
clarify that Registry data will be disclosed only to transmission function (or equivalent planning 
and operations) personnel and will not be disclosed to marketing function employees, consistent 
with the separation of transmission and marketing functions under the Commission’s Standards of 
Conduct, 18 C.F.R. Part 358. 
C. The Commission should clarify that an LSE may access Registry data only for 
the Large Loads that it serves. 
 
The Order confirms that the LSE serving a Large Load is obligated to provide the Registry 
information for that customer.30 It does not follow that every LSE should have access to the 
Registry information of every other LSE’s customers. Many LSEs, particularly competitive retail

suppliers, have marketing functions and compete to serve Large Loads. In restructured states, a 
customer’s supplier relationship is typically confidential, and disclosure of that relationship could 
affect the functioning of retail markets.31 A competitive supplier seeking to serve a Large Load 
should not have access to information that is confidential between that customer and its current 
LSE.32 The Commission should clarify that an LSE’s access to Registry data is limited to its own 
customers and that Registry data may not be used as a marketing tool. 
D. The Commission should clarify that retail customers listed in the Registry will 
have the same rights as PJM Members under OA section 18.17 to designate data 
as confidential and prevent it from being disclosed. 
 
Under the OA, Members are provided with specific rights and protections, such as the right 
to designate information as confidential and to prevent its disclosure to other PJM Members. As 
explained above, many retail customers are not PJM Members. Consequently, to ensure that the 
OA protections apply to retail customers in the Registry, PJMICC and Steel Producers respectfully 
request that the Commission clarify that retail customers’ data is automatically deemed 
confidential upon entry into the Registry or, at a minimum, that retail customers have sufficient 
opportunity to designate their own data in the Registry as confidential. 
These clarifications matter because confidentiality is necessary to support competition in 
the energy industry and key industries that support the nation’s economic growth. Additionally, 
confidentiality protection is needed for retail customers, particularly manufacturers, who must 
compete globally and for whom information in the Registry could be used as competitive 
intelligence by competitors.

E. The Commission should clarify that information on rates will be excluded from 
the Registry and redacted from contracts that are added to the Registry. 
 
A Large Load retail customer’s electric rate or pricing information is one of the most 
sensitive pieces of competitive intelligence in its possession. It is closely guarded by many retail 
customers and is a core component of those customers’ relationships with their respective LSEs. 
As mentioned above, many Large Load retail customers must compete globally in their respective 
industries. Their rates and contract terms are central to these customers’ competitiveness. 
A Large Load retail customer’s rate or pricing information is not necessary for 
Transmission Owners, EDCs, LSEs, or PJM to perform planning or operational responsibilities. 
Because rate and pricing information is highly confidential and central to a retail customer’s 
competitiveness, it should not be included in the Registry or, if it is included in contracts on file 
with the Registry, should be redacted for all recipients.

including industrial customers and other energy-intensive customers, in the Registry.33 The 
Commission recognized that, because IRAS was rejected, industrial customers will be included in 
the Registry only for informational purposes.34 The October 9 Order’s sole stated basis for 
including them is that PJM must have visibility into the full spectrum of Large Loads in its 
footprint.35 The Order identifies no record evidence explaining why that visibility requires 
collecting existing customers’ contracts, power supply rate information, supplier relationships,
