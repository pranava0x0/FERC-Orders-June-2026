Reference:  Deficiency Letter

Dear Ms. Neitzel and Mr. Supino:

On August 17, 2026, Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (MISO), on behalf of American Transmission Company LLC (ATC), submitted proposed revisions to Attachment FF-ATCLLC of MISO’s Open Access Transmission, Energy and Operating Reserve Markets Tariff (Tariff).  ATC states that the proposed revisions address large load integration in the ATC service territory and are intended to be complementary to MISO’s future filings to comply with the Commission’s directives in the Large Load Show Cause Order.[fn:2] 

Please be advised that the submittal is deficient and that additional information is necessary to process the filing.  Please provide the information requested below.
In section IV (Definitions) of Attachment FF-ATCLLC, ATC proposes to define “[ATC] Large Load,” in part, as “loads greater than or equal to 100 MW.”[fn:3]  However, in its answer, ATC states that it agrees to modify Attachment FF-ATCLLC and its Business Practices to eliminate any perceived operational gaps between large load thresholds.[fn:4]  Specifically, ATC states that it agrees to change the ATC Large Load definition threshold to 50 MW for certain operational processes, including:  (1) requiring loads that are greater than 50 MW that are connecting to ATC’s system to “comply with MISO’s [Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads proposal] requirements when they become effective if ATC does not have applicable criteria on a given topic”;[fn:5] (2) ensuring that ATC’s large load planning and interconnection criteria will apply to all ATC Large Loads over 50 MW; and (3) distinguishing between computational loads and other large loads for power factor and other requirements as deemed necessary by ATC for all ATC Large Loads over 50 MW.[fn:6]

Please explain whether and how ATC will consider aggregations of load under its proposed ATC Large Load definition.  

Please clarify whether the proposed ATC Large Load definition includes load used exclusively for charging an electric storage resource, when those loads may be excluded from MISO’s large load definition and therefore may be studied as a large load only in ATC’s planning process.[fn:7]

If the Commission were to accept MISO’s Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads proposal submitted in Docket No. ER26-3650, and ATC has applicable reliability criteria for an ATC Large Load, please explain whether the Distribution Customer[fn:8] serving the large load and acting as the Eligible Customer[fn:9] taking transmission service on behalf of the large load would be required to comply with ATC’s reliability criteria, MISO’s reliability requirements, or both.  In particular, if one of ATC’s applicable criteria is different than MISO’s requirements for the same attribute, please explain which criteria/requirements the Distribution Customer (acting as the Eligible Customer) taking transmission service on behalf of a large load would be expected to follow and how such requirements would be made clear to the Distribution Customer (acting as the Eligible Customer) taking transmission service on behalf of a large load.

ATC explains that its local planning process in Attachment FF-ATCLLC for Distribution-Transmission Interconnections applies to all ATC customers (i.e. Eligible Customers) seeking transmission service on ATC’s transmission system.[fn:10]  ATC proposes to revise the definition of “Distribution-Transmission Interconnection Agreement” (D-TIA) in section IV (Definitions) of Attachment FF-ATCLLC, which is an agreement that sets forth the terms and conditions applicable to the interconnection of one or more distribution systems to the transmission facilities of ATC, to state that a form of the D-TIA is publicly posted on ATC’s website.[fn:11]  In other words, the D-TIA applies to interconnection requests to modify or change an existing Distribution Interconnection[fn:12] or to interconnect proposed new distribution facilities at one or more locations (i.e., a new Distribution Interconnection).  ATC asserts that, in ATC’s footprint, MISO’s definition of Eligible Customer includes ATC’s Distribution Customers with which ATC has a D-TIA.[fn:13]  ATC asserts that the D-TIA serves the same function as a pro forma transmission service agreement and explains that any new Distribution Customer is required to enter into a D-TIA prior to interconnecting to ATC’s transmission system.[fn:14]  Please clarify whether the provisions in a D-TIA between ATC and a Distribution Customer taking transmission service on behalf of ATC Large Load will memorialize terms for ongoing operational requirements (e.g., equipment requirements, data requirements, remote disconnect capability) in the D-TIA.[fn:15]  

ATC states in its transmittal that any new distribution interconnection request that exceeds the large load threshold shall be required to pay an initial non-refundable deposit, in the amount of $50,000, which will be established by the applicable ATC Business Practice.[fn:16]  However, ATC’s proposed Tariff revisions do not specify that the study request charge is non-refundable.  Proposed section VI.A.5 (ATCLLC Large Load Distribution-Transmission Interconnection Study Request Charge) of Attachment FF-ATCLLC states that a Distribution Customer requesting to interconnect an ATC Large Load shall pay an initial deposit in an amount set forth in the applicable ATC Business Practice and “shall be invoiced by [ATC] for costs of time and materials necessary to complete the System Impact Study that exceeds the initial deposit.”[fn:17]  

Please explain how the study request charge’s initial deposit amount of $50,000 was determined.

Please clarify whether the study request charge is non-refundable and where such information is located in the Tariff.  If it is, please explain where any excess initial deposit funds will be applied in the event that the system impact study costs less than the study request charge and which provisions in the Tariff authorize the disbursement of those funds.

In proposed section VI.A.4 (Readiness Requirements) of Attachment FF-ATCLLC, ATC proposes to establish readiness requirements that the Distribution Customer must meet prior to ATC’s initiation of a system impact study, which will be set forth in the applicable ATC Business Practice.[fn:18]  ATC also proposes Tariff revisions to require Distribution Customers to submit a Load Interconnection Request Form (LIRF), as prescribed in the Load Interconnection Business Practice.  In addition to the LIRF, ATC proposes that a Distribution Customer requesting to interconnect an ATC Large Load must also provide a completed Large Load Interconnection Criteria Confirmation Form “and/or any other documentation necessary prior to [ATC’s] initiation of phase 1 of the System Impact Study.”[fn:19]  Please explain how ATC will determine if any additional documentation is necessary for ATC Large Loads and when and how Distribution Customers will be informed of the need for additional documentation.

ATC states that it has created a Large Load Cost Allocation Business Practice and developed two new agreements for Distribution Customers, a Large Load Project Commitment Agreement (LLPCA) and a Minimum Transmission Charge Agreement (MTCA), to provide cost shifting protection in ATC’s footprint.[fn:20]  ATC states that these agreements are required if a new load exceeds 100 MW or if it requires more than $100 million in distribution interconnection projects to enable its interconnection.  ATC also proposes that templates of the LLPCA and MTCA will be posted on ATC’s public website and, when executed, filed for acceptance with the Commission.[fn:21]

In its answer, ATC states that it will use a 100 MW threshold for cost shifting protections, such that all Distribution Customers seeking transmission service on behalf of large loads at or above 100 MW will be subject to the LLPCA and MTCA requirements.[fn:22]  ATC asserts that it should not require Distribution Customers serving ATC Large Loads that are greater than 50 MW but less than 100 MW to execute LLPCAs and MTCAs because the Distribution Customers will be unable, at the retail level, to directly assign the ATC Large Loads their upgrade costs given the Public Service Commission of Wisconsin’s established threshold of 100 MW or greater for large load retail tariffs in ATC’s footprint,[fn:23] thus spreading any upgrade costs to the Distribution Customer’s other retail customers.[fn:24]  ATC also asserts that templates of the LLPCA and MTCA achieve comparable transparency and consistency to a pro forma agreement “without foreclosing project-specific adjustments that may be needed given the individualized nature of large load projects.”[fn:25] 

Please explain how costs will be allocated among ATC’s customers for upgrades necessary to enable Distribution Customers to interconnect ATC Large Loads that are greater than 50 MW but less than 100 MW to ATC’s transmission system if the Distribution Customer (acting as the Eligible Customer) serving the large load takes less transmission service than requested.  

Please clarify whether any of those costs may be allocated to ATC’s customers that are not the Distribution Customer seeking interconnection to serve an ATC Large Load greater than 50 MW but less than 100 MW if the Distribution Customer (acting as the Eligible Customer) serving the large load takes less transmission service than requested.

Please explain how ATC will document with sufficient clarity and consistency any “project-specific adjustments” in the LLPCAs and MTCAs.

ATC proposes to add a new section V.E (Planning Criteria and Business Practices) to Attachment FF-ATCLLC that outlines a list of “performance criteria” to be included in ATC’s local transmission planning criteria.[fn:26]  

In section V.F (Controlling Planning Criteria; Modifications to Planning Criteria), ATC proposes Tariff revisions to state that the ATC local transmission planning criteria and requirements established in ATC’s Business Practices “shall govern in [ATC’s] footprint” and define system performance requirements.[fn:27]  ATC’s proposed Tariff revisions also provide that ATC’s local transmission planning criteria and requirements established by ATC’s Business Practices may be “revised from time to time in response to changes in industry standards, new system conditions, new technologies and new operating procedures, as appropriate and subject to change at any time at ATC’s discretion.”[fn:28]

Please explain how ATC’s proposed local transmission planning criteria, studies, assessments, and requirements will govern in the event that those local transmission planning criteria, studies, assessments, and requirements conflict with those in the MISO Tariff.  

Please explain whether, and if so, how, ATC will inform Distribution Customers and MISO if changes to the Business Practices create diverging transmission planning criteria, studies, assessments, and requirements between ATC and MISO.  Please also explain what standardized process ATC will follow to reconcile these differences in a way that maintains system reliability.

ATC proposes a new section VI.A.3 (System Impact Studies) to require steady state analysis and “applicable special studies” (i.e., the special studies phase or phase 2), as needed.[fn:29]  ATC states that projects that exceed ATC’s Large Load threshold or display unique characteristics will require additional studies during the special studies phase, after completing the steady state analysis required in phase 1.[fn:30]  ATC states that the special studies phase includes, but is not limited to, analysis of transfer, reactive power, angular and voltage stability of nearby generators, load voltage stability, voltage ride through of both generators and loads, power quality, short-circuit, and other transient study analyses that may be required to define reliability metrics for ATC Large Loads.[fn:31]  

Please clarify the process for determining which, if any, special studies in phase 2 will be required.

Please explain the timeline for ATC to (1) inform Distribution Customers of their additional requirements to complete the special studies and (2) complete the special studies.

This letter is issued pursuant to 18 C.F.R. § 375.307 (2025) and is interlocutory.  This letter is not subject to rehearing pursuant to 18 C.F.R. § 385.713 (2025).  A response to this letter must be filed with the Secretary of the Commission within 60 days of the date of this letter by making a deficiency filing in accordance with the Commission’s electronic tariff requirements.  For your response, please use Type of Filing Code 180, Deficiency Filing.[fn:32]  The information requested in this letter will constitute an amendment to your filing, and a new filing date will be established.[fn:33]  A notice will be issued upon receipt of your filing.

Failure to respond to this letter within the time period specified may result in a further order rejecting your filing.

Issued by:  Natalie Y. Tingle-Stewart, Director, Division of Electric Power Regulation Central
