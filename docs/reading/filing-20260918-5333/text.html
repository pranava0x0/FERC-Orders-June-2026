				PROTEST
				
					The Iowa OCA, IL OAG, IL CUB, Michigan Attorney General, MI CUB, Commerce, MN CUB, WI CUB, Citizens Action Coalition of Indiana, Inc., and Alliance for Affordable Energy (“Joint Protestors”) submit this Protest regarding the proposed tariff revisions filed by MISO regarding Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads with the Commission 


				in the above-captioned action.[fn:1]  
					On August 28, 2026, MISO filed proposed revisions to its Open Access Transmission, Energy, and Operating Reserves Markets Tariff (“Tariff”) to establish certain Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads.  As noted in its transmittal letter, MISO’s filing purports to respond to several concerns raised by the Commission in its Show Cause Order in Docket No. EL26-70-000.  Specifically, MISO proposes visibility, ramp rate, and disturbance ride-through requirements for Large Loads, as well as grandfathering transition provisions designed to minimize disruption to existing commercial arrangements.  As demonstrated below, MISO’s proposed Tariff grandfathering transition provisions under Section 37A, discussed at pages 13 and 14 of the transmittal letter do not address the cost-shifting concerns expressed by the Commission in the Show Cause Order.  
	In the Show Cause Order, the Commission found MISO’s Tariff “appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among transmission customers, which may result in unjust and unreasonable rates for transmission service.”  Show Cause Order at P 66.  In the ruling, the Commission expressed concern about the lack of transparency regarding the assignment of costs, including Network Upgrades, necessary to provide transmission service to Eligible Customers or Large Load Customers on behalf of Large Loads to ensure Eligible Customers bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide transmission service, including the cost of Network Upgrades.  Id. at PP 70-77.  
MISO is proposing to fully exempt or “grandfather” any Large Load with a service commencement date on or prior to December 4, 2026, from all requirements of proposed Section 37A.  MISO is also proposing to partially exempt a Large Load or portion of a Large Load, that does not have a Service Commencement Date prior to December 4, 2026, but that meets the MTEP/EPR Condition from the ramp and ride-through requirements set forth in Sections 37A.6 through 37A.7.  The proposal fully or partially exempts approximately 30 gigawatts of existing and in-flight capacity based on an arbitrary date.[fn:2]  The Joint Protestors are concerned the proposed grandfathering terms would exempt a large portion of the new large data center loads in Iowa and potentially other MISO regions from the reliability protections proposed in MISO’s filing.  Additionally, if this exemption is carried forward to the series of future Federal Power Act section 205 filings that MISO intends to make in response to the Show Cause Order, this could deprive electric consumers of the cost shifting and other consumer protections that MISO has been directed to address in the Show Cause Order.  
Large Loads have reliability impacts regardless of operational date.  Under proposed Section 37A, MISO cannot mandate a grandfathered facility install facility-side hardware protections to prevent severe spikes and triggering system protections from cascading through the grid, shifting the burden to the bulk electric system.  Under this proposal, local electric utilities will be forced to remedy the problem by building expensive, large-scale system and network reliability upgrades to cushion the grid against sudden destabilizing effects.  The cost of these system and network reliability upgrades will be passed onto other customers and will not be assigned to the Large Load, the cost-causer.  This will force captive ratepayers to subsidize the integration of Large Loads, violating the core principles of cost causation.  
If the Commission allows MISO to fully or partially exempt or “grandfather” any Large Load from all requirements of the proposed Section 37A, the costs associated with local reliability projects will not be assigned to the Large Load customer, the cost-causer.  Local reliability costs caused or materially accelerated by a Large Load should follow the cost-causer, the Large Load customer.  Broader allocation of costs to other customers should occur only if the other customers receive commensurate benefits.  Moreover, Large Load customers, and not other customers, should bear the risk if the load does not materialize as planned.  MISO’s Show Cause proposals should apply to any incremental large new load, as defined in this filing, that comes online after December 4, 2026.  
WHEREFORE, the Iowa OCA, IL OAG, IL CUB, Michigan Attorney General, MI CUB, Commerce, MN CUB, WI CUB, Citizens Action Coalition of Indiana, Inc., and Alliance for Affordable Energy pray the Commission reject MISO’s proposed Tariff grandfathering transition provisions under Section 37A, to ensure Large Load customers bear the cost and risk of all costs caused by the Large Load customer, and for any additional relief Commission deems appropriate under the circumstances.
