III. PG&E’s Existing Wholesale Load Interconnection study process 9

Q.
What is the purpose of PG&E’s existing Wholesale Load study process? 10

A. When a new Wholesale Load customer seeks to interconnect to PG&E’s 11

transmission system or an existing Wholesale Load customer seeks to modify 12

or expand an existing Wholesale Load Interconnection, PG&E must study the 13

request to determine the impacts on its transmission system.  Specifically, a 14

interconnecting the Load and identifies any upgrades that will be required to 18

accommodate the Load on the transmission system.

Q.
What is an Electrically Independent Large Load?
1
A. As indicated in the proposed TO Tariff definition, an Electrically Independent 2

Large Load is one where its interconnection impacts are not materially affected 3

by one or more other projects.  In these instances, the interconnecting Large 4

Load End User and corresponding upgrade facilities would not impact other 5

Load interconnections.  For example, additional Load in an unconstrained 6

portion of the PG&E system (e.g., which does not depend on pre-identified 7

transmission upgrades to meet the aggregate Load), and where the incremental 8

Load does not affect other planned projects or would not be expected to impact 9

other interconnections would be considered electrically independent.  As a 10

result, PG&E can study this Large Load End User without needing to assume 11

the status, timing, or configuration of other queued projects. 12

aggregate power flow tests, which look at whether the request, on its own and 18

A. Cluster Study Process
5
Q.
Why does the Serial Study Process not work for all TCLL Interconnections? 6

A. It has been my experience that a request to interconnect a significant amount of 7

load, such as a request associated with a Large Load End User, can have 8

electrical impacts on other customers.  The ability of PG&E to interconnect a 9

Large Load may be dependent on other requests from Large Load End Users 10

on PG&E’s transmission system that are being analyzed at the same time.  As a 11

result, if PG&E were to study such Electrically Dependent Large Loads 12

sequentially, the Company would have to complete each study, and the 13

applicant would need to decide on whether to proceed, before finalizing the 14

evaluation of additional interconnection requests.  This would be time 15

consuming and could result in a number of restudies as applicants drop out of 16

the interconnection queue or change their Load configurations based on study 17

results.  PG&E already studies all retail service requests for large customers 18

(those > 20 MW) using a cluster study process because it is more cost effective 19

and efficient for the customers.  PG&E proposes to use this cluster study 20

process as the basis for the proposed Cluster Study for TCLLs (who will now 21

seek interconnection service on behalf of Large Load End Users).  PG&E will 1

continue to use the retail cluster study process for those larger customers (e.g., 2

20 MW to 49.99 MW loads) that are not Large Load End Users.
3
Q.
When does the Cluster Study Process begin, and which applications are 4

included?
5
A. PG&E will run one Cluster Study each year.  Each year has a closing date of 6

the last day of February, by which an application must be complete and found 7

to be Electrically Dependent, or involve a Co-Located Large Load End User, to 8

be included in that year’s cluster.  The Cluster Study Process will begin on the 9

first day of April, or the next Business Day, each year.  A fixed annual 10

schedule allows us to study all of the Electrically Dependent Large Load 11

requests together using the same assumptions and gives TCLLs and their Large 12

Load End Users certainty about when their requests will be studied.  Because 13

the new process will only just be taking effect, the TO Tariff also recognizes 14

that if no Completed TCLL Interconnection Applications have been found to 15

be electrically dependent by the last day of February in 2027, there will be no 16

Cluster Study Process beginning on April 1, 2027.  Shortly after the closing 17

date, each TCLL signs a Cluster Study Process Agreement.  Where it makes 18

sense, PG&E may also divide the Cluster into smaller study groups of requests 19

that electrically affect one another, so that each request is studied together with 20

The timing matters.  An electromagnetic transient study is only as good as the 1

models and assumptions that go into it, including the specific equipment, 2

inverters, and control settings the Large Load End User will install and the 3

condition of the surrounding system when the load comes online.  Early in the 4

interconnection process, much of that information is not yet final, so a study 5

performed at that stage would rest on assumptions that are likely to change and 6

would produce results that are not reliable.  The CAISO faces a similar issue 7

on the generation side, where detailed transient analysis is generally performed 8

later, once equipment and controls are known.  By performing the study about 9

a year ahead of energization, PG&E can use actual equipment data and current 10

system conditions, which allows the study to identify issues on the system 11

more accurately.  A year also leaves enough time to act on the results, such as 12

adjusting control settings or adding protective equipment, before the load is 13

energized.  In short, performing the study at that point makes it a realistic and 14

actionable study rather than a theoretical one.
