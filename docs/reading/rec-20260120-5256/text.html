ANSWER OF CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC 
IN OPPOSITION TO THE INDICATED PJM TRANSMISSION OWNERS’ 
MOTION FOR EXTENSION OF TIME 
Constellation respectfully opposes1 the Indicated Transmission Owners’ motion to delay 
compliance2 with the Commission’s order establishing rules to promptly interconnect large loads 
in the PJM region.3 The TOs seek a thirty (30) day extension of time for submission of 
(i) compliance filings due 60 days from the date of the order; and (ii) initial and reply briefs in the 
paper hearing that the Commission established in the December Order.4 The Commission should 
deny the TO’s Motion for three reasons.

First, the Commission’s 60-day deadline was appropriate in the circumstances given the 
national imperative to facilitate the prompt interconnection of large loads and the long delays in 
doing so in PJM. The Commission’s timeline is consistent with the Secretary of Energy’s proposed 
ANOPR and the Secretary’s statement that the interconnection of large loads to the transmission 
system—the subject of both the ANOPR and this show cause proceeding—“is an urgent issue that 
requires prompt attention.”5 The Commission’s compliance deadlines require timely action to 
break the logjam on serving these critical large loads. 
Second, the TO’s interest in delaying compliance is troubling in light of positions 
Commonwealth Edison Company (“ComEd”)6 has taken in Illinois where ComEd asserts that its 
rates need not reflect the new Commission-mandated transmission services because these are not 
yet final and unappealable.7 ComEd’s approach would simply ignore the current circumstance, 
which is that the Commission has found the existing rate structure where there is only a single, 
gross wholesale transmission rate to be unjust and unreasonable and directed replacement rates. 
Among other things, the replacement rates include a new class of interruptible or non-firm 
wholesale transmission service which, in turn, could reduce collateral and deposit requirements to 
take service from ComEd. The TOs’ attempts to delay compliance in the Commission’s docket

thus are consistent with their efforts to avoid updating their rate structures until there is a final 
Commission order. Such delays are contrary to the December Order8 and the Federal Power Act.9   
Finally, prompt compliance in this proceeding in PJM is also critical because the record 
reflects that Transmission Owners have long benefited by delaying existing large load co-location 
requests unless customers agree in advance to take both transmission and distribution service from 
them.10 The TO’s Motion now seeks further delay.   
For all the above reasons, Constellation opposes the TO’s Motion. The Commission should 
deny the motion and continue with the deadlines as established in the December Order.
