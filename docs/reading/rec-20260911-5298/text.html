Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal 
Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or the “Commission”)1, CyrusOne, LP (“CyrusOne”) 
respectfully submits this motion for leave to file its comments out-of-time (the “Motion”), 
together with its accompanying comments, on the filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”)

August 13, 2026 (the “IRAS Filing”). As grounds for the Motion, and in support of the 
Comments, CyrusOne states as follows. 
I. MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE COMMENTS OUT-OF-TIME 
Rule 214 authorizes the Commission to grant a late-filed motion to intervene, and the 
Commission has broad discretion under Rule 101(e) to waive or extend its procedural rules for 
good cause where doing so serves the public interest and does not prejudice other parties.2 The 
Commission routinely accepts late-filed comments and protests in section 205 proceedings 
where acceptance would not disrupt the proceeding, would not prejudice other parties, and would 
assist the Commission in developing a complete and well-informed record.3

II. BACKGROUND 
CyrusOne has been developing data centers for over 25 years as a global developer, 
owner, and operator, including several multi-year, multi-phase campuses that may be subject to 
PJM’s Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) and IRAS proposals.  CyrusOne is a leading 
global data center developer and operator, providing mission-critical infrastructure for cloud 
providers, enterprises, and hyperscale customers. CyrusOne’s leading global platform of hybrid-
cloud and multi-cloud deployments offers customers colocation, hyperscale and build-to-suit 
environments, which help enhance the strategic connections of their essential data infrastructure

CyrusOne frequently develops multiple, separately contracted facilities in phases over a

CyrusOne would be a “New Large Load” within the meaning of the IRAS Filing with respect to

several states within PJM. Our existing sites should not be seen or classified as “new” and have 
undergone years of studies, development, long lead equipment purchases, and include tenant 
contracts for capacity for years extending beyond 2026. Implementation of IRAS poses 
uncertainty and risk to projects that have been under development for several years. 
I. 
COMMENTS OF CYRUSONE 
CyrusOne submits these Comments to identify and recommend specific, targeted changes 
to the IRAS Filing’s proposed tariff language that, as written, creates unnecessary uncertainty for 
New Large Loads seeking to procure new capacity and comply with the proposed framework in 
good faith. For example, while CyrusOne often develops our sites to include backup emergency 
generation, the intention is to use that resource for testing and emergency purposes only. It is 
CyrusOne’s position that these resources should be utilized in a manner consistent with state and

federal laws. If the Commission accepts the IRAS Filing, CyrusOne respectfully requests that the 
Commission direct PJM to clarify or modify the provisions identified below. 
A. 
The Definition of “Large Load” Should be Revised to Eliminate the One Mile 
Radius Requirement, or in the Alternative be Revised to Make a Rebuttable 
Presumption that Projects within a One-Mile Radius are Independent. 
 
The IRAS Filing defines “Large Load” to mean end-use customer load with a cumulative 
peak load of 50 MW or greater at a single electrical site, and provides that, for configurations 
involving multiple affiliated load facilities or points of interconnection, all affiliated load 
facilities within a one-mile radius will be considered a single electrical site.4 PJM states that this 
one-mile radius rule is adapted from the Commission’s Qualifying Facility siting rules under 
Order No. 872.5 
CyrusOne is concerned that this rule, as proposed, does not account for the way data 
center campuses are actually developed and financed. CyrusOne, like other data center 
developers, frequently constructs multiple, separately contracted facilities within a one-mile 
radius of one another and of existing operating facilities, on different timelines, for different 
customers, and under separate service and financing arrangements. Aggregating such facilities 
into a single electrical site based on proximity alone, without regard to whether they are 
commercially, contractually, or electrically independent, risks two specific consequences that 
CyrusOne believes the Commission did not intend. First, it could potentially cause an existing, 
already-operational facility to become newly subject to IRAS curtailment risk solely because a 
later, unrelated facility happens to be developed nearby, even though the existing facility’s 
capacity needs were accounted for and arranged years earlier. Second, it could cause multiple

CyrusOne agrees that the one-mile aggregation rule should be eliminated. With respect to 
an alternative to elimination, CyrusOne believes that the presumption should be that the facilities

Load definition. Placing the burden on PJM, rather than on the affected customer, is the more 
just and reasonable allocation because PJM, not the customer, is the party proposing to depart 
from the ordinary rule that each electrical site is evaluated on its own. The one-mile radius rule 
was not developed through the stakeholder process but was instead imported from the

Requiring PJM to identify and prove those same facts as a threshold matter, before 
aggregating facilities that would otherwise be treated as independent, ensures the rule is applied 
only where PJM can affirmatively demonstrate that the facilities function as a single integrated 
site, rather than sweeping in longstanding, independently developed campuses by default.  Such 
a demonstration could include, for example, confirming the presence of a contract or utility 
agreement showing that the facilities share a common point of interconnection, common 
ownership or control, or another arrangement establishing that the facilities are electrically or 
contractually connected, rather than merely located near one another. For these reasons, 
CyrusOne’s preferred outcome is elimination of the one-mile aggregation rule altogether. Should 
the Commission nonetheless decline to eliminate the rule, CyrusOne requests that the 
Commission direct PJM to adopt a rebuttable presumption that affiliated facilities within a one-
mile radius are separate electrical sites, with PJM bearing the burden of demonstrating that 
specific facilities are sufficiently integrated to warrant aggregation. 
B. 
IRAS Should Not Apply to Incremental Load Added Before June 1, 2027 or Load 
Served Under Pre-Existing Binding Retail Service Agreements. 
The IRAS Filing defines “New Large Load” to include, among other things, incremental 
load in service after June 1, 2027 that increases existing end-use customer load at a site that did 
not previously meet the Large Load definition.8 PJM’s transmittal letter indicates that the Large

Load Registry is intended to track the in-service date of each increment of load, together with a 
facility’s peak load and ramp schedule, in order to determine which increments are properly 
treated as New Large Load. CyrusOne notes that this June 1, 2027 cutoff does not operate in 
isolation from one-mile radius aggregation rule discussed above.  Because the one-mile 
aggregation rule can cause otherwise independent, previously developed facilities to be 
combined into a single electrical site, that aggregation can in turn cause a facility to newly cross 
the Large Load threshold and subject unrelated, previously arranged load to New Large Load 
treatment based on its in-service date. These two provisions are therefore problematic in 
combination, and CyrusOne requests that the Commission consider and address their interaction 
together, not solely as separate issues.9 
The proposed tariff language does not clearly implement PJM’s stated intent for the 
Large Load Registry to track in-service dates and ramp schedules to determine which increments 
are New Large Load. The New Large Load definition appears to capture only the specific 
increment that causes a previously smaller site to first cross the 50 megawatt Large Load 
threshold.  It does not clearly address subsequent increments at a site that already qualifies as a 
Large Load.  It also does not state whether the resulting New Large Load designation applies 
only to the qualifying increment or to the entire registered peak load of the site.10 This ambiguity 
is directly relevant to CyrusOne’s facilities, many of which will be coming into service over a 
multi-year ramp that will continue after June 1, 2027, and are or will be energized in phases. To 
the extent the Commission determines that multi-year, multi-phase existing projects such as 
CyrusOne's should nonetheless be included within the New Large Load definition, CyrusOne

exposure, which undermines the very objective the IRAS Filing is intended to serve. 
C. 
PJM Should Further Explain the Basis for Excluding New Large Loads from 
the VRR Curve Beginning with the 2029/2030 Delivery Year. 
The IRAS Filing proposes, beginning with the 2029/2030 Delivery Year, to exclude New 
Large Loads that lack sufficient BYONC or Allocated RBP Unforced Capacity (“UCAP”) 
megawatts from the Variable Resource Requirement (“VRR”) Curve used in the Reliability 
Pricing Model auctions, in order to protect existing customers from capacity price increases 
attributable to those loads.12 CyrusOne understands and does not dispute PJM’s stated ratepayer-
protection rationale for this exclusion. CyrusOne notes, however, that other commenters have

II. 
CONCLUSION 
For the foregoing reasons, CyrusOne respectfully requests that the Commission (1) grant 
the Motion and accept these Comments for consideration in this proceeding, and (2) if the 
Commission accepts the IRAS Filing, direct PJM to: (a) adopt a rebuttable presumption for the 
one-mile aggregation rule as applied to independently developed facilities, with PJM bearing the 
burden of demonstrating sufficient affiliation to trigger the New Large Load designation; (b) 
clarify the treatment of incremental load and ramp schedules under the New Large Load 
definition, exclude load under pre-existing binding retail service agreements, and address the 
compounding interaction between the one-mile aggregation rule and the June 1, 2027 cutoff; (c) 
provide a reasonable transition mechanism for multi-year, multi-phase existing projects if such 
projects are included within the New Large Load definition; and (d) further explain the basis for, 
and intended interaction of, the proposed VRR Curve exclusion with incentives for new 
generation investment.
