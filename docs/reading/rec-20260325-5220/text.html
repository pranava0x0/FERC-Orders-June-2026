I. RESPONSE
A. Introduction and Background

Co-location in PJM presents contentious issues. An early proposal, based on PJM’s since- rescinded Guidance on Co-Located Load (“Guidance Document”),4 was filed in Docket No. ER24-2172 as an amended Interconnection Service Agreement (“ISA”) between Susquehanna Nuclear LLC, and PPL Electric Utilities Corporation.5 The Commission rejected the Amended ISA because PJM had not demonstrated a need to deviate from the pro forma ISA.6 Constellation Energy Generation, LLC (“Constellation”) subsequently filed a complaint to amend PJM’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to incorporate the co-location arrangements found in the Guidance Document.7

NOVEC is a not-for-profit electric distribution cooperative owned by its members. NOVEC has significant experience with serving data centers over the past 25 years. NOVEC is one of the country’s largest data center hubs and serves over 50 data center buildings.8 Based on its experience, NOVEC opposed Constellation’s complaint in substantial part because Constellation proposed to relieve the Co-Located Load of any responsibility for transmission costs, even as that load relied on the transmission system.9 NOVEC is very aware of the potential effects on cost allocation that can arise because of the size of data centers and does not agree that data centers or other large loads should be able to use the grid without contributing to its costs.

The Commission instituted a section 206 show cause proceeding, consolidated the Constellation complaint with the new show cause proceeding, and directed interested parties to brief a series of questions regarding whether the PJM Tariff needed to be modified to accommodate co-located load and, if so, how.10

The Commission thereafter issued the Co-Location Order on December 18, 2025. The Co-Location Order found that PJM’s Tariff is not just and reasonable because it does not “contain provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to generators serving Co-Located Load and Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-Located Load.”11 But the Commission included critical protections for existing transmission customers not present in other previous iterations of co-location service, including requiring prospective Co-Located Loads to fully fund any network upgrades before they could take service from an existing generator.12

In order to facilitate the service of Co-Located Load, the Commission described three new types of transmission service for Eligible Customers serving Co-Located Load. The Commission found that additional record evidence was needed to set the appropriate replacement rates, terms, and conditions of those new types of transmission service, so it set a paper hearing on eleven questions involving the rates, terms, and conditions of service for Firm Contract Demand transmission service, Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service, and Interim Network Integration Transmission Service (“NITS”).13 PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners filed initial briefs in response to those questions.14

The proposals set forth in the initial briefs strike an appropriate balance between recognizing the reduced usage of the transmission system for Co-Located Load while also ensuring that such load does not become a “free rider” to the extent that such load does rely on and use the transmission system, but does not pay for it. Several important principles should guide the Commission’s determination of what constitute just and reasonable rates, terms, and conditions for these new services being mandated to accommodate Co-Located Load. First, the proposed transmission rates cannot be just and reasonable unless they recover from the Co- Located Load the full costs associated with the Co-Located Load’s use of and reliance on the transmission system and do not cause or result in subsidization by existing transmission customers.

Second, meaningful financial and operational penalties are necessary. Such penalties must discourage gamesmanship, must be enforceable, and should include guard-rails such as the anti-toggling measures proposed by PJM. But penalties should also be tailored to ensure that they are not written too broadly. As more fully discussed in Section C.4 of these comments, infra at 14-15, the Commission should direct PJM to modify the penalty provisions to clarify that, in the event a single Eligible Customer is serving multiple Co-Located Loads, that the disqualification penalty would only apply to service at the Co-Located Load that disregarded curtailment instructions or whose transmission protection measures misoperated twice. Finally, workable and reliable protections for the transmission system are critical. Although neither PJM nor the PJM Transmission Owners provided specific criteria yet for the “special protection schemes” and other necessary controls, the principles they outlined are sound; the Commission should adopt them.

Notwithstanding the progress that PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners have made in their proposals, NOVEC respectfully urges the Commission to remain flexible as PJM, the PJM Transmission Owners, and other Market Participants gain practical experience serving Co- Located Load. These are entirely new services and may require additional modifications as experience is gained or as unexpected circumstances arise. As this proceeding advances and additional details on the proposals become clear, NOVEC reserves the right to oppose the Co- Location service rates, terms, and conditions if those proposals do not adequately shield existing transmission customers from subsidizing large-load customers or if they endanger the reliability of the transmission system.

B. PJM’s and the PJM Transmission Owners’ proposals to use the NITS rate for the new Firm Contract Demand transmission service, Non-Firm Contract

Demand transmission service, and Interim NITS, impose PJM administrative
and ancillary service charges, and institute a Grid Reliance Charge are just and reasonable, and the Commission should adopt their proposals.

Above all else, the rates for the new transmission services for the Co-Located Load must not result in existing transmission customers subsidizing large-load customers. NOVEC supports the Commission’s acceptance of the proposals of PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners with respect to rates for Contract Demand transmission service and Interim NITS. NOVEC is commenting specifically to highlight how critical it is that the proposed rates appropriately reflect the use of the transmission system by these new services and protect existing transmission customers from subsidizing Eligible Customers providing transmission service to Co-Located Load.

1. Using the NITS rate for Firm Contract Demand transmission service and
Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service is appropriate.

The NITS rate is appropriate in light of the characteristics of Firm Contract Demand transmission service. Unlike point-to-point service, Contract Demand transmission service is not

limited to one source, one sink and one path; an Eligible Customer can draw from the transmission system in a manner comparable to that of a NITS customer. Firm Contract Demand transmission service is NITS-equivalent, just scaled down to be available for less than the Co- Located Customers’ total load. Network upgrades still must be planned and constructed as necessary, with the only difference being that Firm Contract Demand transmission service’s network upgrades are planned based on contract demand requests instead of anticipated load growth.15 Because the service otherwise is fundamentally the same, assessing the same transmission rates as that of NITS makes sense. Because Eligible Customers are receiving a service commensurate with NITS, they should pay a cost commensurate with NITS in recognition of the Commission’s cost-causation principles.

The proposal to charge Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service at the NITS rate net of the Grid Reliance Charge multiplied by their reserved amount of demand also seems reasonable. Netting the Grid Reliance Charge prevents double recovery. Like Firm Contract Demand transmission service, Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service bears a resemblance to NITS service. Although non-firm, it is a network-type service. In Order No. 888, the Commission capped non-firm rates at the firm rate.16 Because an Eligible Customer need only purchase Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service when reserved, transmission costs for an Eligible Customer are lower than firm rates. Further, as the PJM Transmission Owners point out, it has a superior priority to non-firm point-to-point transmission service.17 And because these revenues are credited to the applicable transmission owner’s annual transmission

2. PJM’s administrative charges are appropriate.

The Commission asked whether it would be just and reasonable to include charges for PJM’s administrative services.18 NOVEC agrees with PJM’s proposal to assess administrative service charges on Co-Located Load.19 These charges are associated with the use of the transmission system beyond transmission service rates.20 Any entity that uses the transmission system should contribute to administrative charges associated with the transmission system. The Commission found that Co-Located Load would use the transmission system even if it did not draw power from the grid.21 It is thus entirely appropriate for PJM to assess administrative charges on Co-Located Load. Co-Located Load must bear its fair share of administrative costs.

The Commission should also accept PJM’s proposal to recover Black Start Service charges and Regulation charges based on the gross demand of the Co-Located Load for Firm Contract Demand transmission service and for Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service. The proposal is consistent with the Commission’s finding “that Eligible Customers taking one of the new transmission services on behalf of Co-Located Load must be assessed for regulation service on a gross demand basis at the existing Tariff rate, consistent with the Commission’s cost-causation principles.”22

3. The Commission should accept the Grid Reliance Charge as proposed.

In the Co-Location Order, the Commission asked whether the rate paid by an Eligible Customer on behalf of a Co-Located Load that does not reserve any amount of transmission service should include, besides Regulation and Black Start Services, “an additional charge given that regulation and black start services could not be provided without the transmission system.”23 The answer to that question is an unequivocal yes. The PJM Transmission Owners have proposed a Grid Reliance Charge based on the difference between an Eligible Customer’s firm contract demand (or for Non-Firm Contract Demand customers, the amount of reserved service) and their gross on-site load.24

As a threshold matter, Eligible Customers should pay a charge that reflects the need to deliver these services to their Co-Located Load. Because all other load takes transmission service up to their gross load, their transmission rates implicitly reflect such use of the transmission system. The rates of Black Start Service and Regulation do not include a delivery component. If a Co-Located Load reserved no Firm or Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service, its payment for those two ancillary services would not include any costs of the transmission system that are necessary to deliver those services to the Co-Located Load. Without such a charge, the Co-Located Load would be a free rider on the transmission system, benefiting from the payments of existing transmission customers to maintain the transmission system without making any contribution towards it.25

In short, a Co-Located Load’s net withdrawals do not fully reflect its actual reliance on the grid.”).

So, the question becomes whether the PJM Transmission Owners’ proposed Grid Reliance Charge is just and reasonable. There are many possible ways to calculate a Grid Reliance Charge. The approach has a reasonable foundation, and the Commission should accept it because the Grid Reliance Charge as proposed is just and reasonable, with an important distinction: it is limited to depreciation and O&M, conservatively excluding a return on rate base and associated income taxes. The PJM Transmission Owners only recover their costs and not a return. Because of the novelty of the Grid Reliance Charge, taking a conservative approach initially is justifiable. Until PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners gain more experience, the Grid Reliance Charge is transparent, simple, scales with each utility’s size and cost structure, and promotes rate stability.26 In the future, the PJM Transmission Owners should explore revising the methodology to better align the costs of providing access to necessary ancillary services with the benefits of those services to ensure that Co-Located Load makes an adequate contribution to those costs. NOVEC views the current proposal as a minimum rate. In addition, because revenues earned from the Grid Reliance Charge are returned to NITS and Firm Contract Demand transmission service customers as a revenue credit, the structure avoids any risk of double recovery. The Commission should accept it as filed by the PJM Transmission Owners. The Commission may wish to consider ordering PJM to hold a stakeholder process to determine whether the rate needs to be changed in the future.

3. Additional financial penalties are warranted.

NOVEC agrees with PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners that a penalty for
unreserved use in addition to disqualification and pause-in-service rules is appropriate.36 In the current environment, PJM is correct that “many, if not most, Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of a Co-Located Load could foreseeably elect to absorb financial penalties for unreserved use rather than follow Curtailment or Load Shed procedures or avoid misoperation of the protective technology or system.”37 Financial penalties alone would not be sufficient to ensure that Co-located Loads and co-located generating facilities comply, through the relevant Eligible Customers, with their obligations. But that is not sufficient reason to dispense with a financial penalty.

A complementary financial penalty should be an additional deterrent that would help make sure that “the market and other ratepayers are made whole if a Co-Located Load customer exceeds its contract demand.”38 At this time, neither PJM nor the PJM Transmission Owners have a specific concept for the Commission to consider, but have committed to working together to explore penalty options. NOVEC supports those efforts and supports the principle that a financial penalty should compensate other customers for any harm caused by the Eligible Customer’s departure from Co-Location requirements or, if there is no harm, that the financial penalty should be set at a level sufficient to encourage compliance with the PJM Tariff’s scheduling requirements.

4. PJM’s penalty language should be revised to account for conditions
where an Eligible Customer serves multiple Co-Located Loads.

At present, the Illustrative Tariff provides that, when one of the disqualifying events happens, the Eligible Customer “shall be disqualified from taking Firm Contract Demand Transmission Service and Non-Firm Contract Demand Transmission Service.”39 PJM has crafted this language with the assumption that either the generator serving the Co-Located Load or the Co-Located Load itself would be the Eligible Customer. But the Co-Location Order contemplates that “any electric utility” could be an Eligible Customer.40 That leaves open the possibility that the generator, the Co-Located Load, and the Eligible Customer could each be separate entities. Under those circumstances, the Eligible Customer could reasonably have to rely upon the Co-Located Load and the co-located generating facility for compliance with curtailment instructions from PJM and maintenance of equipment to implement the “special protection schemes.” A third-party Eligible Customer would have limited operational control over the site.

As drafted, the Illustrative Tariff could result in a third-party Eligible Customer being disqualified from serving Co-Located Load entirely because of the actions of the Co-Located Load or the co-located generating facility. This would unreasonably harm the Eligible Customer. If the Eligible Customer were a Load Serving Entity acting as an Eligible Customer for multiple Co-Located Loads, it would unreasonably harm those other Co-Located Loads as well. Accordingly, the Illustrative Tariff should be revised to specify that when a third-party Eligible Customer is not responsible for a disqualifying event, only the culpable Co-Located Load (and

D. PJM’s two-pronged approach to implementing an anti-toggling feature for Firm Contract Demand transmission service is reasonable and should be accepted.

The Commission sought comment on the appropriate mechanisms for an anti-toggling feature for Firm Contract Demand transmission service, including a minimum service period and minimum notice period.41 The Commission explained that without an anti-toggling feature, Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-Located Loads “may attempt to switch between the two new transmission services based on expected capacity market conditions.”42

PJM proposed a two-pronged approach. First, Section 709.4 of its Illustrative Tariff provides that the portion of Co-Located Load served by Firm Contract Demand transmission service is not eligible to be served by Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service for “five years following the effective date of the Service Agreement for Contract Demand Transmission Service.”43 There is an exception for physical changes to the Co-Located generating facility or the Co-Located Load that would materially alter the MW-level of power or MW-level of gross demand.44 The second anti-toggling feature is PJM’s proposed 42-month notice of termination.45

Both of these proposals are just and reasonable and should be accepted. PJM will have to procure capacity resources for Firm Contract Demand, and there may be network upgrades necessary to serve a request for Firm Contract Demand transmission service.46 Setting a minimum service commitment of five years ensures that timely price signals are sent to the

E. The Commission should ensure that any “special protection schemes” are robust enough to ensure system reliability.

The Commission asked what interconnection requirements and operational practices are necessary for “special protection schemes” to maintain system reliability.49 In response, PJM stated that “[t]he interconnection requirements and operational practices necessary for necessary control technologies and protection systems to maintain system reliability in the event that a Co- Located Load or associated generator trips offline will be set forth in the PJM Manuals and, as applicable, [North American Electric Reliability Corporation] reliability standards.”50 At a minimum, PJM’s proposal to incorporate North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) reliability standards is just and reasonable.

The Commission should ensure that there are high reliability standards so that no retail customers are harmed by the failure of “special protection schemes” of large-load customers.
