PROTEST AND COMMENTS OF CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC
Constellation1 protests limited provisions in PJM’s Sixty-Day Compliance Filing2 that are non-compliant with the Commission’s December 18, 2025 Order3 because they fail “to make the directed changes.”4 First, the replacement of “Point of Interconnection” in the definition of Co- Located Load with “Point of Change in Ownership” contradicts the Commission’s order. Second, the proposed Behind the Meter Generation (“BTMG”) rules expand BTMG to include a 50-MW

threshold and thus create a large class of favored resources at the expense of Co-Located Load. Constellation also seeks clarification of PJM’s filing in several other areas to ensure compliance, to accelerate the interconnection of large loads, and to prevent undue discrimination against Co- Located Load and the generators seeking to serve it.

PROTEST
In two important areas, PJM’s proposal is outside the scope of compliance, falls short of the Commission’s intent, and perpetuates existing undue discrimination that has delayed the interconnection of large loads for years.

I. The Commission Should Reject PJM’s New Definition of Co-Located Load as Outside
the Scope of Compliance, or in the Alternative, Require Further Modification
As with PJM’s Thirty-Day Compliance Filing,5 the Commission should reject PJM’s
proposal to use its Sixty-Day Compliance Filing to replace the Commission-mandated “Point of Interconnection” in the definition of Co-Located Load with the “Point of Change in Ownership.”6 Changing the specific definition of Co-Located Load already approved by the Commission is outside the scope of compliance. The December 18th Order adopted the following definition of Co-Located Load: “a ‘configuration [that] refers to end-use customer load that is physically connected to the facilities of an existing or planned Customer Facility on the Interconnection Customer’s side of the Point of Interconnection to the PJM Transmission System.’”7 The

Commission neither sought comment on nor directed PJM to propose a new definition of Co- Located Load. Nor did the December 18th Order permit PJM to propose variations that PJM considered were “consistent with or superior to” the Commission’s adopted definition as the Commission does with amendments to the pro forma Generator Interconnection Agreements.8

Constellation’s example of a new ring bus configuration added to accommodate a large load, included in its Thirty-Day Compliance Filing Comments, illustrates the difference, as shown above.19 A ring bus allows the load to access supply from the generation facility and the grid. I. The Commission Should Require PJM to Clarify the Necessary Studies Process for Project Developers to Ensure Speed to Market

To achieve speed to market, we recommend three clarifications to PJM’s proposed
Necessary Studies Tariff language: (1) clarify the scope of the Necessary Studies analysis to ensure Project Developers are able provide the required information for PJM to timely complete its reviews; (2) clarify Project Developers with existing Necessary Studies for Co-Located Load arrangements do not have to restart the Necessary Studies process and be further delayed; and

(3) establish deadlines to complete the Necessary Studies process expeditiously and accelerate the interconnection of Co-Located Loads.45

II. The Proposed Definition of Co-Located Generating Facility Should Be Limited to the Portion of a Facility Serving Co-Located Load, Consistent with the Commission’s Direction

The Commission should direct that PJM modify its proposed definition of “Co-Located Generating Facility” to confirm compliance with the December 18th Order. PJM proposes to define “Co-Located Generating Facility” as “an existing or planned Generating Facility that is physically connected to a Co-Located Load.”59 PJM’s proposed definition could be interpreted as applying to the entire Generating Facility based solely on physical connection to a Co-Located load. However, the Commission recognized that a generating facility may not be entirely committed to a Co-Located Load, but instead in some cases only a “portion of the generating facility [will] be designated to serve any Co-Located Load.”60 To conform the definition to the Commission’s intent, Constellation recommends that the Commission direct PJM to revise the definition to “mean the portion of an existing or planned Generating Facility that the Interconnection Customer designates to serve and is physically connected to a Co-Located Load.”

III. The Commission Should Require Other Modifications to Ensure Interim NITS
Provisions Comply with the December 18th Order
Three other simple modifications to PJM’s proposed Tariff revisions are needed to effectuate the December 18th Order’s mandate for Interim Network Integrated Transmission Service. First, in Part III of the Tariff, a load’s “option to seek” Interim NITS should be replaced with an “option to take.” This modification conforms the Tariff with the December 18th Order, which stated that Interim Network Integration Transmission Service “must allow an Eligible Customer that has requested NITS on behalf of a Co-Located Load to take non-firm transmission service that can be interrupted until the Network Upgrades necessary to provide NITS are complete.”61 As revised, Part III of the Tariff would read, “Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Co-Located Load must take one of the following services: (1) Network Integration Transmission Service under Tariff, Part III, with the option to take Interim Network Integration Transmission Service….”

Second, Section 28.1 should be revised to clarify that Interim Network Integration Transmission Service is available to Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of Co-Located Load.
