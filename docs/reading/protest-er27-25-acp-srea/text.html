I. 
BACKGROUND 
A. MISO’s Initial and Revised ERAS Proposals 
On May 16, 2025, the Commission rejected MISO’s initial ERAS proposal, finding that 
MISO had “not demonstrated that its ERAS proposal is a just and reasonable and not unduly 
discriminatory or preferential approach for addressing MISO’s stated resource adequacy and 
reliability needs.”5 The Commission identified two defects. First, the proposed Tariff placed “no 
limit on the number of projects that could be entered in the ERAS process,” which could produce 
an ERAS queue with processing times “similar to the challenges with the current DPP queue” and 
“a volume of ERAS interconnection requests untethered to reliability or resource adequacy 
needs.”6 Second, MISO had not shown “how the ERAS process is sufficiently targeted to study 
only interconnection requests needed to meet the anticipated shortfall in generating capacity 
described by MISO.”7 Commissioners Rosner and See each concurred separately.8 
MISO refiled a revised ERAS proposal that the Commission accepted on July 21, 2025. 
The accepted ERAS process is capped at 68 interconnection requests, with ten reserved for IPPs 
that have agreements with entities other than LSEs and eight reserved for requests serving retail-
choice load; it requires verification by a Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authority (“RERRA”) 
of a specific resource adequacy need; and it sunsets on the earlier of August 31, 2027 or completion 
of the sixty-eighth study.9 The Commission explained that these changes “ensure the ERAS 
process is sufficiently limited in scope to swiftly address discrete, demonstrated resource adequacy

C. The LARS Proposal 
MISO proposes LARS as a new, permanent study process under which a large load of at 
least 200 megawatts (“MW”) and one or more new Generating Facilities are studied in parallel, 
with the generator receiving a Large Load Generator Interconnection Agreement (“LLGIA”) for 
NRIS in approximately 120 days.17 MISO will run three LARS study periods per year, each with 
up to ten LARS Applications studied serially on a first-come, first-served basis.18 The LARS 
Application may be initiated and submitted only by the “LARS Associated Transmission 
Customer”—the entity that procures Transmission Service for the load, which MISO 
acknowledges “is often” an LSE.19 The LARS Associated Transmission Customer also pays the 
$250,000 non-refundable application deposit.20 A LARS Generating Facility need only be located 
in the same Local Resource Zone (“LRZ”) as the load, may request NRIS up to 150% of the load’s 
peak demand, and must remain paired with the load for fifteen years, after which its 
Interconnection Service continues regardless of whether the load remains.21 LARS studies will

II. 
PROTEST 
Because MISO filed LARS under FPA section 205, MISO bears the burden of 
demonstrating that its proposal is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or 
preferential.23 MISO has not carried that burden. LARS is unduly discriminatory because it makes 
the LSE the gatekeeper of a Commission-jurisdictional generator interconnection process and 
gives LSEs contracting options that IPPs do not have. It is also unjust and unreasonable on its own 
terms.  It is a permanent, uncapped priority queue untethered to any demonstrated need, it does not 
reflect the electrically proximate configuration the Show Cause Order described, and it shifts 
transmission capacity and costs away from DPP customers.  These are the same defects the 
Commission identified when it rejected MISO’s initial ERAS proposal. Finally, MISO has not 
shown that LARS satisfies the independent entity variation standard. 
A. LARS Would Unduly Discriminate Against Independent Power Producers and 
Create Opportunities for LSE Self-Dealing.

access to Commission-jurisdictional interconnection service.

provider must offer service to third parties on the same or comparable basis that it provides service 
to itself. In Order No. 888, the Commission required “that all public utilities must offer . . . services 
on a non-discriminatory open access basis” and explained that “[a]n open access tariff that is not 
unduly discriminatory or anticompetitive should offer third parties access on the same or

B. LARS Is Not Just and Reasonable. 
i. LARS is a permanent, uncapped expedited queue untethered to any identified 
need, unlike every expedited interconnection process the Commission has 
accepted. 
The Show Cause Order did not direct MISO to create a separate, permanent, expedited 
generator interconnection queue. The Commission expressed concern about the absence of “a 
generator interconnection study process and/or generator interconnection service” reflecting a 
generator’s commitment to limit its output while serving an electrically proximate large load.53 
The Commission did not require that such a process operate outside of, or ahead of, the DPP, and 
it stated that service to a generator serving an electrically proximate large load “should be of an 
interim nature until the interconnection customer obtains [ERIS] or [NRIS].”54 LARS does the 
opposite: it grants permanent NRIS through a parallel process that MISO describes as “a 
permanent MISO study process.”55 Moreover, the Filing makes clear that even where the paired 
LARS Associated Load ceases taking service, or the agreement pairing the generator and the load 
terminates, during the fifteen-year pairing period, the LARS generator may continue to operate 
under its LLGIA—subject to MISO’s Annual ERIS and Quarterly Operating Limit processes—

D. MISO Has Not Demonstrated That LARS Satisfies the Independent Entity 
Variation Standard. 
In Order No. 2003, the Commission permitted RTOs and ISOs to propose “independent 
entity variations” from the pro forma LGIP and LGIA, recognizing that an RTO or ISO “has 
different operating characteristics depending on its size and location and is less likely to act in an 
unduly discriminatory manner than a Transmission Provider that is a market participant.”112 Under 
that standard, an RTO/ISO must demonstrate that its proposed variations “are just and reasonable 
and not unduly discriminatory or preferential and accomplish the purposes of Order No. 2003.”113 
The flexibility the standard affords rests on the RTO/ISO’s independence. Although RTOs have 
far less incentive to discriminate, the same cannot be said of the LSEs and Transmission Owners 
to which LARS delegates control.

pro forma LGIP, an interconnection customer submits its interconnection request directly to the 
transmission provider, and the transmission provider—not a third party—administers access to 
interconnection service.114 LARS instead requires that the request be submitted by the LSE, and it 
requires a POI agreed to and attested by the Transmission Owner before the request may be 
filed.115 Second, the pro forma LGIP establishes a single queue in which requests are studied on

III. 
REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION 
If the Commission does not reject the Filing, it should direct MISO to clarify whether the 
LARS same-LRZ requirement applicable to a LARS Interconnection Customer is determined 
based on a Generating Facility’s physical location or on its POI to the MISO Transmission System. 
Proposed Attachment X, Section 3.12.2 appears to apply the same-LRZ requirement based on the 
Generating Facility’s POI for an individual LARS Interconnection Customer, providing that “[t]he 
Point of Interconnection specified in a LARS Application must be located in the same Local 
Resource Zone as the Point of Withdrawal of the LARS Associated Load.”121 However, where 
multiple Generating Facilities serve a single LARS Associated Load behind different POIs, the 
proposed Tariff states that the Generating Facilities must “[b]e located in the same LRZ as the 
LARS Associated Load,” creating ambiguity as to whether the requirement applies to the physical 
location of the Generating Facilities or to their respective POIs.122 
The Clean Energy Trades request that the Commission direct MISO to clarify that, for 
purposes of the same-LRZ requirement, the relevant location is the applicable POI to the MISO 
Transmission System rather than the physical location of the Generating Facility, including where 
multiple Generating Facilities serve a single LARS Associated Load.
