UNITED STATES OF AMERICA 
BEFORE THE 
FEDERAL ENERGY REGULATORY COMMISSION 
 
 
PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms ​​
​
       Docket No, AD26-7-000 
 
Comments of Multi-State Non-Governmental Organizations  
Thank you for convening the recent technical conference to examine governance and 
stakeholder reforms at PJM Interconnection. We submit these joint comments on behalf of 20 
organizations devoted to protecting the interests of consumers including the affordability of 
rates, the environment and health within the communities we serve.  
We appreciate the Commission's attention to the critical issue of PJM governance. Our intention 
within these comments is to emphasize that structural reforms at PJM must be designed to 
advance the public interest, prioritize affordability, and ensure transparent, accountable 
decision-making.  
Ratepayers shoulder the weight of PJM’s decisions, yet they and the states that represent them 
are structurally disadvantaged in PJM’s governance processes. To restore confidence in PJM 
and ensure that PJM markets serve the public interest, we urge the following reforms: 
1.​
Prioritize the Public Interest in PJM’s Core Identity   
 
●​ Reflect affordability and the public interest in PJM’s governing charter: PJM 
Board members and staff should have an explicit and binding responsibility to serve the 
broader public interest, and to prioritize affordable rates for ratepayers. This 
recommendation is echoed by other stakeholders, such as the ARRC users group, who 
have asked to amend PJM’s operating agreement to effectuate this change.  
●​ Align performance metrics with the public interest: This commitment should be 
underscored at every level of PJM’s decision-making, including by linking PJM staff and 
leaders’ Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to service of the public interest and 
long-term energy affordability. 
 
2.​
Give the Public and States a Meaningful Voice  
 
●​ Expand the Nominating Committee: Giving state representatives a formal role in 
the Board selection process will help to ensure PJM leaders are accountable to the 
public. In particular we support the request of the PJM Governor’s Collaborative for 
three seats on the nominating committee.  
 
●​ Ensure public-interest expertise on the Board: requiring there to be at least one

Board member with expertise in ratepayer protection and another with state regulatory 
experience will help avoid potential blind-spots around ratepayer impacts. 
 
●​ Put states on a level playing field: States should be granted Section 205 "jump ball" 
filing rights, so that alternative state-supported proposals can be evaluated fairly and 
there is a strong incentive for states to come to consensus on important matters. Beyond 
this measure, the commenters also endorse the establishment of a NESCOE-like states 
committee that currently operates in ISO New England. 
 
●​ Respect state authority: PJM must adhere to and actively support state clean-energy 
laws and engage constructively in state resource planning. 
 
 
3.​  Mandate Transparency 
 
●​ Open Board meetings and publish Board votes: The era of closed-door 
decision-making at PJM must end. Publishing Board votes and opening its meetings to 
the public are key to basic accountability.  
 
●​ Improve stakeholder meeting accountability, and require transparency of 
individual firm votes: PJM must record, transcribe, and publish discussions and 
votes at all stakeholder meetings. Individual firm votes must be publicly recorded and 
accessible across all committee levels. 
 
4.​ Reform the Voting Structure 
 
●​ Give the public voice the weight it deserves: Representatives of the public hold 
less than 2% of the voting power at PJM, and individual interest groups have an 
opportunity to dominate decisionmaking. PJM must overhaul its voting structure to 
fairly represent the public interest rather than catering to market incumbents. This could 
include the development of a separate sector that is geared toward the needs of small 
end use customers and would include the consumer advocate members of PJM. 
 
●​ Expand sector-weighted voting to all standing committees and senior task 
forces: At lower committee levels, affiliate voting tends to bias proposals in favor of 
PJM members representing supply interests. Applying sector-weighted voting in all PJM 
standing committees would prevent supply-side affiliate members from dominating 
outcomes and encourage stakeholders across sectors to come to consensus. 
 
Conclusion 
FERC and PJM have a critical opportunity to correct the structural imbalances in PJM’s 
governance. By acting to amplify the public voice and interest at PJM, the Commission can
