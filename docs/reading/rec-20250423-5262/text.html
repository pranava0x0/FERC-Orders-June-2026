ANSWER OF CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC
Constellation submits this answer1 and respectfully requests that the Commission adopt its preliminary finding that the existing PJM Tariff is unjust and unreasonable because it lacks clarity in how it applies to a variety of co-location arrangements.  The position favored by the Indicated Transmission Owners (and PJM to a lesser extent) for a one-size-fits-all solution with full Network Integration Transmission Service (“NITS”) billed on gross load is wrong as a matter of law and policy.  Constellation asks the Commission to (1) adopt most of PJM’s proposed options for replacement rates with the modifications proposed herein, which will permit the prompt interconnection of data centers pursuant to critical national interests while minimizing transmission upgrade costs to consumers and maximizing efficient use of existing energy infrastructure, or (2) initiate time-limited settlement discussions to establish an appropriate replacement rate.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This proceeding is about clarifying the PJM Tariff—and in some cases, adding appropriate rules—so that the significant data center load coming onto the system can be served promptly and efficiently.  The Commission was right that the existing PJM Tariff lacks requisite clarity, and this has paralyzed co-location efforts and service to data centers.  Consequently, the PJM Tariff is not just or reasonable.  This load must be connected promptly because of its critical strategic importance to this nation:  we are in a race with China and other nations for supremacy in Artificial Intelligence (“AI”), and we will lose the race if we are not able to promptly connect the data centers that hyperscalers require to train their models and serve users.  President Trump has explained that the United States needs to be “very competitive with China and others” in the development of AI infrastructure and that co-location enables such development “on a very rapid basis.”2  Limiting this new load to only one type of front-of-the-meter service that requires a years-long interconnection process, or even (as we have seen in Ohio) that are subject to a moratorium on new load connection, will lead to defeat.  The load also must be connected efficiently: the Commission should be looking for ways to bring this new load online while minimizing the need for costly system upgrades paid for by all consumers.

According to the Indicated Transmission Owners, however, all new load should be required to take front-of-the-meter service and become a NITS customer paying for service based on gross load.3  The Indicated Transmission Owners read the existing Tariff to require this one-size-fits-all limitation, and they have refused to perform studies for new load seeking a different configuration.
