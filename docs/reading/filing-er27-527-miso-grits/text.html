II. 
BACKGROUND 
The scale and pace of large load development require transmission service options that 
reflect how a Large Load customer intends to use the Transmission System.  Some Large Load 
Transmission Customers may seek full firm service and need an interim arrangement while 
necessary supporting upgrades are completed.  MISO is proposing in its omnibus filing both an 
Interim NITS product and Firm Service Step Up process that will be implemented within its 
existing NITS framework to address these transmission service needs.  Other Large Load 
Transmission Customers may be able to accept continuing interruption of some or all of their 
demand and seek an ongoing allocation between firm and non-firm service on a permanent basis.  
GRITS addresses both of these needs as it provides the Large Load Transmission Customer the 
option to determine the appropriate combination of firm and non-firm contract demand 
transmission services needed to meet its operational needs, but does not require the Large Load 
Transmission Customer to progress toward firm service for any or all of its GRITS Load Limit.4   
 
In the June 18, 2026, Show Cause Order, the Commission examined whether MISO’s 
Tariff adequately addresses large and co-located load development, including service for loads 
willing and able to limit their withdrawals under certain conditions.  The Commission identified a 
permanent contract-demand-like service option, treatment of regulation and black start service, 
and controls needed to enforce withdrawal limits among the matters requiring attention.5   
 
The Commission permitted respondents to develop regional solutions through section 205 
filings and subsequently held the proceeding in abeyance.  MISO has advanced related proposals 
through separate filings, including Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreements 
(“ZGIA”), Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads (“IRR”), and the Large Load 
Addition Resource Study (“LARS”) and the Large Load omnibus filing submitted separately 
pursuant to FPA section 205.  GRITS provides a distinct Transmission Service option that can 
operate with the applicable study, interconnection, and reliability requirements.  Generator 
Interconnection Service does not itself provide the Transmission Service needed for associated

III. 
  PROPOSED GRITS SERVICE 
A. 
Eligibility, service quantities, and agreements. 
 
GRITS provides an ongoing service arrangement for a Large Load Transmission Customer 
serving a Large Load that may desire a combination of both firm and non-firm contract demand-
like transmission service and, with respect to any non-firm service, is willing and able to limit its 
withdrawals under specified price and reliability conditions.  For example, a Large Load 
Transmission Customer representing a Large Load of 500 MW may desire to only take 200 MW 
of firm transmission service, allowing the remaining 300 MW to be served on a non-firm basis.  
Under Network Integration Transmission Service this participation model is not available while 
GRITS provides a pathway for such an arrangement. 
    
GRITS will only be made available to a Large Load Transmission Customer, cannot be 
combined with any other NITS transmission service, and is not limited to Large Load being served 
by a co-located generator.  GRITS Service requires that a Large Load Transmission Customer 
establish a GRITS Load Limit and a Firm GRITS quantity that may range from zero to the full 
Load Limit in the pro forma GRITS service agreement (“GRITS Service Agreement”), which 
cannot be exceeded without execution of a new pro forma GRITS Service Agreement.  Non-Firm 
GRITS may be reserved within the difference between those quantities as needed and as available.  
The GRITS Load Limit defines the maximum approved Load in the GRITS Service Agreement; 
it does not guarantee that the entire non-firm quantity will be available in every period. 
 
GRITS contains the firm and non-firm components within one GRITS Service Agreement.  
MISO does not propose combining NITS and GRITS for the same load to create that allocation, 
and a change to the GRITS Load Limit or Firm GRITS quantity requires a new GRITS Service 
Agreement.  Where both firm and non-firm quantities are present, separate Commercial Pricing 
Nodes (“CPNodes”) represent those components.  This representation supports the different 
reservation, interruption, billing, capacity, and Auction Revenue Rights (“ARR”) treatment 
applicable to each GRITS service component. 
 
For example, a 300 MW GRITS Load Limit with 60 MW of Firm GRITS permits a 
maximum non-firm quantity of 240 MW.  At 200 MW of operating demand, the proposed 
allocation assigns the first 60 MW to the CPNode representing the firm component and the 
remaining 140 MW to the CPNode representing the non-firm component.  That allocation is 
subject to an available reservation and the applicable operating limit; the agreement’s 240 MW 
non-firm maximum does not itself establish that 240 MW will be available for every hour. 
 
As noted above, GRITS accommodates both stand-alone Large Loads and qualifying co-
located arrangements.  A Large Load Transmission Customer must request GRITS through the 
applicable LLPR process and will be required to execute a GRITS Service Agreement and a 
GRITS Operating Agreement specifying the quantities, operational limits, communications, and 
control requirements.  Service commences after Network Upgrades, Large Load Network 
Upgrades and/or Impacted Transmission Owner Large Load Network Upgrades identified through

B. 
Reservations and service priority. 
 
Non-Firm GRITS will be reserved through the Open Access Same-Time Information 
System (“OASIS”) for periods ranging from one Day to one month.  The reservation rules use the 
existing Point-To-Point Transmission Service framework, including priority for longer requested 
service periods within the permitted range and the order of competing equal-duration requests.  A 
new reservation receives its applicable position in that process.  The underlying Form of GRITS 
Service Agreement does not reserve non-firm service indefinitely, and reservation priority of non-
firm service remains distinct from the priority applied when existing service must be curtailed. 
 
Firm GRITS is reserved for at least one year within the ongoing arrangement.  Reservation 
priority determines the order in which requests are evaluated; curtailment priority determines 
which existing service is reduced when the system cannot support scheduled use.  A customer’s 
continuing GRITS agreement does not establish an indefinite reservation of non-firm capability.

The GRITS proposal also applies gross-basis treatment to applicable service-related 
charges, including regulation and black start, so that a co-located arrangement does not avoid 
charges for services it receives merely because its net withdrawal is below its gross load.  These 
reservation-based charges and gross-basis billing serve different functions and apply through the 
billing determinants established for the particular service. 
 
A reservation-based charge, such as that used for the existing Point-To-Point Transmission 
Service rates, reflects the amount of Transmission Service reserved, rather than actual 
consumption alone.  The applicable schedules govern any billing adjustment or credit for 
interrupted service.  Co-located generation serving a Large Load receiving GRITS service must 
be registered as a Generation Resource, and the Large Load Transmission Customer must procure 
Transmission Service to receive service from the co-located resource.  Co-location therefore does 
not eliminate the obligation to procure Transmission Service or permit the customer to “net away” 
gross load relevant to regulation and black start billing.

Ms. Xie further explains that the GRITS Offer Cap serves two complementary objectives.9  
First, by establishing the GRITS Offer Cap, MISO can use the Security Constrained Economic

establishing the GRITS Offer Cap at the first non-zero step of the Market-Wide Short-Term 
Reserve Demand Curve prevents reliability being degraded for all Transmission Customers by 
ensuring the Non-Firm GRITS Load is offline before a shortfall of operating reserves occurs.  This 
limitation reflects the “as-available” nature of Non-Firm GRITS and is consistent with the 
reliability rationale underlying the Tariff’s authorization to curtail Non-Firm Point-To-Point 
Transmission Service when an Emergency or other unforeseen condition threatens to impair or 
degrade Transmission System reliability.   
 
E. 
Reliability based Curtailment and enforcement. 
 
Non-Firm GRITS Load is also subject to reliability-based Curtailment directives, including 
Curtailment upon the declaration of a Capacity Advisory in the same Local Balancing Authority 
(“LBA”).  During Transmission Emergencies, Non-Firm GRITS Load is curtailed with the same 
priority as Loads served using Non-Firm Point-To-Point Transmission Service of equal service 
duration and before Loads served through Interim NITS and Firm Transmission Service.  Ms. Xie 
explains how Resource Adequacy is maintained if the Non-Firm GRITS Load is excluded from 
the Planning Resource Auction because the GRITS Offer Cap will ensure these Loads are curtailed 
whenever LMP rises above the GRITS Offer Cap.10  In the event a Capacity Advisory declaration 
is made while LMP is below the GRITS Offer Cap, MISO will issue the Curtailment directive for 
the entire Non-Firm GRITS Load.11  Accordingly, Resource Adequacy is assured because these 
Loads will not be competing with other types of Load at the times when Resource Adequacy is 
under pressure.  The portion of a Large Load served through Firm GRITS remains in the 
Coincident Peak Demand forecast and retains the corresponding capacity responsibility to procure 
Zonal Resource Credits.

Resource Adequacy, ARR Entitlements, and market participation.

Planning and commercial model treatment. 
 
The firm component of GRITS Load is included in ongoing MISO Transmission

IV. 
RESPONSE TO THE SHOW CAUSE ORDER 
 
Section 205 requires jurisdictional rates and practices to be just and reasonable and 
prohibits undue preferences and unreasonable differences in service.15  GRITS ties the 
Transmission Customer’s rights to the service it requests: firm quantities retain firm treatment and 
corresponding capacity obligations, while non-firm quantities receive as-needed and as-available 
service, lower priority for reliability interruptions, and a price-based interruption condition.16  
Separate reservation requirements and model treatment for Firm and Non-Firm GRITS makes 
those distinctions administrable.

through 105 of the Show Cause Order by establishing a defined Load Limit, an elected firm 
quantity, separately reserved non-firm service, and enforceable operating conditions.17  The 
proposal’s gross-load treatment prohibits netting and addresses the regulation and black start 
concerns identified in Paragraphs 98–100 of the Show Cause Order.  In addition, the proposal’s 
metering and control requirements address the withdrawal-limit concerns in paragraph 102 of the 
Show Cause Order.  Those features connect the service received with the obligations accepted by 
the customer.18   
 
The planning provisions of the GRITS proposal, which require requests for GRITS to be 
evaluated through the LLPR process consistent with all other requests for Transmission Service 
made by Large Load Transmission Customers, also address the issue identified in Paragraph 124 
of the Show Cause Order.19  Through this process requests for Firm GRITS will be subject to the 
analysis for ongoing firm service, which reflects the Firm GRITS quantity, while total GRITS

A. 
Definitions 
 
 
MISO proposes the following, new definitions for GRITS: 
 
Firm Grid Response Integration Transmission Service (Firm GRITS):  The amount of 
firm Transmission Service that is reserved and/or scheduled to a specific Point(s) of 
Delivery pursuant to the Form of Service Agreement for GRITS.  A Large Load 
Transmission Customer taking Firm GRITS may reserve and/or schedule between 0 MW 
and the GRITS Load Limit. 
 
Grid Response Integration Transmission Service (GRITS):  A Transmission Service 
provided under Module B of this Tariff that permits a Large Load Transmission Customers 
to take Transmission Service.  GRITS permits the reservation of Capacity and of Energy 
on either a firm or non-firm basis from Network Resources to the Point(s) of Delivery.  
GRITS is comprised of three components; GRITS Load Limit, Firm GRITS, and Non-Firm 
GRITS.  A Transmission Customer may not change the GRITS Load Limit or the amount 
of Firm GRITS without a new Form of Service Agreement for GRITS. 
 
GRITS Load Limit:  The maximum amount of Load specified in the Form of Service 
Agreement for GRITS by the Large Load Transmission Customer taking GRITS that may 
be scheduled at a Point(s) of Delivery pursuant to Module B of this Tariff. 
 
GRITS Offer Cap:  The maximum Locational Marginal Price at the Commercial Pricing 
Node associated with a Large Load taking Non-Firm GRITS service that a Market 
Participant may submit in a Bid.  Non-Firm GRITS may be Interrupted if the Locational 
Marginal Price rises above the GRITS Offer Cap. 
 
Non-Firm GRITS:  The amount of Non-Firm Transmission Service that is reserved and 
scheduled on an as-available basis and is subject to Curtailment or Interruption as set forth 
in Section 37B of this Tariff.  Non-Firm GRITS is available only to Large Load 
Transmission Customers for periods ranging from one (1) Day to one (1) Month.  A Large 
Load Transmission Customer taking Grid Responsive Integration Transmission Service 
may reserve and/or schedule between 0 MW and the difference of GRITS Load Limit and 
Firm GRITS.

VI. 
STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT 
 
MISO developed the GRITS proposal through successive discussions with the Large Load 
Working Group (“LLWG”).  At the August 12, 2026 LLWG meeting, MISO presented its Flexible 
Large-Load Interconnections and Transmission Service (“FLITS”) concept, which contemplated 
both interim and permanent non-firm service.  MISO requested written feedback by August 19, 
2026, with particular emphasis on resource adequacy.  Stakeholders addressed curtailment, 
capacity obligations, and the implications of offering permanent non-firm service.  The 
environmental sector recommended developing separate interim and permanent products given 
their different purposes and operating requirements.26 
 
At the September 9, 2026 LLWG meeting, MISO presented interim FLITS and a separate 
permanent Contract Demand service and requested written comments by September 16. 
Stakeholders addressed how the proposed services would differ in rates, curtailment priority, 
resource adequacy treatment, and ARR eligibility.  At the September 23, 2026  LLWG meeting, 
MISO focused its presentation on Interim NITS and confirmed during the discussion that it 
continued to develop the permanent service and would return with an update on October 8, 2026.  
The September 23, 2026 feedback request set an October 2, 2026 deadline for comments on the 
Interim NITS design and an October 16 deadline for comments on the anticipated Interim NITS 
Tariff redlines.27 
 
At the October 8, 2026 LLWG meeting, MISO presented the permanent service as GRITS 
and requested written feedback by October 16, 2026.  The proposal allowed customers to combine 
a firm service quantity with short-term, as-available non-firm reservations.  MISO also proposed 
excluding the non-firm portion from the Coincident Peak Demand forecast and requiring 
interruption under specified reliability and price conditions.  Stakeholders commented on the 
proposed price trigger, coordination and enforcement of curtailment, capacity forecasting and 
modeling, and the treatment of co-located resources.  They also asked how a customer could 
increase its firm service quantity after service began.
