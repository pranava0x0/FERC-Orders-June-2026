COMMENTS OF THE SOLAR ENERGY INDUSTRIES ASSOCIATION 
 
Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (Commission) 
Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 the Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA)2 submits these 
comments supporting the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.’s (MISO) filing 
proposing revisions to establish the Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement 
(ZGIA).3 In addition to establishing the ZGIA, MISO’s ZGIA Proposal would establish a study 
process and operational requirements, and market and resource adequacy rules to accommodate 
Zero-Injection Generation Resources (ZG Resources) that intend to serve load interconnecting at 
the same substation as the Generating Facility. MISO’s proposal represents a just and reasonable 
step toward addressing the resource adequacy issues posed by the rapid addition of large loads. 
SEIA supports MISO’s ZGIA Proposal and urges the Commission to accept it.4

I. 
INTRODUCTION 
The MISO region faces unprecedented growth in energy demand, driven largely by data 
centers, next-generation manufacturing, and electrification.5 The Commission recognized these 
challenges in its Show Cause Order in Docket No. EL26-70-000, which raised concerns about, 
among other things, the absence of generator interconnection service and study processes 
tailored to generation serving co-located or electrically proximate large load.6 The ZGIA 
Proposal is the first in a series of filings MISO intends to make to address those concerns.7 If 
approved, it would address generation serving co-located load at the same substation—the co-
location subset of the Show Cause Order’s concerns—by establishing an expedited 
interconnection pathway while preserving MISO’s interconnection queue and planning 
processes.8 As MISO has indicated, other issues, including transmission service for co-located 
and proximate loads and the integration of electrically proximate generation, would be addressed 
in subsequent filings.9 SEIA supports the ZGIA Proposal as a just and reasonable first step 
toward addressing these co-location and large load interconnection challenges.

II. 
COMMENTS 
A. 
The ZGIA Proposal Appropriately Limits Zero Injection Generation 
Interconnection Service to Co-Located Configurations, Addressing 
Deliverability Concerns. 
 
Under MISO’s existing interconnection framework, a resource that injects power onto the 
Transmission System must demonstrate deliverability through the Definitive Planning Phase 
(DPP). Deliverability is a prerequisite to receiving full capacity accreditation and marketable 
Zonal Resource Credits.10 However, under the ZGIA proposal, a project developed under a 
ZGIA would not grant deliverability rights. The ZGIA Proposal limits Zero Injection Generation 
Interconnection Service (ZG Interconnection Service) to co-located configurations in which ZG 
Resources and the Zero Injection Generation Associated Load (ZG Associated Load) would be 
located at the same substation and voltage level, with no injection beyond the interconnecting 
substation. Because the ZG Resource would not inject onto the broader Transmission System, 
the co-location design would avoid the need for a deliverability analysis.11 Under the ZGIA 
Proposal, ZG Interconnection Service would not grant injection rights or deliverability, and 
MISO would require that no Network Upgrades be needed beyond the interconnecting substation 
(except limited System Protection Facilities).12 A ZG Resource that would trigger broader 
Network Upgrades would not qualify and would instead need to proceed through the DPP.13 The

B. 
The ZGIA Proposal Supports Speed to Reliable Power While Preserving 
System Integrity. 
 
Under the ZGIA Proposal, ZG Interconnection Service would be available only where the 
Generating Facility and the ZG Associated Load are at the same substation and voltage level.18 
MISO considered and rejected broader scopes, including electrically proximate configurations, 
based on stakeholder concerns about injection control, unconstrained power flows, and potential 
state retail service conflicts.19 By drawing the boundary at the substation level, MISO would 
limit ZG Interconnection Service to configurations where injection controls can be effectively 
verified,20 and the expedited 90-day study process would be well-suited to these limited 
configurations.21 
A critical feature of this process is that MISO has designed the ZGIA study track to 
operate independently of the DPP.22 As MISO indicates, ZGIA studies would use a separate 
model base that does not include pending DPP interconnection requests,23 and ZGIA approvals 
would not be added to ongoing DPP studies. MISO would reconcile both sets of results in the 
next MISO Transmission Expansion Plan (MTEP) base case.24 This structural separation
