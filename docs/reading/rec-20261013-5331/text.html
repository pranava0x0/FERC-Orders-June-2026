B. Conditioning ATC’s Proposed Changes to Attachment FF-ATCLLC on
Compliance with Yet-Unknown Uniform Base-Level Rules Without a
Demonstrable Need Will Impose Unnecessary Costs in ATC’s Footprint and
Undermine Policy Goals to Advance AI.

MISO argues that uniform base-level rules are needed to address reliability concerns and that if ATC had different standards, it would create operational risk.9  MISO has asserted potential reliability concerns but has not demonstrated any specific reliability deficiency, operational limitation, or planning concern that would result from ATC's proposed framework. From a planning standpoint, there has been and will continue to be significant coordination between ATC and MISO regarding the planning of the system.  Indeed, all of ATC’s projects must be evaluated in MISO’s regional MTEP process—either through the regular planning cycle or through the Expedited Project Review (EPR) process.10  That will not change. From an operations standpoint, it should be abundantly clear that ATC's proposal does not prevent MISO from obtaining information necessary to maintain reliable system operations.  ATC is not proposing to displace otherwise applicable MISO requirements governing load forecasting, telemetry, real-time information, or operational visibility.11  The

Contrary to MISO’s claims, Attachment FF-ATCLLC does not exceed the traditional
bounds of ATC’s local planning criteria.  Large loads are retail customers of the local distribution companies in ATC’s footprint.  ATC’s planning for large loads interconnecting with the local distribution companies on ATC’s system is fundamental to ATC’s local planning function and will impact retail electric service in ATC’s footprint for many years to come.  Delegating that function to MISO is contrary to Wisconsin law and prior Commission orders, and would subvert ATC’s obligations to its stakeholders.

Moreover, contrary to MISO’s claims, ATC has adequately addressed cost allocation in Attachment FF-ATCLLC to comply with state retail tariff requirements.  Differences in cost allocation policies have not been problematic in the past.  In the generation interconnection context, ATC has had different network upgrade cost allocation provisions from the rest of the MISO region with no negative impacts.16

MISO’s alleged harm is based on conjecture and dependent on tariff provisions that have not yet been filed or accepted by the Commission.  ATC’s amendments to Attachment FF- ATCLLC are supported by studies used in developing the planning criteria to address the needs of its system and will apply to large loads in its footprint.17  We are not aware of any similar
