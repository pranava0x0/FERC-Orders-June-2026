REQUEST FOR ISSUANCE OF AN ERRATA 
To the Commission: 
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued its “Order Instituting Proceeding Under 
Section 206 of the Federal Power Act” in this above captioned proceeding.1  In its Order, 
the Commission found that PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) existing Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory 
or preferential and thereafter directed PJM and forty-five (45) Participating Transmission 
Owners (“PTOs”) within PJM to show cause with respect to a variety of concerns related 
to large load or co-located load transmission and interconnection within sixty (60) days of 
the date of its Order.2 Wabash Valley Power Association, Inc. (“WVPA”) has identified a 
procedural issue with the Order and respectfully requests that the Commission issue an 
errata as soon as practicable.

Before addressing the procedural issue presented below, WVPA desires to clarify 
that it does not dispute the need for the Commission to address issues surrounding service 
to large load and co-located load within organized markets and elsewhere.  Further, WVPA 
acknowledges the Commission’s existing precedent regarding non-jurisdictional entities 
with certain rate schedules within a jurisdictional RTO or ISO.   
The Commission included WVPA as one of the PTOs in the caption of the Order. 
However, at footnote 13 of the Order, the Commission described which PTOs it intended 
to include in the caption and made clear: 
Transmission Owners refers to the entities listed in the caption 
of this order. We recognize that PJM’s transmission owners 
include entities that are not subject to the Commission’s 
jurisdiction because they are not public utilities pursuant to 
section 201(f) of the FPA. See 16 U.S.C. § 824(f); Sw. Power 
Pool, Inc., 180 FERC ¶ 61,022, at PP 44-45 (2022). 
Accordingly, the Commission has not included AMP 
Transmission, LLC; City of Hamilton, OH; East Kentucky 
Power Cooperative, Inc.; and Southern Maryland Electric 
Cooperative, Inc. here as Transmission Owners that must 
respond to this order to show cause. Nonetheless, the 
Commission retains the authority to review each entity’s 
formula rates as a component of PJM’s Tariff in an order to 
show cause directed towards PJM, and PJM must respond to 
this order regarding the non-public utilities’ PJM Tariff 
provisions on file with the Commission. See Sw. Power Pool, 
Inc., 180 FERC ¶ 61,022 at P 40.[3] 
Like each of the PTO entities listed in footnote 13, WVPA is not a public utility 
pursuant to section 201(f) of the FPA.  In January 2025 WVPA informed the Commission 
that as a result of the termination of one of its members, it ceased to be a public utility on

January 1, 2025 because it became exempt from the Federal Power Act as a result of being 
wholly owned by exempt entities.  As of January 1, 2025, WVPA has been wholly owned 
by twenty-one electric cooperatives that receive financing under the Rural Electrification 
Act of 1936 or sell less than four (4) million megawatt hours annually and by one additional 
member, which is wholly owned by WVPA.  The Commission subsequently acknowledged 
WVPA’s acceptance of its withdrawal of jurisdictional rate schedules based on the change 
in status.4   
 
As of today, WVPA is still wholly owned by electric cooperatives that receive 
financing under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936 or sell less than four (4) million 
megawatt hours annually and by the additional member it wholly owns.  In fact, WVPA is 
itself in the process of receiving financing under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936.  
WVPA is a PTO within PJM, however, it is a PTO that is similarly situated to all of the 
other non-jurisdictional entities listed in footnote 13 of the Order.  WVPA respectfully 
submits that it is unaware of any basis, stated within the Order or any facts external to the 
Order, to treat WVPA any different.  WVPA believes that the Order inadvertently 
identified it based on its prior status as a public utility. 
 
In addition, WVPA believes the Commission did not intend to include it as a named 
respondent. In the Show Cause Order issued to the California Independent System Operator 
(“CAISO”) and its transmission owners in Docket No. EL26-71-000, non-jurisdictional
