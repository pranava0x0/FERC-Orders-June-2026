II. RESPONSE TO DEFICIENCY LETTER
Question 1:

SPP explains that, currently, under section 2.22.1(d) of Attachment AE of the Tariff, a CHILL can be curtailed when the sum of the available capacity of its supporting Resources is less than the CHILL.6  SPP proposes to revise its Tariff to provide that a “CHILL will not be curtailed solely due to supporting Resource insufficiency” when its supporting resource takes a Scheduled Resource Outage that is scheduled at least 14 days in advance of the planned start time.7  Please explain whether there are limitations on how long a CHILL can stay online while all or some of its supporting Resources are on a Scheduled Resource Outage or otherwise unavailable to the SPP Integrated Marketplace. Response:

If a CHILL participating under Section 2.22.1(d) of Attachment AE of Attachment AE (“Option 2 CHILL”) of the Tariff has all or some of its supporting Resources on a Scheduled Resource Outage submitted at least 14 Calendar Days in advance, the Option 2 CHILL is limited in its ability to stay online by the duration of the Outage and the results of the system feasibility solve.8

Response to 2.a:

Outside of Emergency Conditions, SPP would consider if a Resource is supporting a CHILL. This matters because requesting that an Option 2 CHILL supporting Resource reschedule an Outage has little benefit as the Tariff limits the Resource output to the load forecast value of the corresponding CHILL.22 A Resource supporting a CHILL must only submit a Resource Offer sufficient to cover the forecasted withdrawals of the CHILL.23 An Option 2 CHILL may be curtailed if the supporting Resource Offer is less than the

During Emergency Conditions, SPP would not consider whether a Resource is supporting an Option 2 CHILL in its determination of which Scheduled Resource Outage it will request to be rescheduled during Emergency Conditions. Response to 2.b:

A supporting Resource for an Option 2 CHILL is only eligible for compensation if it rescheduled its approved Scheduled Resource Outage in Emergency Conditions. The supporting Resource must make itself available by self-committing or setting its commitment status to market or reliability and meet the other requirements for Qualified Outages in Attachment U, Section B of the Tariff. For example, a Qualified Outage must be submitted at least 60 days prior to the start of the scheduled maintenance.25 This proposal only provides compensation for rescheduled Outages during Emergency Conditions because, as explained above, CHILL supporting resources do not support the capacity for Network Resources to take Outages and provides little system reliability benefit outside of those conditions.

Response to 2.c:

All Resources are subject to the criteria in Attachment U, Section B, including Resources supporting a CHILL. SPP’s proposed Tariff changes specify that the requirements for Resources supporting a CHILL are “in addition to the criteria in this Section B.”26 A Resource that is not supporting a CHILL provides two benefits to the system if it reschedules its Outage: (1) it increases the maintenance margin available for other Resources to retain their Scheduled Resource Outage and (2) it is likely to make additional Energy available beyond what would have been available if it had not rescheduled its Outage. A Resource that is not supporting a CHILL is likely to make additional Energy available because it is subject to must offer requirements and associated

By contrast, outside of Emergency Conditions, a Resource supporting a CHILL provides neither benefit: it is not included in the maintenance margin calculation and any Energy it produces is offset by the withdrawals from the CHILL.

Accordingly, the proposed revisions include two additional conditions for compensation that apply only to Resources supporting a CHILL. A Resource supporting a CHILL is only eligible for compensation if it has (1) rescheduled its approved Scheduled Resource Outage in Emergency Conditions and (2) made itself available. First, as described above, there is little benefit to the system if a Resource supporting a CHILL reschedules its Outage because the Outage is not included in the maintenance margin calculation. However, a Resource supporting a CHILL does provide a benefit to the system when it injects Energy beyond the needs of the CHILL, which may only occur during Emergency Conditions.

As described above, Resources supporting CHILLs must only submit Resource Offers sufficient to cover the forecasted withdrawals by the CHILL.28 This limitation exists because the supporting Resource interconnected through the High Impact Large Load Generation Assessment (HILLGA) and is subject to a High Impact Large Load Generator Interconnection Agreement (HILLGIA).29 The HILLGIA provides that the Transmission Provider may take whatever actions necessary during Emergency Conditions to

(i) preserve public health and safety, (ii) preserve the reliability of the Transmission System or Transmission Owner’s Interconnection Facilities, (iii) limit or prevent damage, and (iv) expedite restoration of service.30 The CHILL must also agree to be curtailable in the event of Emergency Conditions.31 Due to the limitations of the HILLGIA, SPP may only request that the Resources supporting CHILLS exceed the forecasted withdrawals by the CHILL during Emergency Conditions.
