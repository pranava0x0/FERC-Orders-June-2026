ER26-3380-000 · 2026-09-10 · PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.

Motion for Leave to Answer and Answer of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. to Comments and Protests re the 07/31/2026 revised Open Access Transmission Tariff filing et al

PJM files a motion for leave to answer and answers the comments and protests on its July 31 Reliability Backstop Procurement, defending the 15-year term, $555/MW-day cap, cost allocation, LSE credit and exit rules and offering some clarifications.

Asks FERC to: Accept the answer and accept the RBP tariff revisions effective September 29, 2026, as requested.

Parallel proceeding. Defends PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement in ER26-3380, the capacity adequacy and cost allocation track that runs beside E-7's request for a report on ensuring adequate generation.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8) E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28)

Key points

  1. PJM says no protest undermines its one-time backstop procurement as just and reasonable.
    PJM’s RBP proposal is a just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential means to procure capacity through a one-time backstop procurement. PDF p. 2
  2. PJM says it frames the RBP around the region's 67 million people in 13 states and the District of Columbia.
    to keep the lights on for 67 million people across 13 states and the District of Columbia PDF p. 3
  3. PJM is open to specific adjustments in a compliance filing if FERC orders them.
    adjustments to incorporate intervenors’ suggestions into the RBP proposal in a compliance filing if so ordered by the Commission. PDF p. 9
  4. PJM says Electric Distributors only report and administer, and do not bear RBP Charges unless they are also the LSE.
    Electric Distributors perform a reporting or administrative role in the RBP PDF p. 12
  5. PJM ties the 15-Delivery Year maximum term to the NEDC and governors' principles.
    the fixed, maximum 15-Delivery Year term is informed by the NEDC/PJM Governors’ Principles PDF p. 31
  6. PJM defends the $555/MW-day UCAP cap as a MW-weighted average on a pay-as-bid procurement.
    PJM has proposed that the levelized procurement cost of all selected RBP Sell Offers not exceed a maximum willingness to pay of $555/MW-day UCAP, on a MW-weighted average basis over all Delivery Years of the RBP. PDF p. 36
  7. PJM defends sizing the cost allocation on a snapshot of the 2028/2029 shortfall.
    The Commission has long held that relying on snapshots is an acceptable and appropriate method for establishing baselines or targets, PDF p. 60
  8. PJM defends the LSE collateral requirement as protecting other ratepayers.
    the basic need for a collateral requirement should not be controversial. PDF p. 74
  9. PJM says its Transmission Owner exit terms give enough notice.
    None of these arguments render PJM’s proposed Tariff revisions unjust and unreasonable. PDF p. 95
