LIMITED PROTEST OF 
AMERICAN MUNICIPAL POWER, INC. 
Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission (“Commission” or “FERC”),1 American Municipal Power, Inc. 
(“AMP”) submits this limited protest of the August 13, 2026 filing by PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. (“PJM”) in the above-captioned docket,2 in which PJM proposes revisions to its 
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among 
Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”). 
AMP submits this limited protest to express concerns with PJM’s proposal for accrediting 
Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”), which fails to properly account for changes in 
accredited capacity values over time. 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
PJM is facing an enormous influx of new data center load, which threatens to 
exacerbate the region’s capacity shortfall and impose billions of dollars in additional costs 
on existing native load customers. It is imperative that PJM have the tools to 
accommodate these new loads while ensuring reliability and affordability for current 
customers. Consistent with the Ratepayer Protection Pledge,3 and longstanding cost

There is at least one aspect of the PJM Filing, however, that the Commission 
should direct PJM to revise, in accordance with PJM’s advance consent to modifications 
that are consistent with its overarching objectives.11 The Commission should not accept 
PJM’s proposal to fix the level of BYONC for a generation or storage resource at the 
resource’s accredited capacity in the first year it is in an arrangement with a New Large 
Load.12 PJM’s proposal to fix BYONC at the first-year accreditation level risks over-
crediting New Large Loads for BYONC given PJM’s projections that the effective load 
carrying capability (“ELCC”) of certain resource classes (particularly storage resources) 
will decline significantly over the next decade. In addition, the PJM Filing does not 
adequately explain how its proposed treatment of BYONC in RPM capacity auctions will 
result in just and reasonable outcomes in light of projected ELCC changes over time. To 
address these concerns, PJM should be required to revise its proposal to better account 
for projected ELCC declines in resources supporting BYONC. 
II. 
LIMITED PROTEST 
A. 
PJM’s proposal to fix the Accredited UCAP of BYONC without 
accounting for declining ELCC could understate the need for IRAS. 
Under the IRAS framework, capacity that qualifies as BYONC would offset the 
amount of New Large Load eligible (i.e., required) to provide IRAS.13 In setting the level 
of this BYONC offset, PJM explains that “[f]or BYONC supported by any resource type 
(generation, storage, and demand-side), the Accredited UCAP of the BYONC ‘shall be 
the Accredited UCAP for the RPM Auction in which the BYONC cleared for the initial

Delivery Year the BYONC is in an arrangement with the New Large Load.’”14 In other 
words, the Accredited UCAP of a resource supporting BYONC would be fixed based on 
the resource’s ELCC in the first Delivery Year that it is supporting New Large Load. 
The core problem with setting BYONC’s Accredited UCAP based on a single year 
snapshot is that ELCC is not expected to remain static for every resource category. The 
ELCCs of storage resources, in particular, are projected to decline significantly over the 
next decade, as shown by PJM’s recently posted preliminary ELCC Class Ratings for 
Delivery Years 2028/29 – 2042/43.15
Under PJM’s proposal, if a 200 MW New Large Load (after accounting for the 
Forecast Pool Requirement), comes online in 2029 and brings 200 MW UCAP of BYONC 
supported by four-hour Capacity Storage Resources for that year, then the load will have 
no IRAS responsibility in all future years. But PJM’s own projections indicate that by 
2031/2032, that storage resource would be accredited with only 119 MW of UCAP.16

Giving New Large Load “credit” for Accredited UCAP of BYONC that exceeds the 
actual ELCC-measured Delivery Year capacity value of the resources supporting the 
BYONC raises reliability concerns under PJM’s IRAS proposal. The portion of a New 
Large Load that exceeds the ELCC-measured UCAP of a supporting BYONC resource 
is effectively the same as unhedged, Eligible New Large Load, but without the 
corresponding IRAS load curtailment obligation. PJM could be forced to implement 
emergency procedures driven by such unhedged New Large Loads, with the adverse 
reliability consequences falling broadly on native load customers and those New Large 
Loads that brought sufficient BYONC, in contravention of PJM’s objectives. 
While PJM projects most resource classes to have relatively stable ELCCs over 
the next decade (see the table above), the significant ELCC declines predicted for certain 
resource classes (particularly storage) highlight the need to address potential UCAP 
declines for all resources in the IRAS framework. Otherwise, PJM could understate the 
amount of New Large Load that should be considered Eligible New Large Load obligated 
to provide IRAS.17

C. 
The Commission should direct PJM to modify its proposal for 
determining Accredited UCAP for BYONC. 
Several solutions were offered during the CIFP process to address concerns about 
declining ELCCs for resources supporting BYONC. One approach would be to re-accredit 
BYONC each year, with penalties if the New Large Load exceeds its supporting BYONC, 
and sell-back rights if the BYONC exceeds the New Large Load. While this would be the 
most accurate approach, PJM argued in the CIFP process that it would add significant 
monitoring and matching requirements to the IRAS framework. 
Another approach would be to set a resource’s Accredited UCAP of BYONC for all 
future Delivery Years using PJM’s preliminary ELCC Class Ratings projection. Under this 
method, PJM would only be required to perform the Accredited UCAP analysis for a 
particular BYONC resource once, but the analysis would reflect projected declines (or 
increases) in ELCC over time. Accordingly, a New Large Load would know up-front 
whether and to what extent it would need to provide for additional BYONC to account for 
ELCC declines in its supporting BYONC resources over time.  
The use of projected ELCC figures to set BYONC Accredited UCAP would, to be 
sure, still result in some level of mismatch between projected and actual ELCC for 
resources supporting BYONC. Given the potential adverse reliability impacts and cost 
shifts associated with resources that have declining accreditations over time, however, it 
is better to err on the side of capturing these projected declines, albeit imprecisely, rather 
than use PJM’s “set and forget” approach to accrediting BYONC. 
PJM’s consent to modifications to the PJM Filing should encompass AMP’s 
proposal to better account for ELCC declines in BYONC resources supporting New Large 
Loads. Such a revision is certainly “consistent with PJM’s overarching objectives in this
