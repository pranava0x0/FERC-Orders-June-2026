II. 
DESCRIPTION AND BASIS FOR THE TO TARIFF REVISIONS 
Viridon developed the TO Tariff Revisions to respond to the concerns identified in the 
Show Cause Order regarding transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads. 
As described below, the proposed revisions address application and study procedures, ongoing 
operational requirements and service agreements, cost recovery, co-located generation, and 
flexible interim interconnection service. Viridon Path 15 has sought to align these provisions with 
CAISO’s proposed tariff framework and the approaches developed with other PTOs, while 
accounting for its role as a non-load-serving PTO and the need for coordination among affected 
transmission owners.

A. 
Application and Study Procedures Applicable to Eligible Customers Seeking 
Transmission Service on Behalf of Large Loads 
The Show Cause Order identifies that the Commission is concerned that CAISO and/or 
PTOs do not currently have tariff provisions expressly addressing how the provision of 
transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads will be studied in a timely 
fashion.16 The Commission therefore directed CAISO and the PTOs “to explain whether their 
tariffs remain just and reasonable without an application process, study procedures, and ongoing 
operational requirements that address the unique characteristics and challenges of providing 
transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads, or to propose appropriate tariff 
revisions.”17 
The Commission specifically identified four particular areas in which tariff provisions 
could be improved to address the process for providing transmission service to Eligible Customers 
on behalf of large loads: (1) definition of large load; (2) the application process for requesting 
transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads and the relevant study 
procedures; (3) ongoing operational requirements for transmission customers serving large loads; 
and (4) pro forma provisions in transmission service agreements, or other applicable agreements, 
to memorialize the terms of the operational requirements.18 
CAISO addresses each of these four topics and states in its Draft Final Proposal that it

B. 
Cost-Recovery Mechanisms that Prevent Cost-Shifts to Third Parties 
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found “that cost transparency 
measures are needed to provide stakeholders with sufficient information and visibility regarding 
the network upgrades, and their associated costs, needed to accommodate transmission service to 
Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads.”49 The Commission therefore called for measures to 
increase transparency.50  
In addition to improved transparency, the Commission cited the need for development of 
a pro forma cost recovery agreement between CAISO, the applicable PTO, and the Eligible 
Customer taking transmission service on behalf of large loads.51 The Show Cause Order identifies 
a concern that speculative transmission service requests could result in cost shifting among 
transmission customers, which could result, for example, if an “Eligible Customer takes less 
transmission service than anticipated because the large load operates at a lower demand than 
anticipated or fails to materialize at all” after network upgrades are already constructed to serve 
that load.52 The Commission urged that cost recovery agreements “would help ensure that the 
Eligible Customer makes a minimum contribution to the transmission owner’s transmission 
revenue requirement commensurate with the costs incurred to provide the requested transmission 
service, including the costs of any needed network upgrades.”53 The Show Cause Order therefore 
“preliminarily find[s] that … cost recovery agreements are necessary to help ensure that the
