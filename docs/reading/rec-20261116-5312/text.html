II. DESCRIPTION AND BASIS FOR THE TO TARIFF REVISIONS
Viridon developed the TO Tariff Revisions to respond to the concerns identified in the Show Cause Order regarding transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads. As described below, the proposed revisions address application and study procedures, ongoing operational requirements and service agreements, cost recovery, co-located generation, and flexible interim interconnection service. Viridon Path 15 has sought to align these provisions with CAISO’s proposed tariff framework and the approaches developed with other PTOs, while accounting for its role as a non-load-serving PTO and the need for coordination among affected transmission owners.

A. Application and Study Procedures Applicable to Eligible Customers Seeking
Transmission Service on Behalf of Large Loads
The Show Cause Order identifies that the Commission is concerned that CAISO and/or PTOs do not currently have tariff provisions expressly addressing how the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads will be studied in a timely fashion.16 The Commission therefore directed CAISO and the PTOs “to explain whether their tariffs remain just and reasonable without an application process, study procedures, and ongoing operational requirements that address the unique characteristics and challenges of providing transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads, or to propose appropriate tariff revisions.”17

The Commission specifically identified four particular areas in which tariff provisions could be improved to address the process for providing transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads: (1) definition of large load; (2) the application process for requesting transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads and the relevant study procedures; (3) ongoing operational requirements for transmission customers serving large loads; and (4) pro forma provisions in transmission service agreements, or other applicable agreements, to memorialize the terms of the operational requirements.18

4. Large Load Operational Requirements
The Commission also identified the potential need to provide for tariff provisions addressing “ongoing operational requirements for transmission customers serving large loads necessary to ensure reliable operation of the transmission system.”36

CAISO’s Draft Final Proposal notes in response that CAISO’s Large Load Technical Requirements Working Group (“LLTRWG”) has been developing requirements to address this issue.37 Based on the LLTRWG’s work and in coordination between CAISO and PTOs, CAISO proposes that its tariff will address: requirements on ramp-rates; frequency and voltage ride- through requirements, forecasts, telemetry, and other necessary operational data; communication protocols for remote disconnection; and maintaining communication channels between CAISO and transmission customers serving large loads.38

In the Show Cause Order, the Commission identified that flexible large loads can potentially defer the need for transmission network upgrades, which in turn would allow for faster interconnection and potentially avoid the need for transmission system build-out.64 The Commission further noted that the CAISO tariff does not currently “include transmission services that reflect the use of the transmission system by Eligible Customers on behalf of flexible large load, on both an interim and permanent basis,” and the Commission directed CAISO to explain whether the Tariff remains just and reasonable without an interim or permanent non-firm network transmission service, or in the alternative, “explain what specific changes to its existing transmission service framework would effectively provide comparable transmission service options for flexible large loads.”65
