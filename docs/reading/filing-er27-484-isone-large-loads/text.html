STANDARD OF REVIEW

the right to file rates and terms for services rendered with its assets.”11  Under section 205, the 
Commission “plays ‘an essentially passive and reactive’ role”’12 whereby it “can reject [a filing] only 
if it finds that the changes proposed by the public utility are not ‘just and reasonable.’”13  The 
Commission limits this inquiry “into whether the rates proposed by a utility are reasonable – and [this 
inquiry does not] extend to determining whether a proposed rate schedule is more or less reasonable 
than alternative rate designs.”14  The revisions filed herein “need not be the only reasonable

The Filing Parties submit that the Tariff Revisions address all of the Commission’s concerns in 
each of the five categories of reform of the Show Cause Order (i.e., efficient study processes, co-location 
rules, flexible load service, generation studies, and cost transparency).     
   
a. Efficient Study Processes 
To address this category of reform, the Tariff Revisions include a definition of Large Load, 
which is based on the characteristics of ISO-NE’s transmission system.  The definition includes a 50 
MW load size, and a 60 kV interconnection voltage threshold.27  The ISO also developed exclusions 
from the definition based on the characteristics of the load, exclusions in other regions, and stakeholder 
feedback.  Importantly, as described in Section IV.B.1 of this transmittal letter, the Tariff Revisions 
include language that accommodates the transition of a load into the definition of Large Load and 
accommodates existing commercial arrangements. 
 
In addition, the Tariff Revisions include an application process that applies to Large Load 
Eligible Customers seeking transmission service on behalf of Large Loads.  This process requires a non-
refundable application fee and sufficient readiness requirements to deter duplicative or speculative

developed transmission services that recognize the ability of Large Loads (including co-located loads) 
to limit withdrawal from the transmission system under certain conditions. 
 
c. Flexible Load Service 
The Tariff Revisions include three new transmission services: firm transmission service for 
Large Loads that bring their own new capacity and have completed network upgrades; interim 
transmission service (i.e.,, non-firm transmission service for Large Loads that are in the process of 
meeting the requirements for firm service), and non-firm transmission service for flexible Large Loads 
that are willing and able to limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.   
 
In building the framework for these new transmission services, ISO-NE was able to capitalize 
on its DARD model,28 which will be used to register Large Loads.  This model allows the use of current 
ISO forecasting, telemetry, and dispatch tools, requiring minimal adaptation and resulting in expedited 
application.  Flexibility for non-firm transmission services will be achieved through a price-based

Price-Based Re-Dispatch Mechanism 
Flexibility for non-firm transmission services (i.e., Interim Contract Demand Service and 
Non-Firm Contract Demand Service) will be achieved through a price-based re-dispatch 
mechanism.  Specifically, consistent with the “non-firm” flexibility of Interim Contract Demand 
Service and Non-Firm Contract Demand Service, all or a portion of a Large Load’s Interim 
Contract Demand Service and Non-Firm Contract Demand Service will be re-dispatched when the 
Large Load’s applicable LMP exceeds a Large Load Re-Dispatch Price of $250/MWh.  Using a 
static price streamlines implementation, and the proposed price reduces cost shifting in the energy 
markets.   
 
This method of providing non-firm transmission service is just and reasonable for several 
reasons.  First, transmission service over the regional transmission facilities in New England is provided 
to Transmission Customers based on economic dispatch rather than through an advance reservation 
system; thus, using a re-dispatch price to achieve non-firm transmission service for Large Load 
Transmission Customers is consistent with that construct while achieving the Commission’s 
expectation regarding non-firm transmission service.  Second, using a static price streamlines 
implementation and is manageable.  Third, the proposed price reduces cost shifting in the energy 
markets54 by re-dispatching large loads when the energy price is at a relatively high price for the region 
as the proposed re-dispatch price has occurred in approximately only 1% of hours in New England 
(based on hourly Load Zone prices) from 2021-2025.  Fourth, the proposed re-dispatch price is aligned 
with the penalty factor for Replacement Reserves; conceptually, since the region is willing to forgo

ineligible to provide Real-Time Reserves.  The revisions state that “[f]or a [DARD] associated with a

submitted Demand Bids without bids from providing reserves would require the ISO to create two 
different energy market models for Large Loads, which would significantly delay the proposed 
implementation date of June 1, 2028.  In the future, the ISO is open to exploring Tariff revisions that 
could allow a Large Load with demand flexibility to provide reserves, provided that the Large Load 
Transmission Customer submits Demand Bids and completes capability auditing.

New Attachment V of the OATT (Large Load Cost Recovery Agreement) 
Consistent with the Show Cause Order, the Tariff Revisions include a new pro forma cost 
recovery agreement designed to ensure that large loads “are ultimately responsible for the costs 
incurred to provide transmissions service, including the cost of network upgrades” and to remedy 
the Commission’s preliminary finding that the Tariff “lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the 
risk of cost shifting among transmission customers.”103  However, the PTOs believe that a cost 
recovery agreement among ISO-NE, the relevant transmission owner, and the Eligible Customer 
taking transmission service on behalf of large loads is an insufficient guarantor of preventing the 
cost-shifting concerns raised by the Commission in the Show Cause Order.  As the Commission 
is aware and notes throughout the Show Cause Order, Eligible Customers may not be (and often 
are not) the large loads actually interconnecting to the transmission system but instead may be the 
applicable electric distribution company (“EDC”) or load-serving entity applying for service on 
behalf of the large load.  In the case where a cost recovery agreement is solely among the Eligible 
Customer, applicable PTO, and ISO-NE, a default or failure to come online by the large load itself 
would not adequately protect either the EDCs or other wholesale transmission customers from cost 
shifting for the reasons described below. 
 
Accordingly, the PTOs are proposing a pro forma Large Load Cost Recovery Agreement 
(“LLCRA”)104 as Attachment V to the OATT among the Large Load Interconnecting 
Transmission Owner (“LLITO”), the Large Load Eligible Customer, and the Large Load Entity 
(the entity owning the proposed Large Load Facility).  Under the LLCRA, no transmission service, 
wholesale service, distribution service, or other electrical service, FERC-jurisdictional or 
otherwise, is being provided.  Rather, the LLCRA governs the construction of, cost responsibility 
for, and crediting of revenues associated with Commission-jurisdictional transmission facilities 
that are used to provide Commission-jurisdictional transmission service to Large Load Facilities.  
As explained below, a contract need not itself provide jurisdictional service to be subject to section 
205 of the FPA; the LLCRA is jurisdictional because it directly and significantly affects 
Commission-jurisdictional transmission rates and services.  The LLCRA establishes the terms and 
conditions under which the LLITO will construct and the Large Load Entity will compensate the 
LLITO for both sole-use facilities necessary to interconnect the Large Load Facility (“Direct

b. 
Cost Allocation Mechanisms of the LLCRA 
 
The LLCRA utilizes four mechanisms to ensure that the costs of serving the Large Load Facility 
are not passed on to other transmission customers: (1) direct assignment of costs, both initial and 
ongoing, for the Direct Assignment Facilities; (2) payment of the Cost Recovery Charge when necessary 
to eliminate any transmission rate increases caused by the interconnection of the Large Load Facility 
and the construction of LLITO’s Network Upgrades; (3) payment of an Exit Fee, as applicable, upon 
termination of the LLCRA; and (4) adequate Credit Support to cover the cost of the Direct Assignment 
Facilities and LLITO’s Network Upgrades prior to commercial operation and the Exit Fee and all 
associated charges following commercial operation.  If the Large Load Eligible Customer and the Large 
Load Entity are the same entity, that entity is responsible under the LLCRA for these payments and the 
Credit Support. 
 
i. 
Direct Assignment Facilities 
 
The Direct Assignment Facilities, analogous to Interconnecting Transmission Owner’s 
Interconnection Facilities in a generator context, are sole-use facilities which will be constructed by the 
LLITO and paid for on a reimbursable basis by the Large Load Entity, consistent with the Commission’s 
cost-causation principles for sole-use facilities.  Following the construction of the Direct Assignment 
Facilities, the Large Load Entity will also pay for operation, maintenance, repair, and replacement of 
such facilities consistent with the individual LLITO’s methodology currently in place for analogous 
generator interconnection facilities.110

ii. 
Cost Recovery Charge 
 
The Cost Recovery Charge is the primary mechanism under which the PTOs will prevent cost 
shifts to other transmission customers caused by the interconnection of the Large Load Facility.  Under 
Article 10 and Appendix C-2 of the LLCRA, the Cost Recovery Charge will be billed monthly to the 
Large Load Entity and will be calculated in the following manner: 
Annual Cost Recovery Charge = Large Load Revenue Requirement – Applicable 
Transmission Service Payments 
The Large Load Revenue Requirement is the gross cost of LLITO’s Network Upgrades multiplied by 
the applicable Regional or Local Carrying Charge Factor and represents the revenue requirement impact 
of those upgrades.  The applicable transmission service payments are the payments made by the Large 
Load Eligible Customer for the applicable transmission service reserved on behalf of the Large Load 
Facility (FCDS, NFCDS, or ICDS).  This difference is the net impact of the upgrades on transmission 
rates, and payment (and associated crediting) of this amount holds other transmission customers 
harmless from the rate impacts of LLITO’s Network Upgrades. 
 
iii. 
Exit Fee 
 
In the event the LLCRA terminates and uncovered rate impacts remain for LLITO’s Network 
Upgrades, the Large Load Entity will pay the Exit Fee described in Article 10 and Appendix C-2.  The 
Exit Fee is equal to the Net Book Value of LLITO’s Network Upgrades along with Decommissioning 
Costs, and will, like the Cost Recovery Charge, be credited against the applicable transmission revenue 
requirement to avoid cost shifting to transmission customers. 
 
iv. 
Credit Support 
 
The LLCRA also requires the Large Load Entity to post sufficient financial assurance (“Credit 
Support”) both prior to commercial operation to cover the costs of the Direct Assignment Facilities and 
LLITO’s Network Upgrades111 as well as subsequent to commercial operation to cover the cost of the 
Exit Fee and any ongoing amounts due under the LLCRA.112  Due to differing financial assurance 
policies among the PTOs, the specific form, process, and amounts for Credit Support will be described 
in a PTO-specific Appendix D to be negotiated by the Parties, but nevertheless complying with Article 
10 of the LLCRA.  Provision of Credit Support throughout the term of the LLCRA will ensure that the 
LLITO is fully covered in the event of default or insolvency by the Large Load Entity, and in turn 
ensures that no costs caused by such default or insolvency will be passed on to other customers.

d. 
The LLCRA is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction 
Although the LLCRA does not itself provide transmission or interconnection service, it is 
subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under section 205 because it directly and significantly affects 
Commission-jurisdictional transmission rates and services.  The LLCRA allocates and recovers the 
costs of Commission-jurisdictional transmission facilities (LLITO’s Network Upgrades and the Direct 
Assignment Facilities) that are constructed to enable jurisdictional transmission service to Large Load 
Facilities, and it requires that the resulting payments be credited against the LLITO’s Commission-
jurisdictional transmission revenue requirement.  The Commission has already found that cost recovery 
agreements of this kind “directly affect Commission-jurisdictional transmission rates” and “should be 
filed with the Commission.”129  At the same time, the LLCRA does not impermissibly intrude on 
jurisdictional areas solely reserved to the states, including retail sales of electricity and siting and 
construction decisions associated with load and transmission facilities.  No jurisdictional concerns for 
the LLCRA were raised as part of the NEPOOL stakeholder process.  Accordingly, it is within the 
Commission’s authority to accept and approve the LLCRA as just and reasonable consistent with 
section 205 of the FPA.

Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) framework, in part because certain aspects of the proposal 
“appear[ed] to present an impermissible intrusion on retail rate regulation.”178  The Commission 
identified three defects in the proposed tariff language: provisions that “appear[ed] to place obligations 
directly on Eligible New Large Loads, which are, for the most part, retail customers”; provisions that

Commission objected that the IRAS tariff created “a framework whereby retail customers will be paid 
by other retail customers” for a purported wholesale service, without specifying which wholesale 
customer would be paid or how.182  The LLCRA’s payment stream is entirely different and fully

The LLCRA addresses the concerns identified in the Show

The PTOs anticipate that the Commission may ask why the LLCRA departs from the three-
party construct described in the Show Cause Order.  As explained above, the Show Cause Order did not 
foreclose alternatives.  It expressly sought briefing on substituting an agreement with the large load 
customer where that agreement affords comparable or greater protection, and it invited regionally 
tailored Section 205 proposals.189  Individual Commissioners reinforced that invitation, noting that “the 
door remains open to other creative, regionally-specific ideas to protect consumers,”190 and that “cost-
recovery agreements between a transmission owner and a large load customer itself could satisfy an 
Eligible Customer’s financial security requirements.”191  The LLCRA retains the Large Load Eligible 
Customer as a party, so that the Eligible Customer’s tariff obligations, information, and transmission 
service payments remain integrated with the cost recovery mechanism, while placing the financial 
obligation on the entity that causes the costs. 
 
The PTOs also note that, unlike the individually negotiated agreements discussed above, 
which the Commission accepted on the basis that the Mobile-Sierra presumption applied,192 the 
LLCRA is a pro forma agreement of general applicability.  The Commission will therefore review the 
LLCRA under the ordinary just-and-reasonable standard, and any non-conforming LLCRA must be 
filed pursuant to Article 3.  This structure provides the transparency and Commission oversight that 
members of the Commission have identified as important when relying on cost recovery agreements 
to protect other customers.193
