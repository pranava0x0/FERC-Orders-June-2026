II. COMMENTS
A. Develop Pro Forma Co-location Interconnection Procedures and Agreements
The comments across the three dockets show that Commission guidance is needed, and it is needed now.  Data center developers, generation developers, and system planners cannot make long-term decisions without understanding what co-location arrangements the Commission will accept.  Ideally, Commission guidance would come in the form of pro forma procedures and agreements governing co-location arrangements.  Standardizing procedures and agreements would give developers and planners certainty, reduce opportunities for undue discrimination or

While standardization is beneficial, it is equally important that the Commission build into any pro forma procedures and agreements flexibility to allow for different co-location configurations and arrangements.  Not all large loads are the same, so not all co-location configurations will be the same either.17  This could be accomplished by allowing different paths that lead to different studies and services for different types of co-location configurations.  The type of co-location arrangement can then lead to different charges.18  A pro forma process and agreements should allow for at least the following three configurations: (i) load and generation are fully islanded from the electric grid, with no service from the grid; (ii) flexible loads or demand response loads; and (iii) loads that will require backup grid service, such as a behind the meter arrangement, with associated studies and costs for each.  Furthermore, the resulting services and charges applicable to each of these configurations should impose charges on load and generation based only on the transmission services used and benefits received from the grid.

B. Rules Should Incentivize Co-Located Load to Bring Its Own Generation
In its answer, PJM recognized that large load bringing its own generation has benefits.23 While large load should not be required to bring its own generation, BrightNight agrees that there are significant benefits when large load brings new generation with it, particularly if that generation is co-located with the large load.  Large load bringing new generation promotes resource adequacy and avoids new constraints or congestion on the transmission system. In its answer, PJM discusses ways to incentivize large loads to bring their own generation, including expedited interconnection studies.24  SEIA outlined a process that incentivizes load to bring its own generation in its Post-Technical Conference Comments.25  An expedited or fast-tracked interconnection process appears to be appropriate.  PJM should be required to net the large load and generation, resulting in faster study time and less impact on the transmission system and load forecast.  It could be handled similarly to the surplus interconnection process, where the load and generation configuration can be screened for potential material impacts to the native transmission system or adjoining systems.  If there are material impacts that cannot be resolved among the affected systems, then the load and generation will be shifted to the typical interconnection and transmission service request processes.  BrightNight encourages the Commission, PJM, and other stakeholders to incorporate in the pro forma co-location procedures incentives for large loads to bring co-located generation.
