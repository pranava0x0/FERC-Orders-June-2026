ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23 · SEIA and Advanced Energy United

Protest of the Solar Energy Industries Association and Advanced Energy United

The Solar Energy Industries Association and Advanced Energy United protest MISO's LARS filing as unduly discriminatory because only the Associated Transmission Customer, often a vertically integrated utility, can submit an application. They also object to generator stranding risk and LARS and DPP coordination.

Asks FERC to: Reject LARS with guidance to let any qualified participant apply directly; alternatively accept it and clarify that the application role must be administered without discrimination.

Answers the order. Protests MISO's LARS filing, offered as its answer to the E-8 show cause order on electrically proximate load, as discriminatory in who may apply and uncertain for generators and queued projects.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. Only the LARS Associated Transmission Customer, often a load-serving entity, can start an application.
    The LARS Associated Transmission Customer is the only party that may initiate the LARS Application. PDF p. 3
  2. They say independent producers can join only if the utility competitor lets them, with no duty to submit.
    Yes, independent power producers can participate in the LARS process PDF p. 5
  3. They ask FERC to reject LARS and direct a refiling that lets any qualified market participant apply directly to MISO.
    reject the LARS proposal, on the grounds that the proposal is unduly discriminatory PDF p. 2
  4. A generator whose paired load leaves in the first fifteen years can lose its LLGIA and must re-enter the DPP.
    the generator's LLGIA ultimately will be terminated and the generator must obtain new Interconnection Service through the Definitive Planning Process (DPP) to continue operating. PDF p. 7
  5. MISO offers only a transition period of up to one year, which they contrast with SPP's five-year HILLGA approach.
    MISO proposes only a temporary transition period of up to one year PDF p. 8
  6. They say LARS studies leave out queued DPP projects without executed GIAs, so the two processes use different assumptions.
    MISO specifically states that LARS studies will not include interconnection requests currently in the generator interconnection queue that have not executed GIAs. PDF p. 10
  7. In the alternative, they ask FERC to clarify that the Associated Transmission Customer must apply the same standards and timelines it uses for its own projects.
    the Commission expects the LARS Associated Transmission Customer to apply the same submission standards and timelines it applies to its own similarly situated projects. PDF p. 13
