COMMENTS OF SPARKFUND ON MISO’S INFORMATIONAL REPORT
​
Spark Community Investment Fund (d/b/a Sparkfund) appreciates the informational report filed by the Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) describing the efforts MISO is undertaking to integrate large loads. Sparkfund submits these comments to provide further information on how MISO’s existing and pending DER models are enabling DERs to be a means to integrate large loads, and how this model can be further replicated throughout MISO. Distributed resources are increasingly providing capacity to integrate large loads, through utility programs and MISO’s existing participation models. This distributed capacity potential is documented through Rewiring America’s Homegrown Energy blueprint;1 the recent 16 GW nationwide VPP announced by Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home;2 and Voltus’s Bring Your Own Capacity model.3 While MISO diligently works towards full implementation of FERC Order 2222 in 2029/2030, aggregators and utilities within MISO are able to deploy distributed resources as capacity today using the existing participation models.

Commission recently approved Xcel Energy’s Capacity*Connect program, to deploy up to 200 MW of front-of-the-meter, distribution-sited 1-3 MW battery energy storage systems (BESS) by the end of 2028,4 with the potential for 500 MW by 2031.5 The Capacity*Connect program is a form of distributed capacity procurement (DCP), in which the BESS would be owned and operated by Xcel, and intentionally sited where the resources could best benefit the distribution grid.6 The BESS will use MISO’s DRR Type 1 Participation model, which allows the resources to participate in MISO’s energy and ancillary service markets.7 DRRs can act as capacity resources that count towards an LSE’s Planning Reserve Margin Requirement (PRMR), just like a generator.8

Xcel’s Capacity*Connect program illustrates several points about distributed capacity supporting large load integration in MISO. First, MISO’s existing participation models provide several options for utilities and third-parties within MISO to deploy distributed energy resources that, in aggregate, reach power plant scale.9 Second, Capacity*Connect provides a mechanism to deploy new capacity to serve data centers while also delivering localized, distribution-level benefits: Google’s new Pine Island, MN data center facility has entered an Electric Service Agreement that includes $50 million towards Xcel’s Capacity*Connect battery portfolio in exchange for matched capacity credits.  Google’s purchase of capacity credits from Capacity*Connect offsets a portion of Xcel’s total cost.10

Third, when well-designed programs align with functioning markets the combination can be net negative on residential electricity bills.  Xcel’s compliance filing calculates that Capacity*Connect will deliver average residential bill savings of approximately $0.13 per month over the twenty-year analysis period, with Google’s $50 million contribution further increasing those savings by another $0.04 per month, to approximately $0.17 per month.11 Capacity*Connect therefore demonstrates that distributed capacity can be deployed to integrate large loads while simultaneously lowering residential bills.

Fourth, distributed capacity could meaningfully contribute to MISO’s resource adequacy: multiple utilities deploying 200-500 MW of utility-owned and operated distributed capacity could amount to gigawatts across the MISO footprint. Other utilities within the MISO footprint have taken notice of Capacity*Connect and are actively exploring and establishing distributed capacity programs of their own. This exploration is taking shape across multiple regulatory and commercial pathways, including existing integrated resource planning (IRP) processes, both existing and newly-proposed large-load tariffs designed to serve data center customers, and emerging bring-you-own-capacity (BYOC) structures. These efforts are particularly notable given the potential for distributed batteries to value-stack across both wholesale market opportunities and distribution-level benefits, an opportunity that Xcel has detailed for the Minnesota Public Utilities Commission during its review of the Capacity*Connect.12 Finally, distributed capacity is a way to “right size” the capacity to meet load growth needs. For example, 127 MW of distributed capacity could be deployed as a bridge solution while traditional thermal generation is being built.
