I. Introduction and Summary of Filing
The proposed revisions establish a transparent, company-specific mechanism for LS Power Grid to recover the costs of transmission upgrades that are identified as necessary, in whole or in part, to provide transmission service to an Eligible Customer6 on behalf of a Large Load (“Large Load-Driven Upgrades”). The revisions include a pro forma Large Load Cost Recovery Agreement (“CRA”), revisions to the Formula Rate Template (“Template”) and Formula Rate Implementation Protocols (“Protocols”), cost-allocation provisions, and new Rate Schedule 10 provisions governing NYISO billing, collection, security, remittance, data exchange, and formula rate treatment of CRA amounts.

The filing responds to the Commission’s preliminary concerns regarding potential cost shifting by assigning the wholesale payment obligation to the Eligible Customer that takes or requests transmission service on behalf of the Large Load Facility and by requiring Construction Security, Operational Security, NYISO billing and collection, Security-draw procedures, and direct collection rights. The framework is designed to mitigate the risk that other customers bear costs associated with a Large Load Facility that is delayed, reduced, or does not materialize, while preserving NYISO’s existing roles and LS Power Grid’s Formula Rate recovery.

No Large Load-Driven Upgrade is listed in proposed Table 1 as of the requested effective date. Accordingly, the filing does not change currently effective Regulated Transmission Facilities Charge (“RTFC”) rates under the NYISO OATT or impose a Large Load Charge on any customer upon effectiveness. The proposed revisions also make conforming corrections to existing Formula Rate Template and Protocol provisions, including provisions governing the cost-of-capital and true-up calculations. Those corrections do not revise the currently effective RTFC; they will be reflected, as applicable, in future Annual Projections, Annual Updates, and True- Up Adjustments under the Formula Rate. An individual Large Load-Driven Upgrade may be included in the RTFC only after a separate FPA section 205 filing adds that upgrade to Table 1 and the Commission accepts the designation.

II. Background
A. LS Power Grid and its Rate Schedule 10 Recovery
LS Power Grid is a non-incumbent Transmission Owner in the NYISO region. LS Power Grid and the New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) developed Segment A of the AC Transmission Public Policy Transmission Need, a 345 kV project between the Marcy/Edic and New Scotland substations (“AC Transmission Project”), which is now in service as part of Central East Energy Connect. LS Power Grid has no franchised retail service territory and no Transmission Service Charge under Attachment H of the NYISO OATT. LS Power Grid currently recovers its Commission-accepted revenue requirement exclusively under Rate Schedule 10 through its formula rate and the associated RTFC.

B. Show Cause Order and Stakeholder Process
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, instituting a section 206 proceeding in Docket No. EL26-69-000 and identifying concerns about cost shifting, transparency, minimum-revenue commitments, credit support, and the rate treatment of large load payments.7 The Commission later granted abeyance through November 16, 2026, for respondents that make timely section 205 filings addressing its preliminary findings.8

LS Power Grid participated in NYISO and stakeholder discussions concerning large load integration and cost recovery. Those discussions confirmed that a transmission owner without retail customers needs a wholesale mechanism to protect its other transmission customers when a Large Load Facility requires a Large Load-Driven Upgrade to that transmission owner’s facilities. LS Power Grid’s proposal is designed to operate alongside NYISO’s study and interconnection procedures. It does not displace the separate arrangements that an Eligible Customer must enter into with the owner of an Affected System for an Affected System Upgrade.9

B. Pro Forma CRA
The pro forma CRA and Limited Acknowledgment are included as Appendix 1 to Attachment 1 to Rate Schedule 10, Section 6.10.7.5.1. The CRA is entered into by LS Power Grid and the Eligible Customer. The Large Load Customer executes a Limited Acknowledgment but does not become a party to the Eligible Customer’s wholesale payment obligations. NYISO is not a party to the CRA; its billing, collection, security, remittance, and data functions arise under the Rate Schedule 10 Large Load Provisions.10

For each Covered Upgrade, the CRA establishes a fixed Annual Minimum Transmission Revenue Contribution (“Annual MTRC”) under the Schedule 5 MTRC Calculation Template. The Annual MTRC is initially estimated and is redetermined to the Final Annual MTRC on the Final MTRC Determination Date, which occurs no later than one year after the applicable Upgrade In-Service Date after actual capital costs are known and accounted for. Schedule 5 uses the applicable capital structure, return on equity, cost of debt, tax, depreciation, and expense-allocation inputs to establish the levelized Fixed Charge Rate and Final Annual MTRC. MTRC Term begins on the actual Upgrade In-Service Date and ends on the day immediately preceding its twentieth anniversary, with the first and final Rate Years prorated. The CRA does not reset the final contractual Annual MTRC through ordinary annual formula rate updates, except for the limited prospective adjustments expressly identified in the CRA.11

The twenty-year MTRC Term is a defined contractual revenue-support period, not the depreciable life of a Covered Upgrade. Schedule 5 establishes a levelized Annual MTRC for that period, while the Covered Upgrade remains subject to ordinary Formula Rate depreciation and other cost-of-service treatment. The term provides a substantial, predictable period of revenue responsibility for the Large Load-Driven Upgrade and is reinforced by Operational Security based on the remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility and a Termination Fee equal to that remaining responsibility less any Supplemental MTRC already billed and remaining unpaid upon an early termination or permanent cessation of service. The excluded unpaid bills remain separately payable.12

The Eligible Customer owes a Supplemental MTRC to the extent Qualifying Transmission Revenues attributable to the Large Load Facility are less than the applicable Annual MTRC. The CRA provides for annual reconciliation. Construction Security protects pre-service commitments and cancellation exposure. After an upgrade enters service, Operational Security equals the nominal remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility, subject to Qualifying Retail Security Support that is legally available for the corresponding wholesale obligation. A termination or permanent cessation after in-service triggers a Termination Fee equal to the Remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility less any Supplemental MTRC already billed and remaining unpaid as of the effective termination or cessation date, as provided in CRA Section 3.3.4. Those unpaid bills remain separately payable as Large Load Charges. NYISO’s receipt of the Termination Fee in full discharges only the remaining responsibility included in the fee and does not discharge the excluded unpaid bills. The Termination Fee is an accelerated contractual termination charge, not a penalty. Rather than producing a one-time Formula Rate Credit, the Termination Fee Balance is held by NYISO and remitted as Annual Termination Fee Remittances over the remaining MTRC Term, so the resulting Formula Rate Credits follow the annual schedule that would have applied absent termination.13

The CRA requires Operational Security equal to one hundred percent (100%) of the nominal Remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility. The Commission preliminarily found that “an Eligible Customer may include a credit support or other financial security posted by a large load as part of a retail agreement to avoid creating duplicative credit support requirements.”14 To avoid duplicative collateral, the direct Operational Security requirement may be reduced dollar-for-dollar by qualifying retail security support only where the Eligible Customer delivers a current Retail Security Certification. The certification must confirm that the retail Large Load Customer has posted security available to the Eligible Customer for the corresponding wholesale obligations and that the Eligible Customer has enforceable draw or application rights, or the benefit of a binding undertaking, permitting it to use the proceeds for that purpose. Qualifying retail security support does not reduce the Eligible Customer’s underlying payment obligations, does not apply to Construction Security or Pre-In-Service Termination Costs, and is subject to notice and verification requirements if the support is reduced, drawn, expires, is not renewed, or otherwise may be impaired.15

LS Power Grid recognizes that, after further stakeholder experience and discussion, a lower Operational Security requirement may be appropriate in defined circumstances where an alternative credit support arrangement provides equivalent protection against a cost shift. The Show Cause Order also invited further consideration of alternative mechanisms that could provide comparable or greater protection against cost shifting.16 This filing nonetheless adopts a conservative requirement of Operational Security equal to one hundred percent (100%) of the nominal Remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility, subject only to the limited treatment of qualifying retail security support described above. LS Power Grid intends to work with NYISO and interested stakeholders to evaluate whether, and under what objectively defined circumstances, a different security level or alternative credit support structure could provide comparable protection. Any future change to the security requirement would be submitted to the Commission for review and acceptance under section 205 of the Federal Power Act.

The CRA does not itself determine whether a facility is directly assigned or included in rate base. That determination is made under the applicable NYISO tariff, study process, and interconnection arrangements. Direct Assignment Facilities and other facilities whose costs are directly assigned to, and paid by, the Eligible Customer and are not included in LS Power Grid’s rate base are excluded from the definition of Large Load-Driven Upgrades and from the MTRC framework.19

Covered Upgrades are separately identified in LS Power Grid’s Formula Rate and use the capital structure and generally applicable return components applicable to LS Power Grid under Rate Schedule 10, including any Commission-approved generally applicable return on equity incentive, as applicable. Covered Upgrades do not receive return on equity incentives that are specific to the AC Transmission Project or become subject to the AC Transmission Project’s Competitive Bid Concession, cost cap, or other project-specific cost-containment commitments.20

D. NYISO Billing Security and Remittance Functions
New Section 6.10.3.7 confirms that the Attachment-level Large Load Provisions are part of Rate Schedule 10, directs NYISO to perform the functions assigned to it under those provisions, distinguishes Large Load Charges from RTFC charges and revenues, and confirms that NYISO does not become a party to a Large Load CRA. New Section 6.10.7.4 requires execution of an applicable CRA and delivery of Construction Security before LS Power Grid undertakes specified construction commitments. It directs NYISO to bill customer-specific Large Load Charges based on LS Power Grid Charge Statements; hold and administer CRA Security; draw and apply Security under defined conditions; hold and remit Termination Fees; provide load and settlement data; and remit collected amounts to LS Power Grid. The provision separately identifies these charges from RTFC charges and preserves applicable NYISO billing and dispute procedures.21

The proposed Template and Protocols provide the rate treatment and supporting workpapers. In the Annual Projection, Large Load CRA Revenue consists of projected Supplemental MTRC, including any prior-period Supplemental MTRC true-up adjustment, and projected Annual Termination Fee Remittances. In the Annual Update, the Formula Rate Credit reflects Actual Large Load CRA Revenue actually received by LS Power Grid during the Rate Year, including Supplemental MTRC payments collected and remitted by NYISO, Applied Security Proceeds applied and remitted to LS Power Grid, direct recoveries from an Eligible Customer applied to Supplemental MTRC obligations, and Annual Termination Fee Remittances. The Annual Update replaces the projected credit with actual cash receipts, and any difference is addressed through the Formula Rate true-up mechanism in the next Rate Year, with the applicable Formula Rate interest treatment. An Eligible Customer remains liable for all unpaid Large Load Charges, and the CRA’s Security and collection provisions are designed to mitigate the risk of nonpayment.22

IV. The Proposed Revisions Are Just and Reasonable
The proposal materially mitigates the cost-shifting concern identified in the Show Cause Order. Before in-service, Construction Security and termination-cost provisions protect against cancellation and related exposure. After an upgrade enters service, the Annual MTRC, Supplemental MTRC, Required Operational Security, and termination provisions establish a defined revenue responsibility associated with the service request that caused the upgrade. Required Operational Security equals one hundred percent (100%) of the nominal Remaining Cumulative Revenue Responsibility, subject to any qualifying retail-security support recognized under the CRA. NYISO billing, mandatory Security draws, and LS Power Grid’s direct collection rights further protect against nonpayment.

The proposal also supplies the transparency and crediting mechanisms the Commission identified. The pro forma CRA in Appendix 1, Table 1, Schedules 1 and 5, the Template and Protocol workpapers, and the Annual Projection and Annual Update provide a traceable record of the relevant upgrades, customer obligations, credits, billings, and reconciliations. The illustrative MTRC Calculation (Exhibit No. GNY-101), example Reconciliation Statement (Exhibit No. GNY-102), and example Charge Statement (Exhibit No. GNY-103) further demonstrate the calculation and administration of these requirements. Customer-specific information is protected under CRA § 6.12 and Protocols § 8, while the required public information remains available in aggregated or redacted form as appropriate.

The proposed use of the existing Rate Schedule 10 RTFC allocation does not assign responsibility for a Large Load-Driven Upgrade to Responsible LSEs on the theory that those LSEs caused the underlying Large Load Facility or its service request. Rather, once the Commission accepts a designation of a Covered Upgrade in Table 1, the existing RTFC provides the established mechanism for collecting LS Power Grid’s Commission-authorized revenue requirement for that transmission facility. The CRA separately requires the Eligible Customer serving the Large Load Facility to provide the Annual MTRC through Qualifying Transmission Revenues and Supplemental MTRC payments, and the Formula Rate credits Actual Large Load CRA Revenue dollar-for-dollar against LS Power Grid’s revenue requirement. Thus, the RTFC allocation supplies the collection path, while the CRA framework assigns the relevant payment obligation to the Eligible Customer and materially mitigates the associated risk through Security, NYISO billing and collection, and direct enforcement rights.

Finally, the proposal is appropriately tailored to LS Power Grid. It provides a cost- recovery and security path for a non-incumbent Transmission Owner with no retail customers or Attachment H Transmission Service Charge. It does not disturb the current AC Transmission Project allocation, existing RTFC recovery, NYISO study authority, or the retail arrangements between an Eligible Customer and a Large Load Customer.
