MOTION TO INTERVENE AND REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION OF 
TALEN ENERGY CORPORATION 
 
Talen Energy Corporation (“Talen”) hereby submits this motion to intervene and 
respectfully requests the Commission clarify one provision of the Reliability Backstop 
Procurement (“RBP”) filing submitted by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”).  PJM states in its 
transmittal letter that the RBP filing is “not intended to disrupt existing commercial agreements,”1 
which is consistent with the directive from the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” 
or “Commission”).  Yet, PJM’s proposal will unequivocally diminish the economic benefit secured 
by new load under previously negotiated contracts for the purchase and sale of capacity from 
existing generation resources.  Accordingly, the Commission should clarify the proposed Tariff 
revisions at Attachment DD, section 18.3(a) to include a reduction to the Electric Distributor’s 
zone/area’s pro-rata allocation of the Initial RBP Target MW equal to the contracted amount of 
large load that has executed a power purchase agreement (“PPA”) or similar supply contract with 
an existing generation resource prior to the January 16, 2026 Statement of Principles.2

I. 
MOTION TO INTERVENE 
Pursuant to Rule 214 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,3 Talen 
respectfully submits this motion to intervene in the above-captioned proceeding.  The formal legal 
name of Talen is Talen Energy Corporation.  Talen is a corporation organized and existing under 
the laws of Delaware.  Talen maintains its principal place of business at 2929 Allen Parkway, 22nd 
Floor, Houston, Texas 77019. Talen is a leading independent power producer and energy 
infrastructure company which owns and operates approximately 15.7 gigawatts of power 
infrastructure in the United States.  Talen produces and sells electricity, capacity, and ancillary 
services into wholesale U.S. power markets, including and primarily in PJM.  A subsidiary of 
Talen has entered into a bilateral contract for the sale of electric power from an existing generation 
resource to new load that will come online during the Delivery Years set forth in the PJM RBP 
Filing.  Accordingly, the actions taken by the Commission in this matter will directly affect the 
interests of Talen.  Talen has a direct and material interest in the instant proceeding that no other 
party can adequately represent.  Talen therefore requests that the Commission grant this motion to 
intervene and permit Talen to participate in this proceeding with full rights as a party thereto. 
II. 
REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION    
A. 
The proposed Tariff revisions do not protect existing commercial agreements 
from disruption.  
The protection of existing private contracts is fundamental to the operations of state and 
federal government, including FERC.4  The Commission has previously acknowledged that 
“uncertainties regarding rate stability and contract sanctity can have a chilling effect on

The past few years have presented historic opportunities and novel challenges.  Although 
all market participants could have benefitted from regulatory certainty during this period, it was 
not an option to wait idly for PJM and the Commission to enact generalized market reform that 
would adequately address the evolving market dynamics and increasing load demand.7  Large load 
developers were faced with three options:  bring their own power, rely on the market, or fix power 
costs through a bilateral PPA.  Speed to power is paramount for new large load customers.  
Accordingly, certain developers chose to fix their costs—or so they thought—by means of a 
bilateral PPA.  These contracts were executed at a time when the RBP was not known to be on the 
horizon.  The PJM RBP Filing, without clarification, treats developers that chose Option No. 3 
(bilateral PPAs) the same as developers that chose Option No. 2 (rely on the market).  Such an 
outcome upsets the reasonable, investment-backed expectations that FERC has historically sought 
to protect.   
The importance of reliance interests and the risk of chilling investment due to regulatory 
instability are not lost on this Commission.  That is why Chairman Laura Swett recently explained

Under proposed Attachment DD, section 18.3(a), each Electric Distributor zone/area’s 
allocation of the Initial RBP Target may be reduced by the quantity of “Qualifying RBP Offset 
UCAP MW” or by an opt-out provision.10  This does not account for new load that has entered 
into a bilateral PPA with an existing generation resource.  As a result, each Electric Distributor 
zone/area will continue to be allocated an inflated share of the Initial RBP Target.  Such new load 
will be subject to RBP Charges, assuming states follow the Commission’s directive to implement 
RBP cost allocation mechanisms.11  Therefore, this “new load” must pay incremental capacity 
charges resulting from the RBP, thereby minimizing the economic benefit the load freely 
negotiated under its fixed-cost, bilateral PPA (essentially, paying twice for capacity).

B. 
The Commission should clarify Attachment DD, section 18.3(a) to further 
reduce each Electric Distributor zone/area’s allocation of the Initial RBP 
Target. 
PJM has expressly consented “to the Commission’s exercise of authority to modify and/or 
sever the proposed Tariff and RAA language, as needed and permitted under the FPA and NRG 
Power Marketing, where such modification is consistent with PJM’s overarching objectives in this 
filing.”12  If the Commission exercises its authority to modify the PJM RBP Filing, then PJM 
requested that “the Commission expressly prescribe the revised rate or rule in its order.”13  The 
Commission should do so here.  Specifically, Talen requests that the Commission require the 
following underlined language to Attachment DD, section 18.3(a) in its approval of the PJM RBP 
Filing:
