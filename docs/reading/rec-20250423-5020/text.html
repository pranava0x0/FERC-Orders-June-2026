COMMENTS OF ENGIE NORTH AMERICA INC.

Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure, 18 C.F.R. § 385.211, ENGIE North America Inc., (“ENGIE”), respectfully submits these comments in response to the filings dated March 24, 2025 of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) (“PJM Filing”), the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, and Public Service Gas & Electric made in response to the Commission’s February 20, 2025 Order Instituting Proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act and Consolidating with Other Proceedings (the “Show Cause Order”).1 ENGIE’s comments recognize the significant efforts by PJM in its filing while also emphasizing the acute need for clear Commission guidance on co- location arrangements, the need to safeguard flexibility in PJM’s tariff, and that fairness dictates compensating co-location arrangements for benefits provided to the grid.

II. INTRODUCTION
ENGIE North America is a subsidiary of ENGIE SA, based in Paris, France. Globally, ENGIE SA is a leading independent power producer, operating 106 GW of installed generation capacity, including 38 GW of renewable energy generation throughout 31 countries. ENGIE also operates the largest natural gas distribution network in Europe, owns 2,485 miles of transmission lines, and is a leading energy supplier globally to commercial and industrial customers. With over 2,000 employees in the United States, ENGIE has been actively engaged in the domestic power industry for decades. ENGIE operates 12 GW of power generation, primarily solar and wind power, and battery energy storage. ENGIE has several major partnerships with universities and medical campuses to manage their energy usage and decarbonization efforts and operates a large electricity and natural gas supply business for large commercial and industrial customers. ENGIE’s retail electricity business leads the way with innovative and integrated renewable energy and risk management solutions in 14 retail electric choice markets to commercial and industrial customers. ENGIE is also engaged in developing hydrogen projects in the Gulf Coast region. ENGIE SA is a partner in the Ocean Winds global joint venture with EDP Renewables which is developing offshore wind projects in the United States off the coasts of Massachusetts, New York and New Jersey, and California.

III. COMMENTS
A. PJM’s filing appropriately acknowledges the acute need for new co-located large load and generating facility participation models.

The significant growth of data center, and other large load development, is reshaping the energy landscape, driving the need for changes to existing laws and regulations, infrastructure development and planning, and electricity market participation arrangements. Although in PJM certain frameworks already exist to enable the co-location of large loads and generation, PJM correctly recognizes that “clarifications and enhancements could well be in order given that this is an evolving area and Tariff provisions did not contemplate the type of arrangements at issue in this docket or their implications.”2

ENGIE agrees. The magnitude and pace at which large loads are seeking to interconnect, and the need for new commercial and technical arrangements to satisfy the parties’ diverse requirements, portend the creation of new co-location frameworks.

In addition to confirming PJM’s three existing co-location market participation models, PJM’s filing to the Commission’s Show Cause Order includes several potential new options for pairing generation with large loads.3 Specifically, new Options 4 through 8 address some key aspects of co-location arrangements beyond what is specifically permitted in PJM’s tariff. ENGIE appreciates the significant effort that PJM has undergone into conceptualizing New Options 4 through 8. ENGIE believes that it is unlikely that meaningful progress can be

To address the significant increase in requests to interconnect large loads co-located with generation, the Commission, PJM and stakeholders must explore new and innovative market solutions.

B. The Commission should provide clear guidance on co-location arrangements. In its filing, PJM acknowledges that its efforts “would be greatly assisted by Commission guidance.”4  ENGIE agrees and believes that it is essential for the Commission to provide guidance on co-location arrangements. Commission guidance would assist PJM market participants and stakeholders in obtaining certainty and clarity about available pathways for integration of large loads consistent with PJM’s principles. Commission guidance would not just provide increased certainty, but it would assist in garnering stakeholder consensus. Further, ENGIE recommends that the Commission also investigate and support additional co-location arrangements beyond PJM’s options 1 through 8. ENGIE believes that the record supports consideration of additional participation options beyond those that already exist and those newly proposed. Therefore, ENGIE recommends that the Commission sanction further exploration of additional options proposed by stakeholders in comments to the docket.

C. The Commission must safeguard flexibility in the PJM tariff.
PJM’s filing outlines eight options for integrating large loads with generating facilities. ENGIE acknowledges that these options aim to provide flexibility, clarity and capture multiple co-location use cases while ensuring fairness in cost implications and maintaining grid reliability. Flexibility in tariff design for meeting the various existing and future co-located configurations is

crucial in the context of co-locating large loads and generating facilities especially in a dynamic and rapidly evolving environment.

First, flexibility will ensure adaptability to changing needs. As data centers, and other large loads, grow and their energy needs change, flexible arrangements will help stakeholders adapt to evolving demands and technological advancements without major disruptions. For example, when needs change, it is foreseeable that shifting between configuration options may be more beneficial and efficient. Further, PJM should expect that proposed new options, beyond what was conceived in the PJM Proposal, will develop, and must be recognized or enabled by the tariff.

Second, flexibility can also enhance system reliability as configuration design can provide multiple pathways for power delivery. Flexibility also allows stakeholders to choose the most cost-effective solutions by optimizing the use of, or not, of the electric grid. Finally, flexibility in the tariff can help to mitigate future legal and regulatory changes. In its submittal, PJM emphasizes that co-located arrangements must comply with state laws and regulations.5 Also, that no configuration can be used if it does not comply with state law by virtue of it being one of the options.6 Therefore, building flexibility into the tariff language can help to navigate the complex regulatory landscapes by offering multiple ways to interconnect.

D. The Commission must ensure that PJM’s participation options capture and
recognize the system and ratepayer benefits of co-location.

ENGIE believes that co-location arrangements offer multiple benefits that are shared across the co-located load and generating facility, ratepayers, and the electric grid. Such benefits should be acknowledged, captured, and where appropriate, compensated in PJM’s tariff.

While PJM’s submittal states that “participants involved in co-location arrangements should pay the costs of any grid services they consume”, PJM does not comment on compensating services provided by co-located arrangements to the grid except to express that further stakeholder engagement is needed.7 Co-located arrangements can provide cost savings by reducing overall system costs which can result in the reduction of costs to ratepayers of otherwise interconnecting the load in a non-co-location arrangement. These arrangements can also make efficient use of existing transmission and generation resources which can forestall the need for new infrastructure investments. Such arrangements may also encourage the development of new generating resources which can contribute to increased capacity and grid reliability.

Further, co-located arrangements can offer environmental benefits by encouraging the use of renewable energy sources and energy-efficient technologies that will deliver cleaner power to the load than the load could draw from the grid.

Finally, co-located arrangements can offer improved grid reliability and stability. PJM addresses the ability of these arrangements to provide back-up service and promises to continue to engage with stakeholders to capture potential opportunities.8 Arrangements that provide benefits to the grid such as ancillary services, frequency regulation, voltage support, back-up service or blackstart capability, should be compensated for providing those benefits like any other market participant.

E. The Commission should address several outstanding issues to ensure
implementation of co-location options.

Although PJM’s Proposal touches on interconnection of co-located arrangements, PJM’s response is brief and does not capture some of the outstanding stakeholder questions or concerns.9 For example, currently the load and generation interconnection study processes are bifurcated. Entities wishing to take advantage of colocation require clarity as to how the arrangements will be studied. ENGIE recommends that the Commission and PJM explore whether efficiencies can be gained by studying co-location arrangements in an alternative manner, such as evaluating the net impact on the grid, which recognizes the co-located nature of the interconnection request.

ENGIE also notes the complexity of this type of study including potential mismatching of the time when the load and the generation come online or the lifetime of the load versus the lifetime of the generation or the variances of operational characteristics of co-located load and generation. ENGIE encourages the Commission and PJM to address these difficult but critical questions and concepts to enable the rapid integration of co-located arrangements. Further, ENGIE notes that the Commission requested information from PJM on how co- location arrangements may provide benefits.10  While PJM did not respond substantively to this request, PJM acknowledges that further engagement is needed. ENGIE recommends that the Commission require that PJM evaluate the benefits of co-located arrangements and design a joint and expedited interconnection review process for large load and generation as well as a compensation framework that fairly compensates the facilities for the services they provide.
