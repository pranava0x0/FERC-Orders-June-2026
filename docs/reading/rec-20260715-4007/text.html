PRE-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF THE PJM POWER PROVIDERS 
GROUP (P3) 
 
 
 
The PJM Power Providers Group (P3) appreciates the opportunity to submit these pre-
conference comments in response to the Commission’s June 5, 2026, Supplemental 
Notice of Commission-Led Technical Conference. P3 is a non-profit organization dedicated 
to promoting properly designed and well-functioning markets in the PJM region.1  P3’s 
members own and operate diverse generation portfolios that are vital to maintaining 
reliability for the 65 million consumers within the PJM footprint. 
 
As the Commission explores actionable structural and procedural reforms under Docket 
No. AD26-7-000, P3 urges FERC to anchor all discussions in a foundational premise: PJM’s 
primary mandate must remain the operation of robust, competitive wholesale 
markets that ensure reliability at the lowest reasonable cost. While the PJM footprint 
faces unprecedented operational, resource transitions, and load-growth challenges, 
governance reforms must not become a vehicle for compromising RTO independence or 
upending the stakeholder model that has historically delivered for the region. 
 
P3 offers the following high-level principles and specific responses to the Commission’s 
questions to guide the upcoming technical conference. 
I. Core Principles for PJM Governance  
1. RTO Board Independence PJM sits at the crossroads of 13 states and the District of 
Columbia—jurisdictions with fundamentally divergent energy policies, resource 
portfolios, and political priorities. While the input of PJM members, state and others, 
is vital to its success, PJM’s Board must remain fiercely independent and focused on

its core mission of running an interstate grid and power market that are properly

2. Principled FERC Oversight – Combining an independent RTO Board that acts in the 
best interests of the organization with regulatory oversight by an independent 
regulator who is similarly dedicated to effective oversight of the markets are the 
bedrock of any governance structure.   If the PJM Board and FERC are fulfilling their 
roles properly, taking appropriate feedback, but ultimately making principled 
decisions in the best interest of the market, the RTO will run well and meet its core 
objectives. 
 
3. Any Changes Should Not Undermine PJM’s Investibility Thesis – At the end of the 
day, PJM’s success hinges on its ability to attract private capital to its markets and 
any governance reforms should not undermine or weaken PJM’s aspiration to be a 
desirable destination for merchant capital. 
 
4. Respect the FPA Section 205 Balance of Filing Rights -The division of Federal 
Power Act (FPA) Section 205 filing rights is a core feature of the PJM region. 
Suggestions to unilaterally broaden the PJM Board’s or any other stakeholder’s filing 
rights over matters traditionally reserved for Transmission Owners or the Members 
Committee risk destabilizing the careful balance that stives to incentivize capital 
investment and market participation.  In addition, filing rights under section 205 are 
available only to entities providing jurisdictional services – wholesale suppliers of 
capacity, energy and ancillary services and transmission owners – and these 
entities cannot be forced to concede any rights under section 205 involuntarily.  
With so many pressing issues in PJM right now, PJM leadership and stakeholders 
should not be distracted by a conversation about 205 filing rights that will certainly 
be controversial and litigated. 
 
5. Enhance Process Efficiency Without Diluting Sector Voice - Delays in proactively 
addressing significant market design issues (such as capacity market rules, reserve 
market pricing, and interconnection queue challenges) are often a symptom of

fractured stakeholder consensus, not inherently a failure of the governance 
structure, which can result in over-reliance on fast-track processes that 
shortchange thoughtful deliberation and stakeholder impact. P3 supports process 
reforms to allow PJM and its stakeholders to proactively address these issues in a 
way that balances opportunities for thoughtful stakeholder feedback and 
collaboration with timely action.  P3 suggests that these issues are best managed by 
PJM stakeholders who are familiar with the process and its challenges. 
 
6. Resist Efforts to Change PJM’s Core Mission – PJM core mission is to reliably run 
an interstate grid while optimizing the value to consumers by dispatching the lowest 
costs resources to meet demand.   PJM should not be diverted from this core 
mission to meet the political priorities of other parties – no matter what those 
priorities are.    
 
II. Responses to Specific Panel Areas 
Panel 1: PJM Governance Mechanics—Board Authority, States’ Role, Filing Rights, and 
Document Architecture 
• 
The Board’s Role and Unilateral Authority: The PJM Board possesses the 
necessary authority under the Tariff to act unilaterally when reliability or market 
design failures pose an imminent threat. However, unilateral Board action should 
remain an extraordinary tool reserved for emergency fixes or when the stakeholder 
process has utterly deadlocked on a federally mandated filing timeline. Expanding 
the Board's routine use of unilateral authority could easily erode investor 
confidence by introducing regulatory unpredictability.   It also could lead to poorly 
formulated proposals to the Commission which will make the Commission’s job 
harder and increase the likelihood of court involvement. 
 
• 
The State Role (OPSI): P3 recognizes that the role of the states in the stakeholder 
process is critical to developing durable market reforms.  Indeed, one would expect 
OPSI to be crucial as stakeholders seek to identify workable compromises.  The 
Organization of PJM States, Inc. (OPSI) provides an effective foundation for state 
utility commissions to interface with PJM and its stakeholders.  Moreover, through

OPSI and other venues, states have unique opportunities for participation that no 
other stakeholders enjoy.  That said, P3 is open to working with the states on 
enhancements to facilitate further productive engagement in PJM’s processes.  
Because the Board’s duty is to the regional transmission system and the wholesale 
market rules approved by this Commission, it is important that changes in states’ 
roles in PJM tariff development be undertaken with careful and thoughtful 
deliberation. 
 
• 
Document Architecture and Manuals: P3 is increasingly concerned about the 
shifting of impactful, market-altering rules out of the Open Access Transmission 
Tariff (OATT) and into PJM Manuals. Because Manual changes bypass FERC Section 
205 review, this practice reduces transparency and limits stakeholder recourse. 
Actionable reform should require that any provisions substantially affecting market 
clearing, capacity accreditation, or seller obligations remain firmly within the Tariff 
or Operating Agreement.   These are changes that PJM can easily address without a 
FERC mandate to do so. 
 
Panel 2: PJM Stakeholder Process—Design, Transparency, and Decision-Making 
• 
Inclusiveness and Sector-Based Voting: PJM’s five-sector voting structure 
(Generation Owners, Transmission Owners, Other Suppliers, Electric Distributors, 
and End-Use Customers) satisfies the core principles of Order No. 719 by balancing 
diverse commercial interests. Generally, the voting structure provides sufficient 
information to the Board by which to make informed decisions that are in the best 
interest of the market – which should be the hallmark of any RTO governance 
structure.   On matters that have broad stakeholder agreement, there is a path for 
those matters to be filed at FERC under Section 205 and stakeholders individually or 
collectively (and PJM) can always bring matters to FERC under Section 206.    
 
• 
Efficient Deliberations:   P3 observes that from time to time the PJM stakeholder 
process does languish and lacks focus.   Occasionally, the proliferation of low 
priority stakeholder efforts and PJM’s desire to hear from multiple viewpoints can 
distract from focus on critical issues that have more pressing timelines.    At the end 
of the day, the desire for an open, transparent and inclusive stakeholder process

must be balanced with the need for efficient resolution of pressing matters.  P3 
believes that this balance is best structured between PJM and its stakeholders and 
not mandated by the Commission. 
 
III. Conclusion 
The technical conference on July 23, 2026, should focus on practical, operational 
efficiency, not structural upheavals that inject political volatility into a market that requires 
long-term investment signals. P3 urges the Commission to focus actionable reforms on 
creating structured, fast-path timelines for critical issues while firmly upholding the 
independence of the PJM Board and the integrity of the competitive, sector-weighted 
stakeholder framework.   Moreover, FERC’s role as the ultimate regulator of PJM cannot be 
understated.   There is no substitute for inspired, excellent and principled regulation – 
which should be FERC’s goal and extends well beyond the July 23rd technical conference.
