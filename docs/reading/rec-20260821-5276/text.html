The Statement of Principles directs PJM to conduct a reliability backstop auction to procure new capacity resources, with the procurement to provide a 15-year term of price certainty.9  The Statement also directs that the costs are to be allocated to load-serving entities with new data centers that have not self-procured new capacity or agreed to be curtailable.10  PJM’s proposal makes no effort to comply with this directive. 3. PJM’s Levelized Contract Framework—Which Permits
Suppliers to “Front Load” the Capacity Contracts—Creates
Unnecessary Affordability Risks.
PJM proposes to clear offers in the RBP Auction based on a levelized cost approach.  PJM bears the burden to show it is just and reasonable.  It cannot, and its approach creates the risk of excessive affordability concerns for retail ratepayers. Specifically, PJM proposes that the levelized cost of all selected RBP offers will not exceed a maximum “willingness to pay” of $555/MW-day, on a MW-weighted average basis over all of the Delivery Years of the RBP.28  As PJM acknowledges, this means sellers can offer substantially different price offers between Delivery Years, so long as the total MW-weighted average of all RBP offers over all of the Delivery Years of the RBP does not exceed $555-day.29

The predictable consequence of this flexibility is that sellers will front-load their costs into the initial years of their contracts, significantly increasing bill impacts in the near term.  A seller, for example, could charge $1,000/MW-day for the first several years of the contract, and then balance that out with sub-market prices in the latter years.  The result is that customers will be forced to bear what could be extremely large increases in capacity costs during the initial Delivery Years of the RBP.  All during a period when there is increasing concern, including concern by members of this Commission,30 about affordability.  Absent a persuasive justification for this levelized offer structure—and there is none—the Commission should reject it.

5. PJM’s Reliability Backstop Procurement Should Be Limited to
Resources That Most Effectively Address Reliability Needs.
PJM proposes to permit a broad range of resources to participate in the RBP, including new generation resources, energy storage resources, demand response resources, and distributed energy resources.40  PJM justifies the RBP as an extraordinary measure necessary to address the approximately 6,831 MW capacity shortfall identified in the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction and to preserve resource adequacy within the PJM Region.41  Because the stated purpose of the procurement is to address an identified reliability need, the Commission should ensure that the procurement is structured to prioritize the resources that most effectively advance that need.

While demand response, storage, and distributed energy resources may provide value under certain circumstances, not all eligible resource types provide the same level of reliability benefits or operational certainty.  Because the stated purpose of the RBP is to address a reliability shortfall, the Commission should ensure that the procurement appropriately prioritizes resources that provide firm and dependable capacity.  New dispatchable generation resources are capable of producing energy when needed and therefore provide a more direct means of addressing the resource adequacy concerns that PJM identifies as the basis for the proposal.

7. PJM’s Proposed “Opt-Out” Mechanism Will Allow
Unreasonable Cost Shifting Among Load Customers.
With regard to Electric Distributors, PJM proposes to reduce the Initial RBP Target based on requests from certain Electric Distributors, such as municipal utilities and cooperatives, to “opt out.”51  Thus, an Electric Distributor whose territory qualifies for an “opt out” relating to capacity procurement will not have any cost responsibility assigned to it.  Such an “opt out” is unduly discriminatory under FPA Section 205 and therefore cannot be approved.  Large load in those territories eligible for the “opt out” is no less real than large load in any other territory, and PJM provides no reasonable basis for treating such loads differently under the RBP.  The proposed ”opt out” is also inconsistent with cost- causation principles because it will result in customers in other territories subsidizing 50

8. PJM Should Strengthen Supplier Performance and Credit
Requirements.
The Commission should ensure that customers are adequately protected against supplier non-performance over the fifteen-year commitment term.  While PJM proposes numerous measures intended to reduce development risk and increase the likelihood that selected resources achieve commercial operation, those protections are directed primarily toward construction risk and the risk that a resource fails to come online in the first instance.52  For example, PJM proposes to assess an RBP UCAP MW shortfall charge when a committed resource fails to achieve commercial operation and provide committed capacity.53  PJM further proposes that a resource’s commitment may ultimately be rescinded if the resource fails to provide any committed RBP UCAP MW after three consecutive Delivery Years.54  These provisions help address development risk, but they again do not fully protect customers against the possibility that a resource satisfies its initial commercial operation obligations and subsequently underperforms during the balance of its fifteen-year commitment term.
