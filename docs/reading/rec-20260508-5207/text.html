However, this argument misstates the Commission’s directive, as the Commission did not propose a generation-based threshold and did not limit netting to a particular customer type (e.g., “smaller customers”). Rather, the December 18, 2026, Order directs PJM to “propose a new MW threshold for the amount of load at a particular electrical location that Network Customers may net by using BTMG.”12 The language of the Order indicates that retail netting should continue at all retail electrical locations but only up to the Retail Threshold that the Commission ultimately approves.13 Unfortunately, PJM’s proposal is based on generation size, meaning that if generation is sized above the Retail Threshold, the electrical location would be entirely disqualified from netting; it also incorporates an “all-or-nothing” design.

As explained in the Industrials/Amtrak’s Protest, PJM’s design choice is contrary to a plain reading of the Order, creates an unfortunate economic “cliff” effect, and marks an unjustified shift from the current definition of BTMG (despite the Order directing that BTMG rules be maintained).14 PJM’s proposal would produce radically different results for an entity just below the threshold versus one just above, creating a paralysis of investment for growing American companies. In contrast, the Commission’s original directive is reasonable because it avoids an “all- or-nothing” design that disincentivizes growth and investment.

C. Regulatory certainty and legally durable rules are essential for investment. While grandfathering rules should be broad to avoid unnecessary economic

harm, they should be complemented by a Retail Threshold that provides
certainty for future growth.
The Industrials/Amtrak appreciate the Transmission Owners’ recognition of the importance of robust grandfathering to protect existing retail customers who would be financially harmed by the elimination of the BTMG operations.16 In particular, the Industrials/Amtrak agree that the “existing contract” exception should be construed broadly and, when in doubt, the grandfathering provision should be applied favorably to the existing customer.17 New or expanded facility operations that have made material progress should be included in applicable grandfathering provisions.

While the Industrials/Amtrak appreciate the Transmission Owners’ robust support for grandfathering provisions, reliance solely on grandfathering provisions adds complexity and uncertainty—two enemies of investment. For example:

 Does a factory remain grandfathered if it needs to add one new production line in the future, increasing its demand by 1 MW? Can it make that investment without

While the Commission may be able to address some of these scenarios, they create a regulatory minefield for industrial and institutional retail customers who may be making energy- efficient investments. Some manufacturers in the PJM region intend to expand through a methodical, well-thought-out process (and without the speed-to-market demands that many data centers now present). Over-reliance on grandfathering rules will make this process more daunting for decision makers who must be accountable to their shareholders and stakeholders. Unfortunately, the risk that a facility may not be grandfathered will chill and disincentivize investment.

Most industrial and institutional entities providing critical goods and services must work vigilantly to remain viable in a competitive global economy. Investments are made deliberately and with care. These customers must have a reasonable degree of certainty about their investments, including regulatory certainty. While grandfathering helps to avoid short-term closure of BTMG units (and potentially closure of some factories), it does not offer a roadmap for growth for existing customers who wish to expand and invest. Robust grandfathering rules should be complemented by a reasonable Retail Threshold of at least 200 MW of load, including all QFs who have rights to cost-based back-up power (which retail BTMG status provides) under PURPA and its implementing regulations.
