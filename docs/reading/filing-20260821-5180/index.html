ER26-3380-000 · 2026-08-21 · Sierra ClubNatural Resources Defense Council

Limited Protest of Sierra Club et al. to one aspect of PJM Interconnection, LLC’s proposed Reliability Backstop Procurement

Sierra Club and NRDC file a limited protest in ER26-3380 of PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement. They back the plan but ask FERC to require backstop supply to sit near large loads or to pay for its own network upgrades.

Asks FERC to: Approve PJM's RBP filing with a modification requiring RBP supply to be located near large loads or to fund 100% of network upgrades, and encourage states to mandate take-or-pay agreements.

Parallel proceeding. Protest in PJM's separate ER26-3380 backstop procurement docket. It borrows the E-7 order's cost recovery agreement concept to argue large loads should bear backstop capacity costs, and it bears on the E-7 adequacy question.

E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28) E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. The protesters support PJM's one-time procurement sized to the 2028/2029 shortfall with costs allocated to zones and LSEs serving new large loads.
    PJM proposes a one-time procurement of new capacity sized to the 2028/2029 shortfall, with costs that will be allocated to the zones and load-serving entities (“LSEs”) serving the new large loads PDF p. 2
  2. They read four objectives into the RBP: right amount, new capacity, on time, and large loads bear their costs.
    Procure new capacity that is additive to the PJM system, not a mere reshuffling of existing resources; PDF p. 3
  3. PJM's RBP has no location requirement, which the protesters say makes success depend on large, slow and costly network upgrades.
    PJM’s proposal to allow RBP supply to be located anywhere in the footprint will make the RBP’s success reliant on large-scale network upgrades that will likely cause significant delays and incur significant costs. PDF p. 4
  4. The protesters cite PJM's forecast of 30 GW of large load by 2030 and 70 GW by 2038, and auction shortfalls of 6.8 GW and 6.5 GW.
    PJM predicts that 30 GW of large load demand will come online by 2030 and 70 GW of large load demand will come online by 2038. PDF p. 6
  5. The last three base residual auctions cleared at the price cap, up from $28.92/MW-day in 2024/2025.
    The past three BRAs cleared at the price cap, clearing at $325/MW-day in the 2028/2029 BRA, $333.44/MW-day in the 2027/2028 BRA, and $329.17/MW-day in the 2026/2027 BRA. PDF p. 8
  6. The primary fix is a locational requirement; paying 100% of network upgrades is a partial alternative that should be additional.
    the Commission view this modification as additional to a requirement that RBP supply be located near large loads, not as a completely sufficient alternative modification. PDF p. 17
  7. In PJM's Reliability Resource Initiative, 10 of 51 projects withdrew after upgrade costs were assigned, which the protesters offer as a warning.
    10 out of 51 RRI projects withdrew from the interconnection process, with two projects dropping out before being studied, and eight projects dropping at the first decision point in TC2. PDF p. 20
  8. They liken state-mandated take-or-pay contracts between LSEs and large loads to the cost recovery agreement in the June 18 order.
    Take-or-pay contracts with respect to the RBP would work similarly to the cost-recovery agreements outlined in the Commission’s Order and would serve a similar purpose. PDF p. 40
