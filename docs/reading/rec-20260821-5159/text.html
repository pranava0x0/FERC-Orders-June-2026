POST-TECHNICAL CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF 
EAST KENTUCKY POWER COOPERATIVE, INC. 
 
East Kentucky Power Cooperative, Inc. (“EKPC”) appreciates the opportunity to 
offer these post-technical conference comments, including responding to the questions 
posed by the Commission. 
EKPC integrated into PJM in 2013. EKPC is a generation and transmission owning 
cooperative that serves 16 distribution owner-member cooperatives. EKPC is in the 
Transmission Owner sector of the PJM membership. EKPC actively engages in the PJM 
stakeholder process, including working across sectors to achieve consensus on contentious 
matters. Most recently, EKPC co-sponsored the Reliability Backstop Procurement 
proposal that achieved a two-thirds, sector-weighted super-majority vote.1 Although there 
are examples where the PJM membership has been challenged to come to consensus, 
developing an appropriately structured Reliability Backstop Procurement was not one. Of 
course, this is a key component to addressing the biggest challenge facing the PJM region

It is EKPC’s view that the current resource adequacy challenge in the PJM region 
may partially be attributed to PJM’s failure to act appropriately and timely with respect to 
matters within the Commission’s jurisdiction where PJM currently has the authority to 
make filings under Section 205 of the Federal Power Act. However, EKPC firmly believes 
that the current resource adequacy challenge is primarily driven by the failure of retail 
choice states to impose appropriate requirements on those who serve retail load within their 
state.2 In other words, those states have allowed: 1) existing load to be served by reliance 
on PJM’s short-term markets; and 2) new load to connect to the system without requiring 
adequate power supplies to serve them. These state policies have effectively encouraged 
leaning on the wholesale power markets at a period in time when large amounts of 
dispatchable generation resources have been retired without replacement. Moreover, policy 
makers in some of these states have condemned the PJM markets and the PJM Board and 
leadership for rapid escalations in retail energy prices when the negative impacts of their 
own state policies have adversely impacted the wholesale capacity market via increased 
RPM Capacity Market prices. 
EKPC fundamentally disagrees with how some have framed the challenge – that the 
crisis was caused by flaws in PJM’s governance and ineffective stakeholder process. The

A key theme in written and oral comments arising from the Technical Conference 
was that there should be a clear delineation of responsibilities between PJM and the “states” 
to achieve resource adequacy and ensure it will persist into the future. In EKPC’s view, a 
clear delineation of roles and responsibilities to ensure resource adequacy (and associated 
accountability to faithfully execute those roles to fulfill those responsibilities) is the most 
critical thing that must be accomplished under the Commission’s leadership through the 
Alternative Dispute Resolution process that the Commission initiated. Only then may 
strategic action plans be developed by the states and PJM to ensure that their respective 
efforts work in concert, not conflict, with the overall resource adequacy objective. 
Collaboration between the federal authorities and state authorities through this effort must 
result in clarity about what needs to happen in the thirteen states and District of Columbia 
comprising the PJM region and how those actions mesh with, and harness, the benefits of 
the wholesale power markets administered by PJM. This must happen regardless of 
whether any specific additional role or responsibility be considered for states in the PJM 
governance or stakeholder process. Governors’ offices, state legislators and state 
commissions have been engaged in this Technical Conference, but primarily led by leaders 
in those states where the requirement to own and operate generation adequate is divorced

from the obligation to meet customer load. Those are the various state entities that must act 
in their retail realm to ensure alignment with the overarching resource adequacy objective 
and action is undertaken to ensure there is no unreasonable leaning on the wholesale power 
markets. Ironically, the states with the greatest need to assure that their states achieve 
affordable energy rates are the very states calling for wholesale reform of PJM’s 
governance, despite the fact that it is their own state policies that have created the 
affordability concern. In some instances, state laws or state commission regulations will 
need to be revised. It would be appropriate to receive commitments that those actions will 
be assessed and undertaken before pressuring PJM to alter a governance structure that, 
historically, has worked relatively well. Indeed, that should be a condition of any state body 
being granted any specific role or responsibility in PJM’s governance or stakeholder 
process. 
EKPC offers its perspective on the questions below and appreciates that the 
Commission recognizes that these questions are inter-related and not exhaustive. EKPC 
also offers an observation that potential acceptance of certain changes below would be 
conditioned on other changes simultaneously being made.

2) 
Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member 
may serve (e.g., 10 years)? 
 
X 
 
Comments: If a Board member has effectively discharged their responsibilities, 
there should be no Operating Agreement imposed limit on terms or years of 
service. However, as a self-policing effort, it is appropriate for the Board to 
impose term and age limits through their bylaws as the PJM Board currently

3) 
Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a 
preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region? 
X 
 
 
Comments: It is appropriate to require some tie to the region, being a resident or 
relevant work experience. However, requiring residency during Board service 
term should not be a requirement. It may limit qualified candidates who 
otherwise had experience that satisfies the intention of a residency requirement. 
4) 
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling 
off period for Board members from two years to one year? 
X 
 
 
Comments: A cooling off period is necessary to address actual or perceived 
conflicts of interest. A 2-year cooling off period reduces the candidate pool. A 1-
year cooling off period would be appropriate and is consistent with state and 
federal lobbying rules and state and federal employment rules. 
5) 
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable 
more communication between the Board and stakeholders? 
 
X 
 
Comments: The ex parte rules are intended to reinforce independence and 
transparency. Relaxing them will disadvantage smaller, less-resourced market 
participants and shift stakeholder efforts from striving to achieve stakeholder 
consensus to striving to influence Board decisions. This concern would be 
heightened should additional FPA Section 205 filing rights be afforded to the 
PJM Board. To the extent the term “stakeholders” encompasses states, it is 
appropriate for the PJM Board to meet with OPSI, as it does today. To have 
impact on what governing document changes are filed with the Commission, 
however, the OPSI consensus position should be injected into the stakeholder 
process to be deliberated with those who own the assets, make financial decisions 
based on the PJM rules. Such entities must fully understand the technical impacts 
of the proposals on their businesses. Members like EKPC owe a fiduciary duty as

6) 
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public 
or the Board be required to explain its reasoning?  
X 
 
 
Comments: The tally of “yeas” and “nays” would be beneficial to understand the 
level of consensus on positions. However, it is not necessary to report individual 
Board member voting positions. Revealing individual votes may increase the 
potential for ex parte lobbying efforts targeting individual Board members, 
which may compromise the Board’s independence. Additionally, it would be 
beneficial for the membership to understand the factors the Board considered in 
explaining its reasoning for decisions involving changes to PJM governing 
documents. The Board makes numerous decisions, including those affecting PJM 
employees and PJM operations. Those decisions do not need to be reported 
publicly or explained publicly.

8) 
Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that 
the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest? 
 
X 
 
Comments: PJM’s purpose is to ensure reliability at the lowest reasonable cost. It 
necessarily needs focus on engineering and economics. Whereas, a “public 
interest” standard necessarily involves broader factors, including those outside of 
PJM’s authority. 
PJM may only be charged with responsibilities that are consistent with the 
Federal Power Act. To the extent that the concept of public interest were limited 
to that embodied in the Federal Power Act, an appropriately caveated reference 
may be considered. 
However, even such caveats are insufficient to address the elephant in the room. 
Resource adequacy is the most significant challenge for the region to overcome. 
Including a reference to “public interest,” while ignoring the fact that PJM does 
not possess all the authorities necessary to accomplish it, would likely further 
subject PJM to political pressure and out-weighted criticism. Only upon 
establishing a clear delineation of, and accountability for, roles and

X

PJM’s Operating Agreement currently charges the PJM Board to ensure

Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes

Additionally, to the extent that the TOA-AC may be considered part of the

who came together to enable the formation of PJM to not have a say in changes 
to the foundational agreement that governs the rules and responsibilities turned

12) 
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and 
ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain 
approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee? 
 
X 
 
Comments: The current challenge is resource adequacy. PJM currently has 
Section 205 rights over its Tariff and the Reliability Assurance Agreement. 
Those are the operative governing documents to address the current challenge. 
13) 
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional 
Transmission Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the 
requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members 
Committee? 
X 
 
 
Comments: Providing PJM with Section 205 rights over the RTEP protocol must 
be conditioned on appropriate dispute resolution procedures being established. 
As noted above, EKPC owes a duty to its owner-members and must comply with 
state regulations. It must have a meaningful opportunity to ensure that its 
perspectives are considered before changes that may impact its ability to plan its 
system are filed with the Commission. 
14) 
Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” 
rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings 
when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal? 
 
X 
 
Comments: The PJM members (not OPSI), acting with a sector-weighted 
supermajority, should be afforded “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy. 
Transmission cost allocation filing rights must remain with the Transmission 
Owners as they need the ability to ensure that their cost to build and maintain the 
transmission system may be recovered. To the extent, however, that OPSI 
achieves a super-majority consensus on issues affecting resource adequacy or 
transmission cost allocation, they should be afforded the opportunity to present 
those perspectives to the PJM membership (for resource adequacy matters) or 
TOA-AC (for transmission cost allocation matters) and have those perspectives 
fully considered as proposals are being developed. Their input should be included 
and considered as part of the deliberative process, not something that occurs 
alongside or outside the deliberative process. In this way, OPSI may influence 
and essentially drive consensus to support a “jump ball” filing but not in and of 
themselves submit a “jump ball” filing. We believe this suggestion is consistent 
with the “jump ball” structure between NEPOOL and ISO New England.

State Participation 
 
 
Questions/Comments

18) 
In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative 
group consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ 
representatives? 
 
X 
 
Comments: Having multiple channels for state voices likely would result in 
confusion for PJM and for the membership. It would be difficult to determine 
which voice should be considered if the voices expressed conflicting views. The 
state perspective is valuable but should be channeled to best enable actionable 
impact. Ultimately, it would be important to consider the role and responsibility 
of any state group in order to determine the appropriateness of a particular 
configuration of state representatives for purposes of influencing outcomes 
affecting PJM and its ability to satisfy its mandate under the Operating 
Agreement consistent with the Federal Power Act. As noted above, EKPC urges 
clarity in the delineation of roles and responsibilities (and accountability to fulfill 
those responsibilities) between PJM and the states regarding resource adequacy.

19) 
Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the 
stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource 
adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the 
consumer advocate votes? 
 
X 
 
Comments: There should be an opportunity for OPSI to insert its consensus view 
on matters under deliberation in the PJM stakeholder process. That should be a 
structured role enabling the offering of proposals, offering feedback on other’s 
proposals, asking questions, and seeking analysis or information. This, however, 
should not be a formal voting role. EKPC supports the approach used in New 
England.  
20) 
Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a 
section 205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost 
allocation? 
 
X 
 
Comments: There should be no explicit requirement that PJM consult with OPSI 
before it be permitted to submit a Section 205 filing with the Commission. 
However, it would be a good practice that PJM staff consult with OPSI 
throughout the process in which the proposal is being developed. As noted in the 
response above, the New England model would invite the OPSI to engage 
directly in the stakeholder process so that the stakeholders too have a meaningful 
opportunity to engage with the ideas initiated by OPSI. Transparency and 
efficiency are reinforced by including OPSI in the process. Creating an entirely 
separate process for engaging OPSI would conflict with transparency and 
efficiency objectives.
