I. 
COMMENTS 
Microsoft submits these comments to highlight concerns with several aspects of 
PJM’s IRAS proposal and to encourage the Commission to consider modifications that better 
advance regional objectives.  First, PJM’s proposal requires certain large retail customers to 
pay for capacity while exposing them to curtailment before compensated capacity resources 
are called upon to perform. Second, the proposal interferes with service expectations 
underlying binding utility agreements and may substantially impair investments made under 
existing retail and wholesale rules. Third, large retail customers currently lack sufficient 
visibility into the load forecasts and project-level assumptions necessary for PJM to 
determine their exposure under IRAS and the Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP). 
Microsoft therefore urges the Commission to direct modifications that preserve the 
connection between customer capacity payments and associated reliability benefits, protect 
existing binding agreements for new load that has yet to ramp to the contracted-for capacity, 
and provide transparent and verifiable treatment of affected large retail loads. Additionally,

A. 
Large Retail Customers Paying for Capacity Should Receive Reliability 
Benefits 
Microsoft supports the principle that large retail customers benefiting from grid 
reliability should contribute appropriately to the costs of maintaining that reliability. 
Microsoft also supports mechanisms that align growing demand with new resource 
development and provide large retail customers meaningful pathways to support the addition 
of new supply. Those principles must operate consistently in both the rates customers pay 
and the service they receive. 
PJM’s proposal requires Eligible New Large Loads to be curtailed before PJM 
initiates certain load-management actions, including dispatch of Demand Response 
Resources that have been compensated through the capacity market to reduce load when 
needed.7 At the same time, the proposal does not clearly provide a corresponding reduction 
in the capacity-related charges paid by large retail customers subject to IRAS. Instead, PJM 
relies on a separate IRAS compensation mechanism, the implementation of which is largely 
left to Electric Distributors and state authorities. This creates a fundamental mismatch 
between cost and benefit: large retail customers may be required to pay for capacity while 
being interrupted before that capacity is used to preserve their service.  PJM has not 
demonstrated why some retail customers that contracted for firm service should be 
involuntarily converted to a materially different service construct. Where a customer remains

B. 
The Proposal Upends Retail Contractual Commitments and Large Retail 
Customers’ Reliance Interests on Those Arrangements.  
Microsoft is concerned with how IRAS changes the retail service framework 
applicable to projects where sponsors have already entered into binding utility arrangements 
and made substantial investment commitments. Large-load projects like Microsoft’s 
investments require years of planning and involve substantial commitments for land, 
construction, network infrastructure, electric facilities, generation procurement, and long-
term utility service. IRAS imposes requirements that could not reasonably have been 
anticipated when a large retail customer and utility entered into their agreement, causing 
abrupt, substantial impairment to the value of these long-lead time projects.  
Many large load projects also ramp up over time rather than entering service at their 
full contract demand. IRAS upsets existing commitments when some portion of its 
contracted load ramps after the PJM-proposed cutoff date; The cut-off date is completely 
arbitrary as to any particular phase of the large load project. Applying IRAS to later 
increments of a phased project already covered by an executed agreement could divide a

single facility into different service categories, further undermining settled commercial 
expectations and imposing new retail service requirements that could not reasonably have 
been anticipated when the large retail customer and utility entered into their agreement. 
If the Commission decides to proceed with IRAS, Microsoft requests that the 
Commission direct changes that provide a clear and effective transition mechanism that 
protects large retail customers that executed binding utility agreements or made other 
substantial, demonstrable commitments they relied upon before PJM proposed IRAS. PJM's 
own proposed Large Load Registry recognizes that large load projects may have phased 
ramp schedules and requires reporting of each increment's in-service date, peak load and 
ramp schedule, and applicable contracts or service agreements.  The transition should 
recognize the full contracted load and reasonable ramp schedule associated with an eligible 
project, rather than protecting only the load energized before a single, arbitrary cutoff date 
without assessment of commercial implications. This approach would preserve legitimate 
reliance interests while maintaining appropriate obligations for genuinely new and 
unsupported load. 
C. 
Transparent, Project-level Information Is Necessary for Retail Customers 
To Assess Their IRAS Exposure.  
IRAS depends heavily on the load forecasts used to establish the relevant baseline 
and identify incremental load.8 Large retail customers, however, have limited visibility into 
how electric distribution companies incorporate individual projects, contract quantities, and 
ramp schedules into the forecasts underlying PJM’s proposal. Without that information, these 
customers are unable to assess which portions of their retail load may be considered

incremental, whether contracted demand was fully reflected in the rolled-up load forecast, or 
what actions may be needed to limit curtailment and cost exposure. 
This uncertainty is particularly consequential where utility forecasts appear to include 
less load than a large retail customer has committed to under an executed service agreement 
with commercial obligations and firm commitments. A large retail customer should not face 
curtailment or new capacity obligations because of forecasting practices of others that it was 
never given the opportunity to verify or correct. Nor should large retail customers ever be 
required to make resource-procurement decisions without clear information regarding the 
delivery years, megawatt quantities, and facilities potentially subject to IRAS. 
Before IRAS is implemented, each potentially affected customer must be provided 
with sufficient project-level information to verify how its load is represented in the 
applicable load forecast and Large Load Registry, including both load reflected in executed 
service agreements and any additional forecasted load, and how any associated supply 
arrangement will be credited. PJM must put into place a process to identify and correct 
forecasting and registry errors. PJM must also clearly distinguish among Qualifying 
Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) Offset Unforced Capacity (“UCAP”) MW, 
allocated RBP UCAP MW, and Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”). These retail 
customer-specific determinations must be made sufficiently in advance of each Delivery 
Year so that affected large retail customers have a meaningful opportunity to procure 
qualifying supply, challenge errors, or adjust project plans before incurring curtailment risk.   
Additionally, PJM must explain whether and how the same resource may receive 
recognition under both frameworks, the extent to which each designation reduces customer-
specific cost or curtailment exposure, and how resources with future commercial-operation

D. 
Microsoft Supports Competitive Market Rules that Achieve Reliability 
without Undermining Signals for New Capacity Investment 
Microsoft supports reforms that maintain reliability while facilitating the 
development of the generation and transmission infrastructure needed to serve growing 
demand. A durable regulatory framework should create transparent pathways for customers 
to support new supply, recognize bilateral procurement and other qualifying resource 
commitments, and facilitate competitive market signals that encourage timely resource entry. 
Any exclusion of new load from capacity-market demand should be carefully 
evaluated to ensure that market outcomes correctly signal the region’s actual long-term need 
for capacity.  In Microsoft’s view, reliability reforms should align customer obligations, 
resource qualification rules, and capacity-market signals in a manner that supports both near-
term operational reliability and long-term supply development.  PJM has not yet cogently 
explained why its proposal to exclude projected load from auction demand does not 
understate the region’s future capacity needs and mask signals necessary to attract new

II. 
CONCLUSION 
Microsoft fully supports PJM’s objective of maintaining system reliability and agrees 
that large retail customers should pay their fair share of the grid costs incurred to serve them. 
The Commission should not, however, approve a tariff framework—and more generally, 
establish regulatory precedents—that disconnect customer payments from reliability benefits, 
disrupts binding utility arrangements and substantial investments made under existing rules, 
or determines large retail customer obligations through forecasts that customers cannot 
verify. 
For these reasons, Microsoft respectfully requests that the Commission direct 
modifications to (1) align curtailment priority and capacity charges with the reliability 
benefits customers receive; (2) establish a meaningful transition to protect existing 
contractual commitments and project ramp schedules; (3) provide transparent, project-level 
information and correction rights regarding IRAS exposure; and (4) preserve accurate market 
signals and workable pathways for customers to support new resource development.   
Additionally, Microsoft respectfully requests that PJM, the Commission, or both explain if 
RBP UCAP Offset MW under RBP can also serve a BYONC under IRAS.
