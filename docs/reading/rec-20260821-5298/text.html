LS Power appreciates the opportunity to participate in the continuing dialogue around PJM 
governance.1  While we have provided in-depth responses to specific questions posed by the 
Commission, below we provide context for those responses: 
1. Independence: The good news: every participant in the PJM market, from government, to 
stakeholders to management to Board believes it is imperative that the PJM Board be independent.  
But that begs the question: independent of whom?  LS Power believes that the PJM Board has an 
obligation to try to understand the various goals and objectives of policy makers, stakeholders and 
PJM management (“Interested PJM Parties”), but the Board is obligated to make the best decision 
for PJM’s core mission of creating and operating robust, competitive and non-discriminatory electric 
power markets, while understanding customer needs and delivering valued service in a cost efficient-
manner.2  While decisions must consider all of the positions of the Interested PJM Parties, the Board 
itself must not be influenced – or concerned about responses -- by any one of the Interested PJM 
Parties.  For this reason, LS Power does not support open meetings or the publication of Board votes, 
as described in more detail below. 
LS Power reiterates its previously submitted comments that creating a Market Surveillance 
Committee to directly advise the Board would provide more of such independence: such a committee 
would not be beholden to stakeholders, policy makers, the Independent Market Monitor or PJM 
management in helping the Board understand and make decisions around very technical issues.  LS 
Power welcomes the opportunity to work with the Commission and stakeholders to create such a 
committee.   
2. Communications.  Again, there is almost unanimous agreement on another issue: communications in 
the PJM process must be enhanced.  The PJM Board and OPSI need to find a more satisfactory path 
to effective communications.  Moreover, ex-parte rules between the PJM Board and stakeholders 
should be relaxed.  As noted in our Initial Comments,3 other Regional Transmission Operators 
provide examples of greater interaction among stakeholders and Board members without

3. State Participation in the PJM Process.  As stated in our Comments and by many participants at the 
Technical Conference,4 the states can and should play a critical role in PJM.  LS Power urges the 
Commission to facilitate the creation of a better funded and technically enhanced OPSI so that the 
states have more meaningful representation on technical market matters.  PJM and OPSI should 
develop a more structured and effective process for discussing PJM matters.   While understanding 
that some participants believe the states need an additional voice to their state commissions 
participating in OPSI, LS Power does not support such an approach.  For one, states should speak 
with a unified voice.  None of the Interested PJM Parties want to pursue a path and then find out that 
a state was not confident in a proposal.  LS Power believes that the OPSI rules should be amended to 
allow for state participation beyond state commissioners, and it is up to OPSI and the state 
representatives to develop an efficient approach.  While the states do not meet the requirements 
under the Federal Power Act to be granted the ability to file 205 rights5, LS Power is on the record 
for allowing states more direct involvement in cost allocation of transmission projects and a formal 
advisory role on other matters.  Nonetheless, the change in composition does not mean the Board 
must endorse OPSI positions, but it must take them into consideration.  States always have the right 
to file comments in 205 proceedings, or to initiate a 206 proceeding. 
 
4. The PJM Nominating Committee.  LS Power is concerned that the state representatives want a vote 
on the Nominating Committee for misplaced reasons.  It appears they would like to drive state-
specific policy by having a say on who becomes a Board member.  The PJM Code of Conduct for 
the Nominating Committee could not be more explicit:  The members of the Nominating Committee 
shall undertake and fulfill their duties in furtherance of the best interests of PJM without regard to 
the individual or affiliated company interests of any member of the Nominating Committee and 
without regard to the interests of any other PJM member or group of PJM members.6 LS Power 
supports the current approach to board member selection.  It is important that candidates meet 
specific experience and technical requirements for filling a particular Board seat when it becomes 
open.   Another important factor is independence – if a candidate has taken consistent specific 
positions over the years, they should be evaluated on the basis of whether those positions might bias 
a decision as a Board member.  Finally, as employees of LS Power have participated in the 
Nominating Committee and can attest: the five sector representatives of the Nominating Committee 
focus on how the nominee’s integrity, leadership, and personality would fit with the Board.  And 
while it is true that industrial customers can represent the End Use Sector, Consumer Advocates can, 
and have served on the Nominating Committee, directly representing consumer interests.  A further 
discussion is provided in our response below.

balance, although we would support – short of explicit 205 rights – more direct involvement of the

6. Board Term.  PJM has offered terms for settlement discussion, which includes a tenure of nine (9) 
years for Board Members.  LS Power strongly opposes such a lengthy term, which is even longer 
than the term of a FERC Commissioner.  Such a term length could diminish the pool of candidates 
who are willing to commit to serving for so long on the Board.   Moreover, no other Regional 
Transmission Operator has such long terms for their Board.10 The strong rationale for a shorter 
tenure extends beyond the world of RTOs.  For example, the Securities and Exchange Commission 
(the “SEC”), in opining on the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, expresses a concern about the 
relationship between auditor and client, noting:  "We believe that a proper balance is one that weighs 
the responsibility for decisions on accounting and financial reporting issues impacting the financial 
statements with the level of the relationship with senior management of the client."11  This is 
analogous to the concern stakeholders have about a Board member’s relationship with PJM 
management potentially coloring their judgment.  In that regard, Congress believed that a five-year 
term of audit was better than seven years, mandating the five-year rotation, and the SEC in 
implementing it established the matching five-year cooling off period.  Similarly, the United 
Kingdom Corporate Code of Governance recommends annual re-election for Board members and a 
limit of nine years for the Chairperson.12   Finally, a term of such a duration would create an 
immunity from the performance review that exists in very little parts of our world: corporate 
directors are voted on, management is fired for non-performance, governmental officials are not re-
elected, and even governmental agency commissioners may be removed without cause by the 
President.13

Should Board members' terms be 
extended to five or more years? 
Yes 
 
Comment: 
LS Power supports a 5 year term.  Many new Board members are not familiar with the PJM markets.  They must invest 
significant time learning them and require time to gain the knowledge of PJM’s operations to make quality decisions.  
Stakeholders benefit from having Board members with institutional knowledge, which a three year tenure is less likely to 
provide.   A term longer than five years is less preferable because it may make Board members less accountable for their 
decisions because of security in a long term and increasing reliance on PJM management.  PJM Board members are well paid, 
and we would not want to see a long term provide incentives to be less proactive over the duration of the term. The risk to 
extending the 3 year term is precisely that: Board members can “coast” on a longer term.  LS Power’s support is based on the 
expectation that the Board will be diligent and “self-police” members that are not contributing according to the requirements of 
the role.

Extension of the initial term will allow for renomination where the board member demonstrates impressive engagement and 
institutional knowledge, while the overall term limit ensures that fresh perspectives are continually introduced to the Board.

No/Yes 
 
Comment: 
The Board votes should not be made public.  This could undermine the independent decision making of individual Board

LS Power believes that for the Board to remain independent, substantive policy-making meetings should not be open.  
Stakeholders have some insight into Board thinking through the Liaison Committee meetings already as do the states through

No 
 
Comment: 
LS Power is concerned that “public interest” means different things to different Interested PJM Parties.  For example, the

robust, competitive, and non-discriminatory electric power market that is designed and operated for the benefit of 
consumers in the PJM region”14 in accordance with the Federal Power Act.

LS Power Proposed OA language (akin to the Western EIM structure16): 
The PJM Board alone may authorize a FERC filing if, and only if, all of the following conditions are met: (i) The PJM Board, 
by unanimous vote, promptly notifies the Members Committee and makes a finding that the Members Committee has reached 
an impasse and that exigent circumstances exist such that a unanimous PJM Board Section 205 tariff or OA amendment is 
critical to preserve reliability or to protect market integrity. Unless the circumstance is so time critical as to require immediate 
action, this finding may be made only after PJM Board management has presented an initiative to the Members Committee a 
second time in an effort to propose a tariff or OA change on an issue that the Members Committee has authority to approve. In 
such a time-critical circumstance where there is not sufficient time to develop and vote on a revised proposal, the Board may by 
unanimous vote approve a PJM Board Section 205 tariff or OA amendment filing on an expedited basis without completing the 
second attempt to find an acceptable proposal. (ii) The Board must set forth the basis for any and all of its findings justifying 
exigent or time critical circumstances in writing and PJM shall be under no Common Interest Agreement with any PJM 
Member. (iii) If the Board authorizes such a filing, the FERC filing shall include any written opinion or other statement the PJM 
Members Committee may decide to offer regarding the proposed tariff amendment. The PJM Members Committee shall have 
the option to retain outside counsel to assist in preparing any written opinion or statement on the proposed tariff amendment that 
it decides to include in the filing.

III. CONCLUSION 
LS Power believes that enhancements to PJM governance are necessary, including explicit rules for state 
engagement with stakeholders and the Board, as well as for decision making in the stakeholder process itself.  
That said, governance enhancements should not be a distraction from the more critical issues facing PJM: 
ensuring its competitive markets will ensure reliability and result in the lowest reasonable cost for 
consumers.  LS Power reiterates our comments previously filed with FERC: PJM is responsible for 
wholesale rates.  PJM’s and the PJM Board’s mandate is to ensure that the markets it administers are 
efficient and produce the least reasonable cost.  It is the states’ responsibility to design retail rates that meet 
their affordability policy goals.  To advance the conversation on effective governance, we have introduced 
specific Operating Agreement language that would trigger in the event of Board unanimity in the face of 
impending reliability or market harm.  As such, we respectfully ask for consideration of our proposed OA 
language discussed herein.
