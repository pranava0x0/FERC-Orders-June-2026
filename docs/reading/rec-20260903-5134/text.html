I. Introduction
The PJM Filing proposes reforms to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”)
and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load-Serving Entities in the PJM Region
(“RAA”) to establish IRAS, a load reduction service associated with PJM’s capacity market, that will be in effect when the capacity market clears short of the PJM Reliability Requirement.4  New Large Loads added to the system without matching new capacity will be required to provide IRAS, i.e., be interruptible at PJM’s direction, during the Delivery Year.  To establish IRAS, PJM proposes several other related changes as part of the framework.

First, PJM proposes to clarify that Load Serving Entities (“LSE”) interconnecting New
Large Loads in a Delivery Year must bring new capacity in an amount that equals or exceeds the New Large Loads’ peak load as of the commencement of that Delivery Year.  To the extent that they do not fully cover the peak load, the difference will be considered IRAS eligible and subject

II. Comments
PJM correctly recognizes that LSEs should have an affirmative obligation to procure new
capacity to cover any new Large Loads they seek to interconnect and serve during a Delivery Year. LSEs are the wholesale entities serving retail customers, and the costs and obligations for Large Loads, which are retail customers, should flow through the LSEs.  Some of the current resource adequacy shortfall in PJM can be attributed to the lack of incentives for LSEs to procure new capacity outside the BRA and LSEs relying solely on the BRA to procure needed new capacity.10 PJM’s proposed amendment to the RAA to clarify LSE obligations is a needed first step toward reestablishing resource adequacy obligations.

Much of PJM’s proposed framework, however, moves needlessly from federal wholesale
jurisdiction into retail jurisdiction and is likely to cause confusion and potential legal challenges. Instead of establishing IRAS obligations with the LSE, the wholesale jurisdictional entity, PJM focuses its proposal from the perspective of Large Loads, the retail customer. For example, PJM chooses to call new capacity eligible to cover New Large Loads, “Bring Your Own New Capacity,” a term that suggests that the only way for New Large Loads to avoid IRAS is to enter their own arrangements for new capacity.  In describing the BYONC concept, PJM states “to the extent a New Large Load does not want to provide IRAS, it needs to contract with new capacity that meets

the BYONC gating criteria.”11 Proposed Tariff Attachment DD-4 Section VII, titled “Bring Your Own New Capacity Arrangements with New Large Loads”, is written to only consider “arrangements” that represent a contractual agreement between a New Large Load and a “resource owner.”  The section places only a reporting obligation on the LSE suggesting that they are not associated with any BYONC designated to cover the New Large Loads they will serve during the Delivery Year.12

This myopic view fails to consider that the regulatory framework for retail electric service can differ between states and also between LSEs operating within those states.  In Virginia, for example, Vertically Integrated Utilities (“VIU”) like Dominion Energy Virginia have an obligation to interconnect and serve new customers, including Large Loads, and utilize integrated resource planning to identify the appropriate build out of new capacity to address resource adequacy needs. The costs for Dominion Energy Virginia to serve Large Loads are allocated through retail rates designed to ensure that Large Load customers pay their fair share.  There is not an obligation for Large Loads seeking to interconnect in Dominion Energy Virginia’s franchised territory to either build or contract for new capacity.  In the IRAS framework, Dominion Energy Virginia as LSE will be the entity arranging for new capacity to cover New Large Load expected to interconnect during the Delivery Year.  New Large Loads “bringing their own new capacity” should not be the only means to avoid IRAS, as conveyed by the Tariff.

VII. REQUIREMENTS FOR ASSOCIATING BRING YOUR OWN NEW
CAPACITY ARRANGEMENTS WITH NEW LARGE LOADS

(a) For any BYONC to be in an arrangement associated with a New Large Load, the Load Serving Entity serving the New Large Load or the applicable Electric Distributor shall report the BYONC associated with the New Large Load in accordance with the PJM Manuals.

(i)   If the BYONC is owned or affiliated with the Load Serving Entity serving the New Large Load, the Load Serving Entity must certify to the Office of Interconnection, in accordance with the PJM Manuals, attesting that the BYONC will follow the requirements of Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section VIII. the Load Serving Entity serving the New Large Load or the applicable Electric Distributor shall report the BYONC in an arrangement with the New Large Load, in accordance with the PJM Manuals. The Office of the Interconnection may also accept information to the extent interconnection customers, Large Loads, Transmission Owners, or other entities voluntarily report any BYONC that may be in an arrangement with a New Large Load; and

(ii)  If the BYONC is not owned by or affiliated with the Load Serving Entity serving the New Large Load, the resource owner or entity with contractual authority over the BYONC to enter into a BYONC arrangement must certify to the Office of Interconnection, in accordance with the PJM Manuals, attesting to the arrangement that the BYONC will be associated with the New Large Load and that the BYONC will follow the requirements of Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section VIII.

(b)   For BYONC associated with an arrangement between a New Large Load and BYONC to be effective for the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years, for any associated BYONC arrangement to be considered, the requirements of Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section VII(a) must be met by April 1 for the first Delivery Year such arrangement association is to result in a reduction in the New Large Load’s Eligible New Large Load Amount. By April 15, the Office of the Interconnection shall update the Large Load Registry to reflect any verified arrangements associations between New Large Loads and BYONC.

(c)   For BYONC associated with an arrangement between a New Large Load and BYONC to be effective for the 2029/2030 Delivery Year, for any associated BYONC arrangement to be considered, the requirements of Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section VII(a) must be reported to the Office of the Interconnection no later than by 50 days prior to the commencement of the 2029/2030 Base Residual Auction or by 30 days prior to the posting of the planning parameters for the Incremental Auctions arrangement for the 2029/2030 Delivery Year. The Office of the Interconnection shall update the Large Load Registry to reflect any verified arrangements associations between New Large Loads and BYONC after the relevant RPM Auction.
