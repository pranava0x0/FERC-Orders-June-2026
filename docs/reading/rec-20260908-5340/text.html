I. BACKGROUND
MISO submits these comments and protest to resolve the mismatch in large load definitions and rules between MISO’s IRR Proposal and ATC’s proposed Tariff revisions.  This mismatch involves conflicting thresholds between the 100 MW large load threshold in ATC’s proposed Tariff revisions and the 50 MW large load threshold in MISO’s pending IRR Proposal, separate study processes, and related downstream impacts.  This issue appears to be a matter of timing resulting from the time lag between the issuance of the Show Cause Order, MISO’s request to hold those compliance directives in abeyance, ATC’s filing in this docket, and submission of MISO’s IRR Proposal.

The Commission issued the Show Cause Order on June 18, 2026.5  The Show Cause Order directed MISO and the Transmission Owners to either: (1) show cause as to why MISO’s Tariff

MISO supports ATC’s stated goal and has long acknowledged the importance of allowing its Transmission Owners to establish additional or supplemental requirements in its footprint that work together with MISO’s Tariff rules. ii. Granting ATC’s requested relief would create reliability
coverage gaps.

Large Loads could face a reliability coverage gap if ATC’s Large Load framework replaces, rather than supplements, MISO’s framework.  As noted, MISO defines Large Loads by applying a 50 MW threshold, whereas ATC Large Load applies a 100 MW threshold.  As such, Loads ranging from 50 MW to 100 MW in ATC’s footprint would be excluded from requirements applicable to comparable loads elsewhere in MISO.  This gap has significant  downstream impacts.

MISO’s IRR Proposal explains that the 50 MW threshold defining Large Load is based on industry precedent and the need for a clear and administratively workable means of identifying significant, concentrated loads that warrant additional reliability visibility.17  A load of 50 MW is sufficiently large that its characteristics and behavior can be relevant to transmission planning and system operations.18  Because Large Loads above 50 MW increasingly exhibit dynamic operating characteristics and concentrated demand and utilize sophisticated power-electronic equipment and controls, additional facility information, telemetry, and modeling detail may be necessary to evaluate reliability impacts under both normal and contingency conditions.19  The data and modeling requirements in the IRR Proposal are intended to support reliable operation of the entire MISO Transmission System by improving the accuracy of planning assumptions, strengthening operational awareness, supporting disturbance analysis and model validation, and enabling more consistent evaluation of system performance associated with Large Load integration.20  As such, allowing ATC to apply the definition of ATC Large Load in the ATC footprint will have wide ranging impacts on reliability, system planning and operational awareness.

ATC’s proposed 100 MW Large Load threshold would exempt 50–99 MW facilities— including both traditional industrial loads and Computational Loads—from MISO’s day-ahead and real-time forecasting requirements.  These forecasts are essential inputs to MISO’s Reliability Assessment Commitment (RAC), Security Constrained Economic Dispatch (SCED), Look-Ahead Commitment (LAC), and real-time dispatch processes.

If the ATC Large Load definition is accepted, neither MISO’s nor ATC’s requirements would apply to a 75 MW Computational Load.  This load source could fall outside MISO’s targeted the Phasor Measurement Unit / high-resolution measurement requirements.  If an oscillation occurs in the area with the 75 MW load, failure to apply large load data monitoring and communication rules would prevent MISO from performing the synchronized measurements needed to determine whether the load is the source, contributing to the oscillation, or responding to it.  This makes source identification and mitigation more difficult, impacting Transmission System reliability.

C. Granting This Protest Is Consistent with ATC’s Stated Intent.

Granting this limited protest would align with ATC’s statements in its filing.  ATC states that ATC’s proposal is intended to be “complementary to MISO’s future filings” and “designed to work together with MISO’s process in the same way ATC’s local planning process in Attachment FF-ATCLLC works with the annual MISO Transmission Expansion Plan (“MTEP”) process today.”27  ATC’s proposed Tariff redlines, however, state that ATC requirements “shall govern” in its footprint and indicates ATC may selectively defer requirements to MISO.28  This provides too much uncertainty to Large Loads and results in inconsistent Large Load requirements. MISO is willing to work with ATC to establish incremental criteria in the ATC region that will complement MISO’s baseline reliability framework.  MISO also understands that ATC made its filing in response to the Show Cause Order, in advance of the submission of MISO’s IRR Proposal, and without time to fully coordinate its proposed framework with MISO.  Now that both parties have filed their respective proposals, there is time for the parties to work together to address the needs of the region while acknowledging that ATC may need to impose requirements on large loads that are in addition to the baseline reliability requirements established in the IRR Proposal. MISO is sensitive to the challenges that ATC faces in filing supplemental requirements that are responsive to needs in the ATC footprint while aligning with MISO processes that are still
