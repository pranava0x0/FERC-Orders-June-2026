What the document says

Unpaginated filing

FERC accepts a shortfall payment for network loads of 100 MW or more.

The purpose of the Shortfall Payment mechanism is to reduce the potential cost impacts of large load service requests on other DEC transmission customers if the Network Load does not materialize or otherwise takes less service than anticipated. Source text

Unpaginated filing

The 100 MW large-load threshold is just and reasonable.

Based on the record before us, we also find that the 100 MW threshold is just and reasonable. Source text

Unpaginated filing

Shortfall payments reduce, not add to, Duke's transmission revenue requirement.

Shortfall Payments serve to reduce, rather than add to, DEC’s revenue collection through its transmission revenue requirement. Source text

Unpaginated filing

Network Upgrade costs roll into transmission rates by default, large-load upgrades included.

Network Upgrades that are part of an integrated system benefit all system users. Source text

Unpaginated filing

Network Upgrade costs roll into transmission rates by default, large-load upgrades included.

That policy does not change based on the size or scope of Network Upgrades constructed to fulfill a transmission customer’s request for network service. Source text

Unpaginated filing

Duke may still file a non-conforming agreement with incremental rates.

the Commission will consider non-conforming NITSAs that include an alternative cost-recovery mechanism such as an incremental rate. Source text

Unpaginated filing

FERC rejects ElectriCities' comparability objection to the 100 MW terms as outside its retail jurisdiction.

The Commission lacks jurisdiction over retail customers, and we therefore dismiss ElectriCities’ argument. Source text

Unpaginated filing

Retail cost-shift worries from load additions are beyond FERC's jurisdiction.

we note that SACE’s concerns regarding retail cost allocation and rates pertaining to load additions are beyond the Commission’s jurisdiction. Source text
