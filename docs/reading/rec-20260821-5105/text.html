COMMENTS OF THE SOLAR ENERGY INDUSTRIES ASSOCIATION 
Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission”) 
Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 the Solar Energy Industries Association (“SEIA”)2 hereby 
submits these comments in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) filing proposing a 
Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP Proposal”). PJM filed the RBP Proposal pursuant to 
Section 205 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”),3 proposing revisions to the PJM Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving 
Entities (“RAA”) to establish a one-time procurement mechanism to address resource adequacy 
shortfalls in the PJM Region.4 As set forth below, SEIA does not oppose the RBP Proposal and 
submits these comments to identify targeted areas where the Commission and PJM may wish to 
consider refinements that could further unlock capacity participation and reduce procurement 
costs.

I.
INTRODUCTION
SEIA does not oppose PJM’s RBP Proposal as a necessary and timely response to the 
resource adequacy challenges facing the PJM Region. The recent Base Residual Auctions 
(“BRAs”) for the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years each cleared 5.6% below the PJM 
Region Installed Reserve Margin of 20%,5 driven primarily by unprecedented additions of large 
loads—particularly data centers—that have outpaced supply additions. PJM’s proposal to conduct 
a one-time backstop procurement to secure approximately 6,831.3 MW of Unforced Capacity 
(“UCAP”) is a reasonable mechanism to address this shortfall, particularly given the immense 
pressure from states, governors, and the White House for PJM to act expeditiously.6
Indeed, the RBP Proposal represents a measured approach to address a serious reliability 
concern. PJM has designed the procurement initiative to balance urgency with market principles—
including a pay-as-bid structure,7 a bilateral contracting offset mechanism,8 and a Contract for 
Difference (“CfD”) settlement approach that mitigates ratepayer costs. While SEIA commends 
PJM for bringing this proposal forward under extraordinary time constraints, SEIA respectfully 
offers the following comments to identify areas where the Commission and PJM may wish to 
consider refinements that could further unlock capacity participation and reduce procurement 
costs—both in connection with the RBP and, where applicable, in the broader capacity market 
reforms that PJM and the Commission may undertake in subsequent proceedings.

II.
COMMENTS 
A.
ELCC Uncertainty and Commercial Operation Date Requirements May 
Limit Timely Capacity Participation. 
The RBP Proposal requires successful resources to commit to a fixed period of 15 Delivery 
Years beginning with the 2028/2029 Delivery Year9 and to achieve commercial operation 
(“COD”) by no later than June 1, 2032. While SEIA does not oppose the 15-year term or the COD 
requirement, it identifies two concerns with how these parameters interact with existing capacity 
market rules in ways that may limit participation and increase procurement costs.  
First, there is a temporal mismatch: the RBP is designed to address a shortfall beginning in 
the 2028/2029 Delivery Year, yet resources are not required to achieve COD until June 1, 2032. 
SEIA notes that PJM’s proposed clearing structure10 partially addresses this concern by ranking 
offers first by the earliest Delivery Year in which capacity would be available and then by least 
cost—thereby prioritizing resources that can deliver capacity sooner. However, the COD deadline 
itself warrants further attention, as discussed below.11
Second, the 15-year commitment exposes resources to long-duration ELCC variability that 
is difficult or impossible for an individual resource to underwrite—a challenge that exists 
independent of PJM’s clearing structure and that may deter participation from resources that could 
otherwise contribute to meeting the reliability need. 
While PJM captures ELCC decline in its published 10-year forecast, PJM has not published 
a forecast out to 15 years.12 An individual RBP resource cannot control, predict, or hedge its ELCC

B.
The Compressed Timeline for Bilateral Contracting May Result in Higher 
Procurement Costs. 
The compressed timeline for the RBP procurement window may limit bilateral contracting 
opportunities and result in unnecessarily higher auction clearing prices. While SEIA does not 
oppose the RBP Proposal in principle, it notes that without sufficient time for load-serving entities 
and prospective suppliers to negotiate bilateral contracts, the Qualifying RBP Offset mechanism 
may not achieve its intended effect of reducing the procurement target. PJM is targeting 
commencement of the procurement window on September 30, 2026, with a three-week offer 
submission window and clearing concluding six weeks thereafter.19
Indeed, the RBP Proposal appropriately incorporates a mechanism by which bilateral 
contracts and self-supply arrangements for new capacity serving new load reduce the Initial RBP 
Target through the Qualifying RBP Offset. In theory, this mechanism should exert downward 
pressure on auction prices by reducing the quantity of capacity that must be procured through the 
competitive auction process.20 However, SEIA is concerned that the accelerated timeline from 
PJM Board approval on July 27, 2026, to the anticipated September 30, 2026 procurement 
commencement may not provide sufficient opportunity for load-serving entities and prospective 
suppliers to negotiate and execute the bilateral contracts that would qualify as offsets. In turn, if 
the time available for bilateral contracting proves insufficient, fewer Qualifying RBP Offsets may 
be available to reduce the Final RBP Target, and the resulting auction may clear a larger volume 
of capacity at potentially higher prices than would otherwise be necessary. SEIA encourages the 
Commission and PJM to monitor the effectiveness of the bilateral contracting mechanism and to

C.
Recommendations for Unlocking Additional Capacity 
In order to enable PJM to unlock additional capacity and reduce procurement costs, SEIA 
respectfully offers the following recommendations for the Commission’s and PJM’s consideration: 
First, PJM should offer a fixed-for-floating ELCC swap option for resources 
participating in the RBP and for bilateral arrangements qualifying as RBP Offsets. Year-over-
year volatility in ELCC accreditation values creates significant uncertainty for resources 
contemplating long-term commitments such as those required by the RBP. Stabilizing ELCC 
values—whether through longer averaging periods22 or other mechanisms—would give 
prospective participants greater confidence in projecting their capacity revenues over the 15-year 
commitment period and would thereby encourage broader participation. When a resource can 
reasonably forecast its accredited capacity value over the commitment term, it can underwrite 
financing and commit to a 15-year obligation with confidence that its revenues will support the 
investment. Absent such predictability, resources face the risk that declining ELCC values will

Second, the Commission should consider whether the minimum contract length for 
bilateral arrangements under the RBP is sufficient to enable meaningful bilateral contracting.23
The minimum five-year contract requirement for bilateral arrangements24 qualifying as RBP 
Offsets may be insufficient to enable meaningful bilateral contracting. While parties are free to 
negotiate contracts of any length, the five-year minimum effectively sets a floor that, as a practical 
matter, is likely to define the standard term for bilateral arrangements under the RBP Proposal. 
Project economics and financing structures for new capacity resources typically require a 
commitment term closer to ten years to justify the capital investment and development risk 
associated with new generation. To the extent the Commission can encourage bilateral contracting 
by providing clarity that longer-term bilateral arrangements are acceptable and supported under 
the Qualifying RBP Offset framework, such action would incentivize supply participation in 
bilateral contracting and thereby reduce reliance on the auction, with attendant downward pressure 
on clearing prices.
Third, moving beyond the RBP, PJM and the Commission should pursue seasonal 
capacity markets.25 A seasonal market construct would better align capacity obligations with the 
periods in which resources can deliver capacity value, recognizing that different resource types 
contribute to reliability in different seasons. Seasonal markets would enable a broader array of

III.
CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, SEIA respectfully submits that while the RBP Proposal 
represents a necessary and timely response to PJM’s resource adequacy challenges, the 
Commission should consider the refinements identified herein—including accommodations for 
bilateral contracting timelines, measures to provide capacity certainty over the commitment term, 
removal of commercial barriers to surplus interconnection, and reforms to unlock additional 
participation—as it evaluates the RBP Proposal as a means of securing the timely addition of new 
capacity. To the extent the Commission identifies opportunities to incorporate such improvements,
