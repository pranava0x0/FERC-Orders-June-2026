Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or “Commission”),1 FirstEnergy Service Company, as agent for its affiliates (“FirstEnergy”),2 respectfully submits this Limited Protest and Comments in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) August 13, 2026 filing, proposing to establish an Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) mechanism.3

In the Statement of Principles Regarding PJM (“Statement of Principles”), the National Energy Dominance Council and 13 of the Governors of the PJM States joined together and identified that there would be a class of new data centers that have not self-procured capacity or agreed to be curtailable.4  Under the Statement of Principles, this class of new data centers was to be excluded from PJM’s procurement of new Capacity Resources.  PJM’s IRAS Proposal purports to outline how service is to be provided at the wholesale level to this class of data centers; the headline being that these new data centers either must bring their own new capacity (“BYONC”) or be curtailable.

LIMITED PROTEST AND COMMENTS
I. PJM is unlawfully attempting to set retail rates by proposing that new data centers that are to be curtailable under the IRAS Proposal are to be paid to curtail.

As the Commission is aware, its ratemaking jurisdiction is limited to interstate transmission and wholesale power sales.10  States regulate retail rates for retail customers.  And new data centers are retail customers.

These broad principles notwithstanding, PJM has filed a tariff that calls for retail payments to be made when new data centers are curtailed.11  PJM even goes so far as to propose a retail rate that is to be paid to the curtailing data center; although PJM leaves final determination of the payment amount in the hands of the jurisdictional state.12

There is no basis under federal law for PJM to propose this rate.  PJM’s IRAS Proposal is careful to exclude curtailable new data centers from its “Reliability Pricing Model” capacity procurement.13  In fact, such loads are not participating directly in any PJM power market.  This is because they are retail loads.  It is true that Load-Serving Entities may buy power at wholesale for resale for end-use consumption by a new data center.  But this does not mean that a curtailable new data center is participating directly in PJM’s power markets.  As such, these loads do not

It is axiomatic that marginal-price market models do not pay for new capital investment in high cost-of-capital fields; an outcome that is reflected by the fact that marginal-price design RPM has brought very little significant new dispatchable generation in PJM. III. PJM is discriminating against new generation that aligns itself with a new data center by proscribing the opportunity for that resource to serve as a Fixed Resource Requirement generator.

PJM proposes that new BYONC resources must participate in RPM.18  That is, PJM is forcing new Capacity Resources that are aligned with new data centers into the RPM market. This is unduly discriminatory and preferential as it treats data centers and data center- aligned new resources differently from new loads and generation that otherwise have the option to opt into a full or partial Fixed Resource Requirement (FRR).

Section B of Schedule 8.1 of the PJM Reliability Assurance Agreement (RAA) sets forth the terms under which a certain load may “opt out” of the RPM program by demonstrating that it is eligible for the FRR program.19  An eligible LSE must demonstrate that all or part of its load falls within the RAA’s FRR Service Area definition (capable of being metered separately from other load), and that it has procured eligible Capacity Resources to cover that load and the FRR- defined associated resource adequacy reserve requirement. A. The definition of “Large Load” should be adjusted, either to take the minimum megawatt threshold to 200 MW, or to limit the definition to new data centers.

As relevant here, PJM proposes to define Large Loads as an end-use customer that has a cumulative peak of 50MW or greater.26  This definition will capture new data centers.  But it will also capture small and medium-sized manufacturing facilities.

The scope of the Statement of Principles document, which is the policy guidance that should control for review of PJM’s IRAS proposal, is limited to new data centers.27  And for good cause.  It is true that similar energy infrastructure must be constructed without regard to whether the new facility is a data center or a manufacturing facility.  But that is where the similarity ends. Manufacturing facilities generate numerous upstream, local and downstream investment and jobs; whereas data centers do not.  That is, manufacturing facilities generate significant upstream, local and downstream benefits for workers, their communities, and the states that comprise PJM; whereas the economic benefits of data centers are different and more limited.

There are material differences between a new data center and a manufacturing facility. First, a new data center will generate many jobs during initial construction; but very few full-time jobs post construction, whereas a new manufacturing facility will generate many permanent full- time jobs.  Next, once it is up and running, the new data center will consume enormous amounts of energy, and that’s it.  In contrast, a new manufacturing facility will consume raw and somewhat processed materials; the extraction/processing/supply of such also will generate many full-time jobs.  Importantly, each full-time job that is supported directly or indirectly by a manufacturing facility will support a family.  And families own homes, pay taxes, and participate in communities.

D. PJM should clarify that “Energy Resources” can be “new” Capacity Resources for purposes of evaluating a Data Center’s compliance with the proposed Bring Your Own New Capacity option for removal from IRAS.

It is clear that PJM is attempting to ensure that existing Capacity Resources are not removed from the Reliability Pricing Model markets and then paired with a new data center so that the new data center can be removed from the IRAS.
