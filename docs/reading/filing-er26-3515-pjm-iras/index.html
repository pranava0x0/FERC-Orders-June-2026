ER26-3515-000 · 2026-08-13 · PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.

PJM proposes a large-load registry, interim service and bring-your-own capacity

PJM files tariff and Reliability Assurance Agreement revisions creating Interim Resource Adequacy Service, a Large Load Registry, definitions of Large Load and New Large Load, and a Variable Resource Requirement curve that excludes new large loads without their own capacity.

Asks FERC to: Accept the Tariff and RAA revisions by October 12, 2026, effective as requested, with PJM's consent to FERC modifying or severing elements under FPA Section 205.

Parallel proceeding. PJM's separate Section 205 filing, ER26-3515, which cites the June 2026 show cause order. IRAS is a curtailable service for new large loads, and the registry defines Large Load, both themes of E-7.

E-7 PJM: Service for flexible loads (P 85, p. 56) E-7 PJM: Application / study process (P 6(a), p. 7) E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. IRAS lets PJM prioritize load reduction in zones where new large loads brought no capacity of their own, and only in real-time shortage conditions.
    a service to be implemented by PJM solely during periods when there are insufficient resources to reliably meet demand in real time operations. PDF p. 7
  2. New large loads avoid IRAS by bringing new capacity, and PJM ties the design to SPP's CHILLS and the June show cause orders.
    This principle lays the foundation for the IRAS paradigm in PJM that facilitates large load interconnections in a manner similar to the Southwest Power Pool’s (“SPP”) Conditional High Impact Large Load Service PDF p. 10
  3. A Large Load is defined as 50 MW or more of cumulative peak load at a single electrical site, with affiliated facilities within one mile combined.
    cumulative peak load of 50 megawatts [(“MW”)] or greater at a single electrical site behind one or more points of interconnection PDF p. 11
  4. PJM would direct each distributor zone to cut load up to the MW tied to eligible new large loads, and states choose which load to reduce.
    each Electric Distributor zone/area may be directed by PJM dispatchers to reduce load up to the MW quantity of capacity attributable to Eligible New Large Loads in that zone/area. PDF p. 12
  5. Compensation for load reduction is capped at 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate.
    50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate—as the maximum rate for IRAS performance. PDF p. 108
  6. From 2029/2030 PJM would exclude new large loads without bring-your-own-capacity arrangements from the demand curve.
    exclude from the VRR Curve New Large Loads, to the extent they do not have arrangements with sufficient BYONC, beginning with the 2029/2030 Delivery Year. PDF p. 29
  7. PJM proposes no end date for IRAS.
    not proposing an end date to the IRAS program. PDF p. 78
  8. PJM requests an October 12, 2026 effective date for the revisions and registry, with IRAS available from June 1, 2027.
    make the enclosed proposed Tariff and RAA revisions effective by no later than October 12, 2026 PDF p. 127

Support for a registry

PJM reported that 82% of 66 poll respondents supported a registration requirement. That was a poll about the registry, not support for every proposed curtailment rule.

82% of the 66 stakeholder respondents agreed that a Large Load registration requirement is necessary PDF p. 36
Filed comments: Registry access

Resource adequacy · PDF pp. 49–79

Bring capacity or accept reductions

PJM asked to require a load-serving entity to bring new capacity for an eligible new large customer or face reductions. FERC rejected this part of the proposal in October.

Requirement for New Large Loads to bring capacity or be subject to load reductions PDF p. 55

Curtailment · PDF pp. 80–103

When the operators would call an event

PJM proposed adding reductions of eligible large loads to its emergency sequence. It emphasized that dispatchers still need discretion over the order of actions as conditions change.

the rapidness of system events influences the timing and order of actions necessary to maintain system reliability PDF p. 87

Customer compensation · PDF pp. 104–120

Payment when a large load cuts demand

The August proposal permitted a retail rate from zero to half the non-performance charge rate. This was part of the proposed service; the registry that survived in October does not create that payment.

a retail rate between 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate and zero dollars PDF p. 112
