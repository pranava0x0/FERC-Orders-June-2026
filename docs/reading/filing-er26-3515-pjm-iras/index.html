Load forecasting · PDF pp. 30–48

Support for a registry

PJM reported that 82% of 66 poll respondents supported a registration requirement. That was a poll about the registry, not support for every proposed curtailment rule.

82% of the 66 stakeholder respondents agreed that a Large Load registration requirement is necessary PDF p. 36
Filed comments: Registry access

Resource adequacy · PDF pp. 49–79

Bring capacity or accept reductions

PJM asked to require a load-serving entity to bring new capacity for an eligible new large customer or face reductions. FERC rejected this part of the proposal in October.

Requirement for New Large Loads to bring capacity or be subject to load reductions PDF p. 55

Curtailment · PDF pp. 80–103

When the operators would call an event

PJM proposed adding reductions of eligible large loads to its emergency sequence. It emphasized that dispatchers still need discretion over the order of actions as conditions change.

the rapidness of system events influences the timing and order of actions necessary to maintain system reliability PDF p. 87

Customer compensation · PDF pp. 104–120

Payment when a large load cuts demand

The August proposal permitted a retail rate from zero to half the non-performance charge rate. This was part of the proposed service; the registry that survived in October does not create that payment.

a retail rate between 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate and zero dollars PDF p. 112
Filed comments: Retail and wholesale authority

Responses and related views

Aggregate figures or individual projects?

Rep. Frank Pallone Jr. (New Jersey) · Direct response

Pallone wanted as much of the registry made public as possible to improve demand forecasts. FERC records his request separately from the states’ requests for protected access. He spoke as a member of Congress.

PDF p. 18, ¶39

Pennsylvania asks to see curtailment data

Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission · Direct response

Pennsylvania’s commission supported creating a registry but wanted state commissions to have access and wanted PJM to record curtailments. Its comments addressed the proposal before FERC rejected interim curtailment service.

PDF p. 8, section II, Large Load Registry

States need usable records to set retail rules

Organization of PJM States · Direct response

OPSI wanted meaningful state access to the registry to set retail curtailment rules and allocate costs. Its September comments distinguish the registry, which it supported, from the compensation mechanism it challenged.

PDF p. 7, section I.D

Give customers a chance to correct errors

Equinix and PJM industrial customers · Direct response

Equinix wanted customers notified and allowed to verify or correct the information PJM would use. Industrial customers also requested an appeal and safeguards for commercial data.

PDF p. 17, ¶38

Who answers for a supplier’s data?

Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative · Direct response

The cooperative asked who bears liability when a distributor reports information obtained from another party, how it could recover its compliance costs, and whether the reporting manual would be filed for review.

PDF pp. 16–17, ¶37

A registry cannot tell an operator what just tripped

Independent Market Monitor for PJM · Direct response

The market monitor distinguished enrollment data from real-time telemetry. It said PJM had not shown how it would measure an actual reduction in the proposed interim service.

PDF p. 18, ¶40

Corporate buyers oppose a uniform retail cutoff

Data Center Coalition · Direct response

The coalition supported letting customers bring their own generation but opposed the proposed interim service. It argued that PJM’s region-wide mandatory curtailment rule would displace state retail decisions.

PDF pp. 2–4

Consumer advocates tie curtailment to an alternative to the backstop

Joint Consumer Advocates · Direct response

The advocates saw value in a choice between contributing new capacity and curtailing with appropriate state action. With suitable eligibility rules, they argued, this mechanism would make a separate backstop procurement redundant.

PDF pp. 2–3, 40

Kentucky, Ohio and Virginia abstained on the IRAS comments

Organization of PJM States, recorded member positions · Direct response

Delaware, D.C., Illinois, Indiana, Maryland, Michigan, New Jersey, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Tennessee and West Virginia commissions supported OPSI’s comments. Kentucky, Ohio and Virginia commissions abstained. The vote covered the whole filing: OPSI supported a registry but wanted clarification or separate treatment of curtailment compensation.

PDF pp. 1, 7–10, footnote 2 and sections I.D–III

Google asked FERC to separate the registry from mandatory interim service

Google · Direct response

FERC records Google’s request to accept the registry on its own. Google argued that getting better large-load data need not depend on approval of PJM’s wider curtailment and compensation design.

PDF p. 16, ¶36, citing Google protest pp. 3, 18
