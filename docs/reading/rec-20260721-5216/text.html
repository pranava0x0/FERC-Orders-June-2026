COMMENTS OF THE PUBLIC UTILITIES COMMISSION OF OHIO’S 
OFFICE OF THE FEDERAL ENERGY ADVOCATE 
 
On May 12, 2026, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (Commission or FERC) 
announced a Commission-led technical conference in this proceeding to be held on July 23, 
2026. The purpose of the technical conference is to discuss PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (PJM) 
governance and stakeholder process, with a focus on identifying and evaluating actionable 
reforms to improve PJM’s ability to address system needs in a timely and efficient manner. The 
Public Utilities Commission of Ohio’s Office of the Federal Energy Advocate (Ohio FEA), 
fulfilling its statutory duty to advocate on behalf of Ohio’s retail electricity consumers,1 offers 
the following comments for the Commission’s consideration. 
I. 
Introduction 
FERC’s consideration of PJM governance at this critical time for our region is important. 
It is undisputed that the current energy landscape in PJM is driven by historic demand, primarily 
from data centers. That historic demand, combined with tightening supply from resource 
retirements, stalled new generation projects, and significant uncertainty over future 
supply/demand conditions, is the central issue facing the region. The heightened scrutiny of 
PJM’s governance is a byproduct of that energy landscape. A robust governance structure, that is

transparent and accountable, will be vital as PJM, its Members, and the states collaboratively 
work together to move the region from where we are today to where we need to be tomorrow. 
As a restructured state and the largest load in PJM, Ohio seeks to be a constructive 
partner in working with the Commission and PJM to find solutions that address the energy 
demands of the region. Ohio relies on PJM to ensure resource adequacy and reliability at least 
cost. While restructured states have a role in the partnership and are responsible for protecting 
retail customers, PJM’s competitive markets must deliver the generation resources necessary to 
meet load across the region. Ohio is doing its part to facilitate a competitive environment for 
project developers to build new generation resources here.  
In 2024, Governor DeWine identified the need for more dispatchable generation to be 
studied and connected by PJM quickly. Ohio supported both PJM’s Reliability Resource 
Initiative and subsequent Expedited Interconnection Track to enable PJM to respond quickly to 
shovel-ready projects. In 2025, the Ohio General Assembly passed Substitute House Bill 15, 
setting a pathway for new generation in Ohio. This bill shortens generation siting processes, 
offers tax incentives for new generation equipment, and expands opportunities for behind-the-
meter generation, which serves as an important tool for data centers to begin operations without 
impacting the regional grid. Governor DeWine also announced the creation of the Energy 
Opportunity Initiative, a $100 million fund to provide grants and low-interest loans over a five-
year period to help offset costs related to natural gas and nuclear power production in Ohio. 
Ohio has already seen positive impacts. We are encouraged and excited by energy project 
development across Ohio that will benefit the region. Since January 2025, more than 17,000 MW 
of natural gas projects have been announced, filed applications, or were approved at the Ohio 
Power Siting Board. Nuclear is also playing an increasingly important role in meeting growing

II. 
Comments 
A. 
PJM Board and Filing Rights  
Notwithstanding the significant challenges currently facing the region, the Ohio FEA 
acknowledges the consistent effort of the PJM staff to maintain open communication with the 
states as well as other stakeholders. Additionally, the relatively recent formation of PJM’s State 
Policy Solutions group meaningfully increased regular dialogue between PJM staff and state 
commission staff as well as other stakeholders. In any conversation on PJM’s going forward 
organizational structure, the vital expertise of PJM staff on the highly complex and technical 
matters related to the electric grid must be maintained.  
The Ohio FEA encourages the Commission to take a pragmatic view on the role of the 
PJM Board. Like other corporations, the PJM Board should set the organizational direction,

4 
serve as a check on upper management, act in the best interest of its members and the public, and 
be responsive to external constituents.  
The Ohio FEA strongly supports the ability of the PJM Board to act independently when 
necessary and encourages the Commission to position the PJM Board similarly to other 
RTO/ISOs across the country in this regard. Specifically, as other commenters in this docket 
have highlighted, the PJM Board does not have authority to act on every governing document 
related to the grid enterprise it administers. For example, the PJM Board could act on matters 
related to the capacity market because the capacity market rules are contained in sections of 
PJM’s Tariff and Reliability Assurance Agreement where filing rights under section 205 of the 
Federal Power Act (FPA) are held by PJM. Conversely, other components of PJM governance 
such as the energy and ancillary services markets, emergency procedures, and regional 
transmission planning are contained in PJM’s Operating Agreement (OA) where filing rights 
under section 205 of the FPA are held by the PJM Members. As a result, the PJM Board cannot 
act independently to change components of the OA without a threshold of 2/3 support from the 
PJM Members.  
This limitation on the independence of the PJM Board must be remedied. It greatly 
hampers the ability of the Board to act on substantial portions of PJM’s governing documents, 
including areas of concern to Ohio like the scope of PJM’s regional transmission plan. With 
virtually every aspect of transmission in the region, including local projects, controlled solely by 
the transmission owners, there is no way for the PJM Board, or the states, to have any 
meaningful input. For example, the 2025 Regional Transmission Expansion Plan approved 
approximately $1.04 billion in supplemental projects in Ohio that are not required for reliability, 
economic efficiency, or operational performance. These projects receive limited review, with

PJM’s study focused only on ensuring that there will be no adverse impact to the grid, rather than 
considering factors such as the cost or need for the project. This is unacceptable and we would 
ask that PJM be directed to address this issue, in collaboration with the states and transmission 
owners.  
Constraints on the PJM Board are magnified by the difficulty the PJM Members have in 
finding consensus on certain issues. Currently, if the PJM Members do not reach supermajority 
support on changes to the OA, no changes are proposed to the Commission. The PJM Board 
should have the authority to independently and unilaterally propose changes to the OA under 
section 205 of the FPA.  
Further, the Ohio FEA agrees that the states should have an expanded role in PJM 
governance and an ability to bring proposed solutions before the Commission, whether through 
“jump ball” rights like ISO New England or other means. As the need for difficult policy 
decisions across the region increases, it is incumbent on the states to collaborate with PJM and 
bring state resources and engagement to bear in tackling the road ahead. Giving states an avenue 
to advance proposals to the Commission without compromising PJM’s authority to do so could 
be beneficial in driving the PJM stakeholder process to timely and durable solutions to the 
critical matters at hand. The Ohio FEA views state filing authority on resource adequacy and cost 
allocation as a tool to be applied in limited circumstances and emphasizes its opinion that the 
Organization of PJM States, Inc. (OPSI), as the states’ technical experts, act upon such authority 
only when strong support of its member states is achieved. As an alternative approach, the Ohio 
FEA also agrees with Pat Wood’s recommendation that PJM consult with OPSI before it submits 
a section 205 filing and that the filing include OPSI’s vote and load weighted positions.

B. 
PJM Stakeholder Process  
Just as a robust governance structure is important to meet the moment, so too is a 
transparent and collaborative stakeholder process. However, something is not working if PJM’s 
stakeholder process is not reaching consensus or able to determine a course of action on pressing 
issues in a timely manner. The current process suffers under its own weight with many private 
interests involved and no timeframe or deadline for action. The Ohio FEA agrees that efforts to 
streamline PJM’s stakeholder process in reasonable ways, such as by establishing timeframes or 
deadlines for action could, if combined with PJM Board and state filing rights, assist 
stakeholders in more readily moving solution packages to the Board for consideration. We also 
agree with Pat Wood’s recommendation that the Commission should consider whether to 
transition to an advisory role for PJM Members. Although the Members provide valuable input 
in the stakeholder process, the PJM Board should be able to render independent decisions on the 
matters before it, as is the case in other RTO/ISOs. 
C. 
Additional State Input 
Separately, the Ohio FEA supports and stands ready to work with the newly formed 
Governors’ Collaborative. There is no question that the states in the PJM region are diverse, and 
the challenge of regional decision making will not go away. It is our hope that the Governors’ 
Collaborative, as collective representatives of the diverse PJM region, will limit individual state 
political and policy preferences from entering the energy equation, which will improve the noted 
challenges with PJM’s stakeholder process and enhance the future of regional coordination and 
well-functioning markets. The Ohio FEA views a critical opportunity in the creation of the 
Governors’ Collaborative: to facilitate regional dialogue and consensus-building, which, in turn, 
should facilitate PJM’s ability to return to its core mission of providing reliability at least cost.

The Ohio FEA feels strongly that any formal actions of the Governors’ Collaborative should, at a 
minimum, represent the diverse political and energy characteristics of the PJM states. In addition 
to the formal recognition of the Governors’ Collaborative, the organization should be vested with 
the authority to bring issue charges and problem statements to the PJM Board, to provide state 
perspectives directly to the Board when Member consensus cannot be reached, and to appoint 
three members to the PJM Board Nominating Committee.  
III. 
Conclusion 
The Ohio FEA appreciates the Commission’s leadership and the ability to offer 
comments on the important topic of PJM governance and stakeholder reform. Ohio depends on a 
strong partnership with PJM, and on its competitive markets to deliver abundant, affordable, and 
reliable energy supplies. Ohio is ready to collaborate with FERC, PJM, and our fellow states to 
achieve these goals.
