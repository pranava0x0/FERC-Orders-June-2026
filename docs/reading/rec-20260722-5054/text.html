(July 22, 2026)

ENGIE North America, Inc.1 (“ENGIE”) commends the Federal Energy Regulatory
Commission’s (“FERC”) leadership for convening this technical conference to explore the fundamental issues and key policy considerations necessary to ensure the long-term success of the PJM Interconnection (“PJM”). We respectfully submit these comments and look forward to following the discussion on July 23 and to future engagement with the Commission and stakeholders.

ENGIE encourages reforms that strengthen confidence in PJM’s governance and stakeholder processes while preserving the critical role that PJM’s competitive wholesale markets play in maintaining reliability, supporting investment, and promoting affordability across the region. More specifically, we propose reforms that:

• Reaffirm the criticality of the PJM wholesale markets to regional reliability, economic investment and affordability in the PJM footprint;

• Refocus the PJM Board role on governance;

• Allow for a framework for state executive-level electricity policy discussions outside of the PJM stakeholder process, such as the New England States Committee on Electricity (“NESCOE”), to enhance states’ interactions with PJM, Board and stakeholders; and

I. COMMENTS

a. PJM markets are critical to regional reliability, economic investment, and affordability in the PJM footprint.

PJM has brought significant value to customers in the region during its 30 years as a regional transmission organization (“RTO”), including improved reliability, lower system costs, more efficient transmission planning, and improved investor certainty.

Consequently, the prospect of PJM’s disaggregation is counterproductive. If ‘speed to power’ is the principal regional and national priority, then disassembling PJM in favor of a yet – to-be determined framework through an undefined transition design and timeline will not satisfy that objective. The foundation of PJM is sound, but some reforms may be needed to regain stakeholder confidence and ensure market competitiveness, while providing consumers with affordable energy.

b. Proposed Reforms on Governance

1. The PJM Board should focus on governance.

Stakeholders advise; PJM manages; and the Board governs. These principles of an independent board include objective oversight, uncompromised integrity, and the protection of long-term stakeholder value. ENGIE supports a Board independent from outside and special interests, and that is responsive and willing to engage with regional stakeholders on topics of strategic importance.

The PJM Board should focus on the traditional and essential matters of governance: providing strategic oversight, ensuring accountability, managing risk, and promoting PJM’s long-term success. The Board should not be excessively involved in the tactical stakeholder

process. It is an inefficient use of time and resources for the Board to participate directly in stakeholder decision-making exercises, such as evaluating a matrix of many complex and competing choices. The Board should instead be advised of and consulted on major reforms so that Board members are appropriately informed, remain accountable and, in turn, can hold the PJM organization responsible for implementation of market design and other changes. Because implementing this change will necessarily require both increased interaction between PJM leadership and the Board and decreased direct Board interaction with stakeholders, in the inevitable transition period, it is important that regional stakeholders are reassured that the Board will continue to keep stakeholder interests in mind in the long term. PJM stakeholders should have regular, formal and informal opportunities to meet with the Board to discuss items of interest. For example, market participants in New England meet with the ISO-NE Board twice a year in meetings with their New England Power Pool (“NEPOOL”) sectors. The Board also engages through other regional opportunities such as with the ISO-NE Consumer Liaison Group and the New England Conference of Public Utilities Commissioners and are generally responsive to individual market participant outreach.

Refocusing the PJM Board on strategic governance matters will affect the role of senior leadership, which will be required to take a more singular role in managing stakeholder relations and making final decisions on market reforms. With PJM leadership taking on a more decisive role in final decision-making PJM leadership must be increasingly available to stakeholders both within and outside of the stakeholder process to demonstrate their understanding and commitment to stakeholder requests and inquiries.

2. Improve states’ ability to engage with PJM and stakeholders
through an independent framework for executive-level electricity
policy discussions.

ENGIE supports a robust, collaborative, and communicative relationship between the PJM member states, the PJM Board and PJM leadership and market participants. States are important customers and policymakers, and their decisions can have significant consequences on the wholesale markets. We offer some observations based on our experience in PJM and other regions.

Generally, in regions where there are mediating institutions outside of the RTO/ISO stakeholder process that facilitate state collaboration on electricity issues states have an enhanced opportunity to communicate and collaborate before engaging with the ISO/RTO and market participants. States can discuss differences, identify areas of common interest, and where possible develop consensus positions before engaging with ISO/RTO leadership, the Board, and stakeholders.

While the Organization of PJM States (“OPSI”) provides one arena for states to communicate concerns, ENGIE recommends further developing a consensus-driven framework for executive level state engagement outside of the PJM stakeholder process. States should consider forming a new organization that represents the PJM governors on electricity issues to facilitate state executive level collaboration. The template we have in mind is NESCOE, who represents the collective perspective of the six New England governors on regional electricity matters. NESCOE is effective in the NEPOOL stakeholder process, and with ISO-NE and the ISO-NE Board and works in collaboration with the New England Conference of Public Utilities Commissioners (“NECPUC”).

Such an organization would be instrumental in facilitating collaborative discussions between the states and PJM leadership and the PJM Board. It could allow PJM leadership to engage regularly with the governors and their representatives on major market reforms, increase engagement on reliability assessments and planning assumptions, allow formal opportunities for state input before major tariff filings, and convene regular meetings with the PJM Board. The PJM Board should, likewise, consider assigning individual Board members to interact with states to increase communication between the Board and the states, as is done in New England. Instituting an independent framework for better organizing and representing governors in the PJM footprint, even when they disagree over policy, will permit the governors to better represent their views before the PJM Board, PJM leadership and with market participants. Further, increased early collaboration amongst governors and PJM leadership and the Board should help to streamline the governors’ role in the stakeholder process and reduce friction.  In turn, this would help streamline the overall PJM decision-making process.

c. Proposed Stakeholder Process Reforms

ENGIE believes that the existing stakeholder process has served PJM well for many years by promoting broad stakeholder participation and some level of transparency. The primary challenge today is ensuring the process can reach timely decisions. While some practices were intended to address critical issues quickly, the pace, scale, and uncertainty of load growth in the region today has thwarted those practices and they may now prolong deliberation and delay the resolution of critical market design issues.

Consequently, a swift, thorough and inclusive stakeholder process in PJM is more critical than ever. Durable solutions to the region’s resource adequacy challenges will require enhanced

collaboration, open dialogue, and a shared understanding among PJM, market participants, and the states.

None of our proposed changes should be construed to undermine the importance of market participants to the PJM markets and stakeholder process.  Market participants’ commercial and operational experience is critical to adequately evaluate proposed market design reforms. Without the benefit of market participant experience, PJM reforms risk being practically or commercially infeasible or having adverse unintended consequences.

States are also critical stakeholders and should have a voice in the stakeholder process. However, PJM’s markets must remain operationally independent and regionally focused. State policies affect the resource mix, transmission development, electrification, and load growth, and therefore should be communicated and considered during market design efforts. Building a voice for the states includes more meaningful and earlier opportunities to shape discussions, particularly where state policies affect resource development.

Finally, ENGIE believes PJM staff have done a masterful job of facilitating collaboration and identifying differences across the diverse stakeholder interests. What ENGIE recommends for faster decision-making is a mandate from FERC, the Board, and stakeholders to allow PJM senior leadership to act more decisively in proposing final decisions on market reforms.

i. Accelerate stakeholder decision-making through greater discipline and focus during the stakeholder process.

The stakeholder process can be accelerated through greater discipline and focus at every level of the governance structure. As issues move from working groups to subcommittees, committees, and ultimately the Board, participants are often required to revisit the same discussions, presentations, and questions multiple times. While stakeholder engagement is essential, excessive repetition can slow decision-making, consume significant resources, and

delay outcomes on matters that are increasingly urgent for the region. A more disciplined approach should ensure that each level of the stakeholder process builds upon prior work rather than revisiting issues that have already been evaluated.

PJM should also adopt stronger meeting management practices to ensure discussions remain productive and focused. Effective facilitation, coupled with active time management during presentations and Q&A, can help curb repetitive or off-topic comments and keep proceedings moving forward. Implementing clear time limits for presentations, questions, and stakeholder comments would encourage concise, substantive participation while ensuring all perspectives are heard. These measures would improve the efficiency of the stakeholder process without diminishing transparency or inclusiveness.

ii. Accelerate stakeholder consideration of PJM proposals through greater
transparency in analytical assumptions and decision rationale.

Data.  Data.  Data.  ENGIE believes that a core responsibility of an ISO/RTO is to provide data, analysis methodology, and assumptions to market participants so they can reach their own conclusions and make commercial decisions accordingly. In general, surprises lead to confusion, consternation at times, and detract from efficient and timely discovery and resolution of needed market reforms.

Greater transparency in both analytical assumptions made by PJM in its proposals as well as in its decision rationale when choosing to move forward with proposals would significantly improve stakeholder understanding of the proposals and at the same time build credibility and garner trust. Earlier publication of modeling inputs, more detailed documentation of sensitivity analyses, better visibility into reliability and market benefit calculations, and discussion and documentation of tradeoffs considered by PJM staff and explanations of why PJM supports or opposes stakeholder proposals are essential to an improved stakeholder process. may disagree on outcomes, but confidence increases when everyone understands the data, assumptions, and rational underlying a decision.

ENGIE is particularly concerned with the lack of information and rigor in the stakeholder process when designing new market products. At times market participants have been assured that new market products will not significantly alter market pricing but the products have instead led to significant price volatility. More upfront information and increased transparency in analysis would improve market participants’ awareness and permit them to hedge risks appropriately.

Generally, process reforms should focus on ensuring robust stakeholder input, improving transparency of PJM proposals and transparency into decision-making rationale, combined with the ability to make timely decisions that support reliability and efficient investment. We recommend the Commission consider the following reforms to PJM’s stakeholder process:

• Replace affiliate voting at lower levels with a ‘one company, one vote’ structure so that no one stakeholder has an outsized influence on the vote outcome;

• Publish votes of all committees to promote transparency and accountability in stakeholder voting;

• Increase transparency of PJM proposals specifically in analytical assumptions which would improve stakeholder understanding of PJM proposals and increase credibility and trust. PJM should focus on earlier publication of modeling inputs, more detailed documentation of sensitivity analyses, and better visibility into reliability and market benefit calculations.

• Increase transparency in PJM staff and PJM leadership decision rationale through more in-depth discussions and documentation of tradeoffs considered by PJM staff and explanations of why PJM supports or opposes stakeholder proposals, especially when PJM leadership selects one avenue when stakeholder views are divided.

• Encourage the states to create a framework for governors to collaborate meaningfully in the stakeholder process through an outside organization, such as NESCOE. PJM should engage regularly with the governors and focus on early

II. CONCLUSION
ENGIE respectfully requests that the Commission consider these recommendations as it evaluates opportunities to strengthen PJM’s governance and stakeholder processes. The proposed reforms would improve decision-making, enhance transparency and accountability, restore stakeholder confidence, and better position PJM to address the reliability, affordability, and investment challenges facing the region without requiring disruptive structural changes to the existing PJM markets.
