Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC” or “Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Midwest TDUs2 respectfully move for leave to answer and answer the November 9, 2026, Motion for Leave to Answer and Answer filed by the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (“MISO”).3 MISO’s Answer responds to, among other things, Midwest TDUs’ Limited Protest4 of MISO’s October 2, 2026 filing to revise its Open Access Transmission, Energy, and Operating Reserve Markets Tariff (“Tariff”) to establish a Large Load Addition Resource Study (“LARS”) process.5

Midwest TDUs previously raised two issues with MISO’s LARS proposal: (1) an important flaw in MISO’s proposed scope for LARS eligibility that requires correction, and (2) the need to ensure that all cost-shifting issues associated with LARS are fully addressed in future filing(s). MISO’s Answer resolves neither of these concerns.

First, Midwest TDUs and others previously protested MISO’s proposal to make paired generation and load within the same Local Resource Zone (“LRZ” or “Zone”) eligible for the expedited LARS process. MISO’s Answer fails to address specific problems raised about this scope. Instead, MISO’s Answer repeats vague assertions about identifying optimized sets of Network Upgrades, which fails to demonstrate that the proposed scope for LARS eligibility is just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory in light of the problems identified in the Commission’s Show Cause Order.6 Second, MISO’s Answer doubles down on MISO’s contradictory clam that

critical cost shifting issues will be addressed in future filing(s), but that the Commission should nevertheless accept LARS independently from those Tariff reforms essential to making LARS just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential. MISO’s argument rests of the false premise that the Commission must choose between timely implementation of LARS or consideration of cost shifting in the context of LARS. Not so. The Commission should instead condition any acceptations of MISO’s LARS proposal on the Commission finding, in future proceeding(s), that MISO has satisfied its obligations with respect to the Show Cause Order’s discussion of cost allocation and transparency as applied to LARS.

Finally, MISO is wrong to suggest that there is no downside to broadening the scope to LARS eligibility.23 MISO proposes to limit the number of generation/load pairs that may be studied in each LARS Study Period.24 As a result, the inclusion in LARS of pairs of generation and load that do not reduce the need for Network Upgrades (e.g., those not electrically proximate) risks crowding out those pairs that do create greater efficiencies (e.g., those that are sufficiently electrically proximate such that their impact on the transmission system is the same as if they were located at the same substation). An overbroad scope thus undermines the benefits LARS is intended to achieve. Moreover, to be just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory, an expedited interconnection process must be “narrowly tailored to the problem being addressed.”25 Here, MISO has failed to show that a zonal scope for LARS eligibility is responsive to the problem identified in the Commission’s Show Cause Order, and MISO has not meet its burden of showing that this scope is just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory.

Relatedly, MISO also argues that concerns about cost shifting should not delay the large load-related reforms included in its LARS proposal. This presents the Commission with a false choice between protecting against cost shifting and delaying implementation of LARS. Midwest TDUs do not seek to delay LARS, and the conditional acceptance we request allows the Commission to facilitate timely implementation of LARS while still fulfilling its statutory mandate to ensure just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory rates.33

Finally, MISO states that “there is nothing in the LARS proposal that would prevent a state regulator from allocating costs of a BRP or Other project more granularly to a specific retail customer.”34 While true, this statement misses the point. The Commission’s Show Cause Order specifically acknowledges the concern that state regulators cannot protect all wholesale transmission customers from cost shifting that occurs through Commission-jurisdictional transmission rates.35 This Commission is statutorily obligated to address cost shifting that occurs through rates subject to its jurisdiction. It can do so with respect to costs incurred for LARS projects, while still allowing timely implementation of LARS, through the conditional acceptance described herein.

CONCLUSION
For the reasons discussed above and in Midwest TDUs’ Limited Protest, the Commission should (1) reject MISO’s proposed geographic scope for paired generation and load eligible for LARS and direct MISO to revise its Tariff language consistent with the Show Cause Order; and (2) condition any acceptance on MISO’s filing in this proceeding on the Commission finding, in future proceeding(s), that MISO has satisfied its obligations with respect to the Show Cause Order’s discussion of cost allocation and transparency as applied to LARS.
