Pursuant to the Notice Requesting Post-Conference Comment issued by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission ("Commission") on July 30, 2026 in the above-captioned docket, Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. ("SMECO") respectfully submits these post- conference comments. SMECO appreciates the opportunity to supplement the record developed at the Commission's July 23, 2026 technical conference regarding the governance and stakeholder processes of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. ("PJM").

I. STATEMENT OF INTEREST
SMECO is a member-owned, not-for-profit electric distribution cooperative headquartered in Hughesville, Maryland, serving customer-members in Calvert, Charles, Prince George's, and St. Mary's Counties in Southern Maryland. SMECO is a PJM Member and participates in PJM's stakeholder process as a member of the Electric Distributor sector. Because SMECO's customer- members ultimately bear the costs of decisions made through PJM's governance and stakeholder processes, SMECO has a direct and substantial interest in ensuring that those processes operate in a transparent, accountable, and timely manner consistent with the public interest.

II. SMECO'S RESPONSES TO THE APPENDIX QUESTIONS
SMECO's responses to the questions in the Appendix to the Notice are provided below. PJM Board Independence

1) Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?
X

Comments:  Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc. (SMECO) is open to revisiting Board term length, but it is opposed to the specific alternative of a single, non-renewable nine-year term. Indeed, in PJM’s recent poll of members, 71% of respondents were opposed to a single nine-year term. As to an appropriate term length, SMECO believes something on the order of five (5) years, with the opportunity for renewal, appears appropriate.

10) Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management
compensation to their achievement?

X

Comments:  If the performance metric is poorly defined, and if the wrong goal is chosen or taken out of context with regards to Good Utility Practice, then linking management compensation to achievement makes a bad situation even worse by dint of the public announcement and compensation linkage. PJM’s recent successful winter operations protocol is a perfect example: If “uplift” minimization was a goal unto itself, it may have prevented PJM from committing gas units in advance of the Day-Ahead energy market, which has turned out to be a key evolution in PJM’s operating procedures to reduce winter loss of load risk. That said, SMECO supports conditioning any bonus or incentive compensation with satisfaction of a set of customer-focused performance metrics.

Section 205 Filing Rights

Questions/Comments
Yes No
11) Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board?

X

Comments: PJM currently has unilateral FPA section 205 rights on its capacity market. Accordingly, all stakeholder activities (e.g., votes and proposals) involving the RPM/capacity market are already advisory.   PJM current resource adequacy/RPM issues are not due to the stakeholders having “power” over the PJM RPM capacity market.  It is PJM that has failed to act.  For the energy, ancillary services and reserves product markets, see SMECO’s response to Question No. 12.

12) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

X

Comments: Perhaps PJM should have FPA section 205 rights over the energy, ancillary services and reserves product markets unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee, but the question of whether Members themselves should have equipoised rights in energy, ancillary services and reserves product markets is part of the decision.

14) Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal?

X

Comments:  As noted, PJM currently has unilateral FPA section 205 rights on its capacity market; accordingly, all stakeholder activities (e.g., votes and proposals) involving the RPM/capacity are already advisory.  Any Member “jump ball rights” over PJM RPM/resource adequacy/capacity market issues should belong to PJM members (via sector-weighted supermajority), not OPSI. As to transmission cost allocation, section 205 filing rights should remain with Transmission Owners to protect cost recovery. If OPSI reaches supermajority consensus on RPM/resource adequacy issues, it should present its views to the PJM Membership (RPM/resource adequacy) or TOA-AC (transmission revenue requirement cost allocation) for consideration.
