EL26-67-000 · 2026-11-05 · US HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

Rep. Victoria Spartz asks FERC and DOE to condition approvals and protect ratepayers

Representative Victoria Spartz writes to FERC's Chairman and the Energy Secretary on November 5, 2026, urging federal action on large-load projects, ratepayer protections, transparency and PJM and MISO governance, and requesting written responses.

Asks FERC to: Identify and use federal authority to condition large-load approvals, build a national large-load inventory, make developers pay full costs, review PJM and MISO governance, and answer in writing within 30 days.

Background. Congressional letter filed in EL26-67 after the show cause order; its calls for large-load disclosure and developer-paid costs touch the E-7 cost transparency and cost-recovery directives but answer no directive.

E-7 PJM: Cost transparency (P 71, p. 51) E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28)

Key points

  1. She asks for a moratorium on discretionary approvals for new and pending large-load projects until reliability, cost and transparency safeguards exist.
    suspend or condition discretionary approvals affecting new and pending major data centers, cryptocurrency mining operations, and associated infrastructure until essential reliability, cost-allocation, transparency, and financial safeguards are established. PDF p. 2
  2. She seeks a national inventory of existing and proposed large loads, including beneficial owners and potential stranded costs.
    Establish a transparent national accounting of proposed and existing major electricity loads, identifying developers, beneficial owners, affiliates, verified demand, financial commitments PDF p. 2
  3. Large-load developers should bear incremental generation and transmission costs, with review of deposits, guarantees, minimum payments and termination fees.
    Review deposits, financial guarantees, minimum-payment obligations, termination fees, and protections against stranded costs. PDF p. 4
  4. She asks for an independent review of PJM and MISO governance that cites docket EL26-67-000.
    FERC Docket EL26-67-000: Preliminary findings concerning potentially unjust, unreasonable, or discriminatory large-load interconnection rules. PDF p. 5
  5. She requests separate written responses from FERC and DOE within 30 days and a corrective-action report within 90 days.
    I request separate written responses from FERC and DOE within 30 days identifying: PDF p. 7

A pause on discretionary approvals

Rep. Victoria Spartz asks FERC and DOE to identify authority to condition or suspend approvals until safeguards exist, and recommend legislation where authority falls short. She expressly preserves vested rights and essential reliability work.

Respect vested rights, enforceable agreements, and essential reliability or safety improvements. PDF p. 2

Cost allocation · PDF pp. 3–4

Who pays and who carries the risk?

Spartz wants major developers to bear incremental costs attributed to their projects, with deposits, minimum payments and termination fees to protect other customers.

Review deposits, financial guarantees, minimum-payment obligations, termination fees, and protections against stranded costs. PDF p. 4

Transparency · PDF pp. 5–6

What counts as one project?

Spartz asks agencies to look across phased permits and affiliates to see the total cost and identify who benefits. She also calls for scrutiny of PJM and MISO governance.

Require consolidated assessment of related developments and disclosure of material financial commitments, beneficiaries, and regulatory decisions. PDF p. 5

Oversight · PDF pp. 7–9

Congressional oversight

Spartz says she intends to seek a GAO investigation. This is a request from one member of Congress; it does not establish a federal moratorium or an Indiana commission position.

I intend to seek an independent Government Accountability Office investigation PDF p. 7
