ER26-3380-000 · 2026-08-21 · Data Center Coalition

Comments of the Data Center Coalition in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s 07/31/2026 proposed tariff filing to establish the Reliability Backstop Procurement

The Data Center Coalition comments on PJM's July 31 Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal. It supports acceptance as a limited, one-time backstop and asks FERC to protect bilateral procurement, assign costs to the large loads driving the need, and keep new capacity on schedule.

Asks FERC to: Accept PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement filing as a limited, one-time measure, consistent with the Coalition's principles on bilateral offsets, cost causation and getting infrastructure built.

Parallel proceeding. Comments in PJM's backstop docket ER26-3380. Its bring-your-own-capacity offsets and cost-causation principle bear on the E-7 adequacy report and on who pays for new load.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8) E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28)

Key points

  1. The Coalition supports the backstop as a one-time measure and wants bilateral procurement kept as the preferred path.
    DCC supports moving forward with the RBP as a limited, one-time backstop. PDF p. 2
  2. PJM should not procure duplicative capacity for a customer that has arranged qualifying new capacity.
    Where a customer has arranged for qualifying new capacity to serve its incremental demand, PJM should not procure duplicative capacity on that customer’s behalf. PDF p. 3
  3. PJM proposes about 6.8 GW of new capacity under commitments of up to fifteen years.
    PJM proposes to procure approximately 6.8 GW of new capacity through commitments that may extend for as long as fifteen years. PDF p. 5
  4. Costs of serving new large loads should fall on the new large loads, not existing customers.
    should be borne by new large loads contributing to resource inadequacy and should not be shifted to existing residential, commercial, or industrial customers PDF p. 4
  5. DCC and the Virginia State Corporation Commission agreed to develop model retail tariff provisions for backstop costs.
    DCC and the Virginia State Corporation Commission have agreed to work together to develop model retail tariff provisions designed to appropriately assign RBP costs PDF p. 6
  6. Success is measured by megawatts reaching commercial operation, which depends on interconnection and transmission upgrades.
    measure of success should not be how many MW PJM contracts for, but how many MW actually reach commercial operation in time to address the identified reliability need. PDF p. 7
