President Donald J. Trump and the Department of Energy (DOE) are committed to ensuring that American households and businesses have affordable, reliable, and secure energy. On day one, President Trump declared a national energy emergency to fix the skyrocketing energy price inflation caused by the prior administration’s policies of reducing reliable, non-intermittent American energy resources.1  President Trump subsequently obtained the pledges of utilities, states, data center developers, and cooperatives to commit to his Ratepayer Protection Pledge. Under the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, large electric energy loads—not American households or other business ratepayers—must fund the electric energy generation and related infrastructure that such new large load projects require.2

In furtherance of the President’s goals and in support of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (Commission) September 29, 2026 order, the Secretary of Energy and DOE files this notice of intervention and statement of position in the captioned docket pursuant to section 405 of the DOE Organization Act and Rule 214 of the Commission’s regulations.3  DOE specifically supports key aspects of the Commission’s guidance to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (PJM), including the counsel that PJM proactively address what the Commission has outlined by

I. Statement of Principles Regarding PJM and President Trump’s Ratepayer
Protection Pledge

On January 16, 2026, DOE Secretary Chris Wright, U.S. Department of Interior Secretary Doug Burgum, and others joined the Governors of all 13 PJM states to announce the Statement of Principles Regarding PJM, which encouraged PJM to adopt market rules to strengthen grid reliability and reduce electricity costs for American families and businesses. Subsequently, following the February 24, 2026 State of the Union address, on March 4 and July 23, 2026, President Trump, Secretary Wright, and others held roundtables regarding the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.  Signatory companies and others agreed to protect American consumers from price hikes due to their addition of large load energy demand and related infrastructure requirements, and to lower electricity costs for consumers in the long term.  Since then, additional signatories have joined the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.

The Statement of Principles Regarding PJM, in turn, called for expeditious action to address reliability and affordability concerns specifically in the PJM region.  It called for accelerating the development of reliable generation resources, protecting ratepayers from rising

II. Current State of Energy Demand in PJM Requires Immediate Action
From 2020 to 2025, demand for electric energy in the United States has increased more than 9% (i.e., 387 terawatt-hours (TWh)), from 3,918 to 4,305 TWh.14  While this load growth occurred across residential, commercial, and industrial sectors, the commercial end-use sector grew the fastest.15  PJM is the largest grid operator in the country, serving 67 million people in 13 states, plus the District of Columbia.  Over the same 2020 to 2025 period, consistent with the national trend cited above, total real-time energy use in PJM increased more than 9% (i.e., 67.9 TWh), from approximately 743.0 TWh to 810.9 TWh.16  Every PJM annual long-term forecast since 2020 has projected increased long-term demand growth, with the projected growth rate accelerating sharply since 2022.17

PJM’s capacity procurement procedures, however, are consistently failing to obtain

A. Cost Allocation That Avoids Free Ridership
DOE shares the Commission’s concerns that PJM’s proposed RBP cost allocation model may be unjust and unreasonable as filed.  The Commission found that PJM did not adequately demonstrate that its Load Adjustment methodology would allocate RBP costs consistent with the Commission’s cost-causation principle.  This principle requires that “all Commission- jurisdictional rates and charges must ‘reflect to some degree the costs actually caused by the customer who pays for them’ and costs must be allocated in a manner that is at least roughly commensurate with benefits.”24  Compliance with this principle is evaluated by comparing the costs assessed against a party to the burdens imposed or benefits drawn by that party.  In furtherance of the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, DOE emphasizes the need to protect ratepayers from the costs associated with rising demand caused by other large load customers.  Such costs include the negative reliability impacts caused by new load growth that well-exceed historic forecasts.

The Commission recognizes the need for accurate and comprehensive load information.25 By way of example, because large load demand in PJM exceeded forecasts for a number of years prior to the 2026/2027 and 2028/2029 delivery years, PJM’s cost allocation methodology (looking back only two years) does not appear to sufficiently allocate the costs of new capacity to serve all large loads that have caused the current resource adequacy shortfall. B.  Mandatory Large Load Identification and Transparency
DOE agrees with the Commission’s call for improved large load identification and transparency to support a just and reasonable cost allocation.  To ensure that existing ratepayers do not bear the costs of new large loads, PJM should have sufficient data to accurately identify the material large load projects reflected in its forecasts and monitor their development.  A capability to continuously track whether a project enters into service—and therefore benefits from the capacity and system investments procured to serve it—or is delayed, reduced, or cancelled, can help assign costs for new generation to the customers responsible for those investments.

The Commission recommends that “PJM should employ the most up-to-date load and resources forecasts to inform the Initial RBP Target.”26  DOE agrees.  Without up-to-date and project-level identification, PJM cannot reliably reconcile its base forecast with Load Adjustments, avoid omissions and double counting, revise the RBP target when forecast load fails to materialize, or attribute demand growth to the appropriate zone and load-serving entity.

IV. Responsive Action
DOE urges PJM to accept the accelerated option proposed by the Commission and to swiftly submit a revised tariff pursuant to FPA section 205 on or before October 29, 2026.  DOE likewise encourages the numerous signatories to President Trump’s Ratepayer Protection Pledge participating in these dockets directly, or indirectly through the filings of trade associations, to support further reforms consistent with the Ratepayer Protection Pledge and DOE’s recommendations herein.  Americans deserve reliable, secure, and affordable energy.  The growth of data centers and other industrial or commercial sector large loads is essential to America’s economic prosperity and a foundation of modern life.  It is also essential that large load entities calling for that power pay for it—and the infrastructure required to deliver it. Respectfully submitted,
