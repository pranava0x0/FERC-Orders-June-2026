UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
BEFORE THE
FEDERAL ENERGY REGULATORY COMMISSION
PJM Interconnection, LLC 			 		Docket No. AD26-7-000
Comments on PJM Stakeholder Governance Reform
of the PJM Cities and Communities Coalition
Introduction
On July 23, 2026, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC or “the Commission”) convened a Chairman and Commissioner-led technical conference to discuss PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (PJM)’s governance and stakeholder processes. On July 30, 2026, FERC issued a notice inviting post-conference comments and answers to a particular set of questions presented in an appendix to the notice.  
The PJM Cities and Communities Coalition (PJMCCC) appreciates FERC’s invitation and encouragement of stakeholders to provide post-conference comments to help shape discussions of PJM’s governance and stakeholder process.  PJMCCC further applauds the Commission’s continued efforts to foster governance reform in PJM.  Herewith, PJMCCC submits its response to the July 30 notice.

Background 
PJMCCC comprises 21 local governments and communities representing approximately 15% of PJM’s population. Though not a PJM member, PJMCCC members are both large end-users and representatives of the public interest of our over 10 million residents. 
Local governments have a fundamental responsibility to act as responsible stewards of public funds and represent community interests in state and regional decision-making. Because of our interest in an affordable, reliable, resilient, clean energy system, many PJMCCC members have taken steps to develop energy projects in their communities. They have siting and procurement authorities that PJM has made it harder to exercise, to the detriment of both local residents and the region more broadly. Our coalition formed because PJM’s governance, operations, market rules, and planning processes directly impact our members’ ability to procure large-scale energy projects, comply with our local laws and policies, and meet our energy goals. 
Today, cities are ready to advance their own energy projects and to help PJM bring on new energy projects to address the current capacity shortfall and rising prices; to do so, however, we need PJM’s governance processes, which are foundational to efficiently navigating changing circumstances, to be effective. This includes maintaining an independent board, enhancing state participation, and making the stakeholder process advisory. Furthermore, PJM should revise its governing documents and mission to ensure that the PJM Board and staff act in service of the public interest and affordability in addition to reliability.
PJMCCC is generally aligned with the positions of the PJM Governors Collaborative and the Organization of PJM States, Inc. (OPSI). In our answers to the questions in the appendix, we emphasize some of those shared concerns and specify additional areas of reform where we believe PJM can be improved further. 
PJMCCC also appreciates PJM’s release of a “FERC mediation term sheet” (“term sheet”) document to represent PJM’s position on what reforms it views favorably.  PJMCCC agrees with PJM’s views on some of these reforms, and on other reforms sees opportunity for PJM and FERC to pursue better paths.  PJMCCC’s views on some of the reforms in the PJM term sheet will be captured in answers to the Commission’s questions in the appendix. 

PJMCCC believes that PJM board independence and an enhanced role for the states are the critical governance issues on which the Commission and PJM should focus reforms to ensure that PJM is responsive to the public interest.
	As articulated in our pre-conference comments, PJMCCC believes that the PJM board’s role is to make responsible decisions for the entire PJM region, including all stakeholders, rather than simply reflecting the preferences of member utilities.[fn:1] As such, PJMCCC believes that the PJM board should be made more representative of regional stakeholders and their interests. However, the PJM board is unlikely to become representative in this way under the current process used to select PJM board members. Under the current process, only PJM members vote to decide who sits on the PJM board - a process that can only guarantee that the PJM board will only represent PJM members and, more specifically, PJM member utilities that have the most voting power. This in turn sets up PJM to be led by a board that prefers policies favoring member utilities’ interests over other stakeholders and the public interest. 
It is a root cause of many of PJM’s issues that the board lacks sufficient independence from the membership, and thanks to FERC’s Order No. 2000, this lack of independence is an issue that falls squarely in FERC’s jurisdiction.  PJMCCC believes this issue of independence must be addressed before certain reforms to other areas of PJM’s governance will be effective. PJMCCC agrees with comments offered during FERC’s technical conference that empowering the board with, for example, greater 205 rights will not necessarily result in improved governance for the PJM region.  As panelist Joseph Bowring articulated, simply allowing the PJM board to act faster and on a broader scope of issues will not necessarily produce better decisions for the region; indeed, it could simply lead to more rapid proliferation of worse decisions for the region.  PJMCCC’s answers to FERC’s questions will outline where PJMCCC thinks the most effective reforms can be made to get the PJM board to a level of independence compliant with Order No. 2000 and sufficient for PJM to serve the region.
	PJMCCC also believes that enhancing the role of the states is a critical issue on which reform is needed.  The fraught relationship between PJM and states has been one of the defining issues of this region, despite the interdependency between both sides in provision of reliable and affordable electric service through effective energy policies.  State authority over generation siting, procurement, and other elements of energy policy is a reality that PJM actions must confront, and as such it behooves PJM to carefully consider states’ policies and viewpoints well in advance of its actions.  As such, PJM’s governance processes and the structures through which PJM engages with the states must be improved.  The states need to be given more power in those processes to take action when PJM ignores their input.  PJMCCC’s answers to the questions on the role of the states will provide more information on which reforms are needed in this area.
Appendix: Responses to questions in the appendix of the Commission’s July 30 2026 notice.

PJM Board Independence


Questions/Comments
Yes
No
1)
Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?
X


Comments: 
Yes, PJM Board members’ terms should be extended beyond the three-year existing limit to five years.  This timeline aligns with the term of FERC commissioners and thus presents a reasonable term length.  Moreover, we find it important to stagger terms such that there is a continuous transition between board members.  This would result in multiple board members up for election each year.
We strongly object to the proposal in PJM’s term sheet to extend the term of board members to nine years.  PJM’s proposal appears grounded in the number of members on PJM’s board and the desire to ensure a smooth transition by putting only one member up for election each year.  However, we do not see support for the principle of setting the term length in years equal to the number of members of the PJM board.  Given the rapidly changing environment in which PJM is attempting to serve its functions, shorter term limits with multiple seats up for election each year are far more reasonable to allow the board itself to adapt to changing conditions.  

	2)
	Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 years)?
X


Comments:
Yes. Board members should be limited to two five-year terms.  This arrangement will balance interests and avoid entrenching modes of thinking and operation. See answer to question one (1) on board terms for additional details.
3)
Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region?
X


Comments:
PJMCCC believes it is more important to ensure that PJM board members do not have any conflicts of interest with PJM members and have relevant experience in serving PJM.  These criteria for choosing board members should not be removed or replaced with a criterion that the board members live in the PJM region.  But that considered, PJMCCC does believe that ideally PJM board members should meet all three criteria - no conflicts of interest, relevant experience, and live in the PJM region.  If a candidate search finds an insufficient number of candidates meet those three criteria, then the requirement that candidates live in the PJM region could be relaxed in order to broaden the pool, but the other two criteria should not be relaxed.
4)
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for Board members from two years to one year?

X

Comments:
PJMCCC believes that the current rules about the cooling-off period should remain unchanged.  PJMCCC views these rules as useful for improving the independence of the PJM board, and PJMCCC sees no urgent problem that revising these rules would help to solve. 
5)
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more communication between the Board and stakeholders?

X

Comments: 
PJMCCC does not see relaxing these restrictions as particularly useful for improving the PJM board’s independence.  PJMCCC does see value in requirements for further structured engagement between the board with stakeholders, as discussed in our pre-conference comments. 
6)
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be required to explain its reasoning? 
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC strongly believes that PJM board votes should be made public and that the PJM board should be required to explain its reasoning.  This simple requirement is foundational for a functional board that represents the interests of regional stakeholders.  Any arguments to the contrary face a rebuttable presumption that the votes should be made transparent and that supportive reasoning should be provided.  PJMCCC sees very little risk arising from these requirements. 
7)
In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the public?
X


Comments:  
PJMCCC strongly believes that all PJM board meetings should be open and available to the public.  This is another simple requirement that is nonetheless foundational to a functioning PJM board.  As with the answer above, any arguments to the contrary face a rebuttable presumption that the meetings should be made open and available to the public.  
It is worth noting that the Commission deals with highly sensitive and urgent matters on a frequent basis and still manages to meet its requirement to hold open meetings with transparent voting and supportive reasoning for decisions.  PJMCCC sees no reason that the PJM board cannot do the same.  
8)
Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC strongly believes that the answer is yes.  The revision should explicate that PJM’s Board, management, and staff obligations are to the public rather than to PJM members.
PJMCCC strongly disagrees with the PJM term sheet’s omission of this revision.  This revision is absolutely essential and there is no adequate reason not to implement it.
9)
Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the Nominating Committee?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC views this as an important measure to address both critical areas of PJM governance - board independence and the role of states.  The interdependence of the states’ authorities with PJM’s own actions creates a strong requirement for cooperation between PJM and the states.  Having the states play a role in determining PJM board membership will improve that cooperation going forward. 
Moreover, PJMCCC believes that consumer advocates should also have a voting member on the nominating committee.  Given the influence of utilities and generators, this will enable the PJM board to better represent the region and its needs.  Given that generators, utilities, and other suppliers have a vote on the nominations of PJM board members, PJMCCC sees no good reason why consumers should not also have a vote. 
10)
Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management compensation to their achievement?
X


Comments:
PJMCCC notes that tying executive leadership compensation to performance metrics is common practice.  However, it will be important to set the right metrics to improve the PJM board’s independence and responsiveness to regional needs.  PJMCCC’s support for this reform would therefore be conditional on the specific metrics chosen.  PJMCCC postulates that metrics like reduced number of 206 complaints from the states and consumer advocates, reduced number of jump-ball filings made by entities like OPSI (if OPSI is granted 205 jump-ball filing rights - see answers below), and other measures of productive collaboration between the PJM board and affected parties in the region (beyond PJM members) would be the best metrics to use for this purpose.

Section 205 Filing Rights


Questions/Comments
Yes
No
11)
Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC believes that the stakeholder process should be advisory so long as other reforms/checks-and-balances are implemented, including any “jump ball” filings and thresholds for evidence in demonstrating public interest. This also includes PJM acknowledging and addressing any conflicts with state jurisdiction or issues, as well as any requirements for transparency in Board decisions.  
However, PJM will still lack broader stakeholder engagement outside membership advisory. Further clarity on the definition of a stakeholder is needed, especially to increase non-member stakeholder engagement opportunities. Regular informational sessions with the Board could provide a venue for these learnings or considerations.
12)
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?
X


Comments: 
PJM plans to remove the restrictions on its exercise of its FPA Section 205 filing rights over the energy and ancillary services markets. PJMCCC agrees that, conditional on further improvements to the independence of the PJM board, the board’s acquisition of 205 filing rights over the energy and ancillary services market can help the region.  However, PJMCCC refers to our earlier text emphasizing that reforms that increase board independence must be implemented before any expansion of the board’s 205 filing rights are pursued.
	13)
	Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?
X


Comments:  
PJMCCC agrees with PJM’s decision to remove restrictions on its exercise of FPA Section 205 filing rights over the RTEP process, conditional on other reforms to improve the board’s independence. As mentioned in our pre-conference comments, we believe that broadening the allocation of 205 filing rights of PJM (along with state regulators) can enhance the PJM board’s responsiveness to regional issues, but we repeat our earlier statement that further action must be taken to make the board independent before these 205 rights allocation will deliver value to the region.
14)
Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC believes that the “jump ball” construct can offer an equal footing for addressing disagreements between the Board and states in how to approach solving challenges. For transmission cost allocation filings, it makes sense since states have little recourse currently and are considered cost takers without any voice in allocation decisions. Letting OPSI work through alignments across states may resolve PJM and FERC having to get in the middle of state battles (the current circumstances surrounding MD, DC, and VA transmission cost allocation is an example). 
For resource adequacy, PJMCCC would support a jump-ball filing rights for OPSI if it came with requirements for OPSI to rigorously and transparently engage with from affected parties, including cities and communities of PJM.  This could involve a comment process wherein affected parties submit comments on OPSI proposals and OPSI responds to those comments in developing its alternative 205 filing.

	15)
	Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the portions of the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC believes that the Members Committee should have “jump ball” filing rights only if states possess 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholder approves a proposal. Granting the Members Committee “jump ball” filing rights ability may be less constructive by slowing down processes or not aligning with broader wholesale market benefits to consumers rather than only members.

State Participation


Questions/Comments
Yes
No
18)
In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ representatives?
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC sees value in having state elected representation in addition to OPSI, due to their responsiveness to constituents as elected leaders, but with a caveat. Respecting governors’ representatives and/or state legislative representatives may introduce certain accountability incentives, which is likely to introduce political complexity that is already complicating (and has plagued) PJM governance. Technically, CAPS is a second state representative group already with official recognition, albeit as one entity in the end-user member category. Elevating these “apolitical” state consumer advocates may be a less volatile option, but not completely divorced from politics (for example, the Virginia consumer advocate serves under the elected Attorney General who is notably responsive to term political interests).
19)
Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the consumer advocate votes?  
X


Comments: 
PJMCCC prefers an advisory stakeholder process, but agrees that states should have some sort of formal voting role. However, they should also have greater influence beyond just a formal voting role, including “jump-ball” filing rights. 
20)
Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation?



Comments: 
PJMCCC believes that the expectation or burden or proof should be that PJM’s filing meaningfully addresses OPSI or state concerns, and if/where there are conflicts, these are documented, acknowledged, and the trade offs fully analyzed and considered. It is also on FERC to meaningfully require sufficient evidence to have, at least, responded to state concerns or issues. This may trigger a consultation with OPSI or other stakeholders, but if these have been historically unproductive, this should move to an evidentiary rigor for filings. FERC may also consider OPSI as an automatic/opt out petitioner for 205 filings though that may be too far upstream of decisions to be productive or meaningful, but could serve as a backstop if other options aren’t available (i.e., a “jump ball” alternative filing).


Conclusion
Again, we applaud the Commission’s focus on governance issues within PJM, and we hope these comments and answers to the Commission’s questions will be of use to PJM governance reform. We thank the Commission for the opportunity to offer further comments, and we look forward to further developments.
