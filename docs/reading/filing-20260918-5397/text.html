Reliability Requirements for Large Loads (“IRR Filing”).2  
I. 
Introduction 
PIOs support enforceable requirements that prevent large loads from imposing avoidable 
reliability costs on other customers, causing increased reliability risk, or triggering reliability 
violations. MISO’s filing, however, does not meet that goal. MISO has submitted tariff revisions 
that give at least 30 gigawatts (“GW”) of large load, though likely more, an unrestricted ability to 
ramp at eye-watering speeds and trip offline unexpectedly, causing voltage and frequency 
instability. Some amount of large load is also free to take these actions without providing 
advanced notice to MISO, and without MISO even having insight into the load configuration or 
operating capabilities. What requirements do exist would be hampered by limited enforcement

II. 
Argument 
 
A. 
The Commission should not allow broad exemptions from reliability 
requirements for existing and planned large loads. 
 
MISO proposes two broad categories of large loads which will receive automatic 
exemptions from some or all of the proposed reliability requirements: 1) large loads with a 
Service Commencement Date on or prior to December 4, 2026 will be entirely exempt from any 
reliability requirements (a status MISO designates as “Fully Exempt”), and 2) large loads 
associated with transmission projects either approved for MTEP 2026 or submitted for EPR 
review prior to June 18, 2026 will be exempt from ride-through and ramp requirements (a status 
MISO designates as “Partially Exempt”).3 In this filing, MISO does not quantify the amount of 
load that will be subject to each of these exemption categories, but in MISO’s August 12, 2026 
Large Load Working Group, MISO staff estimated that a jaw-dropping 30 GW of then-existing 
or proposed load would fall into one of these two categories and thus be exempt from some or all 
requirements.4 
MISO’s proposed exemptions go far beyond what the Commission’s Show Cause Order5 
directed, represent a broad cost shift from large loads to other MISO customers, and threaten

2. 
Categorically exempting some loads improperly shifts costs. 
As discussed above, large loads create significant impacts on system resource needs. 
Computational load can ramp at extreme rates, cause frequency disturbances, and impact system 
voltage. Uncertainty around large load characteristics and operating parameters can also increase 
the risk of short term load forecasting errors, which, in turn, increases the risk of under- or over-
scheduling and dispatching generation resources.19 To the extent these concerns are able to be 
mitigated outside of reliability requirements for the large loads, mitigation will result in 
increased costs for other MISO customers. Over-scheduling of generation resources and clearing 
more ramp resources can mitigate some concerns associated with insufficient ramp but will 
result in higher energy and ancillary services clearing prices for all customers, even though the 
issues themselves are caused by the exempted large loads alone. Similarly, voltage issues can be 
mitigated to some extent by installing static VAR devices or synchronous condensers.20 But the 
costs for these interventions would not be allocated to the cost-causing large loads that 
necessitated the interventions.

“cost-causation” principle.21 The principle requires that “all approved rates must reflect to some

3. The IRR Filing does not make clear when load modifications remove exemptions.

load does not change?

4. It is not clear how a portion of a large load can comply with the requirements. 
Even if the tariff provided an explanation of what constitutes a new “portion” of a large 
load that was both clear and just and reasonable, the tariff is not sufficiently clear regarding how

6. 
It is ambiguous if MISO would be able to subject exempted large loads to 
requirements before they cause reliability issues.

Conclusion 
 
MISO’s IRR Filing creates broad, dangerous exemptions to reliability requirements that 
MISO proposes to inappropriately house in BPMs. Each of these elements renders MISO’s filing

reliability requirements for large loads is critical to ensuring large load interconnection and 
operation will not result in reliability violations. The proposed standards filed simply do not live 
up to that important task.
