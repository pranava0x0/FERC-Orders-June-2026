Protest of Sierra Club et al. re Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.'s 08/28/2026 filing

Seven public interest organizations protest MISO's large-load reliability filing, arguing its exemptions leave about 30 GW free of ramp and ride-through rules, that critical details sit in manuals, and that FERC should reject it.

Asks FERC to: Reject MISO's Interconnection Reliability Requirements filing as not just and reasonable.

Answers the order. Protests MISO's first filing after the E-8 order as exceeding what that order directed and shifting reliability costs to other customers, the cost-shifting concern at the core of E-8.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Minimum cost recovery / security (P 119, p. 74)

Key points

  1. The organizations say the filing lets at least 30 GW of large load ramp fast and trip offline without limits.
    give at least 30 gigawatts (“GW”) of large load, though likely more, an unrestricted ability to ramp at eye-watering speeds and trip offline unexpectedly PDF p. 1
  2. MISO would fully exempt loads in service by December 4, 2026 and partly exempt loads tied to MTEP 2026 or EPR projects.
    large loads with a Service Commencement Date on or prior to December 4, 2026 will be entirely exempt from any reliability requirements PDF p. 2
  3. They say the exemptions go beyond the Show Cause Order and shift cost from large loads to other customers.
    MISO’s proposed exemptions go far beyond what the Commission’s Show Cause Order directed, represent a broad cost shift from large loads to other MISO customers PDF p. 2
  4. They cite a PJM event in which 3.8 GW of load tripped, about a tenth of the load MISO would exempt.
    PJM recently narrowly averted blackouts when 3.8 GW of load–a mere tenth of the load MISO proposes to exempt–tripped offline instantaneously. PDF p. 4
  5. They argue MISO has not shown any study of the reliability effect of the exemptions.
    if MISO has conducted any studies or analysis of the reliability impact of its proposed exemptions. PDF p. 3
  6. They object that most critical restrictions sit in Business Practices Manuals, which limits enforcement.
    MISO proposes to put most of the critical restrictions of the IRR Filing in Business Practices Manuals (“BPMs”). PDF p. 2
  7. An attached declaration puts the exempt load near a quarter of MISO's current summer peak.
    This makes 30,000 MW more than 24% of MISO’s current peak summer demand, and 20% of MISO’s peak summer demand with the new large loads in-service. PDF p. 29
