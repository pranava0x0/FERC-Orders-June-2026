I. DESCRIPTION OF MORONGO TRANSMISSION

Morongo Transmission is a Participating Transmission Owner in CAISO.  It is wholly owned by the Morongo Band of Mission Indians (“Tribe”), a federally recognized American Indian Tribe exercising jurisdiction over the Morongo Indian Reservation (“Reservation”), which spans roughly 40,000 acres in the San Gorgonio Pass, near Banning, California.  Morongo Transmission holds a 30-year lease of a portion of the transfer capability of Southern California Edison Company’s (“SCE”) upgraded West of Devers transmission facilities, which cross the Tribe’s Reservation. 2  Morongo Transmission recovers its transmission revenue requirement through a Commission-approved formula rate under its tariff (“Morongo Tariff”), paid by CAISO’s collection of Transmission Access Charge (“TAC”) rates.3

These areas of reform do not implicate the Morongo Tariff, which expressly provides that “Morongo Transmission LLC is a Non-Load-Serving Participating [Transmission Owner] and has no End-Use Customers.”5  This language reflects the arrangement between Morongo Transmission and SCE, where Morongo Transmission holds a 30-year lease of a portion of the transfer capability of SCE’s West of Devers line across the Tribe’s Reservation.6  The Morongo Tariff is therefore different from the tariffs of other CAISO Participating Transmission Owners that do serve load and have end-use customers and whose tariffs may be subject to the Order to Show Cause.

Furthermore, the Morongo Tariff provides that the tariff provisions governing the load interconnection process—Section Eight (Obligation to Interconnect or Construct Transmission Expansions and Facility Upgrades), Section Nine (Expansion Process (including the study process for necessary facilities)), and Section Ten (Interconnection Process)—“shall be superseded by the

Morongo Transmission accordingly looks forward to continuing to participate as a party in this proceeding and assisting the Commission as it weighs these important matters—in keeping with Order No. 1920’s recognition of the critical role that federally recognized Tribes play in developing policies affecting the interstate transmission grid.10
