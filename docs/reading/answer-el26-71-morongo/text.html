I. 
DESCRIPTION OF MORONGO TRANSMISSION 
 
Morongo Transmission is a Participating Transmission Owner in CAISO.  It is wholly 
owned by the Morongo Band of Mission Indians (“Tribe”), a federally recognized American 
Indian Tribe exercising jurisdiction over the Morongo Indian Reservation (“Reservation”), which 
spans roughly 40,000 acres in the San Gorgonio Pass, near Banning, California.  Morongo 
Transmission holds a 30-year lease of a portion of the transfer capability of Southern California 
Edison Company’s (“SCE”) upgraded West of Devers transmission facilities, which cross the 
Tribe’s Reservation. 2  Morongo Transmission recovers its transmission revenue requirement 
through a Commission-approved formula rate under its tariff (“Morongo Tariff”), paid by 
CAISO’s collection of Transmission Access Charge (“TAC”) rates.3

to Show Cause.  In this proceeding, the Commission has preliminarily ordered CAISO to reform 
its tariff to take jurisdiction over, and facilitate, interconnection of large loads, including by: 
• Developing an efficient application and study process for Eligible Customers seeking

• Increasing transparency regarding network upgrade costs to provide transmission service

• Developing processes to efficiently study generation facilities serving electrically

These areas of reform do not implicate the Morongo Tariff, which expressly provides that 
“Morongo Transmission LLC is a Non-Load-Serving Participating [Transmission Owner] and has 
no End-Use Customers.”5  This language reflects the arrangement between Morongo Transmission 
and SCE, where Morongo Transmission holds a 30-year lease of a portion of the transfer capability 
of SCE’s West of Devers line across the Tribe’s Reservation.6  The Morongo Tariff is therefore 
different from the tariffs of other CAISO Participating Transmission Owners that do serve load 
and have end-use customers and whose tariffs may be subject to the Order to Show Cause. 
 
Furthermore, the Morongo Tariff provides that the tariff provisions governing the load 
interconnection process—Section Eight (Obligation to Interconnect or Construct Transmission 
Expansions and Facility Upgrades), Section Nine (Expansion Process (including the study process 
for necessary facilities)), and Section Ten (Interconnection Process)—“shall be superseded by the

The Order to Show Cause nonetheless targets a range of issues of great importance to 
Morongo Transmission and the Tribe, including resource adequacy and reform of large load 
interconnection processes.  These topics are significant to Morongo Transmission and the Tribe as 
they work to expand economic opportunity on the Reservation while ensuring affordable and 
reliable electricity for the Tribe’s members.  Morongo Transmission accordingly looks forward to 
continuing to participate as a party in this proceeding and assisting the Commission as it weighs 
these important matters—in keeping with Order No. 1920’s recognition of the critical role that 
federally recognized Tribes play in developing policies affecting the interstate transmission grid.10
