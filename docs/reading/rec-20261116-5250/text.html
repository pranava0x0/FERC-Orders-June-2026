MOTION TO WITHDRAW FILING AND TERMINATE PROCEEDING  
IN DEFERENCE TO NEW DOCKET  
 
 
Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s 
(“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedures,1 and section 35.17(a) of the 
Commission Rules and Regulations,2 PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) moves to 
withdraw its August 17, 2026 filing in this proceeding (“August 17 Filing”),3 in which PJM 
proposed revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to comply with 
the Commission’s directives in its Order on Rehearing, Clarification, Compliance Filing, 
and Paper Hearing regarding the terms and conditions of service for Co-Location 
Arrangements (“Co-Location Compliance Order”).4   
Good cause exists to grant this motion.  The Commission’s regulations permit a 
public utility to withdraw a pending rate filing that “has not become effective and upon 
which no Commission or delegated order has been issued.”5  That is the case here.  
PJM is submitting later today a filing pursuant to section 205 of the Federal Power 
Act (“FPA”),6 in which PJM will submit proposed Tariff revisions in response to the

Commission’s directives in the Co-Location Compliance Order and the Commission’s 
show cause order concerning Large Loads that directed PJM to “address[] the integration 
of large loads that are not co-located with generation.”7  As part of that filing, PJM will 
resubmit the Tariff revisions proposed in the August 17 Filing because those revisions are 
interrelated with PJM’s holistic response to the Co-Location Compliance Order and Large 
Loads Order.  Because PJM’s filing later today will moot the August 17 Filing and Docket 
No. ER26-1479-002, they should be withdrawn and terminated.8   
No party will be harmed by this withdrawal and termination because parties will 
have the opportunity to address the same proposed Tariff revisions (in some cases modified 
to align with the addition of provisions addressing Large Loads) in the new docket 
established for PJM’s upcoming filing on November 16, 2026.  Further, because the August 
17 Filing was submitted as a compliance filing, the records from that filing cannot be 
‘overtaken by events’ in the new FPA section 205 filing later today, which means the 
Commission would have to act on the August 17 Filing and the upcoming PJM filing in a 
new docket, despite the fact that the new filing will duplicate the Tariff revisions proposed 
in the August 17 Filing.  Withdrawing the August 17 Filing allows for more efficient and 
streamlined processing of PJM’s Co-Location- and Large Load-related proposed Tariff 
revisions.  PJM will file a notice once the Commission has established a docket for the 
PJM filing later today, to inform participants in this proceeding of the new docket that 
contains the Tariff revisions proposed in the August 17 Filing.
