Protest, Motion to Reject, and Alternative Request for the Imposition of Conditions of Delaware Division of the Public Advocate et al

Six state consumer advocates jointly protest PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement in ER26-3380, ask FERC to reject it as unjust and unreasonable, offer two alternatives, and list conditions if FERC accepts it.

Asks FERC to: Reject the RBP; direct PJM to refile a subscription-based procurement or advance the effective date of its IRAS proposal; if RBP is accepted, impose conditions such as a five-year procurement period.

Parallel proceeding. Protest in PJM's separate backstop procurement docket ER26-3380, which the advocates link to the EL26-67 show cause order they say already found PJM's tariff appears unjust and unreasonable.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. The advocates say RBP cost allocation to large loads depends on state action, and where states do not act, zonal charges fall on the general ratepayer body.
    where a state does not act—or acts late—zonal RBP charges are allocated onto the general ratepayer body within the PDF p. 4
  2. They say RBP's design contradicts its stated 6.8 GW reliability need through opt-outs, 15-year terms and no market mitigation.
    PJM says that reliability demands that nearly 7 gigawatts of new supply be procured in the near-term, yet permits an untold number of megawatts (or possibly gigawatts) to opt-out of the auction PDF p. 9
  3. They say the $555/MW-day price ceiling comes from a one-year capacity design and does not fit a commitment of up to 15 years.
    A cap set on that basis is irrelevant to the determination of what should be paid PDF p. 6
  4. They put 15 years of RBP charges on a 6,831 MW total at about $20.8 billion.
    $555/MW-day x 365 days x 15 years would be $20,756,847,375. PDF p. 30
  5. Option One: refile as a subscription model in which load-serving entities set the procurement quantity and bear credit obligations.
    PJM should be directed to refile the RBP as a "subscription" model PDF p. 30
  6. Option Two: advance IRAS so large loads from 2028/2029 either bring their own resources or are treated as first-off in emergencies.
    the Commission should, in lieu of approving the RBP, advance the effective date of IRAS PDF p. 33
  7. As a fallback, they ask that any approval limit the procurement period to five years.
    The Commission should limit the RBP procurement period to five years PDF p. 34
