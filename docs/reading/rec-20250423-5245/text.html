I.   COMMENTS

A. Large Loads Should be Studied in a Combined Queue with Generation and
Transmission Interconnection.

PJM, in its response to the Show Cause Order,6 proposes to continue its bifurcated approach to studying co-located loads and generation, wherein network load interconnection requests are studied under Attachment M-3 and co-located generator modifications are studied in the generator interconnection and necessary study process,7 while non-network loads will only employ the necessary study used for generator interconnections. Invenergy respectfully encourages the Commission and PJM to dispense with this bifurcated process and to instead jointly study large load and generator interconnection requests. Doing so would more efficiently use the transmission system and decrease ratepayer costs.

Under PJM’s existing rules, PJM’s standardized interconnection procedures only apply to generation and transmission interconnection, but PJM uses the Attachment M-3 process for load interconnections. Attachment M-3 establishes a local planning process in which PJM and transmission owners process load interconnection requests and plan local upgrades and supplemental projects in response to identified load needs.8  While Attachment M-3 has been used to incorporate large load additions into the PJM system, it was originally intended as a transmission planning process for supplemental projects, not as a process to interconnect large loads. As a result, it has several characteristics that make it unsuitable for large load

First, Attachment M-3 does not consider transmission solutions outside of a particular transmission owner’s footprint, artificially excluding potentially less-expensive solutions. Second, because load additions are primarily studied under local transmission owner study processes using a different set of study assumptions, models and timelines, than generator interconnection studies, the load and generator study processes may each identify network upgrades that would not be necessary were these processes combined, resulting in overbuilding of the PJM system at significant expense. Third, because of the independent study processes, large load additions may not get factored into generator interconnection studies in a timely manner, or could be factored in but then never materialize. This creates significant opacity and risk for generators, thereby stifling development that might occur were generation developers provided more clarity on planned load interconnections.

Finally, there is little, if any, transparency on how M-3 studies are conducted, or the timeline for completing such studies.  Large load interconnection customers are already facing interconnection delays as a result of this uncertainty, and the lack of transparency is creating opportunities for undue discrimination that will decrease competition and raise systemwide costs. These defects, however, can be remedied by adopting a joint study process that requires large loads – those over 50 MW or interconnecting to facilities at voltages greater than 115kV – be studied along with generation and transmission interconnection requests. Combining load interconnection with generation and transmission into a standardized study process would promote transparency and ensure that network upgrades identified and allocated to interconnection customers correctly reflect the expected topology of the system. While PJM notes that large load aggregations could be included in regional transmission planning as part of the yearly load-forecasting process,10 it is unclear how often this occurs or what aggregation would trigger planning for large load additions in regional planning. Invenergy recognizes that incorporating large load interconnections into the generation queue across the PJM footprint will involve stakeholder and state engagement.  In crafting these rules, Invenergy makes the following suggestions:

• Large load interconnection requests should be part of the standard interconnection queue with assigned queue positions. This will increase transparency for load and developers, while also ensuring equitable treatment of load and generation as they interconnect to the transmission system.

• PJM could establish commercial readiness criteria or security deposits for large load interconnection requests akin to those required for generator interconnection requests, thereby reducing speculative large load interconnection requests and incentivizing large loads to materialize.

• Like generator interconnection requests, large load interconnection requests could withdraw from the queue if they are no longer viable, with appropriate penalties to offset the harm to other interconnection customers.

B. Co-Location Arrangements Must Include Safeguards to Ensure Other
Interconnection Customers are Not Harmed.

PJM in its Answer identifies numerous avenues for adding large co-located loads. One of those proposals, “New Option 6,” would permit large loads interconnecting as Network Load to bring its own generation to PJM. PJM explains that it might consider mechanisms to process these types of interconnection requests more quickly.11 While Invenergy welcomes improvements that expedite the interconnection process, any such changes must be adopted on a non-discriminatory, queue-wide basis, and not only for preferenced generators. Moreover, to the extent the Commission approves New Option 6, it should adopt reasonable safeguards to ensure that other interconnection customers are not harmed by co-location arrangements, for instance by maintaining queue priority on a first-ready, first-served basis; preventing co-located load from getting priority access to available transmission system headroom; and ensuring that PJM’s processing of co-location interconnection requests does not deprive PJM of the resources needed to process the interconnection queue. Such limitations would be in keeping with the Commission’s long-standing policy against queue jumping.12

C. PJM Should Include a Transition Process Until its New Co-Location Rules are
Effective.

While this Show Cause proceeding could result in new rules accommodating additional
co-location configurations and should include a combined interconnection process for load and generation, properly issuing new rules takes time and benefits from a robust stakeholder process. Therefore, while new comprehensive rules are developed, PJM should implement a

transition process so that large loads customers can move forward with co-location arrangements in the short-term if they so choose. To reduce study timelines and avoid significant modifications to the PJM system while new rules are being fashioned, large-load customers should be allowed to pursue behind-the-meter configurations with new generating facilities in development and without being considered network load, and protections should be required to prevent that load from accessing the grid.  To the extent such protections are not available, behind-the-meter load should be charged commensurate with its expected transmission system use, but less than fully network load.
