POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF NATIONAL GRID

National Grid plc, on behalf of its competitive energy development corporation, National Grid Ventures (“NGV”), respectfully submits these comments in response to the Notice Requesting Post-Conference Comments (“Notice”), issued by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or “Commission”) on July 30, 2026, in the above-referenced docket, following the Commission’s July 23, 2026 Commissioner-led technical conference to discuss PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) governance and stakeholder processes.  NGV is a voting member of PJM in the “Other Supplier” sector and is an active participant in PJM’s competitive transmission processes.  NGV appreciates the Commission’s focus on identifying and evaluating concrete, actionable reforms to improve PJM’s ability to address operational and market needs in a timely and efficient manner.  To that end, below please find NGV’s responses to the questions presented in the Appendix to the Notice regarding PJM’s Board of Directors independence, Federal Power Act section 205 filing rights, stakeholder process, and state participation. PJM Board Independence

Questions/Comments
Yes No
1) Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?
X

Comments: NGV supports an extension of Board member terms to 4 to 6 years.  Longer terms will support continuity during a period of extraordinary industry transformation, strengthen institutional memory, and reinforce Board independence.  The terms of the Board members should remain staggered.

Questions/Comments
Yes No
2) Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 years)?

X

Comments: If terms are extended to 4 to 6 years, NGV supports capping a Board member to the number of terms that equate to 12 years of service.  This brings the current 15-year maximum service limit in PJM closer to the limits of service for other ISOs (e.g., 9 years in CAISO and MISO).

3) Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region?

X

Comments: NGV supports PJM expressing a preference for Board members that reside in the PJM region; however, NGV does not support making PJM residence a requirement.  The Board’s purpose is not geographic representation; it is independent, expert governance of one of the world’s largest and most complicated organized electricity markets.  While residence in PJM has its benefits, the search for Board members should prioritize qualities such as deep electricity sector expertise and experience solving the challenges PJM will face over the next several years.

4) Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for Board members from two years to one year?

X

Comments: NGV believes a cooling off period of one year for Board members is sufficient.  One year still creates meaningful separation, while two years unnecessarily excludes some of the candidates with the most relevant expertise.  Actual conflicts matter more than elapsed time.

5) Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more communication between the Board and stakeholders?

Comments: No comments at this time.
6) In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be required to explain its reasoning?

X

Comments: The Board should be required to explain the rationale behind significant Board decisions.  This will increase transparency, increase confidence in Board independence, and provide important feedback to stakeholders.  NGV has no comments at this time regarding whether individual Board member votes should be made public.

7) In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the public?

X

8) Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest?

Comments: No comments at this time.
9) Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the Nominating Committee?

X

Comments: Yes, states represent an interest that is not adequately captured on the Nominating Committee.  The Nominating Committee should include one voting member representing the collective PJM states.  This could help strengthen the legitimacy of PJM’s independent governance model and build trust between the states and PJM.

10) Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management compensation to their achievement?

Comments: NGV needs more information on the potential metrics, how those metrics would be developed, and who they would apply to before forming an opinion on this issue.

Section 205 Filing Rights

Questions/Comments
Yes No
11) Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board?

X

Comments: PJM’s stakeholder process should provide meaningful input, technical expertise, and a clear measure of stakeholder support, but stakeholder votes should remain advisory.

12) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

X

Comments: NGV supports expanding PJM’s FPA section 205 filing rights.  Giving PJM increased authority would expedite actions and support PJM’s ability to react to the dynamic changes in the market.

13) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

X

Comments: NGV supports expanding PJM’s FPA section 205 filing rights.  Giving PJM increased authority would expedite actions and support PJM’s ability to react to the dynamic changes in the market.

14) Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal?

X

Comments: At this time, NGV does not support OPSI having jump ball rights. Instead, NGV supports providing adequate funding and empowerment of states to organize themselves through a new organization that can more meaningfully advocate for their interests at PJM.

15) Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the portions of the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal?

X

Comments: A supermajority jump ball right for the Members Committee would appropriately balance independent PJM governance with meaningful stakeholder participation.  PJM’s Board should have primary responsibility for exercising PJM’s FPA section 205 filing rights, and ordinary stakeholder votes should remain advisory. However, where an alternative proposal achieves an exceptionally high level of cross- sector stakeholder support, that consensus should have a meaningful procedural consequence.  Allowing both the Board proposal and the supermajority-supported alternative proposal to be presented to FERC preserves Board independence while ensuring that extraordinary stakeholder consensus cannot simply be disregarded.  FERC would remain the ultimate arbiter of the competing approaches.

State Participation

Questions/Comments
Yes No
18) In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’

representatives?
X

Comments: Yes.  This group could complement, not replace, OPSI by providing a state policy perspective that is distinct from OPSI’s remit.  The Northeast provides a useful model.  In ISO-NE, the New England States Committee on Electricity (“NESCOE”) provides a regional vehicle through which the New England governors advance their collective interests on regional electricity matters.  NESCOE is distinct from the New England Conference of Public Utility Commissioners (“NECPUC”), allowing governors’ energy policy perspectives and utility regulators’ perspectives to be represented separately.  A second state organization in PJM could also improve regional coordination.  PJM spans 13 states and the District of Columbia, whose policies and resource preferences differ substantially.  Many of PJM’s most difficult challenges cannot be solved effectively one state at a time.  A governors’ representative organization could provide a forum for states to identify areas of common interest, develop regional positions, and engage PJM collectively.

19) Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the consumer advocate votes?

Comments: No comments at this time.
20) Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation?

X

Comments: PJM should not be required to consult with OPSI as a procedural prerequisite to exercising its FPA section 205 filing rights, including with respect to resource adequacy or transmission cost allocation.  However, PJM should substantially strengthen its engagement with OPSI and other state representatives.  Improving transparency and demonstrating that state concerns have been meaningfully considered would help rebuild trust between PJM and the states without compromising PJM’s independence.
