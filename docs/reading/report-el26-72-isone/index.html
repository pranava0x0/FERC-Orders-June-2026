EL26-72-000 · 2026-07-20 · ISO New England Inc.

ISO-NE reports on resource adequacy and large-load proposals

ISO-NE files the 30-day informational report the E-11 order required. It finds New England long on supply, relies on competitive markets, and proposes a bring-your-own-new-generation rule for large loads and their exclusion from the Installed Capacity Requirement.

Asks FERC to: Accept the informational report.

Answers the order. ISO-NE's filing under the E-11 30-day informational report directive on ensuring adequate generation for existing and new large loads; its CHILLS-style conditional service parallels SPP's approach in E-9.

E-11 ISO-NE: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 7) E-11 ISO-NE: Proximate-generation service (P 7, p. 7)

Key points

  1. ISO-NE says New England has been flat or declining in load and has not yet seen significant large load growth.
    New England has not yet encountered significant load growth attributable to the addition of large loads, such as data centers. PDF p. 2
  2. ISO-NE's guiding view is that existing competitive markets can meet upcoming resource adequacy needs.
    ISO-NE believes that New England’s markets are sufficiently mature and flexible to meet upcoming challenges. PDF p. 2
  3. Pending projects include a lower Pay for Performance rate, capacity auction reforms, uncertainty reserves and demand flexibility.
    the reduction in the Pay for Performance rate; the Capacity Auction Reforms project; improvements to Surplus Interconnection Service; probabilistic forecasting; uncertainty reserves PDF p. 4
  4. ISO-NE says two actions will be needed: large loads bring their own generation and are excluded from the load-side capacity requirement.
    requiring new large loads to “bring their own” incremental new generation; and excluding new large loads from the load-side requirements of the capacity market PDF p. 6
  5. ISO-NE intends rules similar to SPP's CHILLS, letting large loads take energy until designated resources and upgrades are in place, subject to curtailment.
    ISO-NE intends to pursue rules similar to the Southwest Power Pool’s conditional high impact large load service (“CHILLS”) rules PDF p. 7
  6. ISO-NE expects to file the detailed bring-your-own-generation and capacity exclusion rules in 2027.
    ISO-NE anticipates filing the detailed supporting rules implementing this concept in 2027. PDF p. 7

What the document says

Resource adequacy · PDF pp. 2–4

Who pays generators to stay available?

ISO-NE says energy-market revenue and the capacity market both matter to investment; it distinguishes steady electrification growth from a sudden jump in large loads.

the region’s energy market – through which the vast majority of generators’ revenue flows PDF p. 3

Market design · PDF pp. 4–6

A capacity auction closer to the delivery season

ISO-NE describes shorter procurement lead times and seasonal auctions, using fresher information on supply and demand when it buys capacity.

moving from a three-year forward to a prompt seasonal capacity market PDF p. 5

Curtailment · PDF pp. 6–8

New generation and conditional service

ISO-NE was considering requiring large loads to bring new supply, with conditional power before upgrades are complete. It expected detailed implementing rules in 2027.

until sufficient designated resources and network upgrades are in place, subject to flexibility requirements and/or curtailment and interruption PDF p. 7
