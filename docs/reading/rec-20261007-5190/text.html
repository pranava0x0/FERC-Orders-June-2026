I. MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE ANSWER
Rule 213(a)(2) of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure generally prohibits
answers to answers or protests unless otherwise ordered by the decisional authority. Good cause exists to accept this Answer because PJM’s Answer misconstrues EKPC’s portfolio-sufficiency, resource-attribution, and transferability arguments and does not resolve the real-time curtailment and compensation issues identified in EKPC’s Protest and supporting affidavits. This Answer will assist the Commission in its decision-making process4 and clarify the record.5 For these reasons, the Commission should accept this Answer.

The defects warrant different forms of relief. The Commission should reject the compensation provision and any interpretation of an IRAS Action that would permit curtailment of non-IRAS load because those provisions lack an adequate record basis and exceed the stated scope of IRAS. Additionally, the Commission should condition acceptance on tariff revisions establishing a portfolio-sufficiency pathway, interim recognition of operating resources pending their first available RPM commitment, reasonable reassignment of unused Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) UCAP MW, and a Fixed Resource Requirement (“FRR”) exemption tied to a sufficient FRR Capacity Plan.

PJM Must Recognize the Value of an LSE’s Existing Portfolio. PJM’s Answer discusses flexibility within the BYONC construct, but it does not answer EKPC’s separate portfolio- sufficiency argument. PJM repeatedly states that the PJM Region needs new capacity, but that regional observation does not establish that every New Large Load served by every RPM- participating LSE lacks capacity support or contributes to the resource adequacy shortfall IRAS is intended to address. EKPC’s point is not that existing capacity should be “repackaged” as BYONC. Rather, a self-supply LSE that demonstrates, using PJM-validated UCAP values and Large Load included in the Registry and verified to be online in the relevant Delivery Year, that its portfolio is sufficient to serve its aggregate obligations, including incremental load growth, should not be treated as though its load is unsupported merely because PJM’s proposed eligibility rules recognize only BYONC (including BYONC tied to specific loads that ultimately may not need all allocated BYONC) or Allocated RBP UCAP MW. Curtailing portfolio-supported load also breaks the causal link on which PJM relies to justify IRAS because PJM has not shown that an LSE making that demonstration causes the deficiency IRAS purports to remedy. The Commission therefore should

RPM Auction Timing Disconnect for BYONC Qualification. PJM does not explain why a resource that is online, not committed outside PJM, capable of providing energy and ancillary services, and expected to be offered into RPM at the first available opportunity should provide no IRAS offset during the period between commercial operation and its first available RPM commitment. EKPC does not object to requiring a resource that offsets IRAS exposure to assume obligations comparable to those of an Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”)-committed Capacity Resource. But making prior RPM commitment an absolute prerequisite goes further than necessary and can leave load exposed to curtailment after the underlying reliability concern has been addressed by an operating resource. The Liberty RICE and Cooper CCGT examples demonstrate that commercial-operation dates follow permitting, construction, testing, and interconnection schedules—not the June 1 Delivery Year boundary—and that an operating resource may support reliability for months before it clears an RPM auction.6 PJM’s filing therefore treats resources with comparable present reliability capability differently based solely on administrative timing. The Commission should require PJM to recognize an operating resource pending its first available RPM commitment where the resource: (1) has achieved commercial operation; (2) has PJM- confirmed deliverability and a PJM-determined accredited UCAP capability; (3) is not committed outside PJM or counted toward another obligation; (4) is subject to PJM dispatch, outage- reporting, testing, and performance requirements; and (5) is bound to offer into the first RPM Auction for which it is eligible.

Committing Specific Capacity Resources to Specific Loads. PJM’s response answers a different question than the question raised by EKPC. EKPC did not argue that BYONC must be electrically deliverable to or physically serve a particular New Large Load.7 EKPC’s concern is that IRAS exposure turns on whether PJM recognizes specific UCAP MW as assigned to a specific New Large Load, rather than whether the serving LSE’s aggregate portfolio is sufficient to satisfy its aggregate load and reliability obligations. That remains resource-to-load attribution, even if PJM does not require the identified resource to physically serve the customer.8 Resource adequacy studies, integrated resource plans, reserve-margin analyses, and RPM obligations ordinarily test portfolios against aggregate load; they do not require permanent assignments between individual resources and individual retail customers. PJM has not explained why that portfolio-based paradigm suffices for FRR entities but not for self-supply LSEs participating in RPM. Because the resulting curtailment exposure can attach to load supported by an adequate portfolio, the Commission should reject the resource-specific attribution requirement or require PJM to accept a portfolio-sufficiency demonstration as an alternative.

PJM has not justified a rule that permanently strands unused UCAP MW when load does not materialize as initially forecast or when an LSE’s portfolio needs change. Applicability of IRAS to FRR Entities. PJM responds that FRR entities are not similarly situated to RPM LSEs because an FRR entity must provide capacity for all load in its service area and may incur an insufficiency charge.20 Even if FRR and RPM are distinct administrative frameworks, that does not justify an unconditional IRAS exemption where PJM’s premise for the exemption is that the FRR entity has provided adequate capacity support. Proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, section V(a), excludes New Large Load in an FRR Service Area without conditioning the exclusion on the sufficiency of the applicable FRR Capacity Plan. PJM’s Answer therefore does not explain why IRAS should never apply when the FRR Entity’s plan is insufficient. The relevant distinction is not FRR versus RPM in the abstract; it is whether the load is supported by sufficient capacity.21 A portfolio-based demonstration cannot be sufficient for FRR load while an equivalently adequate self-supply portfolio receives no recognition under RPM. The Commission should require PJM to condition the FRR exclusion on a sufficient FRR Capacity Plan and to provide a comparable portfolio-sufficiency pathway for self-supply LSEs.
