I. WAPA BACKGROUND
WAPA is a power marketing administration of the U.S. Department of Energy.  WAPA was created in 1977 by section 302(a)(1)(E) and (F) of the Department of Energy Organization Act.  WAPA’s mission is to market and deliver reliable, renewable, cost-based federal hydroelectric power, transmission, and related services within a 15-state region of the central and western United States.  WAPA was established to assume the marketing functions of power generated by the various dams owned and operated by the U.S. Department of Interior, Bureau of Reclamation (Reclamation), and the U.S. Army Corp of Engineers.  WAPA is engaged in, among other things, marketing, transmitting, and arranging for delivery of power to its customers

– which include Reclamation’s project-use loads and WAPA’s other electric service customers. WAPA markets power to preference entities who are generally nonprofits including public utility districts, municipalities, water and irrigation districts, federal and state agencies, rural electric cooperatives, and Native American Tribes.  WAPA also owns, operates, and maintains high voltage transmission systems to facilitate delivery of its power to its customers, and sells excess transmission capacity.

WAPA’s Sierra Nevada Region (WAPA-SN) sells power at wholesale to preference customers in California from multiple hydroelectric powerplants of the Central Valley Project (CVP) and market purchases, using the CVP transmission system.  The great majority of WAPA- SN’s system operates as a sub-balancing authority area under the Balancing Authority of

WAPA-SN became a Participating TO in the CAISO and signed the Transmission Control Agreement (“TCA”) among and between the CAISO and its Participating TOs in December 2004.  WAPA only placed under the operational control of the CAISO its rights and interests in the Path 15 Upgrade Project, a single, approximately 83-mile, 500-kilovolt transmission line extending from the PG&E Los Banos Substation to the PG&E Gates Substation.  A federal entity, WAPA has reserved various rights and authorities necessary to facilitate its participation as a TO for the Path 15 Upgrade project, including use of its own Tarif or OATT for interconnections to the Path 15 Upgrade to the extent the CAISO provisions are inconsistent.

II. RELEVANT BACKGROUND
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, thereby initiating a proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act.  The Show Cause Order found that the CAISO’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“CAISO Tariff”) and/or the TO Tariffs of each of the CAISO Participating TOs appear to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential to the extent that they do not include provisions establishing the rates, terms, and conditions for the interconnection of and service to certain large and co-located loads.  The Show Cause Order therefore directed the CAISO and the CAISO Participating TOs to either explain how their respective tariffs remain just and reasonable without specific provisions for the interconnection of large and co-located loads or to develop such provisions.3

III. JUSTIFICATION FOR RESCISSION
WAPA is a non-public utility that is exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act.4  The Commission acknowledged that the Show Cause Order included Participating TOs that are not subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction and explained that it would “rescind the show cause order” against such entities at their request, provided that the CAISO “respond[s] to this order regarding the non-public utility’s CAISO Tariff provisions on file with the Commission.”5  Accordingly, as a non-public utility, WAPA requests that the Commission rescind the Show Cause Order against it.

WAPA has and will continue to coordinate and cooperate with the CAISO in responding to the Show Cause Order, to the extent that the CAISO requests assistance from WAPA and other non-public utilities.  WAPA has conferred with the CAISO in advance of the filing of this motion. In accordance with WAPA policy and practice, if in the course of WAPA’s continued coordination with the CAISO that it determines changes to its Tariff are needed to address the issues in the Show Cause Order, WAPA will file proposed revisions to its Tariff with the Commission in due course.
