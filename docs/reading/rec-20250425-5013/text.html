I. CO-LOCATED GENERATION TO SERVE LARGE LOADS CANNOT BE
FULLY ISOLATED FROM THE GRID
As an initial matter, CATF bases its position on the physical reality that existing and new generation that is designated to serve large loads cannot be isolated from the rest of the transmission system. CATF supports the conclusions of PJM, Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, Public Service Gas & Electric, and the Union of Concerned Scientists that all co-located load is Network Load, cannot be fully isolated, and inevitably uses and also impacts the transmission system.4 The co-located load, co-located generator, and transmission system are not only physically connected, but inter-reliant. As such, while fully isolated load configurations may be spelled out in contractual arrangements, the claim that they do not take necessary services from the transmission system is simply not factually supported by the physical operation of the system. Co-located operations routinely include unplanned generator run-backs or reductions in output, generator MW output oscillations, and co-located load utilization fluctuations. When these conditions occur, they result in “brief, but routine, imbalances between the load and the generation that would need to rely on the grid to balance.”5

II. FERC HAS JURISDICTION IN THIS MATTER TO REQUIRE REGIONAL
TRANSMISSION TARIFF PROVISIONS FOR CO-LOCATED LOAD
The Federal Power Act tasks the Commission with the regulation of the transmission and wholesale sale of electricity to protect the public interest and ensure just and reasonable rates. 16 U.S.C. §§ 824, 824d. The Commission’s jurisdiction extends over all facilities used for the transmission or sale of electric energy in interstate commerce; states maintain jurisdiction over the regulation of facilities used for the generation of electricity. 16 U.S.C. § 824. The Commission is required to issue an order setting just and reasonable rates, rules, and practices whenever it determines that existing rates, rules, and practices involving transmission or electricity sales under its jurisdiction are unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential. 16 U.S.C. § 824e. As the Commission sets forth in its Show Cause Order, it has authority and jurisdiction to “ensure that rates, charges, and classifications for . . . wholesale sale and transmission as well as the practices directly affecting such sale and transmission, are just and reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential.”6

As the Commission describes at Section VI.A. of the Show Cause Order, the issues raised by the interconnection of large loads co-located with generation resources (either new or existing), also implicate some state jurisdictional questions.7 However, as noted above and described by others, because all co-located generation configurations necessarily receive essential services from the transmission grid, this makes them subject to FERC’s jurisdiction and consequently all rates, practices, and procedures involving such facilities must be just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory.8 As such, considerations related to the adequacy of PJM’s tariff to properly plan

LOADS ARE FAIRLY ALLOCATED

V. PJM’s SUGGESTION THAT THERE IS NEED FOR LESSENED AIR
POLLUTION STANDARDS FOR CO-LOCATED GENERATION SHOULD
BE REJECTED
The Commission can and should prioritize transmission planning and tariff provisions for co- located configurations that do not rely on additional diesel generator back up supply or the lessening of air pollution standards. In response to the Commission’s original request to PJM and the Transmission Owners to discuss the benefits of back-up service in co-located load situations, PJM suggests an option that would allow more reliance on stationary reciprocating internal combustion engines (“RICE”), as back up resources to serve the co-located load when the primary co-located generator is not available.31 Additionally, PJM suggests that current limitations on the situations and total number of hours when units can be used should be loosened “to address resource adequacy concerns in the short term.”32

VI. CONCLUSION
CATF applauds FERC’s consideration of these important issues. Increased demand for transmission services, driven in part by large loads such as data centers, whether or not co- located with generation have made careful consideration of how tariff provisions properly can support interconnection and transmission access and assure just, reasonable, and non- discriminatory rates.
