A. 
AMP supports the answer of the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners. 
The Indicated PJM Transmission Owners correctly assert that the current PJM 
Tariff remains just and reasonable19 – a position that is echoed by PJM itself.20 As the 
Transmission Owners observe, “[c]o-located load can and does fit within the current 
constructs for providing transmission service” under the pro forma open access 
transmission tariff and the PJM Tariff.21 In particular, load served under co-location 
arrangements should be treated as Network Load (or load served by Point-to-Point 
Service). Treating co-located load as Network Load or load served by Point-to-Point 
Service avoids unjust and unreasonable transmission cost-shifting,22 while promoting 
reliable transmission planning and resource adequacy.23 
The responses filed by PJM and the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners do not 
support a finding that the existing PJM Tariff is unjust and unreasonable (they assert the 
opposite), and, thus, they provide no basis to modify the Tariff under FPA section 206.24 
Like the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, however, AMP is amenable to further 
discussion and guidance regarding possible Tariff changes to clarify the treatment of co-
located load. The Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, for example, suggest a 
reasonable definition of “Co-located Load” that would appropriately clarify how co-located

BTMG is not considered Network Load; the relevant netting provisions are only available 
to Network Customers, and as PJM explains, “all load served by BTM Generation must 
be designated Network Load.”29 
Netting of Network Load served by BTMG conforms to cost causation principles. 
Load eligible for netting under sections 34.2 and 34.3 of the PJM Tariff is served by 
generation that, by definition, delivers energy to load without using the transmission 
system.30 In accepting the original BTMG netting provisions in the PJM Tariff, the 
Commission specifically observed that PJM’s “charges for the use of PJM’s transmission 
system should be allocated to network customers based on a network customer’s actual 
use of PJM's system, consistent with the principle of cost causation.”31 The Commission 
also agreed that the BTMG netting provisions were consistent with its policy of 
encouraging demand response programs,32 as PJM observed that its proposal would 
“encourage the use of behind the meter generation during times of scarcity and high 
prices.”33 
The PJM Tariff provisions addressing netting of load served by Non-Retail BTMG 
were adopted in a 2005 settlement agreement,34 reached after the Commission found 
that “PJM’s current tariff that excludes from the BTM program all generation that is 
connected to load through a distribution system may not be just and reasonable, and may

2. 
The BTMG Tariff provisions provide benefits while protecting 
against adverse impacts. 
AMP and a number of its load-serving utility members rely on generating units that 
qualify as Non-Retail BTMG. These resources, while generally small, provide significant 
benefits to the consumers of AMP’s member utilities. Consistent with the original 
purposes of the BTMG netting approach, the PJM Tariff allows consumers to avoid costs 
that do not reflect their actual use of the transmission system. The transmission cost 
mitigation impacts of Non-Retail BTMG netting are extremely important to AMP’s 
members at a time of sharply increasing transmission expenses in the PJM region. The 
answer filed by PSE&G documents similar benefits for customers in New Jersey, 
including universities, government agencies, manufacturers, and other consumers.36 
Other technical conference commenters cited similar benefits.37 
Continuing to facilitate BTMG resources is also likely to provide reliability benefits. 
As noted above, one of PJM’s original justifications in proposing BTMG netting was that 
it would “encourage the use of behind the meter generation during times of scarcity and

3. 
The record provides no basis to revisit the current PJM Tariff 
provisions addressing BTMG. 
AMP strongly agrees with PSE&G’s admonition that the Commission “should allow 
existing BTMG customers to continue to net and should take no action that would 
disadvantage these existing customers.”43 Indeed, as discussed above, the PJM and 
Transmission Owner responses do not provide any basis for finding that the existing 
BTMG netting provisions are subject to modification under FPA section 206 as unjust and 
unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential.   
While maintaining that its Tariff remains just and reasonable, PJM discordantly 
suggests that “discussions in the record regarding PJM’s BTM Generation rules raise 
questions about the status of those rules,”44 and PJM states that it is “open to exploring . 
. . whether the 20-year-old method of allocating costs pursuant to the BTM Generation 
netting rules remains consistent with cost causation principles.”45 PJM does not provide 
any citations to the “discussions in the record” that supposedly call the existing netting 
provisions into question, nor does it identify any reason why the longstanding practice of 
netting loads served by BTMG might no longer be “consistent with cost causation 
principles.”46 As the Commission found in originally approving the BTMG netting 
provisions in the PJM Tariff, those provisions allocate transmission system costs to 
Network Customers based on their actual use of PJM’s system, “consistent with the 
principle of cost causation.”47 That observation is as true today as it was in 2004. If
