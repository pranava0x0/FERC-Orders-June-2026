MISO Answer to Comments and Protests on the Large Load Addition Resource Study

MISO answers the 21 comments and protests on its LARS filing, defending the Local Resource Zone proximity test, 200 MW threshold, 15-year pairing and application role of the Transmission Customer, and says it will fold in cost reforms due November 16.

Asks FERC to: Accept the answer and accept the LARS filing as proposed, so MISO can implement LARS in the first quarter of 2027.

Answers the order. MISO replies in its section 205 LARS docket to protests that LARS only partly answers the E-8 show cause order on proximity and cost shifting, and points to its November 16 cost recovery agreement filing.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-8 MISO: Cost-recovery agreement (P 6(b), p. 6) E-8 MISO: Minimum cost recovery / security (P 119, p. 74)

Key points

  1. MISO says LARS responds to the show cause order and should not wait for the large load cost reforms due November 16.
    such a delay would frustrate the goals of the Show Cause Order and needlessly sacrifice the benefits of critical reforms for ratepayers and stakeholders without corresponding benefit. PDF p. 5
  2. MISO says the Local Resource Zone fits its system and the order invited tailoring the proximity definition.
    MISO is expanding upon the Commission's definition to fit its needs and resource adequacy construct. PDF p. 9
  3. MISO describes its zone test as electrically proximate plus because it rewards close siting and also serves pairs farther apart.
    MISO's LRZ approach thus can be considered an "electrically proximate plus" construct PDF p. 12
  4. MISO says LARS creates no new cost shift, and the Transmission Customer stays liable for assigned projects even if the load never starts service.
    LARS does not create any new cost shift opportunities, but LARS does mitigate risks that already exist. PDF p. 14
  5. MISO and the Transmission Owners will file pro forma cost recovery agreements by November 16, 2026, and MISO would accept a compliance directive to fold them into LARS.
    MISO is willing to accept a compliance directive requiring such a filing should the Commission deem this necessary. PDF p. 15
  6. MISO says LARS will not take headroom from DPP queue projects because LARS models are built on the existing MTEP model.
    each LARS study cycle model will be built based on the existing MTEP model, which would not remove any available headroom from active DPP queue projects PDF p. 19
  7. MISO rejects the gatekeeper charge: only Transmission Customers have been able to submit EPR requests, and an unpaired generator would make LARS speculative.
    LARS is a load-driven process, and an Interconnection Customer cannot require a Transmission Customer to scope their Transmission Service request around a generator they have not chosen to pair with. PDF p. 20
  8. MISO opposes a sunset date but commits to post pair data each cycle and to review usage regularly.
    MISO commits to publicly post, at the beginning of each LARS cycle, information on each LARS generation/load pair's POI and POW PDF p. 23
  9. MISO defends the 200 MW floor and the 15-year pairing as ways to keep LARS for the largest, committed pairs.
    The 15-year minimum pairing of the generation and load pairs in LARS is a necessary aspect of ensuring certainty for MISO PDF p. 24
