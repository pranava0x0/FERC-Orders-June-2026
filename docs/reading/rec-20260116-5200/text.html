These clarifications should sufficiently answer the relevant questions presented by this proceeding.  While the Indicated TOs appreciate the Commission’s leadership in addressing this issue, the 206 Order goes beyond these clarifications and attempts to fix something that is not broken.  There are indeed issues requiring attention in the PJM wholesale markets—including resource adequacy, reliability, and affordability challenges for reasons other than transmission service offerings and rates—but the creation of new forms of transmission service does not address those challenges and instead risks making those very real problems worse.

“The reasonableness of a rate is assessed in light of the FPA’s goals of promoting reliable service at reasonable rates and developing plentiful energy supplies.”11  The existing transmission services under the existing PJM Tariff are consistent with those goals and are just and reasonable. No one has satisfied the burden under FPA section 20612 to demonstrate that the existing tariff is unjust and unreasonable.  Nor has the burden been satisfied to demonstrate that the Commission’s replacement rate is just and reasonable.  The Commission’s replacement rate that imposes new CD Service and Interim Service options could directly place at risk the reliability of PJM’s electric grid and grant an undue preference to certain categories of customers.  The new services could allow co-located load to pay for a different share of transmission system embedded costs than other customers, thereby resulting in rates that are not just and reasonable.

The FPA requires that the rates and charges of a public utility “in connection with the transmission or sale of electric energy subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission, and all rules and regulations affecting or pertaining to such rates or charges shall be just and reasonable.”14 “[A]ny such rate or charge that is not just and reasonable is . . . unlawful.”15  Under FPA section 206, the Commission may, by its own motion or upon complaint from a third party, remedy any “rate [or] charge” or “any rule, regulation, practice, or contract affecting such rate [or] charge” found to be “unjust [or] unreasonable.”16  “The burden of proving an unjust or unreasonable rate rests with the party that initiated the proceeding—that is, the Commission or the third-party complainant.”17  In other words, a respondent to a FPA section 206 complaint is not required to make an affirmative showing that its existing rate is still just and reasonable. For the reasons below, complainants in the above-captioned dockets (i.e., the Commission and Constellation) have failed to meet their burden of demonstrating that PJM’s current rate is unjust and unreasonable.
