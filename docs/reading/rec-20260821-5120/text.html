I. Introduction
The PJM Filing proposes reforms to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”)
and the Reliability Assurance Agreement to establish a Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) that creates a one-time procurement of new, incremental capacity to address ongoing resource adequacy challenges in the region that are driven by unprecedented load growth from large load in the PJM region.4  While PJM’s proposed RBP is a product of the Critical Issue Fast Past (“CIFP”) process, it is one of several feasible solutions that are aligned with the National Energy Dominance Council’s (“NEDC”) and the PJM Governors’ statement of principles (“Statement of Principles”) and the PJM Board Letter.5   PJM proposes to commence the RBP on September 30, 2026 to procure new capacity in response to the capacity shortfalls observed in the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) Auctions for the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years (“DY”).6

As explained below, Dominion generally supports the RBP as a one-time procurement to bring the PJM region to a more stable state of resource adequacy and pave the way for holistic market design reforms that will support the infrastructure needed to reliably serve load growth in the region.  However, Dominion Energy believes aspects of the PJM Filing’s proposed cost allocation for the RBP are unjust and unreasonable in the way that they determine each Electric Distributor (“ED”) zone/area’s share of the Final RBP Target.  Dominion Energy respectfully requests the Commission approve the PJM Filing subject to PJM submitting a compliance filing to adjust the allocation methodology.

II. Comments
The RBP is not an answer to PJM’s resource adequacy problem but is a necessary stop-gap solution to stabilize the region and allow for a more holistic review of the markets.  The PJM region must address near-term deficiencies to begin focusing on the long-term solution, which is why Dominion Energy supports the RBP.  Ideally, the combination of PJM’s wholesale markets, resource adequacy and transmission planning, interconnection processes, and market participant obligations work together to reliably and affordably build out the infrastructure necessary to support future load growth.  PJM is not there yet, and it may take several years for any holistic reforms to have an impact.  For this reason, the RBP presents an opportunity to bring resource adequacy up to PJM’s reliability requirement through the procurement of new dispatchable generation needed in the region.    However, it is important to recognize that the ability of the RBP to attract significant new investment is because it offers long-term certainty required by developers that is currently not afforded in the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) design.  Future reforms of the capacity and energy markets must address this issue: a 3-year forward auction sending a one- year price signal can no longer be the sole means to attract the generation needed to meet future

III. Partial Protest
A. PJM’s proposed methodology for allocating the Final RBP Target to individual ED zone/areas is unjust and unreasonable.

Dominion does not oppose the underlying principle of PJM’s cost allocation approach for the RBP.  Recognizing that much of the shortfall experienced in the last two capacity auctions is attributable to Large Load adjustments added to the load forecasts, PJM proposes pro-rata allocation to ED zone/areas based on their share of forecasted load growth between the 2026/2027 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years (“MW Load Adjustment Share”).8  However, PJM’s proposed two- step process fails to recognize that a portion of each ED zone/area’s Incremental Large Load Share was covered by the past two capacity auctions.  Instead, PJM would require ED zone/areas seeking to fully offset their potential RBP allocation to demonstrate a capacity offset equivalent to their entire MW Load Adjustment Share rather than just their Initial RBP Target allocation.9  This leads to potential double charging for capacity requirements and is unjust and unreasonable. To understand the issue, it is probably best to first consider what should be the appropriate, just and reasonable RBP Target allocation and offset methodology and then contrast that with what PJM proposes.  Dominion agrees with PJM’s first step in the process whereby the Initial RBP

If an ED zone/area has Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW (“RBP Offset”), it should be able to reduce their Initial RBP Target by that amount on a one for one basis. For example, if an ED zone/area has an Initial RBP Target allocation of 1000 UCAP MW and demonstrates 900 UCAP MW of RBP Offsets, it will have a 100 UCAP MW share of the Final RBP Target and this will become its Reliability Backstop Obligation.  If the ED zone/area submits enough RBP Offsets to cover its entire Initial RBP Target allocation, then it should not receive a Reliability Backstop Obligation or any further cost allocation for the RBP.  No additional allocation step should be required.

PJM does not follow this straightforward approach. Instead of using the Initial RBP Target allocation as the amount that can be offset by an ED zone/area, PJM’s proposal requires the ED zone/area to offset its MW Load Adjustment Share.  To illustrate, the Croop Affidavit’s example shows one ED zone/area fully offsetting its MW Load Adjustment Share (REC) and a second ED zone/area partially offsetting its MW Load Adjustment Share (ODEC).11  Since PJM’s proposal is anchored to the MW Load Adjustment values for purposes of offset, it requires a second step that converts offsetting UCAP MW into their load MW equivalent to recalculate each ED zone/area’s final percentage share of the Final RBP Target.12  There are two significant issues with this approach.

First, requiring ED zone/areas to offset their MW Load Adjustment Share fails to recognize that the BRA has already procured capacity for a portion of that MW Load Adjustment Share. Consider what PJM’s proposal requires at the region-wide level. The Initial RBP Target of 6,831.3 UCAP MW represents the amount of UCAP MW needed to bring the PJM region back to compliance with the RTO Reliability Requirement for the 2028/2029 DY.13  To fully offset the need for the RBP under PJM’s proposed allocation methodology, all identified ED zone/areas would need to demonstrate 8,492.1 RBP Offsets (UCAP MWs), calculated by converting the 9,033.2 MW region-wide Load Adjustment MW by the 2028/2029 BRA Forecast Pool Requirement of 0.9401. As a result, ED zone/areas are being asked to bring a surplus of 1,660.8 UCAP MWs—not needed to meet the Reliability Requirement—to ensure they do not receive a portion of the Final RBP Target. This surplus capacity represents an equivalent amount of load that participated in the 2028/2029 BRA and was covered by cleared capacity, meaning the load will pay for those capacity costs during the Delivery Year.  Requiring ED zone/areas to offset their entire MW Load Adjustment Share to avoid having an RBP Obligation raises the possibility that they will pay for redundant capacity, which is unjust and unreasonable.

Second, PJM’s proposed method for allocating the Final RBP Target to ED zone/areas presents unjust and unreasonable risks of disproportionally benefiting ED zone/areas that have not secured capacity by socializing the benefit of Opt-Outs and RBP Offsets procured in one ED zone/area to all ED zone/areas. As discussed above, PJM requires ED zone/areas wishing to avoid an RBP Obligation to bring RBP Offsets exceeding their allocation of the Initial RBP Target. PJM’s proposal calculates the Final RBP Target as the Initial RBP Target minus RBP Offsets and Opt- Outs on a 1 MW to 1 MW basis. In this calculation, any RBP Offsets brought by an ED zone/area

B. Subtracting Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW from Initial RBP Target
allocations is a just and reasonable methodology for determining the final
procurement target for individual ED zone/areas.

PJM rightfully determines the region-wide Final RBP Target as the Initial RBP Target minus Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MWs and UCAP obligation value of loads opting out of the RBP.  However, an ED zone/areas share of the Final RBP Target should not be further adjusted based on a recalculation of the MW Load Adjustment values.  Simply allowing ED zone/areas to offset their RBP Obligation up to their share of the Initial RBP Target satisfies the overarching RBP objective of securing a sufficient amount of capacity for PJM to meet, not exceed, its Reliability Requirement. It avoids the potential for load to pay twice for capacity and eliminates the potential for ED zone/areas that do not bring RBP Offsets to benefit from ED zone/areas that do.  If the FERC chooses to accept the PJM proposal it should do so subject to PJM submitting a compliance filing that allows ED zone/areas to offset their share of the Initial RBP Target by

C. The Commission has the authority to direct modification of the Final RPB Target Allocation.

PJM has consented to modification of its proposal where such modification is consistent with PJM’s overarching objectives.14 This change will not affect those objectives and will in fact better align with cost allocation principles and the design of the RBP.
