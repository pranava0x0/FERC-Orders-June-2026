I. Introduction and Summary
Pursuant to Rules 211 and 214 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure, 18 C.F.R. §§ 385.211, 385.214 (2026), Sunrun Inc. (“Sunrun”) respectfully moves to intervene and submits these comments in the above-captioned proceeding. On August 13, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) filed proposed tariff revisions under Section 205 of the Federal Power Act to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) and a Large Load Registry. Accession No. 20260813-5118. PJM requests an effective date of October 12, 2026.

Sunrun is the nation’s largest residential solar and battery storage company and operates as a distributed energy resource (“DER”) aggregator serving customers across multiple PJM transmission zones. PJM’s proposed treatment of DERs, aggregation rules, and IRAS compliance obligations directly affects Sunrun’s market participation and its customers’ interests.

Sunrun’s comments address five substantive issues: (1) verified Peak Shaving Adjustment (“PSA”) megawatts under qualifying state programs should offset IRAS obligations on a megawatt-for-megawatt basis; (2) battery storage injections must be settled separately from net-metered solar exports through device-level submetering; (3) residential DERs require broader multi-nodal aggregation across electric distribution company service territories or transmission zones; (4) PJM’s existing registration process requires transparency and accountability measures to address uneven state and EDC readiness; and (5) routine portfolio additions and like-for-like substitutions should receive expedited, ministerial treatment rather than retriggering full review or being barred during a Delivery Year. Sunrun respectfully requests that the Commission accept PJM’s filing subject to a discrete clarification or compliance filing confirming that verified PSA values reduce IRAS

III. Comments
Sunrun respectfully submits the following comments on PJM’s August 13, 2026 Section 205 filing proposing Interim Resource Adequacy Service and a Large Load Registry. These comments address six related subjects, organized in Sections III.A through III.F below. One request is appropriately resolved within the Section 205 standard of review: PJM’s filed tariff and registry already collect each Large Load’s Peak Shaving Adjustment value under a qualifying state program, yet the filing does not specify how verified PSA megawatts reduce a Large Load’s IRAS obligation. The Commission can and should direct a discrete clarification or compliance filing on that point, consistent with the principle that a utility’s Section 205 filing must be just and reasonable in its own terms. See 16 U.S.C. section 824d(a). If PJM cannot demonstrate that its proposal adequately accounts for verified PSA values, the Commission should reject the filing without prejudice to allow PJM to cure the deficiency.

The remaining four subjects, addressed in Sections III.B through III.E, concern implementation details that extend beyond the four corners of the instant filing: the separate settlement of battery injections and net-metered solar exports, multi-nodal aggregation geography, EDC registration readiness, and portfolio substitution rules. These issues bear directly on whether DERs can meaningfully participate in IRAS, but they are better addressed through a technical conference or coordinated follow-up proceeding rather than through

conditions imposed on this filing. Section III.F explains how the full set of requested reforms preserves reliability and avoids double counting.

A. The Commission Should Ensure That Verified Peak Shaving Adjustments Reduce IRAS Exposure

1. PJM’s Proposal Recognizes Approved PSA Values but Does Not Explain Their Effect on IRAS. PJM’s proposed Large Load Registry includes a field for a Peak Shaving Adjustment value under a qualifying state program. The registry does not itself create or approve that value. Under PJM’s existing PSA process, an authorized entity voluntarily submits a Peak Shaving Adjustment Plan, officer certification, and supporting state authorization for PJM review. PJM then approves a peak-shaving megawatt quantity by zone for inclusion in its load forecast. See PJM Manual 19: Load Forecasting and Analysis, Attachment D, at 39–43. The registry records the resulting PSA value. However, the IRAS proposal does not clearly explain how that value affects IRAS allocations. Because an approved PSA lowers forecast peak demand and the associated resource-adequacy need, excluding it from the IRAS calculation would overstate reliability risk and disregard verified load reductions.

2. Virginia’s Enacted Framework Shows Qualifying State Programs Are Concrete and Measurable. Virginia SB 371 (identical to HB 284), 2026 Va. Acts chs. 377-378, codified at Va. Code § 56-596.7, became effective July 1, 2026. The statute defines demand flexibility and capacity-reduction credits and directs Dominion Energy Virginia and Appalachian Power Company to petition the Virginia State Corporation Commission by January 15, 2027 for voluntary demand-flexibility programs. Eligible measures include customer-sited storage, virtual power plants, demand response, efficiency, weatherization, heat pumps, and grid-enhancing technologies, with measured and verifiable results. The statute specifically

PDF page 6

contemplates high-energy-demand customers funding or purchasing capacity-reduction credits from other retail customers. This legislation confirms that qualifying state programs are not hypothetical; they are enacted, scheduled, and designed to produce quantifiable peak-demand reductions.

3. State-Recognized, Verified PSA Megawatts Should Offset IRAS Megawatts, Subject to Safeguards. The Commission should accept PJM’s filing only subject to a discrete clarification or compliance filing confirming that verified PSA megawatts reduce a Large Load’s IRAS obligation on a megawatt-for-megawatt basis. To protect reliability and prevent double counting, the offset should be subject to the following conditions: (a)​

Measurement and verification protocols consistent with the applicable
qualifying state program;
(b)​
Performance scoring that accounts for actual delivery during capacity shortage conditions;

(c)​
Annual reconciliation of PSA values against observed load reductions; and (d)​

A prohibition on counting the same megawatt toward both a PSA offset and any other capacity resource obligation.

B. A Workable IRAS DER Pathway Requires Separate Treatment of Battery Injections and Net-Metered Solar Exports

If DER-backed capacity is to support IRAS compliance, PJM must establish settlement rules that distinguish battery storage injections from net-energy-metered solar exports. Without device-level submetering, a behind-the-meter battery that injects energy during a capacity event may be compensated twice: once through the customer’s retail NEM credit and again through wholesale capacity settlement. Conversely, requiring a customer to

PDF page 7

forfeit NEM export compensation as a condition of wholesale capacity participation would deter enrollment and undermine the very DER pathway PJM purports to create. Other ISOs have addressed this problem. ISO-NE’s Forward Capacity Market Tariff section III.13.1.4 permits passive demand resources, including distributed generation measures, to hold capacity obligations without participating in wholesale energy or ancillary services markets. The Commission accepted ISO-NE’s Order 841 compliance filing requiring direct metering of behind-the-meter storage. ISO New England Inc., 169 FERC para. 61,140 (2019), Docket No. ER19-470-000. ISO-NE’s Order 2222 compliance proceeding, Docket No. ER22-983, further developed device-level parallel metering and submetering configurations; the Commission accepted later compliance changes by delegated letter order in ER22-983-009, dated November 19, 2024, effective November 1, 2026.

PJM’s own October 29, 2024 Regulation Market Only problem statement identifies a path for battery injections at NEM customer sites for ancillary services such as regulation, while acknowledging an implementation gap. That stakeholder process material confirms PJM recognizes the need for separate settlement but has not yet operationalized it for capacity purposes.

The Commission should direct a technical conference or coordinated follow-up proceeding to develop device-level submetering protocols that settle battery injections independently from NEM-compensated solar exports, preserving retail NEM treatment while enabling wholesale capacity value without double compensation.

C. Broader Multi-Nodal Aggregation Is Needed for Residential DERs to Support IRAS PJM’s current aggregation framework generally requires that distributed energy resources within a single aggregation be located at the same pricing node. A limited

PDF page 8

multi-nodal exception exists, but it is capped at 167 MW across the entire RTO. These constraints present a structural barrier to residential-scale DER participation in IRAS. Residential solar-plus-storage systems are individually small, typically ranging from 5 to 25 kW. To reach capacity levels meaningful for IRAS purposes, an aggregator must enroll hundreds or thousands of customers. Residential customers served by a single electric distribution company (“EDC”) are frequently spread across multiple pricing nodes. The single-node requirement forces aggregators to form numerous small, sub-scale aggregations rather than a single portfolio-level resource, increasing administrative burden and reducing the commercial viability of participation. The 167 MW RTO-wide multi-nodal cap, if fully subscribed by even a few aggregators, would foreclose additional residential participation entirely.

Sunrun respectfully requests that the Commission, through a technical conference or coordinated follow-up proceeding, examine whether PJM should permit residential DER aggregations across an EDC service territory or, at minimum, across a transmission zone. Such geographic flexibility would allow residential portfolios to reach the scale necessary to provide meaningful capacity contributions under IRAS while remaining subject to appropriate distribution reliability limits. EDC-level or zonal aggregation is consistent with the Commission’s objectives in Order No. 2222, which directed RTOs to remove barriers to DER aggregation and recognized that overly narrow geographic constraints can impede participation. See Participation of Distributed Energy Res. Aggregations in Mkts. Operated by Reg’l Transmission Orgs. & Indep. Sys. Operators, Order No. 2222, 172 FERC para. 61,247 (2020). Distribution reliability screening by the relevant EDC would remain in place, preserving the safety and operational review that PJM’s framework contemplates.

D. PJM Has Outlined Registration Timing, but State and EDC Readiness Requires Transparency and Accountability

Sunrun does not ask the Commission to require PJM to create a registration process where none exists. PJM has already outlined a sequential process for DER aggregation registration in its 2026 Order 2222 compliance materials and the February 2, 2026 Distributed Resources Subcommittee (“DISRS”) process materials. See PJM, 2222: Aggregate PNODE Creation, Distributed Resources Subcommittee (Sept. 1, 2026); PJM, DISRS Process Materials (Feb. 2, 2026). That process comprises three phases: (1) a 15-day location review; (2) a 15-day PJM and EDC data verification period; and (3) a 45-day EDC distribution reliability review. The 60-day EDC review window (phases two and three) is distinct from the full end-to-end processing timeline. Under this framework, PJM creates aggregate pricing nodes, while EDCs provide electrical location data, locational factors, and distribution reliability review. PJM has also stated that FERC declined to assert jurisdiction over DER interconnection, which remains governed by state and local processes. The primary implementation risk is uneven state and EDC readiness. Some EDCs may lack the staffing, data systems, or procedures to complete reviews within tariff deadlines, and state regulatory frameworks vary in their treatment of DER aggregation. A qualifying resource should not lose IRAS value solely because of administrative delay outside the aggregator’s control. At the same time, an overbroad deemed-approval mechanism could bypass legitimate distribution safety review.

Sunrun respectfully requests that the Commission, through a technical conference or coordinated follow-up proceeding, address the following measures to ensure accountability: (e)​

Uniform data requirements for EDC submissions and aggregator applications across PJM.

(f)​
Transparent status tracking so that aggregators can monitor the progress of each registration phase.

(g)​
A requirement that EDC denials be supported by reasoned, written

E. Routine Additions and Like-for-Like Substitutions Should Not Restart Full Review or Be Barred Throughout a Delivery Year

PJM’s March 9, 2026 DER Aggregator Participation Model materials state that modifications to a DER Capacity Aggregation Resource retrigger the full 60-day EDC review period and that inventory linked to such a resource may not be modified during the Delivery Year. PJM, DER Aggregator Participation Model (Mar. 9, 2026). These restrictions are incompatible with the operational reality of residential DER portfolios and, if applied to IRAS-linked commitments, will render those commitments unacceptably fragile. Residential solar-plus-storage portfolios experience ordinary customer turnover: customers move, cancel service, or replace equipment. A static-inventory requirement that freezes the composition of an aggregation for an entire Delivery Year ignores this reality. The aggregator’s performance obligation is measured at the portfolio level, not at the level of any individual device or customer. When a departing customer’s capacity is replaced by an equivalent resource at a comparable electrical location, the aggregate MW, the distribution

PDF page 11

system impact, and the reliability contribution remain unchanged. Requiring a full 60-day review for such a substitution imposes delay disproportionate to any incremental reliability concern and creates a gap during which the aggregation may fall below its committed capacity.

Sunrun requests that the Commission direct PJM to adopt expedited, ministerial treatment for routine additions and like-for-like substitutions that satisfy each of the following conditions:

(j)​
The substitution or addition does not increase the aggregate committed MW of the DER Capacity Aggregation Resource.

(k)​
The replacement resource does not materially change the relevant electrical location of the aggregation.

(l)​
The change does not introduce a new distribution reliability concern as
determined through targeted EDC screening.
Under this framework, qualifying changes would proceed on a notice basis, with the EDC retaining authority to conduct a targeted screening rather than a full 60-day restart. Measurement, verification, and performance accountability would continue to apply at the aggregation level, preserving the Commission’s and PJM’s reliability objectives. This approach is consistent with the portfolio-level performance construct that PJM itself has adopted for DER aggregations and prevents qualifying resources from losing IRAS value due to routine portfolio management.

F. These Reforms Preserve Reliability and Avoid Double Counting While Making PJM’s Stated DER Pathway Usable

The clarifications and process reforms described in Sections III.A through III.E are consistent with PJM’s stated reliability objectives and with the Commission’s directives in Orders 2222 and 841. Each request is designed to complete PJM’s own DER pathway, not to rewrite the Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal.

The safeguards Sunrun proposes operate in concert to protect reliability and prevent double counting:

(m)​
Measurement and Verification: PSA offsets and DER capacity contributions would be validated through defined protocols before any IRAS obligation is reduced, ensuring that only demonstrated load reductions receive credit.

(n)​
Performance Scoring: Aggregation-level performance obligations hold DER providers accountable for delivery during capacity shortage emergencies, consistent with PJM’s existing performance assessment framework.

(o)​
Reconciliation: Post-delivery-year reconciliation would true up any discrepancy between credited capacity and actual performance, preserving the integrity of resource adequacy accounting.

(p)​
No-Double-Counting Provisions: Device-level submetering of battery
injections, separate from NEM-compensated solar exports, prevents the same megawatt-hour from receiving both retail net-metering compensation and wholesale capacity or energy credit. PSA megawatts credited against IRAS obligations would be excluded from any other capacity supply commitment.

(q)​
Distribution Reliability Screening: Targeted EDC review for routine substitutions and additions, rather than full 60-day restarts, preserves the EDC’s authority to identify and address legitimate safety and reliability concerns while removing unnecessary administrative barriers.
