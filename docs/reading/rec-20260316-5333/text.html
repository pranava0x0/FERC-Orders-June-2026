Joint Parties seek clarification, consistent with the Request for Clarification that the Joint Parties filed in response to the Co-Location Order,7 that PJM’s filing will allow interconnection customers serving Co-Located Loads to nominate net withdrawal limitations for purposes of these interconnection study processes. B. The Commission should reject PJM’s proposed Tariff revisions that preclude retail loads served by behind-the-meter generation from taking the new

transmission services and otherwise limit eligibility for the new transmission services to load that is connecting to a generator with interconnection rights.

PJM’s compliance filing is also unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory for precluding loads connected to behind-the-meter generation without interconnection rights from taking the new Firm and Non-Firm Contract Demand Transmission Services and Interim NITS that will be offered to Co-Located Loads. These retail loads are similarly situated to Co-Located Loads in their willingness and ability to limit their energy withdrawals from the transmission system under certain conditions in the same manner as Co-Located Loads. Just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory treatment requires that these similarly-situated loads receive similar treatment: access to these new transmission service classes.

The Commission is requiring revisions to PJM’s Tariff with respect to certain BTMG loads because such loads “are not fully accounted for in resource adequacy planning and shift costs onto other transmission customers.”24 This directive speaks to a desire to render such loads visible to the PJM transmission system and require them to pay for transmission service used by the load customer that relies on BTMG. The Commission’s stated goals do not suggest a desire to force BTMG to move through PJM’s queue even where it does not plan to inject into the

Alternately, the Commission could provide for the same result by granting the PJM Industrial Consumer Coalitions pending request for clarification or, in the alternative, rehearing seeking to clarify that “[n]ew transmission services outlined in the Order will be made available to retail BTMG customers and load-only customers, not just Co-Located Load.”39 Or, the Commission could ensure non-discriminatory eligibility for the new transmission services in the Commission’s pending paper hearing proceeding in Docket No. EL25-49 et al. to develop the new transmission service replacement rate.40

In the instant proceeding, however, the Commission may not approve PJM’s proposed unjust, unreasonable, and unduly discriminatory compliance filing that allows some loads that can limit withdrawals to take the new transmission services but denies the same opportunities to others with the same technical abilities. Rather, the Commission should reject PJM’s compliance filing and direct PJM to allow customers with generation behind their load to access transmission services that match their ability to limit withdrawals, similar to customers with load connected to Generating Facilities. III. CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, the Commission should reject PJM’s compliance filing and require PJM to: (1) clarify that, in any generator interconnection study process in which PJM studies the Co-Located Load’s withdrawals from the grid for purposes of identifying network upgrades, including the necessary study process, a Co-Located Load may nominate a net withdrawal limit in the same manner that it may nominate a net injection limit; and (2) allow loads that can limit their withdrawals similarly to Co-Located Loads to have access to the same Firm Contract Demand Transmission Service, Non-Firm Contract Demand Transmission Service, and Interim NITS that PJM proposes to provide to Co-Located Loads.
