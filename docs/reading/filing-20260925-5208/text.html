5. PJM Ignores Fundamental Locational Market Requirements. 
The RBP Filing and September 10th Answer ignore the locational requirements that 
are an essential part of the capacity market. The PJM Capacity Market has been, from the 
genesis of the RPM design in 2007 based on locational reliability differences, been a locational 
market. The PJM Capacity Market includes Locational Deliverability Areas (“LDA”). PJM 
calculates a reliability requirement for every LDA with Capacity Emergency Transfer Limit 
(“CETL”) that exceeds the LDA’s Capacity Emergency Transfer Objective (“CETO”) by 115 
percent or meets other criteria. PJM has been using CETO and CETL metrics to account for 
transmission network constraints that prevent capacity in one part of the system from reliably 
serving load in another part of the PJM system. PJM’s failure to account for the actual location 
of data centers and the generation to serve them will create additional and unnecessary costs 
for all customers.  
The central objective of PJM’s Reliability Pricing Model is to limit potential loss of load 
events to approximately one event every 10 years at the RTO level. PJM applies a stricter 
criterion to ensure that the transmission constraints do not reduce reliability. PJM’s standard 
is approximately one event every 25 years for every modeled LDA.  
Many protestors have raised this issue.66 PJM’s assertion that “all capacity in PJM is 
deliverable to all loads in PJM until transmission constraints are met,” misses the point as 
PJM is aware.67 The deliverability analysis conducted when a new resource first interconnects 
to the PJM grid is used to identify necessary transmission upgrades and award Capacity 
Interconnection Rights. However, this type of interconnection study cannot and does not
