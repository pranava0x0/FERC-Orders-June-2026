Answer and Motion for Leave to Answer of the Independent Market Monitor for PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. to the Answer of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. filed on 09/10/2026

The PJM Independent Market Monitor answers PJM's September 10 answer in the backstop procurement docket and asks for leave to answer. It argues the proposal leaves data center costs with other customers and repeats its recommendation that FERC reject the filing.

Asks FERC to: Accept the answer, reject PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement, and direct PJM under FPA Section 206 to file within two weeks a solution requiring data centers to bear their own costs.

Parallel proceeding. Filed in PJM's backstop docket ER26-3380, and it also criticizes PJM's interim resource adequacy filing in ER26-3515. Its argument that data centers should bear capacity costs bears on E-7 adequacy and cost-shift questions.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8) E-7 PJM: Cost-recovery agreement (P 77, p. 28)

Key points

  1. The Market Monitor says the shortfall comes entirely from data center load and PJM's approach keeps imposing those costs on other customers.
    The shortfall identified in the RBP Filing is a result solely of data center demand. PDF p. 2
  2. It cites $29.4 billion of capacity market costs and 20,539.7 MW of data center load left in the 2029/2030 auction.
    add to the $29.4 billion of capacity market costs that have resulted from the inclusion of data centers PDF p. 3
  3. PJM pushes allocation to states and distribution companies instead of assigning data center costs to data centers.
    PJM is attempting to evade its responsibility to directly ensure that data centers pay their own costs by shifting that responsibility to the states and the EDCs. PDF p. 5
  4. The Monitor recommends rejection and a two-week Section 206 directive, including removing unbuilt data center load from auctions.
    The Market Monitor continues to recommend that the Commission reject the RBP Filing and direct PJM, under the Commission’s FPA Section 206 authority, to file, within two weeks, a solution PDF p. 6
  5. New data center loads would need 15-year bilateral contracts with new generation that allow no cost or risk shifting.
    PJM should establish criteria to be included in the RAA defining the obligations of LSEs to require that new data center loads have 15 year bilateral contracts with new generation PDF p. 6
  6. The Monitor says the backstop and the interim service use inconsistent definitions of qualifying capacity, with bilateral terms as short as five years.
    The RBP Filing includes inconsistent definitions of capacity that will result in unintended consequences for reliability and capacity market prices in the future. PDF p. 30
