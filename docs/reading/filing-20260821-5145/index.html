ER26-3380-000 · 2026-08-21 · Public Service Commission of West Virginia

Comments of Public Service Commission of West Virginia in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal

The Public Service Commission of West Virginia comments on PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement filing, opposing it in present form and asking for a new target computed with and without new large loads.

Asks FERC to: Require PJM to set a new backstop target from two capacity model runs, one excluding all new large load and one excluding load bringing its own generation, and delay commitment until states receive the results.

Parallel proceeding. Comments in PJM's separate backstop docket ER26-3380 on how PJM secures capacity for new large loads, the subject of the E-7 30-day adequacy report, with emphasis on keeping costs off other customers.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. West Virginia does not support the filing in its current form and timetable, citing weak support for the backstop target.
    The PSCWV does not support the filing in its present form and timetable because the proposal fails to provide adequate support that the target level of so-called backstop capacity is needed PDF p. 1
  2. PJM sets the initial target at the 6,831 MW shortfall seen in the July 2026 auction rather than waiting on bilateral matching.
    PJM is proposing the RBP based on a perceived 6,831 MW shortfall from its capacity target set for its last capacity auction. PDF p. 2
  3. At the $555 per MW-day cap the 15-year commitment could average $1.4 billion per year.
    could exceed an average of $1.4 billion per year. PDF p. 2
  4. Bilateral contracts let new large loads bear the cost of the capacity they need, shrinking the backstop target.
    Bilateral contracts allow the costs of new capacity to be assumed directly by the contracting new large loads, creating the need for that capacity. PDF p. 3
  5. West Virginia asks for two model runs, one with no new large load, to size any backstop need.
    FERC should require PJM to determine a new target for the RBP based on the model from its last capacity auction run under two different scenarios. PDF p. 4
