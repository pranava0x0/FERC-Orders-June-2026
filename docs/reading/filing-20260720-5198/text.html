The PJM Transmission Owners share the underlying goals driving the Commission’s 
issuance of the Show Cause Orders: namely, to enable new large load customers to efficiently 
interconnect to the transmission system while simultaneously ensuring reliability and protecting 
existing customers.  To that end, the PJM Transmission Owners respectfully seek clarification, or 
in the alternative, rehearing of two preliminary findings in the PJM Show Cause Order:   
1. The Commission should clarify that adopting the definition of “large load” proposed 
in the Show Cause Order, including the 50 MW threshold criteria, does not prevent 
PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners from proposing and supporting additional 
service-specific criteria for large load customers relating to the types of services 
contemplated in the Show Cause Order.  If the Commission declines to make this 
clarification, it should grant rehearing, as a one-size-fits-all approach for all customers 
that meet the definition of “large load” is likely to be over-inclusive and unduly 
discriminatory, and undermine the Commission’s speed-to-power goals for large load 
customers, particularly large computational loads that are crucial to serving AI 
processes. 
2. The Commission should clarify that PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners will be 
permitted to propose and support a definition of “large load” and “electrically 
proximate large load” that are not based on the location of facilities relative to the Point 
of Interconnection,11 e.g., by proposing a definition that relies on the Point of Change 
of Ownership.  If the Commission declines to adopt this clarification, it should grant 
rehearing.  As PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners have explained in the PJM co-
located load proceeding, there are significant problems with adopting such definitions 
based on facilities on the customer-side of the Point of Interconnection, including 
potentially encouraging gaming activities that will undermine the efficient and reliable 
interconnection of large loads.

B. 
Arguments 
1. 
The Commission Should Clarify That the Proposed Definition of 
“Large Load” in the Show Cause Order Does not Limit PJM and the 
PJM Transmission Owners From Proposing Additional Service-
Specific Criteria for the Services Contemplated in the Order. 
In the PJM Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff 
appears unjust and unreasonable because it lacks certain provisions governing the process to 
provide transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads, including a definition 
of large load, as a new category of load.  The Commission further stated that, based on the ANOPR 
record, it would be reasonable to define a large load as a new commercial or industrial customer 
at a single site, behind one or more points of interconnection, with a peak load of 50 MW or greater, 
interconnected at a voltage above 69 kV, and not part of a co-location arrangement.  The 
Commission therefore directed PJM either to show cause why the PJM Tariff remains just and 
reasonable without such a definition or to propose tariff revisions establishing its own definition 
of large load. 
The Commission should clarify that this definition, including the 50 MW threshold, does 
not limit PJM or the PJM Transmission Owners from proposing additional criteria or reasonable 
distinctions between large load customers for purposes of designing and implementing the 
products addressed in the Order.  It may not be appropriate to offer identical services to all large 
load customers.  For instance, considerations such as seasonality versus peak load measurements 
and functional distinctions between types of customers (e.g., hyperscale computational loads 
versus “traditional” manufacturing and commercial customers) may merit distinctions in how 
different products are designed, offered and priced.  The Commission therefore should clarify that 
the proposed large load definition does not prejudge the appropriate criteria for every other purpose 
under the PJM Tariff.

If the Commission declines to adopt this clarification, the Commission should grant 
rehearing.  The PJM Show Cause Order fails to justify a conclusion that the proposed large load 
definition requires that every customer meeting the definition of large load must be offered the 
same services under the same rates, terms and conditions.13  To the contrary, requiring a one-size-
fits all approach for all large load customers with respect to each new service is likely to undermine 
the goals of the Show Cause Order, particularly the desire for speed-to-power. As the PJM 
Transmission Owners explained in their comments on the ANOPR, an overly inclusive definition 
of “large load” risks repeating the same flaws that occurred with inflated generation 
interconnection queues.14  It is therefore of critical importance that PJM and the PJM Transmission 
Owners have sufficient flexibility to design and implement services with appropriate criteria that 
account for meaningful differences between large load customers.  Limiting this flexibility would 
be arbitrary, capricious, and would not reflect reasoned decision-making.   
2. 
The Commission Should Clarify That “Large Load” and “Electrically 
Proximate Large Load” Need not be Defined Based on the Point of 
Interconnection. 
As noted above, the Commission indicated in the PJM Show Cause Order that it would be 
reasonable to define a large load as a new commercial or industrial customer at a single site, behind 
one or more points of interconnection.15  Similarly, in discussing potential rules and procedures 
relating to “electrically proximate large load,” the Commission found that it would be reasonable 
to define “electrically proximate large load” as a large load that is sufficiently electrically close to 
the interconnection customer’s requested point of interconnection such that, with the exception of 
the transmission facilities between the two, the impact on the transmission system of the
