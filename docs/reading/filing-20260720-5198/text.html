The PJM Transmission Owners share the underlying goals driving the Commission’s issuance of the Show Cause Orders: namely, to enable new large load customers to efficiently interconnect to the transmission system while simultaneously ensuring reliability and protecting existing customers.  To that end, the PJM Transmission Owners respectfully seek clarification, or in the alternative, rehearing of two preliminary findings in the PJM Show Cause Order:

1. The Commission should clarify that adopting the definition of “large load” proposed in the Show Cause Order, including the 50 MW threshold criteria, does not prevent PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners from proposing and supporting additional service-specific criteria for large load customers relating to the types of services contemplated in the Show Cause Order.  If the Commission declines to make this clarification, it should grant rehearing, as a one-size-fits-all approach for all customers that meet the definition of “large load” is likely to be over-inclusive and unduly discriminatory, and undermine the Commission’s speed-to-power goals for large load customers, particularly large computational loads that are crucial to serving AI processes.

2. The Commission should clarify that PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners will be permitted to propose and support a definition of “large load” and “electrically proximate large load” that are not based on the location of facilities relative to the Point of Interconnection,11 e.g., by proposing a definition that relies on the Point of Change of Ownership.  If the Commission declines to adopt this clarification, it should grant rehearing.  As PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners have explained in the PJM co- located load proceeding, there are significant problems with adopting such definitions based on facilities on the customer-side of the Point of Interconnection, including potentially encouraging gaming activities that will undermine the efficient and reliable interconnection of large loads.

B. Arguments
1. The Commission Should Clarify That the Proposed Definition of
“Large Load” in the Show Cause Order Does not Limit PJM and the
PJM Transmission Owners From Proposing Additional Service-
Specific Criteria for the Services Contemplated in the Order.
In the PJM Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff appears unjust and unreasonable because it lacks certain provisions governing the process to provide transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads, including a definition of large load, as a new category of load.  The Commission further stated that, based on the ANOPR record, it would be reasonable to define a large load as a new commercial or industrial customer at a single site, behind one or more points of interconnection, with a peak load of 50 MW or greater, interconnected at a voltage above 69 kV, and not part of a co-location arrangement.  The Commission therefore directed PJM either to show cause why the PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable without such a definition or to propose tariff revisions establishing its own definition of large load.

The Commission should clarify that this definition, including the 50 MW threshold, does not limit PJM or the PJM Transmission Owners from proposing additional criteria or reasonable distinctions between large load customers for purposes of designing and implementing the products addressed in the Order.  It may not be appropriate to offer identical services to all large load customers.  For instance, considerations such as seasonality versus peak load measurements and functional distinctions between types of customers (e.g., hyperscale computational loads versus “traditional” manufacturing and commercial customers) may merit distinctions in how different products are designed, offered and priced.  The Commission therefore should clarify that the proposed large load definition does not prejudge the appropriate criteria for every other purpose under the PJM Tariff.

If the Commission declines to adopt this clarification, the Commission should grant rehearing.  The PJM Show Cause Order fails to justify a conclusion that the proposed large load definition requires that every customer meeting the definition of large load must be offered the same services under the same rates, terms and conditions.13  To the contrary, requiring a one-size- fits all approach for all large load customers with respect to each new service is likely to undermine the goals of the Show Cause Order, particularly the desire for speed-to-power. As the PJM Transmission Owners explained in their comments on the ANOPR, an overly inclusive definition of “large load” risks repeating the same flaws that occurred with inflated generation interconnection queues.14  It is therefore of critical importance that PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners have sufficient flexibility to design and implement services with appropriate criteria that account for meaningful differences between large load customers.  Limiting this flexibility would be arbitrary, capricious, and would not reflect reasoned decision-making.

2. The Commission Should Clarify That “Large Load” and “Electrically
Proximate Large Load” Need not be Defined Based on the Point of
Interconnection.
