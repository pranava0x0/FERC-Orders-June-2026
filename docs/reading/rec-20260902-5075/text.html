II. 
COMMENTS 
On August 21, 2026, East Kentucky Power Cooperative, LLC (“EKPC”) filed a protest in 
this docket. EKPC’s protest states several important concerns related to the RBP proposed by 
PJM.6 The Attorney General is equally concerned that steps should be taken to ensure that 
ratepayers are protected from the potential unjust and unreasonable cost allocation that could occur 
under the proposal. 
Of particular concern is whether Load Serving Entities who have met their obligations are 
protected from cost allocation in the event of default by the cost causer.  As detailed on pages 11, 
et seq, of the EKPC protest and in the Testimony of Adam Bednarczyk, the potential appears to 
exist for inappropriate cost shifting in the instance of default by responsible entities.  This potential 
is exacerbated by the lack of protection for Reliability Pricing Model entities who have prudently 
planned to fully serve their load. Under the RBP proposal, it does not appear that PJM 
appropriately decrements the target, or otherwise provides sufficient offsets, for utilities who have 
already taken appropriate actions to serve their load.7   
In general, the Kentucky Attorney General is concerned with the potential for others to free 
ride on the prudent resource adequacy decisions made by Load Serving Entities in Kentucky.  By 
allocating these costs unjustly, Kentucky utilities who have steel in the ground to serve their load
