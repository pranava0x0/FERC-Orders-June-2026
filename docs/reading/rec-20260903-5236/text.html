United recognizes and agrees with the rationale behind IRAS: New large loads that are contributing to the region’s resource adequacy shortfall, i.e., those that do not bring their own new capacity, should be the first to face curtailment if and when load shed is needed. I. INTRODUCTION

PJM is navigating a genuinely difficult situation, and IRAS reflects real effort to design a workable response to New Large Loads connecting to the PJM system faster than new supply can be brought online. United and its members have been active participants throughout the Critical Issue Fast Path (“CIFP”) stakeholder process leading to both the RBP and IRAS filings, and IRAS reflects meaningful refinement from the concepts PJM initially floated through that process.

However, the IRAS mechanism as proposed leaves important questions unresolved, particularly with respect to how a New Large Load’s efforts to reduce its own footprint and secure its own capacity are actually credited against its curtailment exposure, and how retail- level cost allocation and compensation will be implemented consistent with the Commission’s jurisdictional limits. United urges the Commission to direct PJM to act on these issues promptly, both to ensure IRAS functions as the fair, incentive-aligned mechanism it is meant to be, and to avoid creating a gap between wholesale design and retail-level outcomes.

United does not oppose the concept of a curtailment and BYONC mechanism as a necessary tool to manage an urgent reliability challenge, but United’s support for the specific approach taken in IRAS is tempered by a fundamental issue: PJM’s proposal assumes a degree of control over retail-level curtailment outcomes that PJM itself does not actually have. II. COMMENTS

United respectfully identifies the following specific clarifications and commitments needed to ensure IRAS achieves its intended purpose without creating unnecessary barriers to the very loads and resources best positioned to help close PJM’s resource adequacy gap.

A. Peak Shaving Adjustment Should Be Credited in the Eligible New Large
Load Amount Formula
The Large Load Registry tracks each load’s Peak Shaving Adjustment value, but the Eligible New Large Load Amount formula that determines a load’s IRAS curtailment exposure credits only BYONC and Allocated RBP UCAP MW.4 As a result, a load that has taken concrete, state-sanctioned steps to reduce its own contribution to peak demand receives no reduction in its IRAS curtailment exposure, the same practical result as a load that has done nothing at all to address the shortfall it is helping to cause.

Peak Shaving Adjustment is not a novel mechanism specific to this filing. It is an existing tool under Tariff, Attachment DD-2, available to any end-use customer whose load reduction is authorized under a state-codified program, and it predates IRAS entirely.5 PJM’s decision to add Peak Shaving Adjustment as a tracked field in the Large Load Registry accordingly reflects PJM’s own judgment that this value is relevant to a New Large Load’s profile and its capacity

B. Opportunities for DR/DER Aggregation as BYONC Should be Clarified and
Expanded
i. PJM Should Clarify or Revise what counts as “New” DR/DER
BYONC
Planned Demand Resource and Planned DER Capacity Aggregation Resources are
eligible as BYONC, an important recognition of demand-side value.7 These resources are among the most affordable and fastest to deploy, making them well situated to meet the speed-to-power imperative facing the PJM region. However, this eligibility is limited by the failure of the BYONC framework to clarify what qualifies as “new” capacity for purposes of satisfying the BYONC requirement. As proposed, DR/DER BYONC eligibility is limited to sites not registered in or before the 2026/2027 Delivery year, or to incremental capacity tied to newly registered generation or storage assets.8 This registration-based text excludes a site that has previously participated, went dormant, and is now capable of providing the same incremental value. In contrast, generation and storage can satisfy BYONC through Capacity Interconnection Rights transferred from a deactivated resource or through repowering a generator that retired before April 10, 2026.9 This provision is, appropriately, intended to allow new capacity from new generation and storage assets at existing sites; this pathway to bring on new capacity at existing sites has no demand-side counterpart. The treatment of future IRAS periods in PJM’s proposed tariff revisions similarly lacks clarity. PJM has proposed a separate eligibility test for DR/DER

ii. PJM Should Clarify that Demand-Side DER can Substitute
Individual Sites or Customers

United additionally requests affirmative clarification that demand-side BYONC should have the option to substitute a departing site or customer for an equivalent new one.11 PJM requires the Accredited UCAP value of a Demand Resource or DER Capacity Aggregation Resource to “be demonstrated each year for a 10-year period” using the installed capacity the resource had in its first Delivery Year.12 This requirement should not preclude a DER Aggregator whose portfolio loses a site to ordinary customer turnover, relocation, sale, or non- renewal from replacing that site and preserving its arrangement, such that its portfolio would continue to reflect the same installed capacity it started with.

The absence of a substitution mechanism would force a DER Aggregator to either hold a shrinking, non-compliant portfolio as sites depart, or exit the arrangement altogether. This consequence would fall with particular force on residential DER aggregators, since their

iii. IRAS Must Accommodate DER Aggregations as Aggregations

iv. PJM Should Create Additional DR Products to Expand the Role of
DR in Addressing the Region’s Capacity Shortfall

Some customers—including New Large Loads—may have the ability and willingness to participate as DR if given additional options to do so, specifically limited duration DR products that would carry more manageable curtailment risk given their particular customer profile. Outside of this proceeding, United recommends that PJM separately develop additional DR product options, calibrated to a capped number of event hours per year, that would expand available DR in the PJM region, including allowing a New Large Load to self-provide a meaningful share of its BYONC obligation through its own demonstrated ability to curtail or shift load during those hours.

A capped-hours structure of this kind would expand the types of customers willing and able to participate as DR and would give New Large Loads a lower-cost, faster-to-implement option for satisfying part of their BYONC obligation, particularly during the multi-year window before new generation or storage can realistically come online, without requiring the load to curtail an unlimited or unpredictable number of hours annually.

This ask sits outside the instant proceeding but would meaningfully contribute to PJM’s ability to address its resource adequacy shortfall, and would give New Large Loads another tool to support that goal.

C. IRAS Includes Important Elements That Should Be Maintained
United supports several elements of IRAS that will promote fair outcomes and recognize the standing value of demand-side and alternative capacity solutions and urges that these elements be maintained in IRAS or any successor curtailment and Bring Your Own New Capacity ("BYONC") framework.

First, BYONC's gating criteria explicitly recognizes incremental capacity created through the utilization of Surplus Interconnection Service as qualifying new capacity,14 a more permissive approach than PJM's own RBP proposal, which excludes SIS-based resources from selling into the RBP. United’s comments in the RBP proceeding also identified several critical barriers to full utilization of SIS that should be addressed separate from these two proceedings.15 Second, PJM should likewise be commended for locking a BYONC arrangement's

Accredited UCAP value at whatever the resource clears in the RPM Auction for the first Delivery Year of the arrangement, fixed for the full 10-year term, rather than subject to ongoing re-accreditation.16  This is the right design choice, and stands in useful contrast to RBP, where resources remain exposed to unhedgeable ELCC risk in revenue settlement for the life of the term, an issue United's RBP comments raise directly.17

Third, BYONC's inclusion of Planned Demand Resource and Planned DER Capacity
Aggregation Resources as eligible new capacity is likewise an important recognition of demand- side value,18 though additional clarity and flexibility is needed, as discussed above. Likewise, the Large Load Registry's tracking of Peak Shaving Adjustment values is a positive concept,19 though it is currently undermined by the Eligible New Large Load Amount formula, as discussed above.

But United is concerned that the entire incentive structure of IRAS rests on a premise that PJM’s own filing does not itself deliver: that a New Large Load bringing sufficient BYONC will actually be protected from curtailment. Eliminating or tightening the gap between PJM implementation and state curtailment will be critical to success of IRAS. Specifically, PJM designates only an aggregate megawatt figure for each Electric Distributor zone or area. This is explicit in the filing, in which PJM says that it “will identify only aggregate MW values that need to be reduced,” while “Electric Distributors, in coordination with the appropriate states and/or RERRAs, LSEs, and LSE customers will determine which loads should ultimately reduce subject to IRAS.”20 PJM does not direct curtailment of a specific retail customer. That authority belongs to the states and the Electric Distributors that operate under state jurisdiction, consistent with the FPA’s traditional divide between wholesale and retail regulation.

This is not a peripheral detail, it is the lynchpin of whether IRAS functions as designed. A New Large Load that invests in BYONC does so because PJM’s Eligible New Large Load Amount formula promises that investment will reduce its exposure to curtailment. But PJM has no mechanism to ensure that promise is honored once an actual Interim Resource Adequacy Alert is called. Nothing in the proposed Tariff or RAA obligates an Electric Distributor, or the state authority directing it, to actually exempt a load with sufficient BYONC from curtailment, or to curtail non-BYONC load first, when the Electric Distributor makes its own retail-level determination of which customers to reduce. But PJM has no mechanism, and by its own account no authority, to ensure that promise is honored once an actual Interim Resource Adequacy Alert is called.  A load could do everything IRAS asks of it and still have no assurance that the entity

The compensation side of IRAS has the identical structure: PJM sets a wholesale rate and Electric Distributors implement it “in conjunction with applicable state authorities and New Large Loads,”21 leaving open the same question of whether the load that reduced its exposure through BYONC is the load that actually receives the benefit of that effort, whether measured in reduced curtailment or in compensation for load actually reduced.

This same gap, wholesale design assuming an outcome only retail-level action can actually deliver, is one that United’s RBP comments already raise with respect to cost allocation, and United’s RBP comments state that this concern “applies with equal force to ensuring consistency between RBP's cost-allocation and opt-out provisions and IRAS's curtailment mechanics.”22 But unlike cost allocation, which the states are well positioned to address, and which does not ultimately impact the outcomes of PJM’s implementation of RBP, here PJM is delegating elements that are foundational to the function and impact of IRAS. This outcome could have been avoided had PJM taken responsibility for curtailment of New Large Loads that do not BYONC through adjustments to its own emergency procedures—while still allowing flexibility for states to take additional action to more precisely target curtailment at the retail level.

IV. CONCLUSION

United recognizes the unfortunate reality that curtailment of new large loads may be necessary to ensure reliability for existing customers in PJM as demand rises faster than supply and conceptually supports introduction of a mechanism to ensure that curtailment impacts those customers contributing to the resource adequacy shortfall through their own failure to bring new capacity to the system. The clarifications and commitments identified in these comments are offered as opportunities to ensure IRAS achieves its intended purpose. United appreciates the Commission’s consideration of these comments and looks forward to continuing to work with PJM and other stakeholders as this proceeding moves forward.
