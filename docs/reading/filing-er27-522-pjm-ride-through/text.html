The targeted application of ride-through requirements to Large Loads that meet the Large 
Computational Load definition is just and reasonable.  First, the proposed definition recognizes 
that ride-through and other operational requirements being developed are intended to apply only 
to Eligible Customers serving Large Loads with operating characteristics warranting additional 
reliability consideration.  Thus, for purposes of managing operational risk, defining Large 
Computational Load supports a more targeted approach than applying the same ride-through and 
other operational requirements to all Eligible Customers serving Large Loads without regard to 
facility type or operating behavior.   
Second, the proposed definition of Large Computational Load is aligned with NERC’s 
proposed definition of computational load entities, which has likewise recognized that 
computational loads may warrant reliability requirements distinct from those applicable to 
traditional loads.32  Both PJM’s proposed definition of Large Computational Load and NERC’s 
developing definition recognize that power electronic conversion equipment that serves 
information technology, such as servers, storage, and networking hardware, similarly focus on the

Fourth, PJM’s proposed approach to defining a specific subset of Large Load reflects 
PJM’s review and evaluation of emerging large-load and computational-load definitions under 
development by industry organizations and transmission providers.36 These industry efforts reflect 
a common recognition that certain computational loads present reliability considerations that differ

recognition that large computational facilities can present unique reliability considerations 
requiring enhanced planning, modeling, and operational oversight.  Consistent with these industry 
developments, PJM’s proposed Large Computational Load definition identifies facilities 
containing substantial computational load that may create material reliability impacts if they

B. 
PJM Is Proposing Ride-Through Requirements Applicable to Eligible Customers 
Taking Transmission Service on Behalf of Large Computational Loads 
PJM is proposing to revise its Tariff to establish a framework for setting minimum voltage 
and frequency ride-through requirements, with which Eligible Customers taking transmission 
service on behalf of both existing and new Large Computational Loads must comply.39  Under the 
proposed language in Tariff, Part III, section 28.2, the Transmission Provider shall establish 
“minimum voltage ride-through and frequency ride-through requirements with which Eligible 
Customers taking transmission service on behalf of Large Computational Load must comply, 
which shall be set forth in the PJM Manuals.”  The proposed language sets a floor for disturbance 
ride-through requirements while also leaving room for Transmission Owners or Eligible 
Customers to establish ride-through requirements that are “no less stringent than the minimum 
requirements established by Transmission Provider in the PJM Manuals[]” where “the 
Transmission Owner determines that such requirements are necessary to maintain the reliability, 
security, or operational performance of its transmission facilities or the Transmission System.”40  
Furthermore, any ride-through requirements established by an Eligible Customer or Transmission 
Owner must “be based upon reliability, planning, operational, stability, protection coordination, 
voltage performance, frequency performance, restoration, balancing, reserve management or other
