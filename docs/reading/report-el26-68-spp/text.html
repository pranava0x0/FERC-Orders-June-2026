BACKGROUND  
 
A. 
SPP 
 
SPP is a Commission-approved Regional Transmission Organization (“RTO”).11 It 
is an Arkansas non-profit corporation with its principal place of business in Little Rock, 
Arkansas. SPP currently has 154 members, including 16 investor-owned utilities, 16 
municipal systems, 26 generation and transmission cooperatives, 6 state agencies, 28 
independent power producers, 11 power marketers, 18 independent transmission 
companies, 3 federal agencies, 18 large retail customers, 4 alternative power entities, and 
8 public interest entities. As an RTO, SPP: (1) administers, across the facilities of SPP’s 
Transmission Owners, open access transmission service over approximately 85,000 miles 
of transmission lines in the Eastern and Western Interconnections covering portions of 
Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, 
Nebraska, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, and 
Wyoming; and (2) administers the Integrated Marketplace, a centralized day-ahead and 
real-time Energy and Operating Reserve market with locational marginal pricing and 
market-based congestion management.12 SPP also implements a resource adequacy

revisions to the Tariff, Bylaws, Membership Agreement, and business practices are

A. 
Current Resource Adequacy Outlook  
SPP's most recent Summer 2026 Resource Adequacy Report concludes that the SPP 
Region is adequately positioned to meet expected Summer 2026 demand while also 
providing an outlook through the Summer 2031 planning horizon.20 The report evaluates 
existing accredited Resources, anticipated retirements, planned generation additions, and 
projected demand growth across the SPP East Balancing Authority Area. For the 2026 
Summer Season, all sixty-five LREs satisfied their RAR.21 Based on data submitted in the 
2026 Summer Season Workbook, the East BAA maintains a reserve margin of 17.1%, 
representing approximately 5,752 MW of excess accredited capacity above the total RAR. 
This is an increase of approximately 2,500 MW of excess capacity compared to the 2025 
Summer Season. More than half of all LREs maintain reserve margins exceeding 16%, 
representing capacity in excess of approximately one-third of LRE forecasted Net Peak 
Demand.22 The 2026 Summer Season Workbook does not address the West BAA because 
the West BAA is not required to meet SPP’s resource adequacy processes and procedures 
until summer 2027.

A. 
ERAS 
On May 22, 2025, SPP submitted Tariff revisions to add the ERAS process 
establishing a one-time, accelerated pathway for new generating resources to help address 
urgent reliability needs through 2030.25 The Commission accepted the ERAS Filing on July 
21, 2025, with an effective date of July 22, 2025.26 SPP developed ERAS in collaboration 
with the RSC, REAL, and other stakeholders in response to the growing need to bring new 
generation resources online on an accelerated timeline. ERAS enables LREs to identify 
and secure the generating resources needed to maintain reliability across the SPP footprint, 
comply with the resource adequacy requirements set forth in Attachment AA of the Tariff, 
and satisfy their obligation to serve under applicable state or other governing law. To 
qualify for ERAS, a project was required to address an LRE's projected resource adequacy 
deficit, commit to achieving commercial operation within five years, and satisfy additional 
criteria demonstrating that the project was sufficiently developed and reasonably likely to 
achieve commercial operation in time to address identified resource adequacy needs.27 
ERAS operates separately from, but in parallel with, SPP's standard Generator 
Interconnection Procedures under Attachment V of the Tariff.

CPP 
In addition to initiatives specifically focused on large loads and resource adequacy, 
SPP has undertaken broader reforms to its regional transmission planning processes that 
will improve the region's ability to identify and address transmission needs associated with 
evolving load growth, changing resource portfolios, and increasing system complexity. 
One such initiative is CPP, which streamlines multiple existing transmission planning 
assessments into a single, coordinated planning framework. 
Historically, SPP conducted several planning studies independently, each with 
distinct objectives, assumptions, and study horizons. While these planning processes 
provided valuable information, the separate study cycles could result in duplicative 
analyses, differing assumptions, and limited opportunities to evaluate transmission needs 
holistically. As the region experiences accelerating load growth—including requests to 
serve large new loads—along with significant generation interconnection activity and 
evolving resource adequacy needs, SPP recognized the value of integrating these planning 
efforts into a unified process. 
The CPP establishes a coordinated planning cycle that evaluates reliability, 
economic, operational, and public policy-driven transmission needs using a common set of

PDF page 23

23 
assumptions and coordinated stakeholder input. By consolidating these planning activities, 
SPP is better positioned to evaluate transmission solutions across multiple planning 
objectives, improve consistency in forecasting future system conditions, and identify 
projects that provide broad regional benefits. The process also enhances transparency by 
providing stakeholders with a single planning framework through which assumptions, 
study methodologies, and recommended transmission solutions can be reviewed and 
refined. 
Although the CPP is not directed solely at accommodating large loads, it 
strengthens the planning foundation needed to address significant changes in demand 
across the SPP footprint. As large industrial facilities, data centers, electrification, and 
other emerging loads increasingly influence long term transmission needs, the CPP 
provides a more integrated process for evaluating how those loads interact with generation 
development, resource adequacy requirements, and regional transmission expansion. This 
integrated planning approach enables SPP to assess transmission needs using consistent 
planning assumptions while considering the cumulative impacts of multiple drivers 
affecting the Transmission System. 
The CPP complements SPP's other initiatives discussed in this report, including 
enhancements to the HILL framework, the HILLGA process, resource adequacy reforms, 
and other transmission planning improvements. Together, these efforts are intended to 
improve SPP's ability to plan for and reliably serve substantial new load growth while 
maintaining an efficient, transparent, and stakeholder-driven regional planning process.

SERVICE (PALS) 
 
A. 
Overview

The Price Adaptive Load (PAL) and Price Adaptive Load Service (PALS) 
framework establishes a new market participation model and transmission service option 
specifically designed for large, price-responsive loads.62 Unlike the initiatives discussed in 
Section IV, PAL/PALS remains under active stakeholder development and has not yet been 
submitted to the Commission. Accordingly, the discussion below summarizes the current 
proposal and anticipated stakeholder schedule. 
The PAL/PALS framework is intended to accommodate a new class of commercial 
and industrial customers—such as certain data centers and advanced manufacturing 
facilities—that are capable of adjusting or interrupting electricity consumption in response 
to real-time market conditions while reducing reliance on traditional firm transmission 
service. Participating PALs would be economically dispatched through SPP's Integrated 
Marketplace based on submitted price-sensitive bids and would be required to respond to 
dispatch instructions in both the Day-Ahead Market and Real-Time Balancing Market. To 
support this market participation model, the proposal also creates Price Adaptive Load 
Service (PALS), a new non-firm transmission service designed specifically for 
dispatchable, flexible loads. Unlike traditional transmission service, PALS would utilize a 
usage-based billing structure and require participating loads to be fully dispatchable, 
capable of rapidly reducing consumption, and subject to Curtailment during transmission 
constraints, Emergency Conditions, or local reliability events.  
The PAL/PALS framework represents the next evolution of SPP's large load 
strategy by providing a market-based option for flexible loads that complements SPP's
