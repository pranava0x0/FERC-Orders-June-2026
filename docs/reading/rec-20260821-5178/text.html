UNITED STATES OF AMERICA 
FEDERAL ENERGY REGULATORY COMMISSION 
PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms 
Docket No. AD26-7-000 
 
POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF 
TENASKA POWER SERVICES COMPANY 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Tenaska Power Services Co. (“TPS”) respectfully submits these post-technical conference 
comments in response to the Commission’s examination of PJM Interconnection LLC’s (“PJM”) 
governance and stakeholder processes.  TPS is a leading energy manager and marketer nationwide, 
providing a robust range of services to generation and demand-side customers across all major 
power markets, including PJM.  TPS is a PJM Voting Member, and is registered as an Other 
Supplier.  TPS submits the following responses to the Commission’s Notice Requesting Post-
Conference Comment dated July 30, 2026.  These responses are intended to only address the 
questions posed by the Commission following the technical conference, and are not intended to 
address the merits of any contested proceeding pending before the Commission. 
 
II. 
PJM BOARD INDEPENDENCE 
Questions/Comments 
Yes No

Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?

Comments: The current three-year terms work well.   
2)

primary residence within the PJM footprint will instill additional confidence 
within PJM stakeholders and end use customers.   
4) 
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling 
off period for Board members from two years to one year?

5) 
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable 
more communication between the Board and stakeholders? 
X 
Comments: Enabling the Board to have the opportunity for more direct

6) 
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public 
or the Board be required to explain its reasoning? 
X 
Comments: We support making the Board votes public, but we would not support 
requiring the Board to explain its reasoning.   
7) 
In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open 
or available to the public? 
X 
Comments: TPS supports making Board meetings open for PJM members, but 
this access should not be given to the general public.  
8) 
Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that 
the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest? 
X 
Comments: PJM’s legal obligations are to generally operate consistent with its 
FERC-approved tariff.  The Commission has an obligation to ensure that PJM’s 
rates are just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory.  The Commission 
thus safeguards the public interest by acting within the scope of its authority under 
the Federal Power Act to ensure that the rates and terms for PJM service are just 
and reasonable.  PJM’s current obligations to reliably operate the grid and run 
competitive markets clearly serve the public interest.  However, PJM’s first duty 
should be to the reliability and competitiveness of the markets it is charged with 
administering, and not to the public interest generally.  TPS believes that the 
Commission is in a better position to assess the PJM market’s affect on the 
general public interest.  
9) 
Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the 
Nominating Committee? 
X 
Comments: One voting member representing the PJM states would afford the 
right for the states’ perspective to be heard within the Nominating Committee 
while not providing a disproportionate presence.  
10) 
Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management 
compensation to their achievement? 
X 
Comments: The words public performance are too vague and would need to be 
explicitly defined for further consideration. The role of the PJM Board of 
Stakeholders is to direct the PJM Senior Executives in their organizational 
leadership. This includes evaluating their achievement of operational and 
economic performance metrics and how they are compensated for those results.

III. 
Section 205 Filing Rights 
 
Questions/Comments 
Yes No 
11) Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only 
inform the Board? 
X 
Comments: The current balance established by the PJM Governing Documents 
does not require revision. Market Participant confidence in the process of 
establishing the most effective market design is critical to investment.     
12) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and 
ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval 
of two-thirds of the Members Committee? 
X 
Comments: No, PJM should not have section 205 filing rights over the energy 
market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain 
approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee.  PJM currently has Section 
205 filing rights over its Capacity Market, and it has not resulted in an effective 
market design.        
13) Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission 
Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to 
obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee? 
X

rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings 
when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal? 
X 
15) Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the 
portions of the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a 
supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal?

IV. 
Stakeholder Process 
 
Questions/Comments 
Yes No 
16) Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered 
from two-thirds sector-weighted voting? 
X 
Comments: Two-thirds sector-weighted voting has worked to require a significant 
amount of agreement and requires market participants to be willing to 
compromise, which strengthens the market designs, so no change is necessary. 
17) Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior 
standing committees, also apply to lower-level committees? 
X 
 
V. 
State Participation 
 
Questions/Comments 
Yes No 
18) 
In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative 
group consisting of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ 
representatives? 
X 
Comments: If this is desired within the states then the OPSI by laws should be 
changed to allow this input within its process. Each individual state should be 
heard as one voice as PJM executives have advocated for.   
19) Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the 
stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource 
adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the 
consumer advocate votes? 
X 
Comments: States are not market participants. Their state commissions have 
direct oversight over their transmission and vertically integrated utility companies 
who are active PJM stakeholders.  
20) Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 
205 filing related to resource adequacy and transmission cost 
allocation? 
X 
Comments: The purpose of consulting is not clear. PJM has no obligation to 
consult with any other stakeholder so this would unacceptably benefit one 
stakeholder over another. In the course of normal business, the PJM Board and 
State Governmental Affairs group already regularly communicate with OPSI.
