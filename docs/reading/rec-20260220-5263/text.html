Second, the Market Monitor suggests that such an arrangement is inconsistent with “[t]he
obligations of capacity resources,” which “include the requirement to offer their full [capacity] in the energy and reserves markets every day.”11 But the Commission has previously clarified that PJM’s “must-offer requirement, when implemented, should not impede resources from exporting energy on a non-firm or recallable basis, i.e., that the must-offer requirement will not operate in [a] manner that requires the resource to sit idle.”12 Thus, a capacity resource is not required to sit idle by the phone 8,760 hours per year waiting to see if PJM might call.13 To the extent the generator can demonstrate that its obligation to provide energy to the co-located load is non-firm or recallable, and the load demonstrates that it will either curtail or be served by backup supply when generator and grid supply are unavailable, there is simply no reason for the generator to delist and exacerbate any supply shortfall. The Clarification Request seeks nothing more than the same type of treatment already afforded non-firm exports.14 Constellation acknowledges that there may have to be adjustments to the energy offer price rules to accommodate the non-firm supply of co-located load from a capacity resource.
