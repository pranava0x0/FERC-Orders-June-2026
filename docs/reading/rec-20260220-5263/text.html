Constellation1 moves for leave and submits this answer in response to the Independent 
Market Monitor for PJM’s answer2 to Constellation’s Request for Clarification, or in the 
Alternative, Request for Rehearing3 of the Commission’s December 18, 2025 Order.4 In relevant 
part, Constellation sought clarification that generators selling non-firm electricity to co-located 
large loads do not need to delist their capacity.5 The Commission should not force a generator to 
delist its capacity when the generator’s co-located load obligation is non-firm and service can be 
curtailed by both PJM and the generator. In such an arrangement, the generator’s output remains

Second, the Market Monitor suggests that such an arrangement is inconsistent with “[t]he 
obligations of capacity resources,” which “include the requirement to offer their full [capacity] in 
the energy and reserves markets every day.”11 But the Commission has previously clarified that 
PJM’s “must-offer requirement, when implemented, should not impede resources from exporting 
energy on a non-firm or recallable basis, i.e., that the must-offer requirement will not operate in 
[a] manner that requires the resource to sit idle.”12 Thus, a capacity resource is not required to sit 
idle by the phone 8,760 hours per year waiting to see if PJM might call.13 To the extent the 
generator can demonstrate that its obligation to provide energy to the co-located load is non-firm 
or recallable, and the load demonstrates that it will either curtail or be served by backup supply 
when generator and grid supply are unavailable, there is simply no reason for the generator to 
delist and exacerbate any supply shortfall. To the contrary, there is every reason for the 
Commission to encourage such arrangements, which allow new large loads to interconnect without
