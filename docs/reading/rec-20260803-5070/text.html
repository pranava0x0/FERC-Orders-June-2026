Re: Docket No. AD26-7-000 - PJM Governance and Stakeholder Process Reforms
Commissioners:
Persistence Analytics Group LLC respectfully submits these post-conference comments in response to the Commission's invitation for bold, actionable reforms to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. governance and stakeholder processes.

The Commission should not accept governance reform that merely rearranges representation, modifies voting mechanics, or creates another layer of consultation. PJM is confronting decisions with system-wide financial and reliability consequences. Its governance must therefore establish clear responsibility for the evidence underlying those decisions, the assumptions embedded in them, and the costs imposed when those assumptions fail. THE OPERATING RULE IS SIMPLE:

NO MATERIAL PLANNING ASSUMPTION WITHOUT A NAMED OWNER, A
DOCUMENTED EVIDENTIARY BASIS, AND A PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY
PATH.
This standard is especially urgent as PJM evaluates unprecedented large-load growth, transmission needs, capacity procurement, interconnection readiness, and cost allocation. Announced demand is not the same as executable demand. Executable demand is not the same as durable demand. Governance that does not force those distinctions into the decision record is not governance adequate to the risk.

The attached comments propose a direct accountability architecture: verified load classifications, assumption ownership, decision-grade disclosure, independent challenge rights, full electrical cost-causation review, formal escalation, and measurable implementation deadlines.

Respectfully submitted,

POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS
I. THE COMMISSION SHOULD DEFINE THE OUTCOME BEFORE REFORMING THE
PROCESS
The purpose of PJM governance is not stakeholder participation for its own sake. The purpose is to produce timely, evidence-based, reviewable decisions that preserve reliability, assign costs fairly, and withstand scrutiny after capital is committed.

Any reform package should be judged against one question: Does it make a responsible party answerable for the assumptions that drive a material PJM decision? If the answer is no, the reform is procedural, not operational.

II. PJM MUST CREATE A FORMAL ASSUMPTION-ACCOUNTABILITY RECORD
For each material planning, market, procurement, or cost-allocation decision, PJM should publish a concise assumption-accountability record identifying:

• The decision owner: the PJM officer or committee accountable for the final recommendation.
• The critical assumptions: load, timing, persistence, interconnection readiness, financing, operating profile, flexibility, retirement, supply, and cost-allocation assumptions.

• The evidence: what was submitted, by whom, when it was validated, and what remains unverified.
• The consequence range: the reliability, capital, market, and customer-cost outcomes if the assumptions are wrong.

• The dissent and escalation path: how a material evidentiary challenge is preserved and elevated before implementation.

III. LARGE-LOAD FORECASTS REQUIRE DECISION-GRADE CLASSIFICATION
PJM should stop treating projected load as a single category. At minimum, large-load additions should be separated into the following stages:

Classification
Required meaning
Announced
Publicly stated or submitted, but not yet supported by enforceable commercial, site, equipment, power-delivery, and schedule evidence.

Committed
Supported by defined financial obligations, deposits, contracts, or equivalent
commitments.
Electrically executable
Interconnection path, upstream reinforcement, equipment, permitting, and
energization dependencies are sufficiently established.
Operationally durable
The expected operating profile, persistence, ramp behavior, curtailment capability,
and withdrawal risk have been evaluated.
PJM should disclose which classification supports each planning decision and prohibit a lower-confidence classification from being presented as if it were a higher-confidence one.

IV. COST CAUSATION MUST FOLLOW THE FULL ELECTRICAL CONSEQUENCE
Direct customer funding of interconnection facilities can be appropriate, but governance fails if review ends at the point of interconnection. A 1 GW to 3 GW customer can alter flows, contingencies, substation configuration, local reliability needs, and future expansion options across the broader network.

PJM must therefore distinguish, on the record:
• facilities uniquely required by the customer;
• upstream reinforcements materially caused by the customer;
• upgrades that produce measurable regional benefits; and
• baseline needs that would exist without the customer.
A shallow cost-causation methodology can socialize upstream costs even when the customer directly funds facilities near the site. Governance reform must prevent that outcome.

V. MATERIAL EVIDENTIARY CHALLENGES NEED INDEPENDENT ESCALATION
A stakeholder process cannot police itself when voting power, commercial interests, and institutional incentives are embedded in the same decision. PJM should establish a formal independent review channel for disputes involving:

• load forecast durability or readiness;
• material cost-causation disputes;
• use of confidential evidence that cannot be meaningfully tested;
• assumption changes introduced after stakeholder review; and
• decisions where the downside risk is transferred to customers who did not create it. The review channel should have authority to require a written response, preserve minority evidence, and refer unresolved matters to the PJM Board or the Commission on a defined timetable.

VI. THE SEPTEMBER DISPUTE-RESOLUTION PROCESS MUST END WITH
DELIVERABLES
The Commission's facilitated process should not conclude with generalized principles. It should produce a public implementation matrix containing:

• Action: the exact reform to be implemented.
• Owner: the accountable PJM body or officer.
• Deadline: a fixed date, not an open-ended commitment.
• Evidence of completion: tariff filing, manual revision, reporting protocol, or governance document.
• Commission checkpoint: the date on which progress will be reviewed publicly.
PJM DOES NOT NEED MORE PROCESS WITHOUT CONSEQUENCE.
IT NEEDS GOVERNANCE THAT CAN IDENTIFY THE ASSUMPTION, NAME
THE OWNER, TEST THE EVIDENCE, TRACE THE COST, AND CORRECT
THE DECISION.

VII. CONCLUSION
The Commission should require PJM to convert governance reform into a durable accountability system. Representation matters. Voting matters. But neither is sufficient unless the resulting decisions are anchored to verified evidence, assigned ownership, transparent cost causation, and enforceable implementation. Persistence Analytics Group LLC respectfully requests that the Commission incorporate these accountability requirements into the next phase of Docket No. AD26-7-000.
