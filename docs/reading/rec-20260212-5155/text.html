Constellation filed its motion and answer in response to the Indicated TOs’ January 16, 2026 rehearing request. In its Answer, Constellation also attempts to stretch the testimony of Exelon’s witness, David Weaver, to suggest his testimony provides record evidence that PJM’s open access transmission tariff (“PJM Tariff”) has been interpreted inconsistently.12  It does not.  Mr. Weaver stated the following during FERC’s technical conference: “[W]hat we could really use is . . . some clarity around the rules of the road, so that . . . we don’t have non-conforming agreements, and we don’t have language that doesn’t exist in the tariffs.”13  Mr. Weaver’s desire for “clarity” was not a statement that the PJM Tariff or Network Service were inadequate; it was a concern that customers were trying to avoid following the PJM Tariff, and avoid paying for Network Service, and that those attempts at evasion and bypass led to vague requests that violated tariff procedures and were thus causing problems. Constellation’s Answer is merely a continuation of its debate with the Indicated TOs; it is not worthy of acceptance by the Commission.  It begins with two pages dedicated to parsing the number of parties in the Indicated TOs’ coalition.  Constellation claims the Indicated TOs are incorrect in arguing that reliability concerns were ignored,15 but points to no record evidence undermining the Indicated TOs’ position.  Indeed, where Constellation says these arguments are “premature,”16 it underscores the Indicated TOs’ argument that the Commission has not yet engaged in the analysis.  Constellation claims that the Commission precedent regarding “split load,” which the Indicated TOs rely upon, is inapplicable.17  It is not.  The cases cited by the Indicated TOs were exactly on point,18 and Constellation’s continued need to debate the issue in its Answer does not serve the Commission’s decision-making process.

Ultimately, there is no good cause for the Commission to disregard its own regulations by granting Constellation’s request.  Nothing submitted in Constellation’s Answer undercuts any of the arguments presented in the Indicated TOs’ rehearing request and Constellation had several opportunities throughout the course of this proceeding to raise its arguments in the record. Constellation’s motion should be denied, and its Answer should be rejected.
