B. The Commission Did Not Justify Mandatory Price-Taker Treatment
1. The Commission Failed to Engage in a Reasoned Balancing Analysis
In its Order, the Commission rejected the arguments of Constellation and other commenters objecting to the RBP Proposal’s price taker requirement, stating that “the Commission has consistently found that Regional Transmission Organizations and Independent System Operators can reasonably require resources that are compensated outside of the capacity market because they are needed to maintain reliability to offer as price-takers in capacity auctions to avoid double procurement, even at the risk of price suppression.”26

Notably, as support for its finding, the Commission cited to its RMR precedent, without providing any additional analysis. The Order’s reliance on RMR precedent does not supply the missing analysis.27  Unlike in the RMR proceedings, in the Order the Commission failed to engage in a reasoned balancing analysis to determine whether it is just and reasonable in these circumstances to require out-of-market resources to offer as price takers.  Any such meaningful analysis would find that on balance the facts presented here, unlike in the RMR proceedings, weigh in favor of finding that the RBP Proposal’s price taker requirement is unjust and unreasonable given the material and potentially long-lived effect it may have on RPM price formation for years to come.

2. The Order Did Not Address the Matched Isolation Mechanism,
Which Was Supported by Expert Testimony
PJM justified mandatory price-taker treatment as necessary to prevent RPM from procuring capacity already secured through the RBP. Paragraph 53 accepted that rationale without adequately considering the countervailing market and long-term resource adequacy impacts of such approach or addressing the mechanism Constellation proposed.

PJM suggested that RBP resources must be offered into RPM as a price-taker to ensure load does not “double pay” for capacity. But as Constellation explained in previous filings, the price-taker requirement will undermine RPM as a credible price-forming market and resource adequacy will be hindered. A mandatory $0/MW-day offer is not just or reasonable, and does more harm than good, because it will signal excess supply if anticipated load is delayed or does not show
