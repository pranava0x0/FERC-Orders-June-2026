I. ARGUMENT

The Stay Motion is unnecessary as the record in this proceeding is clear on two fundamental points.  First, all parties have expressed interest in the Commission providing regulatory guidance as soon as possible to bring clarity and assist with the current challenges and disputes.  Second, the existing PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“PJM Tariff”) is just and reasonable in regard to co-location arrangements and therefore the Commission has everything it needs to provide the expeditious regulatory guidance everyone seeks.

In order to take any action imposing changes to the PJM Tariff in a Federal Power Act (“FPA”) section 206 proceeding,3 the Commission must first find that the existing PJM Tariff is not just and reasonable.  No such showing has been made and thus there is no basis to pause the Commission’s effort to expeditiously provide guidance without first satisfying that threshold requirement.  Indeed, the record in this proceeding demonstrates that the existing PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable.  Unless and until the Commission finds to the contrary under FPA section 206, there are no grounds to amend the PJM Tariff.

Numerous commenters in this proceeding address the national security implications of service to these new large loads.  The truth is that the national interest will be best served by a quick dismissal of this proceeding, and a ruling that the existing PJM Tariff remains just and

a. The PJM Tariff Has Not Been Shown to Be Unjust and Unreasonable.
In a proceeding under section 206 of the FPA, the Commission can only remedy a “rate [or] charge” or “any rule, regulation, practice, or contract affecting such rate [or] charge” after it is found to be “unjust [or] unreasonable.”4  “The burden of proving an unjust or unreasonable rate rests with the party that initiated the proceeding—the Commission [(as in this case)] or the third- party complainant.”5  Only after a successful showing that the prevailing rate is unjust and unreasonable may the Commission order a replacement rate.6  No such showing has been made here.

The Stay Motion succinctly summarizes the arguments for why the existing rate is purportedly unjust and unreasonable.7  The arguments boil down to the following: while the PJM Tariff provides that wholesale customers serving load in the PJM Interconnection L.L.C. (“PJM”) region should do so with Network Integration Transmission Service, a certain class of wholesale customer would rather take some new and different type of service that would cost them less. Of course, nearly everyone who buys nearly any service would rather pay less.  But in PJM, the transmission owners, as in virtually every other piece of the grid regulated by the Commission, assess on a postage-stamp basis, without consideration of the distance between load and generation, the embedded costs of their respective portions of the transmission grid from network

II. CONCLUSION
The national interest will not be well served if service requests under the existing rules are further delayed by the outcome of this proceeding in which a number of parties attempt to rewrite the PJM Tariff.  Instead, the best way to promote co-located load customers is to provide certainty, which the Commission can accomplish quickly by simply clarifying that co-located loads should seek service under the well-established, just and reasonable terms of the existing PJM Tariff. For the reasons stated above, the Indicated TOs respectfully request that the Commission reject the Stay Motion.
