RESPONSE BRIEF OF CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC

Constellation1 submits this response brief to discuss three points with broad support in the reply briefs regarding how PJM and the Transmission Owners2 must modify their proposals to ensure compliance with the Commission’s December 18th Order.

First, a proposed effective date of June 2029 runs counter to the spirit of the Commission’s December 18th Order.3 PJM and Transmission Owners must act expeditiously. Winning the AI race requires a sprint, not a stroll. The Commission could help accelerate the interconnection of large loads by setting the new transmission services on separate tracks. B. Without Intervention, the Commission’s Requirement for Special Protection Schemes is at Risk for Misuse

On balance, the Commission must be on guard for attempts by Transmission Owners to effectively block access to the new Interim NITS and Contract Demand Services. One proposal with the potential to continue to delay and thwart Co-Location Arrangements is the request that Co-Location Arrangements reflect Transmission Owners’ mandates for special protection schemes.47 In our Reply Brief, we explained that several recommendations on special protection schemes proposed in the Transmission Owners’ Initial Brief are “unsupported and unduly discriminatory towards Co-Located Load arrangements,” including recommendations to require double special protection schemes on both the customer and Transmission Owner side of the Point of Interconnection, to allow Transmission Owners to own an Eligible Customer’s special protection schemes, to limit the amount of load that can be covered by the special protection scheme, and to test special protection schemes for Co-Located Loads more frequently.48 Constellation explained “[p]rotection schemes ‘regularly employed on the PJM transmission system’ are not subject to these new requirements” and there is “no legitimate basis for treating Co-Located Load arrangements differently.”49

The reply comments by Indicated Transmission Owners imply three incorrect assumptions about special protection schemes: (1) the purpose of special protection schemes is to “ensure that a Co-Located Load does not exceed its specified level of transmission service,”3 that is, to deter unreserved use; (2) special protection schemes “must be implemented”4 for all Co- Location Arrangements to maintain reliability; and (3) special protection schemes should be required on “both the customer’s side and the Transmission Owner’s side of the point of interconnection.”5 Below, I rebut each assumption in turn while explaining the purpose of special protection schemes and how PJM currently determines their necessity.

5. First, the purpose of special protection schemes is not to deter unreserved use of the transmission system. That is the purpose of penalties. Special protection schemes are intended to maintain reliability by protecting the system from actual overloads and conditions that could cause cascading failures that PJM cannot mitigate through normal operations.
