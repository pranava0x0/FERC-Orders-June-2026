Attachment A

a. General
i. Do you agree with the jurisdictional principles outlined in section VI.A and should the Commission reach any other conclusions

regarding jurisdiction in a final order it may issue in this
proceeding?  Why or why not?

AES Ohio generally agrees with the Indicated TOs response.

ii. Does the Tariff sufficiently address co-location arrangements with respect to each issue listed in this order?  Please include specific

tariff provisions or other governing documents that may apply;

AES Ohio believes that a framework for co-location is addressed by the PJM Tariff, and that the Tariff is just and reasonable as to service to co-located load taken under PJM Network Integration Transmission Service or Firm Point to Point Service.  However, recognizing that co-location is rapidly developing and evolving, AES Ohio is open to working with PJM and stakeholders to explore alternative transmission service arrangements for certain co-located load configurations that may be appropriate to reflect specific co-located load circumstances and associated system needs. In certain circumstances, co-located large loads may benefit the grid by providing reliability benefits, decreasing congestion costs or limiting transmission upgrades.  In such cases, co-location could be incentivized by appropriately allocating costs, and allowing such load to share in the benefits, of the arrangement.  However, in all cases, co located load must pay for its use of the PJM Transmission System in a manner that avoids unjust and unreasonable outcomes and that holds other AES Ohio transmission customers harmless.

iv. Parties may also address how co-located loads are similar to or
disparate from other types of arrangements where load and
generation share the same point of interconnection (such as Behind
the Meter Generation as defined in PJM’s Tariff or non-retail
behind the meter generation) and should consider how any
proposed replacement rate would interact with these other

As explained by PJM in the Guidance Document, the PJM Tariff allows for generation to be treated as being “behind the meter” when such generation is co-located with a load and meets the specific defined criteria in the PJM Tariff.  AES Ohio agrees with PJM’s explanation of (1) how such “Behind the Meter Generation” interacts with the co-located load for the purposes of energy netting and (2) the restrictions placed on such “Behind the Meter Generation” with respect to that generation’s participation in the PJM market. AES Ohio does not view “Behind the Meter Generation” as disparate to other co-location arrangements, but rather as one of the arrangements under which co-location may occur in PJM.  To the extent that parties wish to expand or revisit the definition of “Behind the Meter Generation” with respect to current or future co-location configurations, AES Ohio believes that these discussions could be considered through the PJM stakeholder process, subject to the requirement that such additional configurations do not unduly discriminate against other transmission customers.  See response to a.ii.

iv. To what extent does a co-location arrangement raise potentially distinct cost-allocation issues relative to a deactivating generator that causes the need for transmission upgrades?  What principles should the Commission apply to allocate the costs of any

transmission upgrades needed to mitigate the impacts of co-
locating load?  What steps should PJM take to ensure these costs are transparent to all parties?

Section V of the PJM Tariff addresses how PJM studies the reliability impacts associated with potential generator deactivations.  AES Ohio’s understanding is that, pursuant to the PJM Tariff, if the generator changes its ISA to enable it to operate in a co-located load arrangement, then any system upgrades must be paid for by the existing removed generator in order to avoid cost subsidization by existing transmission customers.  This is different than if a generator actually retires and shuts down, as any necessary system reinforcements resulting from shut down are paid by transmission customers.
