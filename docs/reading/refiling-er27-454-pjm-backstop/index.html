ER27-454-000 · 2026-11-09 · PJM

Proposed Amendments to the Reliability Backstop Procurement; Request for Waiver

PJM refiles under FPA section 205 its October 29 amendments to the Reliability Backstop Procurement, responding to the September 29 order on cost allocation, load-serving entity credit and exit responsibility, and conditionally seeks waiver of two shortage rules in Attachment DD.

Asks FERC to: Accept the RBP amendments effective February 28, 2027 (one record January 29, 2027), issue an order by January 29, 2027, and grant a one-time waiver of Attachment DD sections 16.2 and 16.3.

Parallel proceeding. Revises the backstop procurement docket's cost allocation, credit and exit terms after FERC's September 29 order, the capacity-adequacy track running beside E-7's report on how PJM will ensure generation for large loads.

E-7 PJM: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 8)

Key points

  1. The refiling matches the October 29 filing in ER27-330 and changes only its metadata code from compliance to section 205.
    substantively identical to the October 29, 2026 filing submitted in Docket No. ER27-330-000 PDF p. 1
  2. PJM asks for a February 28, 2027 effective date, one record on January 29, 2027, and an order by January 29, 2027.
    PJM is also requesting that the Commission issue an order on all aspects of this filing no later than January 29, 2027 PDF p. 2
  3. PJM seeks conditional waiver of shortage investigation and backstop declaration rules if the 2029/2030 auction clears short.
    conditionally requests waiver of the requirement in Tariff, Attachment DD, section 16.2 PDF p. 3
  4. Electric Distributors will report incremental Large Load growth from the 2026 peak to the 2028/2029 expected peak, replacing the Load Adjustment proxy.
    rather than relying on Load Adjustment data as a proxy for Large Load growth, PJM is proposing to require Electric Distributors PDF p. 7
  5. PJM allocates the Initial RBP Target by zone share of incremental Large Load growth, net of bilateral offsets, fixed for the 15-year commitment.
    That allocation to the zone/area will remain fixed throughout the 15-year RBP commitment period. PDF p. 12
  6. PJM cuts the RBP Credit Rate from $1.5 million per MW to 40 percent of net present value, about $686,000 per MW at the cap.
    the proposed methodology produces an illustrative RBP Credit Rate of approximately $686,000 per MW. PDF p. 23
  7. PJM lengthens LSE collateral notice to at least 75 days and broadens acceptable collateral forms.
    PJM is proposing that LSEs be provided at least 75 days PDF p. 26
  8. An LSE that withdraws from PJM stays responsible for its remaining RBP Charges, rather than the exiting Transmission Owner.
    such Load Serving Entity shall be responsible for the remainder of the RBP Charges allocated to it PDF p. 32

Transmittal: why the backstop has a new docket

PJM says the November 9 filing repeats the substance of its October 29 proposal. It changed the electronic filing code to identify a new section 205 rate filing, rather than compliance with an earlier order.

updates the eTariff metadata code to reflect this filing as a FPA section 205 filing rather than a compliance filing PDF p. 2

Filing record · PDF pp. 3–5

I. What FERC left unresolved

The September order set three questions for paper hearing: who pays, what collateral protects against default and who pays after exit. PJM offers revisions under section 205 and consents to FERC severing individual provisions if necessary.

modify, reject, or sever discrete elements of the proposed Tariff revisions while accepting the remainder of the filing PDF p. 5

Cost allocation · PDF pp. 5–11

II.A.1. Which growth counts

PJM replaces its load-adjustment proxy with distributors' reports of incremental peak demand between summer 2026 and the expected 2028/29 peak. A qualifying agreement backs each identified load. This one-time measurement for backstop costs is initially separate from the ongoing registry.

the information required under this proposal will initially be reported separately from the Large Load Registry PDF p. 11

Cost allocation · PDF pp. 11–13

II.A.2. From regional need to a zone's share

PJM proposes to assign the initial target to zones or areas in proportion to incremental large-load growth. Qualifying bilateral capacity and peak shaving can reduce procurement and a zone's allocation. Croop's affidavit works through the arithmetic.

in proportion to each zone/area’s share of the total incremental Large Load growth PDF p. 11

Retail jurisdiction · PDF pp. 13–16

II.A.3. From a zone to a supplier and retail customer

A state or retail regulator can direct the allocation among load-serving entities. With no reported allocation, PJM would use each supplier's share of daily unforced capacity obligation. The state retains authority over recovery from retail customers.

PJM does not propose, and lacks authority, to dictate how any LSE subsequently allocates or recovers those costs from retail customers PDF p. 16

Credit and default · PDF pp. 16–24

II.B.1. How PJM prices default protection

PJM proposes collateral equal to 40% of discounted total payment obligations. Kelly compares posting costs with expected losses borne by other members. At an assumed $555/MW-day clearing price, the illustration produces about $686,000/MW; it is neither a clearing price nor a bill.

40% of the net present value of the total RBP payment obligation PDF p. 23

Credit and default · PDF pp. 24–27

II.B.1. Which resources require security

The uniform rate would apply only to committed capacity in commercial operation by the start of the relevant delivery year, excluding rescinded MW. PJM describes how it could release excess collateral as the payment obligation declines.

no collateral would be required for RBP Resources not in service for that Delivery Year PDF p. 24

Credit and default · PDF pp. 27–29

II.B.2–3. When and how to post collateral

The initial allocation would be reported 120 days before 2028/29; suppliers still post collateral 45 days before delivery. PJM adds surety bonds to cash and letters of credit, giving suppliers another way to secure the obligation.

LSEs must post RBP Collateral at least 45 days before the start of a Delivery Year PDF p. 27

Credit and default · PDF pp. 29–31

II.B.4–5. Review and double counting

PJM proposes to review collateral after all committed resources enter commercial operation and take any recommendations to members. It would exclude payments for those resources from the separate Peak Market Activity calculation.

shall not be included in Peak Market Activity PDF p. 31

Exit liability · PDF pp. 31–40

II.C. Who pays if an entity leaves PJM

PJM puts the continuing obligation on the withdrawing load-serving entity, including when a transmission owner's exit forces it to leave. A later rate filing would set the terms and amounts. The supplier could seek recovery from another entity responsible for the exit.

subject to a rate filing with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission that details the specific terms and amounts owed PDF p. 36

Cost allocation · PDF pp. 40–41

III.A. Municipal and cooperative opt-out

PJM proposes to remove the opt-out available solely because a distributor is not state-regulated. FERC had questioned whether similarly situated large loads would escape costs because of the type of distributor serving them.

eliminate the provision that permits an Electric Distributor PDF p. 41

Demand flexibility · PDF pp. 41–43

III.B. Peak shaving as an offset

PJM would let qualifying peak shaving reduce the procurement target only with a commitment of at least five years. This matches the minimum duration of a bilateral supply offset.

committed to participate in the applicable Peak Shaving Adjustment program for a minimum term of five years PDF p. 42

Seller eligibility · PDF pp. 43–45

III.C. Correcting an incomplete offer

A seller would have five calendar days from notification to cure a deficiency, even if the submission window closes before those five days are up.

five calendar days from receipt of a deficiency notice to cure any deficiencies identified by PJM PDF p. 44

Demand flexibility · PDF pp. 45–50

III.D. When bilateral capacity reduces the target

PJM limits the offset to the load's capacity obligation. A planned demand resource or distributed aggregation must identify its supporting customers and show incremental capability rather than count the same existing capacity twice.

the Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW must be the subject of a bilateral agreement with a Large Load PDF p. 47

Seller eligibility · PDF pp. 50–53

III.E. Which sellers must prove site control

PJM would reserve the separate three-year, 100% site-control showing for projects outside current or completed PJM interconnection studies. A project already showing site control in that process would avoid duplicating the proof.

only projects that are not currently undergoing, and have not previously completed, an interconnection study PDF p. 52

Procurement target · PDF pp. 53–56

III.F. How much capacity to buy

PJM keeps 2028/29 as its reference year but uses updated load forecasts and capacity ratings. The initial target would be the smaller of the recalculated shortfall and the region's aggregate large-load quantity, in unforced capacity MW.

the lesser of the calculated value of the 2028/2029 Delivery Year shortage and the region-wide total of Large Load MW PDF p. 54

Seller obligations · PDF pp. 56–58

III.G. When a committed resource must offer

PJM proposes a must-offer obligation beginning with the first delivery year for which a resource is committed and expected in service. The text makes a timing exception for certain 2030/31 resources without a prior auction notice of intent.

That must offer obligation, regardless of resource type, will continue through the end of the term of the RBP commitment PDF p. 57

Reliability and timing · PDF pp. 58–61

IV. The conditional waiver

If the December auction produces a third qualifying shortfall, PJM asks to waive the existing investigation and backstop-auction filing requirements. It argues the planned procurement addresses the shortage. This is a request for a one-time waiver.

This one-time prospective waiver satisfies all four of the Commission’s waiver criteria PDF p. 60

Consultation · PDF p. 61

V. Stakeholder consultation

PJM reports consulting its Members Committee and transmission owners on October 22. Its account of consultation is not a stakeholder endorsement of the filing.

PJM consulted the Members Committee and the Transmission Owners on October 22, 2026 PDF p. 61

Reliability and timing · PDF pp. 61–63

VI. A March start and a longer offer window

PJM requests a January 29 ruling and early effectiveness for the distributor reporting rule; most provisions would take effect February 28. PJM proposes starting March 1 with a four-week offer window. These are proposed dates.

extend the RBP Sell Offer window by one week so that it will be open for a total of four weeks PDF p. 62

Filing record · PDF pp. 64–66

VII–X. Filed evidence and relief requested

A marked tariff, a clean tariff and two affidavits accompany the letter. Kelly supports the credit calculations; Croop explains the procurement target and allocation. PJM asks FERC to accept the revisions.

PJM respectfully requests that the Commission accept the proposed Tariff revisions PDF p. 66
