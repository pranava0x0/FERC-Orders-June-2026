ANSWER OF AMERICAN MUNICIPAL POWER, INC. 
IN OPPOSITION TO MOTION FOR STAY AND  
SETTLEMENT JUDGE PROCEDURES 
 
Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission (“Commission” or “FERC”), American Municipal Power, Inc. 
(“AMP”) hereby submits its answer in opposition to the Joint Parties’ April 22, 2025 motion 
to stay the above-captioned proceedings and establish settlement judge procedures.1  
The Commission should deny the Joint Motion. The Joint Parties have not 
established the basis for their proposed settlement discussions. Contrary to Joint Parties’ 
assertions, the record supports a finding that the Open Access Transmission Tariff 
(“Tariff”) of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) remains just and reasonable and not 
unduly discriminatory or preferential, and, thus, there is no reason to convene settlement 
judge proceedings to develop a replacement rate. The Commission should instead 
confirm the justness and reasonableness of the existing Tariff, while providing guidance

I. 
BACKGROUND 
In its February 20, 2025 order in the above-captioned proceedings,3 the 
Commission preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff “appears to be unjust and 
unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential because it does not contain 
provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions 
of service that apply to co-location arrangements.”4 The Commission accordingly initiated 
a new proceeding under section 206 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”) (“Show Cause 
Proceeding”) and directed PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners: (1) to show cause as 
to why the PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or 
preferential; or (2) explain what Tariff changes “would remedy the Commission’s identified

Based only on the PJM and Transmission Owner submissions, Joint Parties now 
ask the Commission to “conclusively find the PJM Tariff to be unjust, unreasonable and 
unduly discriminatory and preferential.”12 Assuming such a finding, the Joint Parties argue 
that the Commission “should defer to the parties in this proceeding to develop a new

II. 
ANSWER 
The Commission should deny the Joint Motion. The Joint Parties have neither 
supported their assertion that the PJM Tariff is unjust and unreasonable or unduly 
discriminatory or preferential, nor shown that settlement judge procedures would be an 
effective way to establish a replacement rate if the Commission were to find deficiencies 
in the existing Tariff. 
A. 
The record supports a finding that the PJM Tariff remains just and 
reasonable. 
The Joint Parties’ request for a stay and settlement judge procedures is premised 
on the assumption that the Commission will make a “conclusive” finding that the PJM 
Tariff is unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory and preferential.15 The record,

however, supports the opposite conclusion. Both PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners 
explained in their respective answers to the Show Cause Order that the existing PJM

Given the detailed answers filed by PJM and the Indicated PJM Transmission 
Owners defending the justness and reasonableness of the Tariff, there is no validity 
whatsoever to the Joint Parties’ assertion that “no party responding to the Co-Location 
Show Cause Order has demonstrated that the PJM Tariff is just, reasonable and not 
unduly discriminatory or preferential, or even made a meaningful effort to do so.”17 PJM 
and the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners each made just such a demonstration in 
response to the Show Cause Order, and the Joint Parties offer no meaningful rebuttal; 
they simply make a number of conclusory assertions reflecting their own positions on the 
Tariff’s alleged lack of clarity regarding co-location agreements.18 
Because the record supports a finding that the PJM Tariff remains just and 
reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential, there is no reason to convene 
settlement judge proceedings (or any other process) to develop a replacement rate under 
FPA section 206 as proposed by the Joint Parties.19

B. 
The Commission should provide guidance on application of the 
existing Tariff to co-located load arrangements. 
Although the existing PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable, AMP noted in its 
response that additional clarification and guidance concerning the treatment of co-located 
load could be beneficial.20 Such guidance should clarify that load co-located behind the 
interconnection point of an adjacent generator in PJM must be considered Network Load 
(or load taking Point-to-Point Transmission Service). Further, the Commission should 
confirm that the existing PJM Tariff provisions addressing netting of load served by BTMG 
and Non-Retail BTMG remain just and reasonable, which would provide assurances to 
entities that have relied on the longstanding provisions allowing for netting of Network 
Load served by BTMG and Non-Retail BTMG to support investment in numerous small 
generating facilities that contribute significantly to maintaining resource adequacy in PJM. 
The Commission can provide this guidance based on the extensive paper record that has 
already been developed in these dockets. 
The Commission could also provide guidance regarding possible Tariff changes 
that PJM could propose to clarify the treatment of co-located load arrangements, 
particularly given the concerns expressed by the Joint Parties about the lack of such 
clarity. The Indicated PJM Transmission Owners, for example, suggested a reasonable 
definition of “Co-located Load” that would appropriately clarify how co-located 
arrangements fit within the existing PJM Tariff framework.21 Thus, instead of the 
unwarranted settlement judge procedures proposed by the Joint Parties, the Commission

C. 
Even if the Commission concludes that the Tariff is unjust and 
unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential, it should not 
convene settlement judge procedures. 
Even if the Commission were to conclude that the existing PJM Tariff is unjust and 
unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential, it should not stay these 
proceedings or convene a settlement judge process as proposed by the Joint Parties. 
Rather, given the importance of the issues relating to co-located arrangements and the 
need for regulatory certainty in the PJM region on these issues, the Commission should, 
based on the paper record developed in these dockets, provide clear instruction on the 
design of a replacement rate to address any deficiencies the Commission may identify in 
the PJM Tariff.  
Simply “defer[ring] to the parties in this proceeding to develop a new replacement 
rate”22 would unnecessarily delay resolution of the matter while participants engage in an 
additional round of discussions on issues that they have thus far been unable to resolve 
without clear Commission guidance. As PJM has noted, for example, the PJM guidance 
document on co-located load arrangements – which PJM has now rescinded23 – was only 
“issued by PJM as an initial attempt to provide limited guidance after over a year’s worth 
of unfruitful stakeholder discussions.”24 There is no reason to believe that participants 
would now be able to reach consensus on these issues in ninety days. While a predicate

There would also be practical challenges associated with a settlement judge 
process. Numerous parties that are not PJM members and that do not customarily 
participate in PJM-related proceedings before the Commission have intervened in the 
Show Cause Proceeding. These participants would presumably be entitled to participate 
in any settlement conferences, and would have an opportunity to comment on any 
proposed settlement.26 These parties may have valid and legitimate interests in the Show 
Cause Proceeding that are not necessarily related to particular co-located load provisions 
in the PJM Tariff. Indeed, the Show Cause Order is not exclusively focused on the PJM 
Tariff. The Commission, for example, also solicited input from interested parties on 
jurisdictional issues related to co-located load arrangements generally.27 Non-PJM 
parties interested in general Commission guidance on jurisdictional and other generic 
issues might have limited incentive to support a non-precedential settlement limited to the 
PJM Tariff, all of which would likely complicate settlement discussions. 
Further, as PJM observed in opposing Constellation’s similar request for 
settlement judge procedures in consolidated Docket No. EL25-20, addressing these 
important co-located load issues – including issues that may impact stakeholders beyond
