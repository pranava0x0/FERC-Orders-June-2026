II. ANSWER
A. The Motion Is Inconsistent with the Commission’s Regional Approach
As stated in the SPP Show Cause Order, the “[i]ndividual show cause proceedings will allow the Commission to ensure that solutions to the problems the Secretary [of the Department of Energy] identified are tailored to the specific, varied circumstances and market constructs of each region.”10 The Commission goes on to state that the Show Cause Orders “reveals the many ways in which those orders are customized for each market’s unique circumstances and progress (or lack thereof) towards serving large load…and “leave room for each market to tailor operational requirements for large loads that are particular to their region [] and otherwise account for incumbent regional differences on topics such as cost allocation and cost transparency, study processes, and network upgrade procedures..”11 The Commission found that “[p]roceeding via [the Show Cause Order] allow the markets (and their respective transmission owners) to explain, in the first instance, how to address the Commission’s concerns.” 12

B. The Motion is Premature
The Commission has only recently instituted these proceedings. Pursuant to the SPP Show Cause Order, SPP is currently evaluating the issues identified by the Commission and preparing its response. The Commission specifically afforded SPP the opportunity to utilize its stakeholder process to develop potential tariff revisions and, if appropriate, request additional time to do so. That process has not yet run its course. Because no proposed tariff revisions have been filed and no administrative record has been developed, there is presently no concrete controversy suitable for settlement. Settlement procedures are designed to facilitate resolution of identified disputes—not to substitute for the initial development of proposals that may ultimately become the subject of Commission review. Granting the Motion at this early stage would invert the ordinary sequence contemplated by Sections 205 and 206 of the FPA by requiring settlement discussions before parties have articulated the proposals that might ultimately require resolution. Finally, Although Rules 601 and 603 of the Commission’s Rules of Practices and Procedure authorize the Commission to convene settlement procedures or appoint a settlement judge, those rules are discretionary rather than mandatory. The Motion identifies no extraordinary circumstance warranting departure from the Commission's recently established procedural framework.
