I. BACKGROUND AND INTRODUCTION
Under the existing PJM Tariff, load served by all Behind the Meter Generation (“BTMG”), and more particularly, Non-Retail BTMG is, subject to certain requirements and limitations, eligible to be netted from daily load under section 34.2 of the PJM Tariff for purposes of calculating charges for Network Integration Transmission Service and other PJM charges.4 In the December 18 Order, the Commission found PJM’s existing

II. PROTEST
A. PJM’s proposed definition of Non-Retail BTMG should be rejected
because it is unnecessary and could exclude otherwise eligible
generation facilities from Co-Located Load configurations.
The PJM Filing proposes the following revised Tariff definition of Non-Retail BTMG (new language underlined):

“Non-Retail Behind The Meter Generation” shall mean Behind the
Meter Generation that is used by municipal electric systems, electric cooperatives, or electric distribution companies to serve load. Any

facility that qualifies as a Non-Retail Behind The Meter Generation
shall be excluded from Co-Located Load configurations.14
As a threshold matter, there is no evident reason to modify the definition of Non- Retail BTMG as proposed by PJM. The PJM transmittal letter is silent as to why the underlined language above has been added to the definition of Non-Retail BTMG, or why such language is needed to comply with the directives in the December 18 Order.15

Indeed, the Commission was clear that that it was “not direct[ing] PJM to change its rules for non-retail BTMG.”16 Accordingly, the proposed revisions to the definition of Non-Retail BTMG should be rejected as outside the scope of the compliance filing.17

Even if PJM’s proposed changes to the definition of Non-Retail BTMG are deemed to fall within the scope of its compliance obligations, the revisions are at least ambiguous and could produce a result inconsistent with PJM’s stated intent and the Commission’s directives. The problem with PJM’s proposed definition of Non-Retail BTMG arises principally from the use of the word “qualifies” in the newly added text. For practical purposes, generation capacity that is designated as Non-Retail BTMG for load netting purposes under revised section 34.3 of the Tariff and is actually utilizing such netting provisions and therefore “excluded from Co-Located Load configurations” in the event and to the extent that the generator output is netted against load, as PJM apparently intends to convey through the new definitional text, may not be denied access to relevant usable transmission service. But by triggering the exclusion from Co-Located Load configurations based on whether generation merely “qualifies” as Non-Retail BTMG (rather than focusing on whether it is actually engaged in netting and limiting the preclusion to the extent of that netting), the proposed definition sweeps too broadly. Currently, generation “qualifies” as Non-Retail BTMG under the Tariff if it: (1) meets the definition of BTMG; and (2) is “used by municipal electric systems, electric

products the Commission has directed in the [December 18 Order].” PJM Filing, Transmittal Letter at
18. As discussed herein, however, PJM’s proposed revised definition of Non-Retail BTMG would reduce the clarity of options available to generation owners and developers considering pathways to serve co- located load under the Tariff.

It is possible that owners of generation resources including municipal electric systems, electric cooperatives, or electric distribution companies (“EDCs”) with generator capacity that is not netted against load (i.e., are not serving in whole or in part as Non- Retail BTMG) may seek to serve Co-Located Load, to the extent such resources are otherwise eligible to do so under the PJM Tariff. Significantly, Part VIII, Subpart E, section 415 of the currently effective Tariff allows Non-Retail BTMG resources to be partially treated as Non-Retail BTMG and also to obtain Capacity Resource or Energy Resource status in PJM’s markets. Given that, there does not seem to be a valid rationale for excluding these resources from a Co-Located Load configuration.

But to the extent that such resources might still be said to “qualify” as Non-Retail BTMG under the Tariff, these resources could be needlessly excluded from Co-Located Load arrangements under PJM’s proposed revisions to the Non-Retail BTMG definition. PJM identifies no rationale for excluding otherwise eligible non-netting generation owned by municipal electric systems, electric cooperatives, or EDCs from participation in Co- Located Load configurations simply because they might be deemed to “qualify” as Non- Retail BTMG. Such an (apparently unintentional) exclusion would frustrate the objective of making the three proposed new transmission service options available in these

circumstances to serve Co-Located Load, thereby restricting Co-Located Load from accessing service from this broad set of PJM generators.

Finally, it is not clear that PJM’s proposed exclusionary language is needed in principle. To the extent that all or a portion of a municipal, cooperative, or EDC generator is engaged in netting under the existing Non-Retail BTMG Tariff provisions, the netted portion of its output necessarily will not need access to one of the three newly proposed types of transmission service. Furthermore, PJM has not explained on the record why it would be reasonable to prohibit, for example, a municipal generating unit from serving a portion of a Co-Located Load, while the balance of that load is served from other generating resources or market purchases that are supported by any of the three new types of transmission service. Absent PJM’s proposed modification of the Non-Retail BTMG definition, it seems that the balance of PJM’s proposal, coupled with existing Tariff provisions, would permit such a supply combination. Thus, without a rational explanation in the record, the Commission should reject PJM’s revisions to the Non-Retail BTMG definition as unduly discriminatory and inconsistent with the Commission’s directives in the December 18 Order.
