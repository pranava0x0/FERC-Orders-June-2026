II. BACKGROUND
8
Q.
What is the background for this proceeding?
9
A. [Blazunas, Schafer, and Hammer] On December 18, 2025, the Commission issued its 10

order in PJM Interconnection, L.L.C., 193 FERC ¶ 61,217, at P 193 (2025) (“Order”). In 11

the Order, the Commission found that PJM’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) 12

“is unjust and unreasonable because it does not contain provisions addressing with 13

sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to 14

generators serving Co-Located Load and Eligible Customers taking transmission service 15

on behalf of Co-Located Load.”1 Specifically, the Commission found PJM’s Tariff “unjust 16

and unreasonable because it does not include types of transmission service that reflect 17

Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of Co-Located Loads that are willing and able 18

to limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.”2 19

The Commission ordered PJM to offer three new forms of transmission service 20

under its Tariff, and identified these three new forms of transmission service as: (i) Firm 21

IV. FINANCIAL PENALTY PROPOSAL
4
Q.
Should unreserved use penalties apply to Eligible Customers taking Firm Contract 5

Demand Service, Non-Firm Contract Demand Service, or Interim NITS on behalf of 6

Co-Located Load?
7
A. [Hammer] Yes. Robust penalty provisions are a necessary component of the new services 8

required by the Commission. First, and most importantly, any penalties must be sufficient 9

to meaningfully deter Co-Located Load customers from exceeding their contract demand 10

or Interim NITS limits and making unreserved use of the transmission system. Because 11

network upgrades are planned and built only to accommodate load covered by Firm 12

Contract Demand Service or NITS, it is crucial that Co-Located Loads respect their 13

contractual limits, including any related curtailment or other instructions from PJM. This 14

concern is particularly acute because many of these arrangements will likely involve 15

substantial amounts of load—hundreds, if not thousands of MW—being served by on-site 16

generation but still connected to the grid. If these limits are not respected, the reliability 17

consequences to the system—and to existing customers that rely on the reliable operation 18

of that system—could be severe.
19
Q.
Has PJM proposed any penalties?
20
A. [Hammer] Yes. PJM’s Initial Brief includes a table summarizing PJM’s proposed 21

operational penalties applicable to Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of Co- 22

Q.
Do the PJM Transmission Owners propose that the Commission adopt a separate 3

“Failure-to-Curtail” Financial Penalty?
4
A. [Hammer] No. The PJM Transmission Owners are not proposing that the Commission 5

adopt a separate “failure‑to‑curtail” financial penalty. Instead, failure to curtail should be 6

treated as a form of unreserved use and addressed through a single, unified unreserved use 7

financial penalty framework. Failure to curtail inherently results in unauthorized use of the 8

transmission system. As such, it is part of unreserved use and does not warrant a distinct 9

penalty mechanism. However, as indicated above, the financial penalties proposed here 10

will account for any failure to curtail by factoring any curtailments directed by PJM into 11

the determination of an Eligible Customer’s authorized transmission service limits. 12

Q.
Please describe the PJM Transmission Owners’ proposed financial penalties for 13

unreserved use.
14
A. [Hammer] The PJM Transmission Owners propose a financial penalty charge structure 15

for unreserved use with two components: (1) a base penalty charge, which applies to each 16

individual hour in which the reserved transmission service limit is exceeded, and (2) a 17

penalty ratchet, which applies when such exceedances occur repeatedly.
18
Q.
What is the base penalty charge?
19
A. [Hammer] As described in more detail below, when an Eligible Customer taking service 20

on behalf of Co-Located Load exceeds its reserved transmission service limit, the base 21

penalty charge will be assessed for any hour in which at least one five-minute interval 22

occurs during which a customer’s telemetered load at the Point of Delivery exceeds its 23

reserved transmission service limit. This five-minute interval is defined as an Exceedance 1

apply to each Exceedance Hour.
3
Q.
What is the penalty rachet?
4
A. [Hammer] As described in more detail below, the penalty rachet applies when multiple 5

Exceedance Hours occur during a particular time period. It is designed to discourage 6

recurring reliance on unreserved transmission capacity. Under the penalty rachet 7

framework, the penalty charges escalate as the frequency of unreserved transmission use 8

increases. Specifically, if more than one Exceedance Hour occurs on the same calendar 9

day, an additional daily penalty applies. If exceedances occur on multiple calendar days 10

within the same calendar week, an additional weekly penalty applies. Likewise, if 11

exceedances occur in multiple calendar weeks within the same month, an additional 12

monthly penalty applies, and if exceedances occur in multiple months within the same year, 13

an additional yearly penalty applies. By progressively increasing the economic 14

consequences of repeated penalties, the penalty ratchet reinforces the expectation that 15

Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of Co-Located Load customers manage their 16

load in a manner consistent with their reserved transmission service.

Q.
How is the reserved transmission service limit determined?
6
A. [Hammer] For an Eligible Customer taking only Firm Contract Demand Service, the 7

reserved transmission service limit will equal the Firm Contract Demand minus any 8

curtailed MW.  For an Eligible Customer taking only Non-Firm Contract Demand Service, 9

the reserved transmission service limit will equal the approved Non-Firm Contract Demand 10

minus any curtailed MW. For an Eligible Customer taking Firm Contract Demand Service 11

and Non-Firm Contract Demand Service, the reserved transmission service limit will equal 12

the Firm Contract Demand plus the approved Non-Firm Contract Demand minus any 13

curtailed MW.  For an Eligible Customer taking Interim NITS, the reserved transmission 14

service limit will equal the Interim NITS demand minus any curtailed MW. 15

A. [Hammer] The PJM Transmission Owners’ also propose a penalty ratchet to address 13

Q.
What is the minimum system method?
5
A. [Schafer] The minimum system method is a cost allocation approach sometimes used in 6

electric distribution cost of service studies to distinguish between customer-related and 7

demand-related components of a portion of distribution plant investment. The method is 8

based on the principle that a portion of the distribution system must be constructed to serve 9

customers regardless of their level of demand, while additional investment is driven by the 10

magnitude of load served.
11
Under this method, a “minimum system” is defined as the lowest-cost configuration 12

of the distribution system that would be required to provide basic service to customers, 13

assuming minimal load requirements. This minimum system typically reflects the facilities 14

necessary to connect customers to the grid and ensure system operability, independent of 15

higher demand levels. The costs associated with this minimum system are classified as 16

customer-related, as they are incurred to serve customers regardless of their usage. 17

The customer-related portion of distribution plant investment identified through the 18

minimum system method is typically allocated on the basis of the number of customers in 19

each class, reflecting the premise that these costs are driven by customer count rather than 20

by demand or energy use.
