I.
BACKGROUND 
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued a show cause order under section 206 of the FPA 
to PJM, together with its transmission owners, directing PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners 
to justify or reform the tariff provisions governing how large loads connect to and receive service 
from the transmission system.4
The Show Cause Order preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff appeared to be unjust, 
unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential because of a lack of sufficiently clear and 
consistent provisions to address service for large loads and related configurations.5 The 
Commission therefore directed PJM and its transmission owners, within 60 days, to show cause 
why the PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable without provisions addressing:  (1) the application 
process, study procedures, and ongoing operational requirements for Eligible Customers seeking 
transmission service on behalf of large loads; (2) additional transparency concerning Network 
Upgrade costs for such service, together with the absence of a pro forma cost recovery agreement 
and a mechanism to ensure related payments are properly credited to transmission owners’ revenue 
requirements; (3) transmission service options tailored to flexible large loads willing and able to 
limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions; and (4) rates, terms, and 
conditions applicable to interconnection customers serving “electrically proximate large load” or

A.
Development of a Pro Forma Cost Recovery Agreement 
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found that PJM’s Tariff appears 
to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost 
shifting among transmission customers.14  The Commission indicated it was concerned about the 
apparent lack of a pro forma cost recovery agreement to help ensure that Eligible Customers taking 
transmission service on behalf of large loads bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs 
incurred to provide transmission service, including the cost of Network Upgrades.15 The 
Commission noted that such an agreement would require “the Eligible Customer to make a 
minimum contribution toward the transmission owner’s transmission revenue requirement, if the 
Eligible Customer ultimately takes less transmission service than anticipated because the large 
load does not materialize as planned or is not developed at all.”16  The Commission stated its 
finding is not intended to alter or call into question existing regional cost allocation methods for 
Network Upgrades, but instead is focused on helping to prevent cost shifting among transmission 
customers.17

B.
Rate Design Related to Transmission Services for Large Loads 
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff appears 
to be unjust and unreasonable because it does not include transmission services that reflect Eligible 
Customers taking transmission service on behalf of “flexible large loads” that are willing and able 
to limit their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.21 The Commission 
preliminarily found that new transmission services should be available to “flexible large loads”  
willing and able to limit their withdrawals, specifically the three new services it required for co-
located loads:  (1) Interim Network Integration Transmission Service, (2) Firm Contract Demand, 
and (3) Non-Firm Contract Demand.22  The Commission also preliminarily found that the PJM 
Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it does not contain provisions allowing a 
generator interconnection customer to seek interconnection service(s) that reflect the operational 
dynamics of serving either:  (1) an “electrically proximate large load,” or (2) a co-located load.23
Because the PJM Transmission Owners have the FPA section 205 filing rights with respect 
to regional rates and rate design under the PJM Tariff, the PJM Transmission Owners’ section 205 
filing(s) made in response to the Show Cause Order will include the rate-related provisions for any 
new transmission services that PJM offers to “flexible large load,” as well as any services 
applicable to “electrically proximate large load.”  The PJM Transmission Owners will also work 
to coordinate any proposal with the rate design proposals at issue in the PJM Co-Located Load 
proceeding24 for these new services, subject to the Commission’s directives on compliance set

There are two workstreams here relevant to the PJM Transmission Owners.  First, the PJM

Network Upgrades necessary to accommodate the interconnection of new large loads to the 
transmission system.  This effort will involve working through a number of important issues, 
including ensuring appropriate confidentiality protections, compliance with anti-trust laws, and 
updating, where applicable, websites and databases to house such information.28

modifications they will need to include in their own FPA section 205 filing(s) relating to

III.
REQUEST FOR EXPEDITED CONSIDERATION AND SHORTENED 
COMMENT PERIOD 
The PJM Transmission Owners respectfully request expedited consideration of this motion 
for abeyance and request that the Commission issue an order granting the motion no later than 
August 10, 2026.  Expedited consideration and the issuance of order by that date will provide the 
PJM Transmission Owners sufficient assurance that they will have adequate time to develop and 
submit one or more FPA section 205 filings necessary to implement the rates, terms, and conditions 
necessary to address the Show Cause Order, as discussed above.  Expedited consideration will also 
allow the PJM Transmission Owners to focus their efforts on developing their FPA section 205 
filing(s) rather than spending critical time preparing separate responses to the Show Cause Order.  
Consistent with this request, the PJM Transmission Owners also seek a shortened comment period 
on this motion for abeyance.  Specifically, the PJM Transmission Owners request that the 
Commission establish a comment period of five (5) days.  Good cause exists to grant a shortened 
comment period for the reasons set forth above.  Moreover, no party will be prejudiced as a result 
of granting a five (5) day comment period, as the additional time and certainty afforded by an 
expedited order will accrue to the benefit of stakeholders by providing the PJM Transmission 
Owners sufficient time to solicit and consider stakeholder feedback on their proposals.
