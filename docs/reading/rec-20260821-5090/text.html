II. LIMITED PROTEST

A. The TO Exit Provisions Concern Transmission Owner Rights and Obligations
and Therefore Belong in the CTOA.
The CTOA is the bilateral contract establishing the rights, obligations, and commitments between PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners.  Indeed, the contract explicitly codifies as one of its core purposes “[to] establish certain rights and obligations that will apply to the Parties and PJM.”8  By contrast, Attachment DD to the PJM Tariff is not a bilateral contract establishing the rights, obligations, and commitments between PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners.  It is a Tariff9 of general applicability that contains the rules of PJM’s

B. The TO Exit Provisions Raise Standards of Conduct Concerns, Allocate
Responsibility for Capacity Commitments in a Manner That Warrants Further
Scrutiny, and Do Not Account for Differences Among Transmission Owners.
By their terms, the TO Exit Provisions apply to “any Transmission Owner seeking to
exit PJM,” (emphasis added) and, as described above in Section II.A, seek to impose residual commitments on “any” exiting Transmission Owner that are predicated on the continuing obligation of an exiting Transmission Owner to serve load within its service territory.14 There are (at least) two significant flaws with this proposal.  First, the proposal ignores the functional separation that must exist within a Transmission Owner’s organization.  For those Transmission Owners that will also own RBP Resources, it is unclear how compliance with the TO Exit Provisions could be effectuated in the absence of intimate coordination

The Commission has explained that an RTO has “[an] obligation to set forth its Commission- jurisdictional rates clearly and specifically in its tariff.”24  The need to set forth rates and charges “clearly and specifically” is essential for the Commission’s own FPA section 205(c) analysis, as “[a] minimum level of specificity within the tariff is necessary for the Commission to determine whether the charges that result are just and reasonable.”25  It is also important in fulfilling the fundamental purpose of FPA section 205(c) to provide notice to customers, as the requirement “allows [] customers the opportunity to assess for themselves whether they were properly charged.”26

The TO Exit Provisions in proposed Section 18.17 fall short of this statutory requirement and the Commission’s implementing regulations in 18 C.F.R. 35.1(a).  For example, under the proposed language, a PJM Transmission Owner is required to pay “all costs for RBP Resources to pseudo-tie into the PJM Region.”  The proposal does not define what constitutes “all costs” or identify a limiting principle for determining when those costs have been paid, creating uncertainty regarding a Transmission Owner’s obligations under the provision.

Similar questions arise with respect to the requirements that a Transmission Owner “pay costs associated with the remaining term of the RBP Resource” and “pay all forward obligations on an accelerated basis.”  While PJM’s proposed language for these requirements

III. REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION
PPL Electric requests that PJM provide additional clarity in its responsive pleading regarding its approach to a specific credit collateral contingency.  In the event that PJM does not provide the requested clarity, PPL Electric respectfully requests that the Commission articulate its expectations regarding this contingency, and provide any additional guidance it deems appropriate.

Regarding the collateral requirements for Load Serving Entities, PJM states that “[b]uyers of RBP UCAP MW, i.e., LSEs that have been allocated cost responsibility for RBP Charges . . . will be required to post RBP Collateral to mitigate the risk of potential defaults from non-payment of RBP Charges, while those LSEs not allocated cost responsibility generally will not have any credit requirement.”27   To this end, PJM explains that “the RPM

However, PJM then explains that “for LSEs serving load in an Electric Distributor zone/area in which no RBOs have been reported, an LSE’s ‘allocated RBP UCAP MW shall equal the Load Serving Entity’s pro-rata share of the Daily Unforced Capacity Obligation for the zone/area as determined in RAA, Schedule 8.’”29  PPL Electric understands that if there are no RBOs reported to PJM for a zone with RBP UCAP MW assigned, or if there are “stranded” RBOs (because of how state or local regulatory authorities determine they should be assigned) then the credit requirements will move to all Load Serving Entities in a zone based on Peak Load Contributions reported to PJM.  Presumably, once credit requirements are assigned based on Peak Load Contributions, RBP charges will also flow to those Load Serving Entities for that Delivery Year, and possibly beyond.

PPL Electric is concerned that this scenario could result in LSEs that do not serve large loads receiving these obligations, thereby having nowhere to assign them except to other (non- large load) customers.  This seems inconsistent with the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.30    PPL Electric requests that PJM provide guidance on what would happen if the additional Load Serving Entities in the zone are unable to post collateral and default under this contingency.
