MOTION FOR LEAVE AND ANSWER OF 
CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC 
Constellation Energy Generation, LLC (“Constellation”) moves for leave and submits this 
answer1 in support of the Commission’s preliminary finding that the PJM Tariff is unjust and 
unreasonable and unduly discriminatory and preferential2 because, as recent filings plainly show, 
no one knows the rules for the co-location of large loads and generation.  That lack of clarity is 
allowing certain transmission owners to erect discriminatory roadblocks to safe and reliable 
interconnection configurations that would reduce the need for costly transmission upgrades and 
preserve each state’s jurisdiction to decide how to regulate sales to end-use customers.  The need 
for clarity in the tariff cannot wait any longer.  President Trump has emphasized that the United 
States’ competitiveness with other nations depends on the development of Artificial Intelligence 
(“AI”) infrastructure and that co-location enables such development “on a very rapid basis.”3

THE RECORD CONCLUSIVELY DEMONSTRATES THAT THE PJM TARIFF 
IS UNJUST AND UNREASONABLE 
This is “a show cause proceeding pursuant to section 206” of the Federal Power Act 
(“FPA”)4 which includes “a two-step procedure that requires … an explicit finding that the existing 
rate is unlawful before setting a new rate.”5  Thus, as a threshold matter, the Commission must 
first “determine whether an existing [rule] is ‘unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or 
preferential….’”6  The Commission initiated this proceeding with a preliminary finding under 
section 206 of the FPA that 
the existing PJM Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable or unduly 
discriminatory or preferential because it does not contain provisions addressing 
with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that 
apply to co-location arrangements.  The absence of such provisions may leave 
entities unable to determine what steps they can or must take to effectuate co-
location arrangements of various configurations and how to do so in a manner that 
is just and reasonable.7  
 
The approximately fifty filings submitted in response to the Show Cause Order conclusively 
demonstrate the correctness of the Commission’s preliminary finding.

THE RECORD SUPPORTS THE COMMISSION’S ADOPTION OF A 
REPLACEMENT RATE OF CO-LOCATION CONFIGURATION OPTIONS 
With the step one showing made under section 206 of the FPA, the Commission must move 
to step two and fix a replacement rate.  Under the plain terms of the FPA, the Commission has no 
discretion to do otherwise.  The record in this proceeding already does the heavy lifting and 
provides substantial evidence and support for adopting new “generator-contingent” options (PJM 
Options 4 and 5) and a provisional “interruptible” option (PJM Option 7), as well as clarifying the 
Tariff to allow a “firm” co-location configuration based on net generation and load at a point of 
interconnection (PJM Option 2, which essentially would clarify that the same rules for PJM Option 
3 should be available at the co-located load and generator’s election).  With appropriate rate 
treatment as demonstrated in the record, these three categories of potential co-location 
configurations collectively provide a just and reasonable rate that maximizes efficient use of 
existing energy infrastructure and minimizes expansive transmission upgrades and other costs.  
Each type of configuration also appropriately allocates costs commensurate with the level of 
transmission service being used by the load.  Constellation introduced extensive record support for 
the configurations and the proposed rate treatment for each option, and Vistra Corp. and Enchanted 
Rock, LLC, also provided expert support for the generator-contingent configuration.40   
While many of the proponents of a NITS-only approach—including the Indicated 
Transmission Owners—chose not to put forward any expert evidence in response to the Show 
Cause Order or even meaningfully address PJM’s eight options in their March submittals, their 
steadfast refusal to engage on the key issues, despite the overwhelming record evidence identifying
