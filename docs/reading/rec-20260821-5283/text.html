III. BACKGROUND
To address the near-term reliability concerns exposed by PJM’s 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction (“BRA”), which cleared short of PJM’s target reserve margin by 6,831.3 MW at the Commission-approved price cap of $325/MW-day, PJM proposes to conduct a one-time RBP for procuring new capacity from September 30, 2026 to October 21, 2026.6  The proposed rules governing the RBP are set forth in PJM’s Tariff and PJM’s RAA and are designed to allow PJM to procure the capacity needed to meet the shortfall from the 2028/2029 BRA.7  The January 16, 2026 National Energy Dominance Council/PJM Governors’ Principles urged PJM to procure new capacity through a backstop auction commencing no later than September 2026, provide 15-year price certainty for new capacity resources, and allocate the costs of that capacity to LSEs with new data centers that have not self-procured new capacity or agreed to be curtailable.8  PJM explains that the initial RBP procurement target (“Initial Target”) for the RBP will be set at 6,831.3 MW, subject to reduction after PJM has accounted for certain self-supplied new capacity and opt-outs.9 Noting that PJM members have expressed a strong preference for bringing new capacity to the PJM system voluntarily rather than contracting through PJM, PJM has facilitated a bilateral matching process to connect new capacity with new load. Unreasonable

3. Any Imposed Exit Terms Should Be Consistent with Cost Causation
And Should be Capable of Determination in Advance of Withdrawal
In addition to the issues addressed above, the RBP Proposal reflects a disconnect between a departing load’s responsibility for RBP Charges upon withdrawal and PJM’s proposed exit charges without explanation regarding how the proposed exit fee is tied to the departing load’s initial allocation of RBP Charges.  The starting point for this analysis is PJM’s decision to propose an RBP process with no locational deliverability requirement.55  The RBP Proposal expressly assumes that new generation anywhere in PJM is deliverable to any load in PJM.  Under its proposal, PJM could acquire all 6,831.3 MW of new supply in New Jersey, or North Carolina, or even suburban Chicago.  More to the point for AEP, PJM could decide to acquire all—or at least a disproportionate share of — the new capacity in Ohio.  This is a stark departure from the RPM’s design, which does include locational elements, and relies on Capacity Emergency Transfer Objectives (“CETO”) and Capacity Emergency Transfer Limits (“CETL”) modeling to ensure that cleared capacity can address the reliability needs of constrained areas.56  As a result, customers may be required to fund RBP Resources that are not deliverable to their zone, while resources that are necessary to address local reliability needs may not be procured at all. Indeed, under PJM’s proposal, a departing Transmission Owner would essentially have to indemnify each RBP Resource in its zone for the remainder of its RBP capacity commitment, even though—due to the lack of a locational element in the RBP—said RBP Resources may not be at all tied to that Transmission Owner.

Based on the exclusion of locational parameters from the RBP’s design, the location of selected RBP Resources may bear little relationship to the RBP Charges allocated to any particular zone/area.  Thus, if PJM procures a substantial amount of RBP capacity in one zone/area, the Transmission Owner for that zone/area could face exit-related obligations materially greater than those imposed by the RBP Charges otherwise allocated to that zone/area.  PJM’s potentially punitive proposal threatens to place an undue burden on departing Transmission Owners solely by virtue of where RBP Resources are located.  This outcome would create an undue barrier to a Transmission Owner’s ability to exercise its long-held contractual right to leave.

Furthermore, it would potentially impose a disproportionate financial burden and expand the contractual requirements on Transmission Owners seeking to withdraw from PJM. Stated differently, PJM’s proposed exit framework could transform each LSE’s allocated RBP Charge into an unduly burdensome exit fee for a departing Transmission Owner.  The Commission should reject the proposed exit provisions and require PJM to limit a departing Transmission Owner’s liability to the RBP Charges allocated to its zone under PJM’s proposed cost allocation methodology, as accepted by the Commission. At a minimum, any RBP exit fee must be defined through an objective formula that can be calculated using the information known once the RBP results are final.  The cost of exiting an RTO must be clear and transparent so that a Transmission Owner can act in the best interests of its customers.
