ER26-3552-000 · 2026-08-18 · Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.

MISO proposes a zero-injection generator interconnection agreement

MISO files a section 205 transmittal creating the Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement, a 90-day study path for generation serving load at the same substation, as the first of several filings answering the E-8 show cause order.

Asks FERC to: Accept the ZGIA Tariff revisions, with an October 18, 2026 effective date.

Answers the order. MISO's first filing answering the E-8 order on co-location, covering the generator interconnection and study side only. Transmission service, flexible loads and proximate generation are deferred to LARS and later filings.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. MISO says the ZGIA gives expedited review to generation with little transmission impact because it serves load at the same substation.
    The ZGIA proposal will help streamline the MISO generator interconnection process by providing expedited review of the addition of ZG Resources that have little or no impact on the Transmission System because they are limited to serving load at the same substation and voltage level. PDF p. 1
  2. MISO requests an October 18, 2026 effective date.
    MISO requests that the Tariff revisions submitted herewith be made effective October 18, 2026 PDF p. 2
  3. The filing is partial: it is the first in a series and does not cover all E-8 issues.
    MISO notes that this filing is not intended to address all issues raised in the Show Cause Order. PDF p. 2
  4. MISO excludes electrically proximate load from the ZGIA and defers it to the LARS process.
    Accordingly, this filing is limited to addressing generation and load at the same substation and does not attempt to resolve the separate issues presented by generation serving electrically proximate large load. PDF p. 7
  5. The generator cannot inject past the substation, and load and generation are not netted for transmission service or settlement.
    the new generator with ZGIA Interconnection Service cannot inject past the interconnecting substation, and netting of the load and generation for transmission service or market settlement purposes is not allowed PDF p. 8
  6. Neither the ZGIA nor LARS creates new transmission services or addresses flexible large loads.
    neither the ZGIA construct nor LARS proposal include new transmission service products specific to serving co-located or proximate load or address flexible large loads willing to limit transmission service. PDF p. 10
  7. MISO expects to file LARS by November 16, 2026, and has not proposed a Large Load definition here.
    MISO will continue to develop the LARS process through the LLWG and expects to submit the LARS filing by November 16, 2026, or earlier, if feasible. PDF p. 11
  8. A request that meets the criteria gets a 90-day accelerated study.
    If all of these criteria are met, the ZG Interconnection study process affords a 90-day accelerated study process leading to a ZGIA that memorializes the proposed configuration and limited injection. PDF p. 13
  9. MISO requires only an intent to use the full output for at least five years, not a firm commitment.
    MISO therefore only requires that the agreement specify an intent to use the full output of the Generating Facility for at least five years rather than a firm commitment. PDF p. 33

What the document says

Co-location · PDF pp. 10–16

Which generator and load can use the agreement?

The generation and load must use the same connection point or a withdrawal point at the same substation and voltage. The agreement does not buy transmission service for the load.

ZG Interconnection Service is not Transmission Service and does not exempt a Transmission Customer from procuring Transmission Service. PDF p. 12
Filed comments: Connecting load and generation

Capacity accreditation · PDF pp. 16–22

How the proposal treats capacity credits

MISO specified where a load-serving entity could use capacity credits associated with a zero-injection resource. It separately proposed penalties if those resources participate as behind-the-meter generation.

all ZRCs associated with a ZG Resource must be used as part of an LSE’s FRAP or RBDC Opt Out PDF p. 20

Queue eligibility · PDF pp. 27–39

Why the request must be ready on day one

MISO proposed a 90-day study and would bar changes to the request after submission. It argued that changes would disrupt the study timetable for other customers.
