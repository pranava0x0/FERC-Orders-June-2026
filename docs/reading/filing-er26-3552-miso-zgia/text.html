B.

The MISO region is experiencing a significant influx of large load additions—primarily 
from data centers, cryptocurrency miners, and other processes that require larger load 
interconnections.  Large load growth presents a unique challenge because MISO must balance 
multiple objectives simultaneously.  MISO seeks to facilitate economic development and timely, 
cost-effective access to the Transmission System while maintaining reliability and operational 
integrity.   
 
MISO, its member states, and stakeholders are developing multiple initiatives to address 
evolving system conditions and to support efficient interconnection prior to and after the issuance 
of the Show Cause Order.  In recognition of urgent needs to address large load and co-located load 
growth, MISO established a temporary, targeted Expedited Resource Addition Study (“ERAS”) 
process to accelerate the generator interconnection process limited to generators necessary to serve

II. 
OVERVIEW OF ZGIA PROPOSAL AND HOW IT ADDRESSES THE SHOW CAUSE ORDER 
The ZGIA construct allows for generation to connect to the Transmission System without 
injection rights, making it suitable to serve loads, including large loads, at the same substation.  
This proposed construct not only formalizes the existing practice, but it also addresses a discrete 
subset of the concerns identified by the Commission in the Show Cause Order, specifically those 
concerning generator interconnection service and the study of generation serving co-located load.   
More specifically, the ZGIA construct provides a limited, structured option to support large 
load integration through: (i) an interconnection service framework for generation that is supporting 
co-located load; (ii) defined interconnection study processes tailored to zero-injection 
configurations; (iii) potentially reduced network upgrade requirements where appropriate; and (iv) 
clear allocation of operational and planning responsibilities.  The ZGIA construct enables one 
pathway for certain large load configurations to achieve speed to reliable power while maintaining 
system reliability.  The ZGIA framework is not, however, limited to large loads.  While the ZGIA 
is particularly responsive to the needs of a large load, a ZG Associated Load38 does not have to be 
a large load. 
MISO notes that this filing does not include a proposed definition of “Large Load.”  MISO 
is continuing to refine the definition of “Large Load” through the ongoing stakeholder process at 
the LLWG and expects to file the proposed definition of “Large Load” with the Commission in a 
subsequent FPA section 205 filing that is responsive to certain additional concerns raised in the 
Show Cause Order that are not specifically addressed herein.  The ZGIA proposal remains just and 
reasonable without inclusion of a definition of “Large Load” because the ZGIA proposal and the 
identification of ZG Associated Load is open to application for all loads in the MISO region and 
is not limited to only large loads.  In addition, the Show Cause Order explains that it appears that 
it would be reasonable to define a large load as: (1) a new commercial or industrial customer, (2) 
located at a single site behind one or more points of interconnection, (3) that has a peak load of 50 
MW or greater, (4) interconnects to the transmission system at a voltage level of greater than 69 
kV, and (5) is not part of a co-location arrangement.39

stakeholders across differing MISO sectors did not support such a broad reach, citing concerns

generation and load were allowed to be at separate substations, the likelihood of unconstrained

pragmatic and cost-effective approach than expanding one substation bay to house both lead lines

adjacent bay would be electrically indistinguishable from housing both the Generating Facility and

Point of Interconnection and the Load’s Point of Withdrawal to be at the same voltage level of the

Section 3.11.1 includes one exception made necessary by process sequencing.  MISO 
proposes to require, and therefore allow, the ZG Interconnection Customer to move its Point of 
Interconnection to the Transmission System if the ZG Associated Load subsequently moves the 
Point of Withdrawal to not be at the same substation and voltage level as the Point of 
Interconnection.  In such circumstance, the Interconnection Customer would need to move its 
proposed Point of Interconnection to match the move made by the ZG Associated Load. MISO 
proposes to permit this move for several reasons.  First, because the premise of ZG Interconnection 
Service is based on the relative location of the ZG Associated Load and the ZG Resource, it is 
more important from a system impact study perspective to prevent the Point of Interconnection 
and Point of withdrawal from separating than it is to prevent the Point of Interconnection from 
moving if the load’s location moves.77  Second, MISO expects that Interconnection Requests for 
ZG Interconnection Service often will be submitted to MISO before transmission service is 
requested by or on behalf of the ZG Associated Loads.  This sequencing allows MISO to consider 
the executed ZGIA in the transmission service study. Because the Interconnection Request for ZG 
Interconnection Service likely will precede the transmission service study process, it is not 
practical to hold the load to a proposed Point of Withdrawal before it has even submitted its own 
application; doing so would undermine the scoping discussions between the load, its Load Serving 
Entity, and the applicable Transmission Owner that occur during the load study process. This is

9. Section 3.11.2 Evaluation Process and Study Limits  
 
MISO proposes a quarterly study cadence for ZG Interconnection Service Interconnection 
Requests as well as a limit of ten such Interconnection Requests per quarter.  The quarterly cadence 
aligns with the expected duration of the ZG Impact Assessment in order to avoid overlapping 
studies.  MISO determined that 10 such Interconnection Requests is an appropriate quarterly limit 
because it balances opportunities for prompt study with avoiding the reduction of personnel and 
resources needed to process the DPP, which will remain the primary focus of MISO’s 
interconnection process.  ZG Interconnection Service, while valuable, is a niche tool.  MISO does 
not expect to receive more than ten such Interconnection Requests a quarter as the process matures.  
The proposed limit thus is a safeguard in case ZGIAs prove more popular than expected. 
 
In order to avoid undue disruption to the DPP, MISO proposes to allow Interconnection 
Requests in the DPP to withdraw and commence the ZG Interconnection Service study process 
only if they have not yet reached Decision Point II of their DPP cycle. This avoids the risk of 
unscheduled restudy to other DPP Interconnection Requests because Interconnection Customers 
currently can withdraw from the DPP at Decision Point II with such withdrawals being accounted 
for in Phase 3 of the DPP.  For the same reason, MISO does not propose to excuse withdrawing 
Interconnection Requests from the currently-applicable penalty provisions of Section 7.6.2.1.1 and 
7.8 of the DPP.  The prohibition withdrawals after Decision Point II will be enforced through a one 
year waiting period before an Interconnection Request for the same project can be processed for 
ZG Interconnection Service.  MISO believes that this is a sufficient deterrent against late stage 
transfers.
