II. 
BACKGROUND 
The construction and operation of data centers in the U.S. has doubled in the past four to five 
years.6 Developers of data centers, as well as other large loads and fleet electrification facilities, 
have experienced delays, constraints and expense due to the limited physical capacity of both 
transmission and distribution systems around the country. The constraining nature of existing 
infrastructure and the costs to plan and construct new transmission and distribution capability is 
not unique to data centers, new loads or even the electric power industry.7

C.    Transmission Service Requirements 
Please explain whether co-location arrangements are or should be required to take 
Network Integration Transmission Service or Point-to-Point Transmission Service; or whether a 
more tailored new category of transmission service is warranted. 
  
Co-location arrangements should be required to take either Network Integration 
Transmission Service or Point-to-Point Transmission Service. The existing terms and conditions 
of these services are sufficient.  As noted in the Show Cause Order, the Commission has defined 
transmission service as the provision of “electric transmission, network or point-to-point service, 
ancillary services or other methods of electric transmission, or the interconnection with 
jurisdictional transmission facilities.”44 The co-location arrangements all include one or more 
pieces of equipment (conductors, breakers, and or substations) between the co-located load and 
the co-located generator. 45 These are attached to the generator’s own Interconnection Facilities 
(specifically the Interconnection Customer’s Interconnection Facilities) which are 
jurisdictional.46 By the definition cited, there is Commission-defined transmission service 
separate and prior to the initiation of operations and the variable flow of energy to serve the load.

IV. 
Conclusion 
In these comments we explain what it means to be “electrically connected and synchronized 
to the PJM Transmission System when consuming power” and how that implicates the use of 
both ancillary services and transmission service. We illustrate how the fully isolated load idea is 
a fiction, supported only by a private contract that does not establish different physical facts. The 
loads require transmission and ancillary services because the host generators are unable to 
operate without the grid. The host generators are unable to provide all the required capabilities to   
match the variable load. There is evidence enough to conclude there are no fully isolated loads in 
these cases of existing generators proposals. “In order for a co-location arrangement (i.e., the 
generator plus data center) to obtain these services, the grid itself must exist. Ancillary services 
pass through transmission lines, not the air.”48
