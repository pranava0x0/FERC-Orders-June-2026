OPENING STATEMENT OF HON. MIKE LEE, 
U.S. SENATOR FROM UTAH 
The CHAIRMAN. The Committee will come to order. I apologize for 
the late start time. I serve on the Foreign Relations Committee, 
along with a couple of our members, and as we often find ourselves 
in that position, we were in a markup, and had any of us left, they 
would have lost a quorum. So, we ran over here as quickly as we 
could. 
Today, the Committee on Energy and Natural Resources holds a 
hearing to conduct oversight over the Federal Energy Regulatory 
Commission (FERC). Pursuant to the Federal Power Act and the 
Natural Gas Act, the Commission regulates the interstate trans-
mission and wholesale market of electricity and natural gas, as 
well as oil and gas pipelines and hydroelectric power. It ensures 
that electricity rates are just and reasonable, and licenses facilities 
within its statutory jurisdiction. All of these things happen to be 
vital to America’s energy dominance, ensuring both our security 
and prosperity at home and also our ability to advance the inter-
ests of the United States on the world stage by supplying the en-
ergy needs of others. 
Today, we will be hearing from FERC’s five Commissioners to re-
port on their priorities and the Commission’s operations. They are 
Chairman Laura Swett, and Commissioners David Rosner, Lindsay 
See, Judy Chang, and David LaCerte. Following my opening state-
ment today and that of Ranking Member Heinrich, we will hear 
opening statements from each of the Commissioners, followed by 
questions from each of the Senators in attendance. 
There was a time when the Federal Energy Regulatory Commis-
sion operated largely outside the public spotlight. Those days, of 
course, are now over. FERC now sits at the center of some of the 
most important questions facing our country—reliable electricity, 
affordable energy, data centers, manufacturing, and national secu-
rity. Every one of these issues, in its own way, now runs directly 
through FERC. Some estimates suggest that the Commission regu-

confident that if you maintain your current trajectory, you, as our 
Commissioners, will be more than up to that great task.

permitting process for natural gas infrastructure. FERC is modern-

that don’t provide any additional value added on the environmental 
front—and it’s expanding the use of blanket certificates for routine 
upgrades. Every year that we happen to delay needed infrastruc-
ture is another year that Americans have to pay higher prices and

Committee continues its work on permitting reform, I hope we can 
build on the bipartisan momentum that has emerged around many 
of these issues. I look forward to getting perspectives from each one 
of you today on the challenges ahead and to working with each of 
you to ensure that we lower costs for consumers while simulta-
neously improving grid reliability.

OPENING STATEMENT OF HON. MARTIN HEINRICH, 
U.S. SENATOR FROM NEW MEXICO 
Senator HEINRICH. Thank you, Chairman Lee, and special 
thanks to all five of FERC’s Commissioners for attending this hear-
ing today. We very much appreciate having you here. 
The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission has long been an 
important independent agency. It serves the public interest by reg-
ulating electricity, hydroelectric dams, natural gas, and oil in a 
manner that is both fair and impartial from the President and from 
Congress, as required by statute. Now, historically considered by 
some to be a bit of a sleepy agency, FERC faces a very pivotal time 
in our nation’s history right now. Electricity prices are rising, and 
electricity supply has not been able to keep up with demand. Ac-
cording to a recent report, 75 percent of U.S. adults say that they 
have noticed their home energy costs increasing in recent years. 
Some of that blame can be squarely placed on our current Presi-
dent. I recently released a report on how this administration is 
waging a war on cheap, clean energy, and the impact of that is ris-
ing energy costs. The administration is slow-walking installing 73 
gigawatts—think about that, gigawatts—of solar, 43 gigawatts of 
storage, 30 gigawatts of onshore wind, and 16 gigawatts of offshore 
wind. Constraining this buildout would increase economy-wide util-
ity costs by approximately $11.6 billion per year. But that is not 
the only reason that costs are rising. 
We are also seeing a rapid increase in electricity demand and are 
hampered by not getting enough power from generators to end- 
users and struggling to quickly add new needed generation sources. 
Luckily, we can take steps to improve our grid to help move elec-
tricity more efficiently and bring new power sources online. I re-
cently launched an initiative called Grid for Growth, a plan to meet 
surging electricity demand without shifting the costs of that to con-
sumers. This effort complements ongoing efforts to pass bipartisan 
permitting reform. And as part of the effort, last week, I introduced 
the Grid Connection and Congestion Management Act to create a 
faster path for generator interconnection. Modeled after ERCOT’s 
connect-and-manage process, projects are rewarded for being flexi-
ble in their output by getting onto the grid faster. 
Other bills will follow in the next few weeks and months, but our 
ability to meet the rising needs of the grid are based in large part 
on our ability to build more interregional transmission lines. That 
is why permitting reform is so important. Interregional trans-
mission lines can be the best line of defense against extreme 
weather conditions, improving reliability, and reducing consumer 
costs. While FERC requires some interregional transmission plan-
ning under Orders 1000 and 1920, this coordination has not, unfor-
tunately, produced a substantial number of interregional trans-
mission projects in the last decade. We need to find a way to build 
more high-voltage transmission lines in the next decade. Our abil-
ity to grow our economy depends on it. And to FERC’s credit, the 
agency has taken active steps to confront energy challenges. Last 
month, FERC issued six show cause orders, requiring grid opera-
tors and their transmission owners to justify or propose changes to 
their current tariffs with respect to large-load customers. These 
steps by FERC can help protect consumers and encourage grid op-

bipartisan and unanimously supported.
