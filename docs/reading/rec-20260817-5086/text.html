I. Introduction and Summary
Wolf Meridian Group respectfully submits these comments in response to the Commission's July 30, 2026 notice inviting post-technical-conference comments concerning PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. governance and stakeholder processes. Wolf Meridian Group is an independent infrastructure and strategic analysis firm focused on power, compute, infrastructure, and the decision processes that shape large-scale energy development.

These comments are submitted from the perspective of an independent outside participant. Wolf Meridian Group does not represent a PJM member, market participant, utility, governmental entity, or other stakeholder organization in this proceeding. Its recommendations are offered in response to the Commission's request for concrete, actionable reforms and draw on practical analysis of grid capacity, large-load growth, load forecasting, infrastructure readiness, and the physical systems required to support new electrical demand.

These comments focus on the Commission's questions concerning accountability and
responsiveness in PJM governance, transparency, state participation, committee delay, and the potential use of fast-path or time-bound stakeholder procedures. Wolf Meridian Group recommends that PJM consider a permanent critical-issue decision track that builds on the experience of existing Critical Issue Fast Path processes while using objective activation criteria, a defined decision schedule, a common technical record, formal state and consumer consultation, a written Board disposition, and post-implementation review.

A faster process should not mean a less rigorous process. The objective should be to compress sequencing, reduce duplicative review, and support decisions against one transparent technical record. For matters with material reliability, market, or infrastructure consequences, delay can itself create risk. A well-defined expedited process can preserve stakeholder input while creating a dependable path from problem identification to decision.

II. Why Governance Speed Matters in a Large-Load Era
The Commission convened the July 23, 2026 technical conference specifically to examine whether PJM's governance mechanics and stakeholder processes allow the organization to address operational and market needs in a timely and efficient manner. The conference agenda asked whether existing committee structures contribute to delay and whether PJM should increase the use of fast-path or time-bound review procedures for high-importance topics. PJM has already recognized this problem in practice. Its Critical Issue Fast Path process for Large Load Additions was initiated to develop reliability-focused solutions for integrating large loads rapidly and reliably. The governance lesson is broader than any one class of load. When assumptions affecting reliability, resource adequacy, transmission planning, market design, or customer costs change materially, PJM needs a process capable of separating verified information from projections, rapidly developing alternatives, obtaining state and stakeholder input, and reaching a reasoned decision on a known schedule.

III. Recommended Reform: A Permanent Critical-Issue Decision Track
A. Objective Activation Criteria
PJM should establish written criteria for activating an expedited decision process. The process should be available when an issue presents a material reliability or market consequence, requires action within a defined time horizon, crosses multiple PJM committees or functional areas, or presents a meaningful risk that delay will increase system cost, reliability exposure, or implementation difficulty. Stakeholders should be able to request activation, but the Board or PJM management should make the activation decision through a short written finding.

Objective activation criteria would improve predictability and help prevent an expedited process from becoming either an extraordinary tool used too rarely or a routine shortcut used too often. The standard stakeholder process should remain the default. A critical-issue track should be reserved for matters where the need for expedited action can be demonstrated.

B. A Defined Decision Schedule
A critical-issue track should begin with a published issue charter identifying the problem, decision authority, scope, governing documents implicated, lead technical group, required data, and target decision date. As a default framework, PJM could target a 90-day process unless the Board determines that a different schedule is necessary. A model schedule could include the following milestones: ●​Day 0: Publish the issue charter, scope, decision authority, and schedule.

●​By Day 15: Publish the common technical record and identify material data gaps. ●​By Day 45: Publish a limited set of technically viable alternatives with reliability, market, implementation, and cost implications.

●​By Day 60: Complete a defined consultation period for states, consumer advocates, and other affected stakeholder groups.

●​By Day 75: Complete stakeholder voting or written position submissions, as applicable. ●​By Day 90: Publish a Board or PJM disposition and, where Commission action is required, direct the appropriate filing.

Extensions should be permitted, but they should require a written explanation identifying the unresolved issue and a new decision date. A deadline that can disappear without explanation is not a meaningful deadline.

C. One Common Technical Record
Expedited governance will only work if participants are debating policy choices from the same technical baseline. PJM should therefore create a common technical record at the beginning of each critical-issue process. That record should identify the assumptions, data sources, uncertainty ranges, and known limitations that will be used to compare alternatives.

For issues involving significant load growth, the record should distinguish between verified system data and projected demand, and projected demand should be categorized by confidence based on objective development milestones. The purpose is not to exclude uncertain future demand from planning. The purpose is to prevent highly uncertain projections and near-certain obligations from being treated as though they carry the same evidentiary weight.

The technical record should also identify, where relevant, the effects of each option on reliability, generation adequacy, transmission needs, implementation timing, customer cost exposure, and the ability to revise the decision as better information becomes available. Confidential commercial information can be protected, but the assumptions used for regional planning should be disclosed at a level sufficient for meaningful review.

D. Formal State and Consumer Consultation Without Creating a Veto
The Commission's agenda appropriately asks how states should participate in PJM's governance and stakeholder processes. On critical issues, state participation should be structured rather than informal. The expedited process should include a defined consultation window for the Organization of PJM States, Inc., individual state commissions where appropriate, and state consumer advocates. Their written positions should become part of the decisional record.

This consultation should not convert regional grid governance into a state-by-state veto structure. PJM must retain the independence required to perform its regional functions. But where a decision may materially affect retail customers, state resource planning, infrastructure development, or local implementation, the process should require those concerns to be surfaced early enough to influence the analysis rather than after a preferred solution has effectively been selected.

E. A Written Board Disposition
At the conclusion of a critical-issue process, the Board should publish a concise decisional memorandum. The memorandum should identify the alternatives considered, the principal technical findings, major stakeholder disagreements, material uncertainties, the reasons for the selected path, and any matters that require Commission approval or further stakeholder work.

This would directly improve accountability and transparency. It would also create an institutional record that later Boards, states, stakeholders, and the Commission can use to understand why a decision was made under a particular set of facts.

include a scheduled post-implementation review, ordinarily within 12 to 18 months. PJM should compare forecast assumptions with observed results, identify unintended effects, and determine whether the governing rule or process should be modified.

This creates a feedback loop between planning and reality. It also reduces the pressure to make every expedited decision permanent on day one.

IV. Large Loads Demonstrate Why the Reform Is Necessary
Large-load growth provides a useful example of why governance structure and technical analysis can no longer be separated. A single large project may implicate load forecasting, generation adequacy, transmission planning, interconnection timing, distribution utility coordination, market rules, cost responsibility, and state policy. If those questions are handled serially through separate processes, the system can spend substantial time debating pieces of a problem that is operationally one problem. PJM's existing Large Load Additions Critical Issue Fast Path shows that expedited treatment can be appropriate. The next step should be to convert that experience into a durable governance capability rather than treating each new urgent issue as a one-off emergency. The critical-issue track should provide a reusable structure for high-impact questions while allowing the technical content to vary by issue.

Load forecasting illustrates the need for this structure. Forecasts necessarily contain uncertainty, and emerging large loads can change quickly as projects are announced, delayed, resized, financed, connected, or cancelled. Governance should require decision-makers to see not only a single forecast number, but the confidence and development maturity underlying material forecast adjustments. That approach would improve the quality of decisions without requiring PJM to wait for perfect information that will never exist.

V. Relationship to the Commission's Questions
The recommendations above directly respond to several questions posed in the July 23 technical conference agenda:

●​Panel 1, Question 3 - Accountability and responsiveness: objective activation criteria, a fixed schedule, and a written Board disposition create an auditable decision process.

●​Panel 2, Question 2 - Transparency: a common technical record makes assumptions, uncertainties, and alternative impacts visible before decisions are made.

●​Panel 2, Question 3 - State participation: a defined consultation window gives states and consumer advocates a meaningful role without compromising PJM's regional independence.

●​Panel 2, Questions 5 and 7 - Stakeholder-process reform and committee delay: a single issue charter and lead process reduce serial referrals and duplicative committee work.

VI. Conclusion
PJM does not need to choose between inclusive governance and timely decisions. It needs a process designed to do both. The Commission should encourage PJM to consider formalizing a permanent critical-issue decision track that includes objective activation criteria, a published schedule, one common technical record, structured state and consumer consultation, a written Board disposition, and a mandatory post-implementation review.

The physical system is changing rapidly, and governance processes must be capable of responding to new information without sacrificing transparency or accountability. Large loads, changing forecasts, new generation needs, transmission constraints, and emerging infrastructure demands will continue to create difficult regional decisions. A durable fast-path structure can help PJM address those decisions more efficiently while preserving meaningful stakeholder participation and a clear public record.
