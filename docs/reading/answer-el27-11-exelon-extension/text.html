II. ANSWER

The default comment period for this complaint proceeding is twenty days under Rule
206(f),9 or until November 23, 2026.  In justifying its extension request through December 21, 2026, PJM states that “[t]he PJM Board is scheduled to consider these very issues at its November 17, 2026 meeting, and the PJM Board’s determinations are expected to substantially inform PJM’s position regarding the issues raised in the Complaint.”10  PJM further explains that, “following the

However, the scale of PJM’s proposed extension is excessive for the narrow scope of the allegations it must answer.  PJM requests 28 days in addition to the default 20 days.  While PJM’s approach to a future “comprehensive package of capacity market enhancements” is certainly of interest, it is simply not what PJM must respond to in this docket.  The only legal question PJM is required to respond to in its answer is correspondingly the only legal question presented in the complaint: based on the evidence presented, has the three-year forward period of the Base Residual Auction (“BRA”)12 become unjust and unreasonable under the Federal Power Act (“FPA”) and, if so, whether a “prompt” auction schedule is a just and reasonable replacement rate.  As the established market administrator for decades, and during a period of time in which it has for months (or years) been deeply considering the current market shortcomings and needed remedies, it should not take PJM 48 days to express its opinion on this narrow legal question. Nor does PJM need to, in its answer, present a comprehensive road map of future stakeholder meetings and theoretical proposed enhancements to the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”).  While Exelon has

Furthermore, the PJM Motion provides no explanation as to why the PJM Board could not convene sooner to consider the limited issue raised in the complaint and provide the direction necessary for PJM to submit a timely answer within the comment period prescribed by the Commission’s regulations. Exelon submits that this date strikes a reasonable balance between PJM’s request for additional time and the exigency of Commission action sufficiently in advance of the May 2027 BRA, as discussed below in Section II.B.

B. Exelon Urges the Commission to Act Sufficiently in Advance of the May 2027 BRA.

Exelon takes no position in this Answer regarding PJM’s analysis as to when legal consequence attaches to a Commission-jurisdictional auction process, and in many ways this is beside the point.  Rule 206(h) does not specify a fixed temporal deadline for a Commission decision on the merits of a complaint requesting fast-track processing, and provides the Commission with broad discretion in how to act.14  Whatever the action date the Commission ultimately selects, Exelon respectfully requests the Commission issue its order sufficiently in

of the three-year forward period of the BRA—is of critical importance to Market Participants across the PJM Region.  Irrespective of when the consequence of Commission inaction will attach legally, it will certainly attach practically, as parties try to position themselves sufficiently in advance of the uncapped surge in capacity prices coming this May. III. CONCLUSION
For these reasons, Exelon requests the Commission establish an extended comment date of
December 7, 2026, and consider expeditiously the instant complaint so the Commission can act sufficiently in advance of the May 2027 BRA.
