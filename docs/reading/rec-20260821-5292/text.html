POST TECHNICAL CONFERENCE COMMENTS
PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms
Docket No. AD26-7-000
Post-Conference Issues List Responses of Energy Artisans, LLC, 8/21/2026
The questions below focus on broad themes, shared concerns, or potential points of consensus that emerged during the technical conference. They are not intended to be exhaustive, dispositive, or a comprehensive catalog of all matters relevant to governance and stakeholder issues. Although the questions contain interrelated elements and a change to one element may affect others, each question is answered independently.

PJM member sector: Representing the voice of members in Generation and End Use Sectors

Energy Artisans' answers are guided by the principles stated in its previously filed comments: power markets exist to serve customers; governance should be measured by results; customers require a stronger voice; and rules should solve demonstrated problems without creating unnecessary barriers to investment.

PJM Board Independence

Should Board members' terms be extended to five or more years?
Comments: Longer terms may provide continuity, but under the present structure they would increase insulation without a corresponding increase in accountability. Until Board votes, reasons, performance measures, and reappointment criteria are transparent, Board terms should remain relatively short.

2
Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10
years)?
Comments: A fixed maximum is not necessary if terms remain short and reappointment is conditioned on transparent performance. If five-year or longer terms are adopted, however, a cumulative service cap should accompany them.

X
3
Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some Board members that reside in the PJM region?

Comments: At least some directors should have substantial residence or work experience in the PJM region. Regional familiarity should be a preference, not an absolute requirement, and should complement customer, market, operational, financial, and regulatory expertise.

X

4
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling-off period for Board members from two years to one year?

Comments: The two-year cooling-off period should be retained. Board independence and stakeholder confidence outweigh the modest benefit of expanding the candidate pool. Any exception should be narrow, publicly disclosed, and subject to recusal safeguards.

X
5
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more communication between the Board and stakeholders?

Comments: Restrictions should be relaxed through structured and transparent channels, including public listening sessions, written stakeholder questions, published responses, and disclosure of substantive meetings. More communication should not mean selective access or private commitments. X

6
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be required to explain its reasoning?

Comments: PJM should publish each Board vote and a concise statement of reasons, including how reliability, customer cost, competition, and significant stakeholder positions were considered. Limited exceptions may apply to personnel, litigation, cybersecurity, and other legitimately confidential matters. X

In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the public?

Comments: Policy and governance meetings should be open, with recordings or transcripts available. Executive sessions should be limited to defined confidential subjects, with a public agenda and a brief explanation of why closure is necessary.

X

8
Should PJM's governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest?

Comments: The governing documents should state that PJM and its Board serve the public interest through cost-effective, reliable service, competitive markets, timely infrastructure, and just and reasonable customer costs. That duty should be implemented through public metrics and reporting, not left as an aspirational statement.

X

9
Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the Nominating Committee? Comments: At least one voting member should be selected collectively by the PJM states. This would add regional and public accountability without giving the states control over Board selection. The Nominating Committee should also include meaningful customer-side experience.

X

10
Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management compensation to their achievement?

Comments: PJM should publish an annual dashboard and tie a material portion of incentive compensation to outcomes within management's control: time from issue identification to decision, filing, and implementation; queue-cycle schedule adherence; completed interconnections and new capacity in service net of retirements; stakeholder responsiveness; and written explanations for missed milestones. Meeting counts and procedural activity are not results.

X

Section 205 Filing Rights

Questions / Comments
Yes
No
11
Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board? Comments: The stakeholder process should not be reduced to a purely advisory role. Stakeholders, especially customers who ultimately bear the costs, must retain a meaningful role. For urgent matters, PJM may use a time-bound process, but the Board should publish its rationale and supported alternatives should remain available for Commission review.

X
12
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

Comments: Unencumbered filing rights would remove an important accountability check. The better approach is a defined decision deadline: if consensus is not reached, PJM may file its proposal together with any stakeholder alternative that achieved a specified level of support.

X
13
Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members Committee?

Comments: RTEP costs and consequences are borne by customers and affect state policy and infrastructure. PJM should not have unilateral filing authority without meaningful stakeholder and state review. A time-bound process and simultaneous filing of supported alternatives would avoid both paralysis and unchecked authority.

Should the Organization of PJM States Inc. (OPSI) have 'jump ball' rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal?

Comments: A separate OPSI filing franchise could add another institutional veto and fragment regional decision-making. OPSI should receive early notice, access to relevant data, a defined opportunity to consult, and a clear path to present a supported alternative to the Commission.

X
15
Should the Members Committee have 'jump ball' filing rights over the portions of the tariff where PJM has section 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal?

Comments: When a supermajority supports an alternative, PJM should file that alternative alongside its own proposal. This gives the Commission a complete record, preserves stakeholder accountability, and prevents broad consensus from being disregarded. Neither proposal should receive an automatic preference.

Stakeholder Process

Questions / Comments
Yes
No
16
Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-thirds sector-weighted voting?

Comments: The threshold should be lowered modestly while remaining a supermajority and preserving sector weighting. The current two-thirds standard can empower blocking coalitions and delay; a simple majority would be too low. The revised threshold should be tested and reviewed after an initial period. Perhaps a 60% threshold, like the U.S. Senate, is a sensible balance.

X

17
Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior standing committees, also apply to lower-level committees?

Comments: The same economic interest should not multiply its voting power at lower-level committees. Affiliate aggregation and disclosure rules should apply consistently across voting bodies, subject only to narrow and transparent exceptions where technically necessary.

X

18
In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting of governors' representatives and/or state legislators' representatives?

Comments: OPSI represents state utility commissions; governors and legislators bring distinct policy, affordability, reliability, and economic-development perspectives. A second, coordinated group should have a defined role and information access, but it should not create duplicative vetoes or added procedural delay.

X

19
Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process (limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in addition to the consumer advocate votes?

Comments: PJM should create a practical pathway for bona fide state and customer representatives to obtain meaningful voting participation, preferably through the End-Use Customer Sector or an equivalent structure. Any special state vote should be limited to defined topics and include safeguards against duplicative representation. Consumer advocate votes should remain independent. X

Comments: Consultation should not be a legal condition precedent that can prevent or delay a filing. PJM should nevertheless provide early notice, relevant data, and a reasonable consultation window, and its filing should explain material state concerns that remain unresolved.

Additional Comments:
Energy Artisans incorporates by reference its July 22, 2026 comments and avoids repeating the evidence presented there regarding interconnection delay, reciprocal accountability, and the inability of price signals to overcome administrative bottlenecks.  The primary goal should be to add new generation resources expeditiously into service.  However; that goal should be implemented by ensuring that the queue process expeditious and is fair to both large and small resources, rather than creating special paths for large resources to go around the queue.  Objective measures should be employed to measure performance with the primary measures being new MW in-service, ratio of projects entering the queue vs. completion, and number and average size of completed projects.  In so doing the new resources will improve reliability of power delivery.  Reliability is isolation is subjective, and without any balance of cost is an invitation to raise costs to end-users, who ultimately, pay all of the costs of everything in PJM.
