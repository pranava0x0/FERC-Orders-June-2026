COMMENTS OF CAPITAL POWER CORPORATION 
 
Capital Power Corporation ("Capital Power") appreciates the opportunity to participate in 
the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“the Commission”) examination of PJM 
Interconnection, L.L.C.'s ("PJM") governance structure. 
Capital Power is a growth-oriented North American power producer headquartered in 
Edmonton, Alberta, with U.S. headquarters in Phoenix, Arizona. The company owns 
approximately 12 GW of generation capacity across 35 facilities in the United States and 
Canada, including natural gas, wind, solar, and battery energy storage resources. We are the 5th 
largest independent power producer in North America. 
Capital Power entered the PJM market in 2025 through the acquisition of Hummel 
Station and Rolling Hills, adding approximately 2.2 GW of natural gas-fired generation in 
Pennsylvania and Ohio. These investments established a significant operating presence within 
PJM and reflect Capital Power's commitment to investing in markets that support reliability, 
efficient price formation, and long-term infrastructure development.

As a relatively new PJM participant, Capital Power offers a perspective informed by 
experience operating and investing across multiple North American electricity markets. 
Based on Capital Power's experience entering PJM during a period of significant market 
change, several themes emerge: 
• PJM's stakeholder-driven governance framework supports reliable grid operations and 
competitive wholesale markets, but significant opportunities remain to improve 
transparency, prioritization, accountability, and process efficiency. 
 
• PJM's current challenges are primarily driven by unprecedented load growth, resource 
adequacy concerns, interconnection delays, transmission needs, and evolving priorities. 
 
• A structured forum for early and ongoing engagement between the PJM Board and the 
States would enhance transparency and strengthen stakeholder confidence in the 
governance process.  
 
• PJM would benefit from a formal work plan identifying priority initiatives, responsible 
committees, key milestones, expected timelines, and decision pathways. 
 
• Stakeholders need greater visibility into how initiatives move through the governance 
process, how priorities are established, and how related initiatives fit within PJM's 
broader strategic agenda. 
 
• The Board's primary role should remain strategic oversight and organizational 
accountability rather than detailed market design. Consistent with PJM's stakeholder 
model, responsibility for market design should remain grounded in the established 
stakeholder and committee process, with the Board providing oversight and strategic 
direction. 
 
• Fast-path procedures should remain available for exceptional circumstances but should 
not substitute for effective planning, prioritization, and stakeholder engagement. 
 
• Market participants making long-term infrastructure investments benefit from greater 
certainty regarding process, timelines, and governance accountability, even when 
stakeholders disagree on policy outcomes. 
 
• PJM should consider simplifying its committee structure by reducing the number of 
committees and assigning broader mandates to the remaining forums to address cross-
cutting issues.

Capital Power therefore encourages the Commission to focus on practical governance 
improvements that enhance transparency, accountability, and process discipline while preserving 
PJM's stakeholder-driven framework. Below we address specific questions posed by the 
Commission within Panel 1 and Panel 2 of the Technical Conference. 
I. 
COMMENTS 
A. Panel 1, Question 1: Board Role 
 
Capital Power supports a Board focused on strategic oversight, organizational 
accountability, and long-term market health rather than detailed market design decisions. The 
Board's primary responsibility should be to ensure that PJM is identifying, prioritizing, and 
addressing the issues that present the greatest risks to reliability, market efficiency, and 
investment. 
As a newer PJM participant, Capital Power has entered the market during a period 
characterized by rapid load growth, resource adequacy concerns, interconnection backlogs, 
transmission expansion needs, and significant policy change. In this environment, the Board can 
play an important role by establishing clear organizational priorities, monitoring progress, and 
holding management accountable for execution without becoming directly involved in resolving 
stakeholder disagreements over market design. While the PJM Operating Agreement establishes 
a clear distinction between the roles of the Board and management, circumstances may arise in 
which stakeholders believe a significant issue warrants review beyond the normal stakeholder 
process. In such cases, and subject to a clearly defined standard for escalation, PJM should 
provide a transparent mechanism for stakeholders to elevate concerns to management and, where 
appropriate, ultimately to the Board.

While extraordinary circumstances may occasionally require Board engagement, such 
intervention should remain the exception rather than the rule. The stronger long-term solution is 
a transparent and effective stakeholder process capable of addressing emerging issues before 
extraordinary action becomes necessary. 
Ultimately, Board effectiveness should be measured not by the number of initiatives it 
influences directly, but by whether PJM consistently delivers timely decisions, supports 
reliability, maintains stakeholder confidence, and provides the certainty required to support long-
term infrastructure investment. 
 
 
B. Panel 1, Question 2: State Involvement 
Capital Power supports earlier and more structured engagement between PJM and the 
states on issues with significant reliability, market, or public-policy implications. PJM should 
explore opportunities for regular dialogue between the Board and state officials to ensure state 
perspectives are heard before major initiatives become fully developed within the stakeholder 
process. Capital Power further supports each Board member being assigned a designated state 
engagement responsibility to maintain a regular schedule of outreach and consultation with 
government officials in that jurisdiction. Earlier engagement can help identify concerns before 
positions become entrenched, improving transparency, reducing conflict, and supporting more 
durable outcomes.  
 
C. Panel 1, Question 3: Accountability and Responsiveness 
Accountability and responsiveness begin with clear prioritization. PJM is currently 
managing numerous important initiatives related to reliability, resource adequacy, load growth, 
transmission planning, and market reform. As a market participant making long-term investment,

one of the greatest challenges has not been disagreement over policy outcomes but understanding 
how these initiatives are prioritized and when decisions are expected to occur. 
Accordingly, Capital Power believes the most valuable governance reform would be the 
development of a formal PJM work plan identifying priority initiatives, responsible committees, 
major decision points, expected timelines, and desired outcomes. Such a framework would 
provide stakeholders, regulators, investors, and PJM management with a common roadmap for 
understanding PJM's strategic priorities. 
A formal work plan would also improve accountability by allowing stakeholders to assess 
whether initiatives are progressing as expected, whether priorities remain appropriate, and 
whether PJM is successfully delivering on identified objectives. Rather than limiting interactions 
to formal proceedings, PJM should explore mechanisms for regular and transparent strategic 
engagement between the Board and stakeholders to inform the development of its work plan, 
while maintaining appropriate protections against preferential access or undue influence. 
For investors making long-term infrastructure commitments, predictability in governance 
processes is nearly as important as policy outcomes themselves. Greater clarity regarding 
priorities and timelines would strengthen confidence in PJM's ability to address emerging 
challenges while preserving its stakeholder-driven model. 
D. Panel 1, Question 5: Governing Documents and Process Complexity 
PJM's governance framework has evolved over decades and reflects the complexity of 
the markets and reliability responsibilities it manages. However, the number of committees, task 
forces, manuals, and governing documents can make it difficult for stakeholders to understand 
where decisions are being made and how initiatives move through the process.

Capital Power's experience suggests that the primary challenge is not access, but 
navigation. It can be difficult to determine which committees are responsible for advancing an 
issue, how recommendations move between stakeholder bodies, and where participation is likely 
to have the greatest impact. 
From an investment perspective, process complexity can also reduce confidence in the 
predictability of decision-making. Greater clarity regarding decision pathways, committee 
responsibilities, and initiative ownership would improve participation while reducing 
unnecessary complexity. 
E. Panel 1, Question 6: Lessons from Other Markets 
Capital Power participates in multiple North American electricity markets and has found 
that transparency and accessibility of information are critical components of effective market 
governance. 
In Alberta, market participants can readily access operational information such as system 
conditions, outages, and supply-demand fundamentals. This transparency allows stakeholders to 
engage from a common factual foundation and promotes confidence in market outcomes. 
A similar principle applies to governance. PJM already publishes substantial information 
regarding stakeholder activities and ongoing initiatives. The opportunity is not necessarily to 
publish more information, but to make existing information easier to understand and use. 
Ontario's IESO supplements its technical stakeholder processes with a Strategic Advisory 
Committee that provides policy-level advice directly to the Board of Directors and Executive 
Leadership Team. PJM could consider a similar executive-level forum focused on strategic 
priorities, emerging issues, and stakeholder process effectiveness. Such a forum would follow 
Chatham House and rules and could improve communication and mutual understanding. Another

lesson from Ontario that PJM should consider adopting is the publishing of a consolidated 
strategic plan, business plan, and budget. While substantial information is publicly available in 
PJM, the information is often dispersed across multiple forums and documents making it less 
transparent to stakeholders. 
Stakeholders should be able to readily identify PJM's strategic priorities, understand how 
initiatives relate to one another, determine who is responsible for advancing them, and track 
progress against expected timelines. Greater transparency in these areas would improve 
participation, accountability, and investor confidence. 
F. Panel 2, Question 2: Transparency Reforms 
PJM's transparency challenge is not one of information availability. PJM publishes 
extensive committee materials, issue-tracking resources, and stakeholder documentation. The 
challenge is helping stakeholders understand how information fits within PJM's broader strategic 
agenda. 
As a newer participant, Capital Power has often found that significant effort is required to 
determine the status of a particular initiative, identify the responsible decision-making bodies, 
and understand how an issue relates to other ongoing reforms. This challenge becomes more 
pronounced as the number of major initiatives grows. 
The most valuable transparency reform would therefore be a framework that allows 
stakeholders to quickly identify initiative status, responsible committees, key milestones, 
expected timelines, and relationships to other reforms. 
Transparency ultimately supports predictability. Stakeholders do not require certainty 
regarding outcomes, but they do benefit from greater certainty regarding process, timelines, and 
decision-making pathways.

G. Panel 2, Question 7: Committee Structure and Delays 
Capital Power's experience suggests that process visibility presents a greater challenge 
than stakeholder representation. PJM's committee structure provides many opportunities for 
participation, but the number of forums involved in developing proposals can make it difficult to 
determine where key policy discussions are occurring and where engagement will be most 
effective. Given the increasingly interconnected nature of PJM's policy initiatives, Capital Power 
believes PJM should consider simplifying its committee structure by reducing the number of 
committees and assigning broader mandates to the remaining forums. A more streamlined 
structure would improve transparency, reduce duplication of effort, and make it easier for 
stakeholders to follow issues as they evolve across multiple subject areas.  
Many concerns regarding delays stem less from the existence of multiple committees and 
more from limited visibility into how issues move through those committees and how priorities 
are established. Stakeholders can participate more effectively when they understand decision 
pathways, ownership, and remaining milestones.  
Accordingly, Capital Power believes PJM would benefit more from improved 
transparency regarding committee responsibilities and decision pathways than from significant 
changes to stakeholder sectors or voting structures. 
H. Panel 2, Question 8: Fast-Path Procedures 
Capital Power supports retaining fast-path procedures for urgent reliability, operational, 
or market concerns. In a complex and rapidly evolving system, extraordinary circumstances may 
occasionally require expedited action. 
However, expedited processes should remain exceptional tools rather than routine 
mechanisms for advancing major policy reforms. Long-term market stability is best supported

through transparent stakeholder processes that provide adequate opportunity for participation and 
analysis. 
Repeated reliance on expedited procedures may indicate broader challenges related to 
prioritization, planning, and issue management. PJM's long-term objective should be to identify 
emerging issues earlier, establish clear priorities, and provide realistic timelines that allow 
stakeholder processes to function effectively. 
Accordingly, Capital Power views fast-path procedures as an important safety valve, but not 
as a substitute for strong governance, effective prioritization, and transparent stakeholder 
engagement. 
 
II. 
CONCLUSION 
Capital Power appreciates the Commission's attention to PJM governance and 
stakeholder process reform. 
As a relatively new participant in PJM, Capital Power's perspective is informed by recent 
investment and operational experience during a period of significant change for the region. PJM 
is confronting substantial challenges, including accelerated load growth, increasing reliability 
needs, interconnection constraints, and the need for market frameworks that continue to support 
investment in generation and infrastructure. While these challenges place significant demands on 
PJM's governance processes, they do not necessarily require wholesale restructuring of the 
governance framework. 
Instead, Capital Power believes the greatest opportunities for improvement lie in 
enhanced transparency, clearer prioritization, stronger accountability, and more visible decision-
making pathways. Stakeholders, regulators, and investors alike would benefit from a governance

process that more clearly communicates priorities, timelines, ownership, and progress toward 
key objectives. 
Accordingly, Capital Power encourages the Commission to focus on practical reforms 
that improve process discipline and stakeholder visibility while preserving the benefits of PJM's 
stakeholder-driven model. Such improvements would support more informed investment 
decisions by market participants considering generation development, asset acquisitions, plant 
enhancements, and other long-term commitments necessary to maintain reliability and 
accommodate future load growth.
