I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The NYTOs submit this filing under section 205 of the FPA and their reserved filing rights under section 3.10(a) of the NYISO-TO Agreement7 to address the concerns the Commission identified in the Large Load Show Cause Order that fall within the NYTOs’ responsibilities.  This filing complements, and is submitted in parallel with, the NYISO’s section 205 filing establishing new Large Load Integration Procedures in Attachment LL of the OATT.  The substantial majority of the proposed tariff revisions submitted herewith are amendments to the currently effective NYISO OATT.  In addition, certain amendments submitted in exercise of the NYTOs’ unilateral filing rights propose to amend revisions to the NYISO OATT sponsored by the NYISO in its contemporaneous section 205 filing.8  While the NYISO’s filing establishes the study process, readiness requirements, and new Firm and Non-Firm Withdrawal Services for Large Loads, the NYTOs’ filing addresses the complementary elements necessary to recover the costs of facilities built to serve Large Loads, to protect other customers from cost shifts, and to integrate Large Loads reliably onto the NYTOs’ systems.

The NYTOs present in this filing pro forma Large Load Facilities Study, Security, Cost Recovery, and Interconnection Agreements.  While they relate to the NYISO Large Load Integration Process as proposed in new Attachment LL of the NYISO OATT, they are not appropriate agreements for intermediaries between the Large Loads themselves and the NYTOs responsible for interconnection studies, cost recovery, associated security and interconnection, including certain operational reliability requirements. Historically, in New York, energy service companies (“ESCOs”) have provided
commodity supply unbundled from retail delivery services and have been Eligible Customers for purposes of the NYISO’s energy and capacity market settlements.  ESCOs have been Eligible Customers under the NYISO Tariffs for limited purposes, enabling retail access programs intended to introduce competition for supply, but not for purposes of retail customer interconnection studies and associated agreements to effect retail interconnections with grid owners.

ESCOs or other intermediaries have not functioned as the primary interfaces or
contracting parties in the interconnection process for new Large Loads.  This is not by accident; unlike serving as a supply source to enable retail access, ESCOs would serve no legitimate business function as an intermediary in the Large Load Interconnection Process.  In fact, their insertion would only serve to frustrate that process, making it riskier and, not insignificantly, costlier.

Until recently, the interconnection process for loads was subject to NYPSC regulation and not FERC regulation.  The interconnection of retail loads has been to facilitate local distribution of electric energy which the Commission found to be involved in every retail transmission transaction33 and to facilitate retail sales of electric energy.  These functions are subject to State jurisdiction, and the NYPSC regulates such functions.

In the compressed time that the NYTOs have to handle the tasks listed above, direct communications with the Large Load Customer will be necessary in order to have an efficient and effective interconnection study process which inevitably involves discussions with many customers simultaneously.
