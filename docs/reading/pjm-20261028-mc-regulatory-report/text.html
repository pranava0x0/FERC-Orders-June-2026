ORDERS 
 
On October 15, 2026, in Docket No. ER24-2045-005, the Commission issued an order partially accepting 
a compliance filing in response to the Commission's directives in its April 16, 2026 order. The further 
compliance filing revised PJM's Tariff in accordance with the Commission's Final Rule in its Order No. 2023 
proceedings. The Commission directed PJM to submit a compliance filing that addresses the directives in 
this order within 60 days of the date of this order and make a compliance filing to establish the effective 
date of the records. 
 
On October 12, 2026, the Department of Energy issued Order No. 202-26-50 pursuant to Federal Power 
Act section 202(c), finding that an imminent electric reliability emergency exists, directing PJM to dispatch 
Specified Resources as needed to maintain reliability, and authorizing PJM to direct backup generation 
resources at large loads to operate as a last resort before declaring an Energy Emergency Alert (“EEA”) 3 
or during an EEA 3. The order is effective October 13, 2026, and expires at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Prevailing 
Time on October 16, 2026. 
 
On October 9, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3515, the Commission issued an Order Accepting Tariff 
Revisions in Part and Rejecting Tariff Revisions in Part. The Commission directed PJM to submit a 
compliance filing by November 8, 2026, of the tariff sections with the Large Load definition and the Large 
Load Registry to remove the elements that were not accepted. 
 
On October 7, 2026, in Docket Nos. EL15-18-006, et al., FERC issued an Order: (i) denying requests for 
rehearing regarding FERC's findings related to the de minimis exception to the SBDFAX methodology and 
associated recalculation and rebilling requirements, (ii) granting limited rehearing regarding historical Tariff 
requirements, (iii) granting certain clarifications regarding implementation; and (iv) setting several matters 
for hearing and settlement proceedings. Further, FERC affirmed that PJM should not implement 
recalculations or rebilling until it issues a further order. 
 
On October 1, 2026, in Docket No. EL26-99-000, the Commission issued an Order denying the complaint 
filed by Advantage Capital Renewables, LLC (Advantage Capital) against PJM challenging PJM’s 
withdrawal of its Cycle 1 project. In denying Advantage Capital’s complaint, FERC found Advantage 
Renewables failed to meet its burden to show that PJM violated the Tariff during the interconnection 
process for the project. The Commission found that the Tariff entitles applicants one opportunity to cure, 
that Advantage Capital did not cure the deficiencies identified by PJM, and upheld PJM’s decision to reject 
and withdraw the Advantage Capital project due to the unresolved deficiencies that remained as consistent 
with the Tariff. 
 
On October 1, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3496-000, the Commission issued an order accepting PJM's 
submission for filing proposed revisions to the PJM Operating Agreement, Schedule 12, and the RAA, 
Schedule 17, to reflect new members, removals or withdrawals, current signatories to the RAA, and 
corporate name changes through July 1, 2026. The agreement is effective as of July 1, 2026.

On September 29, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3344-000, the Commission issued an order accepting PJM's 
submission on behalf of UGIU, proposals to update and align the depreciation rates in its transmission 
formula rate with those most recently submitted by UGIU to the PA PUC based on the most recent 
depreciation study performed by an independent third-party expert. The updated depreciation rates are 
effective October 1, 2026. 
 
On September 29, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3380-000, the Commission accepted PJM’s proposed 
Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP) Tariff revisions, subject to refund and further proceedings and 
effective February 28, 2027. Further, the Commission opened a new section 206 proceeding in Docket No. 
EL26-108-000 to establish paper hearing on certain topics related to cost allocation, transmission exit 
responsibilities, and collateral requirements for load serving entities. 
 
On September 24, 2026, in Docket No. EL26-101-000, the Commission issued an Order denying the 
complaint filed by Oklo Inc. (Oklo) against PJM challenging the lawfulness of PJM’s withdrawal of its Cycle 
1 project. In denying Oklo’s complaint, FERC found that the Tariff entitles applicants one opportunity to 
cure, found that Oklo did not cure the deficiencies identified by PJM, and upheld PJM’s decision to reject 
and withdraw the Oklo project due to the unresolved deficiencies that remained as consistent with the 
Tariff. 
 
On September 21, 2026, in Docket No. EL26-67-001, FERC issued an Order dismissing the requests for 
rehearing and clarification concerning its June 18, 2026 Show Cause Order. FERC concluded that the 
Show Cause Order is not a final agency action, because it made only preliminary findings and initiated 
further proceedings, so it was not eligible for rehearing under the Commission’s rules. FERC also declined 
to provide clarification while this proceeding remains in abeyance through November 16, 2026, to allow 
PJM’s stakeholder process and potential section 205 filings to proceed. Therefore, FERC formally 
dismissed all requests for rehearing and clarification requests as premature. 
 
On September 17, 2026, the Department of Energy issued Order No. 202-26-45 pursuant to Federal 
Power Act section 202(c), finding that an imminent electric reliability emergency exists, directing PJM to 
dispatch Specified Resources as needed to maintain reliability, and authorizing PJM to direct backup 
generation resources at large loads to operate as a last resort before declaring an Energy Emergency Alert 
(“EEA”) 3 or during an EEA 3. The order is effective September 17, 2026, upon issuance and expires at 
11:59 p.m. Eastern Prevailing Time on September 18, 2026.
