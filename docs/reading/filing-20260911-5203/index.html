ER26-3552-000 · 2026-09-11 · Organization of MISO States, Inc.

Motion for Acceptance of Late-Filed Comments and Comments in Conditional Support of the Organization of MISO States, Inc. re Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.’s proposed tariff revisions

The Organization of MISO States moves for acceptance of late comments and conditionally supports MISO's Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement, asking FERC to require reliability, cost-shifting, resource adequacy and transparency safeguards.

Asks FERC to: Accept the late comments, and approve MISO's ZGIA only if reliability, cost, resource adequacy and transparency protections are clear and enforceable, while treating it as one part of a broader large-load framework.

Parallel proceeding. State regulators comment on MISO's ZGIA filing in ER26-3552, MISO's first response to the E-8 order on co-located load, and ask FERC to weigh it with the rest of the E-8 response.

E-8 MISO: Services for flexible / co-located loads (P 6(d), p. 7) E-8 MISO: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 7) E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. OMS offers conditional support for the ZGIA and does not oppose it.
    OMS cautiously supports the ZGIA proposal PDF p. 1
  2. OMS treats the ban on injection beyond the substation as a reliability requirement and wants studies before execution.
    That limitation must be treated as a reliability requirement, not merely a contractual condition. PDF p. 4
  3. OMS objects that MISO left the Transient Tolerance Window definition to its Business Practice Manual.
    MISO has deferred establishing an explicit definition of the appropriate Transient Tolerance Window to the development of its Business Practice Manual. PDF p. 5
  4. OMS backs keeping the ZGIA limited to generation and load at one substation and voltage level.
    recommends that the Commission accept the common-substation and voltage-level limitation as the appropriate scope of ZGIA. PDF p. 7
  5. OMS supports the requirement that the generator's full output serve the associated load for at least five years.
    with an expected period of at least five years. PDF p. 7
  6. OMS says upgrades needed if supporting generation fails to materialize should not automatically fall on existing customers.
    the costs of that incremental need should not automatically shift to existing transmission customers. PDF p. 7
  7. OMS asks MISO to explain ZGIA treatment across its resource adequacy models, including LOLE, UCAP, SAC and the Planning Resource Auction.
    OMS requests that MISO explain in a compliance filing, subsequent BPM rules, or a workshop how ZGIA resources and associated loads will be represented in PDF p. 9
  8. OMS wants any later move to injection rights sequenced so it does not bypass the Definitive Planning Process queue.
    must not allow a ZGIA resource to circumvent or obtain an advantage over interconnection customers already participating in the DPP queue. PDF p. 10
  9. OMS asks FERC to judge the ZGIA together with MISO's other large-load reforms on a complete record.
    the Commission should evaluate the ZGIA Filing together with other reforms developed during that process only after a complete record is developed PDF p. 13
