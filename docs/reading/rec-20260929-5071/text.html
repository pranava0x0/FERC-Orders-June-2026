SUPPLEMENTAL COMMENTS OF VOLTUS
IN RESPONSE TO PJM’S ANSWERS
Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC’s” or “Commission’s”) Rules of Practice and Procedure, Voltus, Inc., (“Voltus”) hereby moves for leave to answer and respectfully submits these brief comments in response to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM’s”) answers filed September 10, 2026, and September 23, 2026, in the above-captioned Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) and Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) proceedings, respectively. Voltus’s purpose is to supplement the record related to Demand Resource and DER Capacity Aggregation Resource (“DR/DER”) eligibility criteria in light of PJM’s updates, in order to assist the Commission in ensuring alignment between these two closely related frameworks and enable the urgent addition of new capacity to the PJM system in furtherance of overall RBP/IRAS design objectives. 1

I.​
PJM’S ANSWERS IN THE INTERLINKED RBP AND IRAS DOCKETS
HELPFULLY ADDRESS DR/DER ELIGIBILITY BUT INTRODUCE A
KEY DISCREPANCY
At issue is the allowable composition of DR/DER resources that may be considered new system capacity that, if bilaterally contracted by a relevant large load, may perform two distinct but related functions: (1) reducing the RBP procurement target—the Qualified RBP Offset (“QRBPO”) function—and (2) reducing the large load’s IRAS curtailment exposure (more accurately, the volume of IRAS MW assigned to the Electric Distributor zone/area in which the large load is located)—the Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) function.

PJM’s original proposed tariff revisions to implement RBP did not specify the allowable composition of DR/DER resources for QRBPO purposes,1 and the original proposed IRAS tariff revisions appeared to expressly limit the composition of DR/DER resources to retail customer sites that had never before been registered toward a capacity resource.2 “Dormant” sites—those that had once supported a wholesale capacity resource but had since terminated participation—were disallowed, as were, it appeared, capacity increases at existing sites tied to new assets. These exclusions represented a departure from positions PJM had taken during the stakeholder process and amounted to undue discrimination between the treatment of DR/DER and utility-scale generation/storage, since PJM had proposed to include the repowering of deactivated generators and various forms of uprates/increments at utility-scale facilities as allowable new capacity for BYONC, QRBPO, and even RBP offer purposes, and had not 2 PJM IRAS Filing of Aug. 13, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3515 (hereinafter “PJM IRAS Filing”), proposed Tariff Attachment DD-4, section VI(b)(ii) (specifying BYONC resource eligibility for DR/DER); see also Comments of Voltus of Sept. 3, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3515 (hereinafter “Voltus IRAS Comments”) at § II.A, pp. 7-9. 1 PJM RBP Filing of July 31, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3380 (hereinafter “PJM RBP Filing”), proposed Tariff Attachment DD, section 18.2(b)(vi) (specifying QRBPO resource eligibility); see also Comments of Voltus of Aug. 21, 2026, in Docket No. ER26-3380 (hereinafter “Voltus RBP Comments”) at § II.A, pp. 4-5. 2

II.​
VOLTUS SUGGESTS A STRAIGHTFORWARD EDIT TO ADDRESS THE
IDENTIFIED DISCREPANCY
Voltus respectfully but strongly recommends one additional, critical revision to PJM’s proposed tariff language as revised by its RBP Answer, to align incentives and ensure a continuity in PJM’s overall approach: PJM’s QRBPO resource eligibility definition should allow for the reactivation of a “dormant” registered location, in line with the same allowance PJM has supported on the BYONC side.

The following straightforward edit to proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, section 18.2(b)(vi) would achieve the necessary alignment:

(vi) Planned Demand Resource or Planned DER Capacity Aggregation Resources, as described in the RAA, Schedule 6 and Schedule 6.2, where such incremental capacity are not reliant on locations which previously provided capacity for the 2026/2027 Delivery Year unless such previous location represents additional capacity resulting from an investment in new generation or new storage resources at that location.

The above redline takes as its starting point the updated language PJM proposed in its RBP Answer and implements the same definition of a “dormant” location that PJM provided in its IRAS Answer.

III.​
ENABLING THE SAME RESOURCES TO SUPPORT BOTH QRBPO
AND BYONC ALIGNS INCENTIVES AND SUPPORTS THE OVERALL
DESIGN
OF
PJM’S
LARGE
LOAD
RESOURCE
ADEQUACY
SOLUTIONS
Without this or similar adjustment in the final tariff filing, BYONC arrangements incorporating reactivated locations will not function to reduce the volume of RBP central procurement, resulting in duplicative matching of new capacity volumes to DY27/28 and DY28/29 load additions, along with the unnecessary imposition of socialized risk across all ratepayers in the PJM footprint. It is the shared preference of a wide range of stakeholders to shift capacity procurement from RBP to bilateral transactions so as to avoid this socialization and 4

PDF page 5

make good on the Ratepayer Protection Pledge by ensuring that large loads bear the costs associated with serving their growth. To effectuate this shift from centralized to private-market funding, the same bilateral contracts that relieve a load of IRAS exposure and return the load and its matched supply to RPM must also decrement the RBP target. This requires that the underlying BYONC and QRBPO resource eligibility criteria be the same. Indeed, for utility-scale generation and storage, PJM has defined eligibility with exactly the same tariff language for both functions.8 Thus each and every generation/storage MW that can support BYONC is also eligible as a QRBPO MW—provided, of course, that commercial operation date (“COD”) meets the QRBPO requirement. This same alignment is necessary on the DR/DER side, and it is Voltus’s understanding from stakeholder conversations with PJM staff that alignment of BYONC and QRBPO resource eligibility has been PJM’s ongoing design intention. Note also that the specific category here under consideration—the capabilities of dormant locations—is uniquely relevant in the context of QRBPO. As Voltus has explained elsewhere, DR/DER aggregations are functionally precluded from direct RBP participation.9 Yet they represent the set of resources with perhaps the earliest average commercial operation date (because DR/DER aggregations take advantage of capabilities already “latent” on the grid) and thus are among the most attractive resources for large load growth that will be subject to IRAS in the 27/28 and 28/29 Delivery Years. both exposures. Allowing dormant locations to qualify as QRBPO is therefore crucial to effectively incentivizing large loads and aggregators to reactivate this capacity. Note that without such allowance, a large load prioritizing RBP cost avoidance will prefer a new capacity resource with a June 1, 2032, COD over DR/DER reactivation capacity available in 2027/2028—a perverse outcome for grid reliability and ratepayer affordability.

 The volumes at issue are not small. Voltus estimates that dormant commercial and industrial locations are capable of providing in the range of 2 GW of new capacity given BYONC price signals.10 Incentivizing such capacity to be brought online in the next two delivery years will relieve grid strain immediately and improve near-term affordability. Indeed, new capacity contracted to come online in the 27/28 and 28/29 Delivery Years has a unique ratepayer affordability benefit. Beginning with Delivery Year 29/30, New Large Loads will be excluded from the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) by default and brought back to the Variable Resource Requirement (“VRR”) curve only if matched by new capacity that enters the relevant auction as a price taker. The expected impact will be price neutral. But since New Large Loads have not been excluded from 27/28 and 28/29 RPM, any new capacity contracted in time to enter, as price taker, the 27/28 and 28/29 Incremental Auctions will exert downward pressure on the capacity costs borne by all ratepayers as 27/28 and 28/29 RPM cost allocation, with the potential to provide immediate relief.

Counting demand-side reactivations as QRBPO will have the additional impact of protecting “native” load from the risk of RBP cost-shifting over the next 15 years. For all these reasons, affording DR/DER reactivations equivalent BYONC and QRBPO treatment is not a drafting detail but an impactful policy choice.

V.​
CONCLUSION
PJM has proposed to allow reactivated DR/DER locations for BYONC but not QRBPO purposes, introducing a discrepancy that impedes the overall design of the RBP/IRAS framework. The impact is highly material, because reactivations are among the only resources available to match 27/28 and 28/29 large load additions, and they are available to do so in significant volumes. However, because DR/DER resources are functionally excluded from RBP, DR/DER reactivations are only able to match large load additions and reduce IRAS exposure via bilateral contracts.  Unless these same bilaterals also reduce RBP cost exposure, large loads will be disincentivized from procuring DR/DER reactivations as compared to other resources. The final result will be less new capacity coming online over the next several delivery years and a reduction in the volume of emergency DR available for PJM to dispatch. The negative impacts on reliability, near-term affordability via RPM price impacts, and long-term RBP cost-shifting risk may be avoided by the simple tariff revision Voltus has recommended herein. Voltus respectfully asks that PJM take up such revision, or similar, with Commission support. Voltus deeply appreciates PJM’s ongoing collaboration with stakeholders on effective RBP/IRAS design and thanks the Commission for this opportunity to supplement the record.
