The record in this proceeding demonstrates that the existing PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. 
(“PJM”) Open Access Transmission Tariff (“PJM Tariff”) remains just and reasonable and that co-

recommend adopting a proposed definition of co-located load clarifying that it is Network Load,

in the Indicated TOs’ initial response accomplishes that goal.

this Federal Power Act (“FPA”) section 206 proceeding.  The Commission should act promptly to 
provide interested stakeholders much needed and much desired certainty.

a. The Existing PJM Tariff Is Just and Reasonable. 
PJM—the administrator of the PJM markets and the PJM Tariff—and virtually all of the 
PJM transmission owners, including investor-owned utilities, municipalities, and cooperatives, are 
in agreement: the “existing Tariff remains just and reasonable.”5  As PJM explains, “[t]he Tariff 
today permits co-located loads to interconnect through existing load interconnection processes.”6  
Those end-use load connection processes, governed by the states and fully consistent with the PJM 
Tariff, are available to all, and those processes work.  Moreover, the transmission service provided 
to co-located load under the PJM Tariff is available to all on a non-discriminatory basis.  Nowhere 
in the record is there an allegation—let alone evidence—that the current PJM Tariff impedes the 
development of or service to any load, co-located or otherwise.  Therefore, there is no basis to alter 
the existing PJM Tariff under section 206 of the FPA.  
In a proceeding under section 206 of the FPA, the Commission can only remedy a “rate 
[or] charge” or “any rule, regulation, practice, or contract affecting such rate [or] charge” after it 
is found to be “unjust [or] unreasonable.”7  “The burden of proving an unjust or unreasonable rate 
rests with the party that initiated the proceeding—the Commission [(as in this case)] or the third-
party complainant.”8  Only after a successful showing that the prevailing rate is unjust and 
unreasonable may the Commission order a replacement rate.9  No such showing has been made 
here.

b. Behind the Meter Generation Is Not Similarly Situated to Co-Located Load. 
 
Although the vast majority of PJM transmission owners, including traditional transmission 
owners, municipalities and cooperatives are aligned, a single transmission owner also claims that 
“[a]n approach that aligns with cost causation principles is one that treats all co-located customers 
as [Network Integration Transmission Service] customers with netting rules applied” and that “this 
is the current construct for [Behind the Meter Generation (“BTMG”)] customers under the PJM 
Tariff.”15  Notwithstanding this allegation, the two configurations—BTMG and co-located load—
remain distinct.  Accordingly, reference to behind the meter generation is wholly inapposite to the 
co-located load inquiry here.    
Netting is permitted under the PJM Tariff for BTMG when load takes Network Integration 
Transmission Service (“Network Service”) from a network service customer and the load has its 
own generator.16  Netting occurs when a generator provides power directly to a load and the billing 
determinant used to compute the Network Service costs for that network load is reduced by the 
quantity of power consumption offset by its BTMG.  By way of example, electric cooperatives in

As explained by the Indicated TOs, there is no such thing as data center load configured to be 
“fully isolated” behind the meter through “system protection facilities,” because co-located load 
that is connected to the electric grid (a) necessarily relies on reliability services, such as ancillary 
services, including black start; (b) retains the potential to draw real or reactive power from the grid 
at any time; and (c) relies on and benefits from its connection to the transmission system at all 
times.25  In other words, there is no such thing as a co-located configuration, connected to and 
synchronized with the electric grid, that is “fully isolated.”  While protection systems may serve 
important roles, they do not isolate the load from the grid, and the grid must be maintained—with 
the attendant costs—to accommodate this reality. 
Indeed, PJM has recognized this, stating that it 
objects to the [Commission’s] use of the phrase “wholly isolated 
from the grid” [in a question] as it is contrary to engineering and 
economic principles.  The notion that any of the proposed co-
location arrangements can be “wholly isolated” is misleading.  The 
load and generator are connected to and synchronized with the 
multi-state bulk power system.  It is not operating as a micro grid 
(nor can it if the generating facility is a nuclear unit).  Nor is the 
generator delivering power through an intermediary to a load with a 
breaker open.  Further, by way of example, there are obligations to 
ensure power to nuclear plants to ensure safe shutdown (among 
other things).  The load and generator are benefiting from ancillary 
services (including but not limited to black start).  Any system 
protection facilities are designed to only handle less complex N-1 
conditions, and can misoperate (and indeed have misoperated).26

II. 
No Replacement Rate Is Necessary or Appropriate Under FPA Section 206. 
a. PJM’s Potential Options Are Unnecessary to Resolve the Show Cause 
Proceeding. 
 
The Indicated TOs appreciate PJM laying out several “potential options for additional large 
load co-location.”28  However, it is important to emphasize that the mere presentation of potential 
options does not establish that the existing PJM Tariff is unjust and unreasonable and therefore 
cannot be the basis upon which to impose a replacement rate.  In this case, moreover, the options 
are not of all one kind—some are fully available options now; others are not practical.  In fact, 
four of the eight—options 1, 2, 3, and 8 (described below)—are currently available without any

The potential options PJM presents are as follows: 
1. Load with a separate Point of Interconnection that is Network Load and the load uses 
and pays for Network Service, Energy, Ancillary Services, and Capacity.  
2. Co-located load that is at the same Point of Interconnection but separately metered that 
is Network Load and the load uses and pays for Network Service, Energy, Ancillary 
Services, and Capacity.  Additionally, the large load is studied and could be connected 
to the grid pursuant to the existing Transmission Owner/electric distribution company 
(“EDC”) load interconnection process. 
3. Existing BTMG where the load is the customer, the BTMG rules apply, and the load

the net withdrawal quantity and dependent on if that withdrawal is during the Peak.

energy to serve the load, but relies on and takes grid services such as ancillary services 
and should pay its fair share of those charges, and carries a risk that should system 
protections fail, significant energy would be drawn from the system.

III. 
Vistra’s Motion for Clarification Should Be Denied. 
On April 4, 2025, Vistra filed a Motion for Clarification and Request for Expedited 
Treatment.  Vistra seeks the Commission’s interpretation of its order issued in November 2024 in 
Docket No. ER24-2172 (“Susquehanna ISA Proceeding”).39  According to Vistra, there is 
confusion on the scope and holding of the Susquehanna ISA Order.  The Commission should reject 
Vistra’s motion.  
In the Susquehanna ISA Proceeding, the Commission rejected the amended 
interconnection agreement between Susquehanna Nuclear LLC, PJM, and PPL Electric Utilities 
Corporation because the Commission found that “PJM has not demonstrated that the proposed   
non-conforming provisions in the Amended ISA are necessary deviations from the pro forma ISA 
due to specific reliability concerns, novel legal issues, or other unique factors.”40 
On April 10, 2025, the Commission issued in Docket No. ER24-2172, an Order Addressing 
Arguments on Rehearing and Granting Clarification, In Part.41  Therein, the Commission 
addressed Vistra’s request for clarification in the Susquehanna ISA Proceeding.  In Susquehanna 
ISA Rehearing Order, the Commission clarified that it “uses the necessary standard . . . to 
determine whether the filing of a non-conforming agreement is justified” and that the Susquehanna 
ISA Order “does not prevent entities from filing non-conforming interconnection agreements to 
address matters related to a generator serving co-located load, or prejudge any such future filing.”42
