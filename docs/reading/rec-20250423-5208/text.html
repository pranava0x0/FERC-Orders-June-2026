a. The Existing PJM Tariff Is Just and Reasonable.
PJM—the administrator of the PJM markets and the PJM Tariff—and virtually all of the PJM transmission owners, including investor-owned utilities, municipalities, and cooperatives, are in agreement: the “existing Tariff remains just and reasonable.”5  As PJM explains, “[t]he Tariff today permits co-located loads to interconnect through existing load interconnection processes.”6 Those end-use load connection processes, governed by the states and fully consistent with the PJM Tariff, are available to all, and those processes work.  Moreover, the transmission service provided to co-located load under the PJM Tariff is available to all on a non-discriminatory basis.  Nowhere in the record is there an allegation—let alone evidence—that the current PJM Tariff impedes the development of or service to any load, co-located or otherwise.  Therefore, there is no basis to alter the existing PJM Tariff under section 206 of the FPA.

In a proceeding under section 206 of the FPA, the Commission can only remedy a “rate [or] charge” or “any rule, regulation, practice, or contract affecting such rate [or] charge” after it is found to be “unjust [or] unreasonable.”7  “The burden of proving an unjust or unreasonable rate rests with the party that initiated the proceeding—the Commission [(as in this case)] or the third- party complainant.”8  Only after a successful showing that the prevailing rate is unjust and unreasonable may the Commission order a replacement rate.9  No such showing has been made here.

b. Behind the Meter Generation Is Not Similarly Situated to Co-Located Load.

Although the vast majority of PJM transmission owners, including traditional transmission owners, municipalities and cooperatives are aligned, a single transmission owner also claims that “[a]n approach that aligns with cost causation principles is one that treats all co-located customers as [Network Integration Transmission Service] customers with netting rules applied” and that “this is the current construct for [Behind the Meter Generation (“BTMG”)] customers under the PJM Tariff.”15  Notwithstanding this allegation, the two configurations—BTMG and co-located load— remain distinct.  Accordingly, reference to behind the meter generation is wholly inapposite to the co-located load inquiry here.

Transmission Service (“Network Service”) from a network service customer and the load has its own generator.16  Netting occurs when a generator provides power directly to a load and the billing determinant used to compute the Network Service costs for that network load is reduced by the quantity of power consumption offset by its BTMG. II. No Replacement Rate Is Necessary or Appropriate Under FPA Section 206.
a. PJM’s Potential Options Are Unnecessary to Resolve the Show Cause
Proceeding.

The Indicated TOs appreciate PJM laying out several “potential options for additional large load co-location.”28  However, it is important to emphasize that the mere presentation of potential options does not establish that the existing PJM Tariff is unjust and unreasonable and therefore cannot be the basis upon which to impose a replacement rate.  In this case, moreover, the options are not of all one kind—some are fully available options now; others are not practical.  In fact, four of the eight—options 1, 2, 3, and 8 (described below)—are currently available without any

III. Vistra’s Motion for Clarification Should Be Denied.
On April 4, 2025, Vistra filed a Motion for Clarification and Request for Expedited Treatment.  Vistra seeks the Commission’s interpretation of its order issued in November 2024 in Docket No. ER24-2172 (“Susquehanna ISA Proceeding”).39  According to Vistra, there is confusion on the scope and holding of the Susquehanna ISA Order.  The Commission should reject Vistra’s motion.

In the Susquehanna ISA Proceeding, the Commission rejected the amended
interconnection agreement between Susquehanna Nuclear LLC, PJM, and PPL Electric Utilities Corporation because the Commission found that “PJM has not demonstrated that the proposed non-conforming provisions in the Amended ISA are necessary deviations from the pro forma ISA due to specific reliability concerns, novel legal issues, or other unique factors.”40 On April 10, 2025, the Commission issued in Docket No. ER24-2172, an Order Addressing Arguments on Rehearing and Granting Clarification, In Part.41  Therein, the Commission addressed Vistra’s request for clarification in the Susquehanna ISA Proceeding.  In Susquehanna ISA Rehearing Order, the Commission clarified that it “uses the necessary standard . . . to determine whether the filing of a non-conforming agreement is justified” and that the Susquehanna ISA Order “does not prevent entities from filing non-conforming interconnection agreements to address matters related to a generator serving co-located load, or prejudge any such future filing.”42
