EL26-69-000 · 2026-07-20 · New York Independent System Operator, Inc.

NYISO reports on resource adequacy and large-load proposals

NYISO files its 30-day informational report on resource adequacy for new large loads. It says its markets and planning processes remain adequate, reports a possible statewide shortfall above 1,800 MW from 2033, and lists reforms under stakeholder review.

Answers the order. Answers the E-12 30-day directive for an informational report on how NYISO will ensure adequate generation for existing and new large loads, with proposals and a schedule.

E-12 NYISO: 30-day report on serving large loads with adequate generation (p. 7)

Key points

  1. NYISO says its existing markets and planning processes remain available even though large load growth presents challenges.
    the NYISO's markets and existing planning processes remain available to address resource adequacy. PDF p. 1
  2. Preliminary 2026 RNA results could show a statewide resource adequacy violation from 2033, exceeding 1,800 MW by 2036 with large load growth.
    the NYISO could identify a statewide resource adequacy criterion violation that would first occur in 2033 and increase through 2036 with a deficiency greater than 1,800 MW PDF p. 4
  3. The interconnection queue holds 89 completed projects over 11 GW awaiting operation and a 91-project, 15 GW transition cluster.
    the New York region has 89 projects, representing over 11 GW of generation, that have completed the interconnection study process but have yet to enter commercial operation. PDF p. 5
  4. For regulated generation solutions, the NYPSC decides cost allocation, including for shortfalls caused by a large load.
    The allocation of the costs for the development of the regulated generation or demand response solution would be determined by the NYPSC PDF p. 8
  5. NYISO is discussing an accelerated interconnection study path for resources needed to address an identified reliability need.
    a proposal to establish an accelerated interconnection study path for resources that are needed to address a reliability need PDF p. 10
  6. NYISO is considering assigning responsibility for a resource deficiency to a large load that directly contributes to it.
    If the NYISO identifies a resource deficiency and the large load(s) directly contributes to such deficiency, then the NYISO is considering assigning the PDF p. 11
  7. Planning reforms would go to committees in January to March 2027, with a section 205 filing around March 2027.
    with a filing submitted to the Commission under Section 205 of the FPA in or around March 2027, subject to necessary approvals. PDF p. 13

Why the New York City need changed

An updated demand forecast removed a previously identified actionable New York City need. NYISO still saw longer-term risk from retirements, load growth and dependence on imports.

NYISO says faster queue studies cannot resolve the siting, financing and supply-chain obstacles that decide whether a project is actually built.
