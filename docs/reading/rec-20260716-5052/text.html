PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms
Docket No. AD26-7-000
Supplemental Comment of Persistence Analytics Group LLC / United Grid Regarding
PJM Governance, Large-Load Verification, and Ratepayer Protection
Persistence Analytics Group LLC / United Grid respectfully submits this supplemental comment in Docket No. AD26-7-000 in response to the Commission's Third Supplemental Notice of Commission-Led Technical Conference regarding PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms.

The Commission's notice frames the technical conference around concrete, actionable reforms to improve PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.'s ability to address operational and market needs in a timely and efficient manner. That is the correct focus.

However, PJM governance reform should not be limited to stakeholder voting rules, committee structure, document architecture, or internal process mechanics.

The deeper issue is that infrastructure assumptions are being converted into market outcomes and ratepayer obligations before those assumptions are sufficiently verified.

Core Infrastructure Assumptions at Issue
- large-load growth;
- data-center demand durability;
- resource adequacy;
- capacity-market scarcity;
- interconnection timing;
- transmission need;
- cost allocation;
- demand response and distributed energy resource performance;
- state authority; and
- ratepayer exposure.
Response to Panel 1 Questions 1 and 3: PJM Board Accountability and Governance
Reform
The PJM Board's role should include ensuring that major planning, market, and reliability decisions are supported by decision-grade evidence before they create durable financial obligations for ratepayers. Governance reform should therefore include a formal Infrastructure Assumption Verification requirement for major PJM actions involving large-load additions, capacity-market design, transmission planning, and cost allocation.

At minimum, PJM should be required to identify:
- What load is being treated as durable?
- What evidence supports that load?
- What evidence is missing?
- Which dependencies must hold?

- Which infrastructure costs are caused by specific customers or customer classes?
- Which costs are proposed to be socialized?
- Who benefits if the assumption is correct?
- Who bears downside if the assumption fails?
Without this verification layer, governance reform risks improving process speed without improving decision quality.

Response to Panel 1 Question 2: State Role
States must have access to decision-grade evidence when PJM decisions affect retail affordability, economic development, public policy, reliability, and state-regulated utilities.

State participation should not be limited to consultation after market or planning assumptions have already hardened. States should be able to review the underlying assumptions driving:

- large-load forecasts;
- data-center demand;
- transmission need;
- capacity-market exposure;
- cost-causation findings;
- resource adequacy risk; and
- ratepayer impacts.
A stronger state role should include structured access to the evidence supporting PJM's major assumptions, not merely access to stakeholder debate.

Response to Panel 2 Questions 2, 5, 7, and 8: Transparency, Stakeholder Process,
Delay, and Fast-Path Review
Transparency reform should include more than meeting access, voting records, and committee process. PJM should disclose, in a structured and usable format, the assumptions behind major decisions and the evidence supporting those assumptions.

For high-importance topics, including large-load integration, data-center growth, capacity-market scarcity, transmission expansion, and ratepayer exposure, PJM should use a time-bound review process that requires an assumption-verification screen before costs are imposed or market outcomes are treated as unavoidable. A fast-path process should not mean faster cost socialization. It should mean faster identification of:

- unverified load;
- weak cost-causation evidence;
- ratepayer exposure;
- implementation dependencies;
- demand-side alternatives; and
- risks that should not be shifted to customers without proof.
Recommended Concrete Reform

The Commission should require PJM governance reform to include an independent implementation-verification layer for major actions affecting reliability, markets, transmission, and ratepayer exposure. That layer should include:

1. Independent verification of large-load and data-center demand assumptions;
2. Transparent cost-causation review before cost socialization;
3. Ratepayer-protection screens before new obligations are imposed;
4. Minimum evidence standards for demand durability and load integrity;
5. State access to decision-grade planning evidence;
6. Review of demand-side alternatives before infrastructure obligations harden;
7. Time-bound review for critical issues without bypassing evidence quality; and
8. Accountability for failed assumptions.
