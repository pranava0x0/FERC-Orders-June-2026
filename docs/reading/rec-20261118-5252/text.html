II. BACKGROUND
A. The HILL Process

On January 14, 2026, the Commission accepted SPP's Tariff revisions establishing the HILL study process in Attachment BA and the HILLGA process in Attachment BB.11 Those revisions (1) defined HILLs as a new category of load; (2) established enhanced study requirements for HILLs, including the HDPS, to enable SPP to assess the reliability impacts of a HILL on the Transmission System; and (3) imposed ongoing operational requirements on HILLs to ensure the continued reliable operation of the Transmission System. Those operational requirements, in Attachment BA, Section 2.2, include: compliance with non-conforming load requirements; real- time telemetry; remote disconnect capability; a ramp-rate limitation; phasor measurement unit installation; ride-through requirements; and a prohibition on withdrawals without appropriate transmission service.12 The Commission found the HILL process to be “a just and reasonable approach for integrating transmission customers’ new large loads onto SPP’s transmission system in a reliable manner.”13

B. Generator Interconnections to Facilities Outside the Transmission System

Attachments V and AY of the Tariff govern interconnection to SPP’s
Transmission System.14 Those Attachments also include Affected System Study procedures for proposed interconnections to other transmission systems that may affect SPP’s Transmission System.15 Some generation is Behind-The-Meter Generation, co- located on-site, or connected to distribution or other facilities that are not part of the Transmission System. SPP currently addresses that type of generation through Business Practice 7250, which uses "non-jurisdictional" terminology. The Tariff does not define that term or use it consistently. As a result, the Tariff does not clearly state when such generation will be studied for its impact on the Transmission System, by whom, or through which study process. Nor does it identify the responsible party when such generation fails to register in the Integrated Marketplace and is not subject to an interconnection agreement with the Transmission Provider or a Transmission Owner.

III. DESCRIPTION OF AND JUSTIFICATION FOR THE LARGE LOAD
REVISIONS

The Large Load Revisions respond to the gaps the Commission identified and build on a framework the Commission has already found just and reasonable. They apply uniformly to all similarly situated customers and advance the Commission’s stated objectives of protecting consumers from unnecessary costs, safeguarding reliability, and improving transparency and consistency for customers, Transmission Owners, Market Participants, and federal and state regulators. III.A through III.C describe the HILL Transmission Service Revisions, Section III.D describes the Non- Transmission Facility Revisions, and Section III.E identifies the directives SPP is addressing in separate filings.

Under FPA Section 205, SPP must show that its proposal is just and reasonable. It need not show that the proposal is the only reasonable approach or that it is more reasonable than the alternatives.36 As Commissioner LaCerte observed, FPA Section 205 provides a “relatively capacious standard” under which the Commission may accept a broad range of proposals shown to be just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential.37 The Large Load Revisions meet that standard. For each concern the Commission identified, SPP:

1. places in the Tariff the binding obligation the Commission found missing;
2. ties that obligation to an existing process the Commission has already accepted (the HDPS, the pro forma service agreements, the STEP, the Affected System Study framework, and the Attachment AE registration requirements) instead of creating a parallel process; and

3. places implementation details, such as technology-specific evaluation methods and size- and impact-based study thresholds, in Business Practices that are developed and revised through SPP's stakeholder process.

Third, Section 2.1.3.4 of Attachment BA of the Proposed Tariff requires that the HDPS report include an explanation of the results of SPP’s GETs evaluation.54 Business Practice 7850 requires each HDPS report to explain the results for each identified technology and provides a model format that documents, technology by technology, whether the technology was selected, was less cost-effective than the selected mitigation, could not resolve the identified constraint, or was screened out for a stated technical reason.55 This provides the Eligible Customer the transparency the Commission called for in the Show Cause Order.56 Specifically, where SPP selects traditional Network Upgrades, the HDPS report will explain, technology by technology, whether the alternative technology would not resolve the identified reliability need, would not result in lower costs, or would not provide a faster timeline.

Finally, the revisions respect engineering judgment and do not mandate GETs. As the Commission directed, the revisions require evaluation, not deployment.57 Under the proposed language, SPP selects a GET only when doing so is consistent with Good Utility Practice, Applicable Reliability Standards, and other Applicable Laws and Regulations.58 This protects reliability for existing customers while ensuring cost- effective, faster solutions are not overlooked and is consistent with the Commission’s statement that it is not mandating the use of alternative transmission technologies, as well as the goal, articulated in Commissioner See’s concurrence, of respecting transmission providers’ engineering judgments while protecting against unnecessary upgrade costs.59 By requiring SPP to consider lower-cost and faster-to-deploy alternatives before selecting traditional Network Upgrades, the proposed revision also addresses the Commission’s concern that large load driven Network Upgrade costs rolled into transmission revenue requirements may be borne in part by other transmission customers.
