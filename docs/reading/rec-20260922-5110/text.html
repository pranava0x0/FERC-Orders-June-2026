Pursuant to Rule 213(a)(2) of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners2 respectfully move for leave to submit this answer in response to the answer of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) filed on September 10, 2026.3 This proposed answer addresses a narrow but important issue:  whether it violates cost- causation principles to impose new exit fees related to a generation/capacity product on a transmission owner (“TO”) as opposed to a load-serving entity (“LSE”).  PJM’s answer does not confront that issue or the arguments raised by the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners in their initial protest in the instant proceeding.  PJM does, however, concede that the cause of

I. MOTION FOR LEAVE TO ANSWER
Just as the Commission’s rules do not permit as of right PJM’s proposed answer to the various protests in this Docket, they do not permit as of right the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ proposed answer to PJM’s proposed answer.5  The Commission nonetheless retains authority to permit such an answer in its discretion.  The Commission should do so here because the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ proposed answer highlights an important concession in PJM’s proposed answer that bears on cost-causation principles that PJM otherwise does not address.

II. PROPOSED ANSWER
The Indicated PJM Transmission Owners’ protest explained that, under cost-causation principles and the Commission’s precedent applying them, RBP-related exit fees cannot be imposed on TOs as opposed to LSEs:  “The Commission has never allocated generation- and capacity-related costs to TOs and, more specifically, the Exit Amendment [of the RBP] conflicts with highly relevant precedent in the Duquesne Light Co. proceeding where the

Commission notably did not apply generation-related costs to an exiting TO in its capacity as a TO,” but only in its capacity as an LSE.6

PJM’s answer does not address this argument or the relevant portion of Duquesne Light Company.7  It does, however, concede that loads and LSEs are the causes of the RBP:  “Given that the RBP is to address the capacity shortfall from the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 RPM Auctions, which is primarily driven by the unprecedented addition of large loads (in the form of Load Adjustments), the loads causing the RBP—through their LSEs—should bear the costs of the RBP Resources throughout the full term.”8 (emphasis added).

PJM does not and cannot explain how it is consistent with Duquesne Light Company and cost-causation principles to impose RBP exit fees on a TO as opposed to an LSE.  At a minimum, therefore, even if all of the RBP’s other deficiencies could be overlooked, the Commission should strip out the exit fee provision before the RBP is allowed to go into effect.

III. CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners respectfully request that the Commission accept this answer and reject PJM’s proposed Exit Amendment. Dated:  September 22, 2026
