EL26-71-000 · 2026-08-03 · California Independent System Operator Corporation

CAISO requests more time to answer the June 18 show cause order

CAISO moves to hold the show cause proceeding in abeyance for 90 days. It lays out a stakeholder schedule leading to a November 16, 2026 FPA section 205 filing, made together with its Participating Transmission Owners.

Asks FERC to: Hold the EL26-71 section 206 proceeding in abeyance for 90 days, until November 16, 2026, while CAISO and the Participating Transmission Owners prepare section 205 filings.

Changes the schedule. Requests the up-to-90-day abeyance option in the E-10 CAISO show cause order, offering the description of a future section 205 filing and the timeline the order required. FERC granted it August 14, 2026.

E-10 CAISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5) E-10 CAISO: Application / study process (P 5, p. 25)

Key points

  1. CAISO wants 90 days from the response deadline to November 16, 2026, aligned with the Participating Transmission Owners' requests.
    hold the instant proceeding in abeyance for 90 days from the deadline to respond PDF p. 1
  2. The filing is meant to address all preliminary findings and five reform categories in the order, with simultaneous PTO filings.
    will address all the preliminary findings and five categories of reforms set forth in the Order to Show Cause, as appropriate for the CAISO region. PDF p. 5
  3. CAISO's Large Load stakeholder initiative is the vehicle; an issue paper came in January 2026 and a technical-requirements straw proposal in June 2026.
    The CAISO’s ongoing Large Load stakeholder initiative will become the vehicle for CAISO reforms to address the Order to Show Cause. PDF p. 5
  4. Most tariff changes should take effect on approval; software-dependent items such as new settlement charges may take longer.
    The CAISO expects that the vast majority of its tariff revisions will be able to become effective as soon as approved by the Commission. PDF p. 5
  5. The schedule runs a straw proposal in August, a draft final in September, governing body votes October 20 and 28, and a November 16 FERC filing.
    two discrete proposals: a straw proposal in August and draft final proposal in September, before seeking Western Energy Market Governing Body and Board of Governors approval PDF p. 7
  6. CAISO and the PTOs keep the existing split of responsibilities and coordinate weekly.
    including multiple weekly meetings among various teams of subject-matter experts for the categories of issues identified by the Commission. PDF p. 9
