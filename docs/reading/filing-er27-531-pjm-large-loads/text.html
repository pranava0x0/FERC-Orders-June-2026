I. INTRODUCTION
As it is elsewhere in the country, data center construction and operation is causing unprecedented load growth in the PJM Region.26  PJM’s 2025 long-term load forecast shows a peak load growth of 32 gigawatts (“GW”) from 2024 to 2030, with 30 GW of that growth coming from data centers.27  PJM’s 2026 long-term load forecast projects a 3.6% annual increase in its summer peak load over the next 10 years;28 in 2023, PJM projected just a 0.8% annual increase over a 10-15 year horizon.29  The extraordinary growth of data centers and other Large Loads presents unique reliability and resource adequacy challenges as compared to traditional loads.  Data centers have significant energy and capacity demands and can be built faster (12-18 months) than the generation and transmission facilities necessary to meet those demands.30  The North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) recognizes this as a resource adequacy issue, explaining that Large Loads are “magnitudes larger than most loads historically seen on the system and
