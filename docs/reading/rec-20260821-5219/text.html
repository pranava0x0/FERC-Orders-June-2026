POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF CTC GLOBAL 
 
CTC Global Corporation (“CTC”)1 respectfully submits these comments in response to the 
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission” or “FERC”) Notice Requesting Post-
Technical Conference Comments concerning PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) governance 
and stakeholder processes. 
I. 
Comments  
CTC agrees that governance structure, Board independence, stakeholder participation, 
transparency, and clearly allocated decision-making authority are important. But governance 
reform should ultimately be judged by results: whether PJM is better able to identify urgent system 
needs, evaluate available solutions, make decisions, and implement those decisions on timelines 
responsive to the reliability and affordability challenges facing the region. 
One of the clearest tests of effective governance is PJM’s ability to respond to the rapidly 
increasing need for transmission capability. PJM faces extraordinary electricity demand growth

A. Governance Reform Should Be Evaluated by Whether It Enables PJM to Address 
Urgent Reliability, Affordability, and Transmission Needs 
Many of the challenges currently confronting PJM—resource adequacy, generation 
interconnection, large-load integration, affordability, and reliability—are closely linked to the 
availability of sufficient transmission capability. New generation cannot reliably serve customers 
if the transmission system cannot accommodate and deliver its output. Likewise, even well-
designed energy and capacity markets cannot function, much less produce their full benefits, if 
inadequate transmission prevents resources from responding to market signals. Although PJM and 
the PJM Board have taken steps to increase generation, neither has taken significant steps to either 
accelerate transmission development or hasten transmission capacity increases by upgrading 
existing infrastructure in existing rights-of-ways.  
While PJM needs to significantly increase the region’s transmission development, not 
every transmission need requires a new transmission corridor. A central part of meeting PJM’s 
near-term challenge should be to establish timely mechanisms to evaluate the full range of 
technically mature solutions that can maximize the capability of infrastructure already in service 
and of existing transmission rights-of-way. Mature, widely deployed advanced transmission

Advanced reconductoring is an important example. Carbon composite core advanced 
conductors can substantially increase the capacity of existing transmission corridors while 
reducing electrical resistance and conductor sag. In many applications, reconductoring can use 
existing structures and existing rights-of-way to double the amount of electricity that can move on 
the line while reducing system losses by up-to 40%.  Reconductoring, which can be done with live 
lines, can be implemented on timelines materially shorter than construction of new greenfield 
transmission. These attributes are particularly important where the system is in critical need of 
significant additional capability within the next few years rather than the next decade.  These same 
attributes enabled by the carbon core of advanced conductors, allow for shorter towers and longer 
spans (fewer towers) on both line rebuilds and new transmission lines, reducing siting risk and 
project costs while improving the performance capabilities of the line.   
The governance question for the Commission is therefore not whether PJM should select 
any particular technology. It is whether PJM’s governing structures and stakeholder processes 
permit the organization to identify urgent needs, evaluate the full range of technically mature 
solutions, and reach timely decisions based upon capacity, reliability, cost and deployment 
time. With these measures, PJM will be able to move resolutely past its current resource adequacy 
and governance crisis.  
B. PJM Should Establish Time-Bound Processes for Critical System Needs and Ensure 
Mature Transmission Technologies Receive Timely, Technically Sound Evaluation 
The Commission specifically asked whether PJM should make greater use of fast-path or 
time-bound procedures for matters of high importance. CTC believes it should. For designated

critical reliability, transmission-capacity, or load-growth issues, PJM should establish a process 
containing, at a minimum: 
1. Clearly defined system need and planning criteria that recognize that the speed at which 
new transmission capacity can come online is a critical component for both reliability and 
consumer costs; 
2. A defined, reasonably expedited timetable for technical analysis and stakeholder input; 
3. Technology-inclusive, performance-based evaluation of mature alternatives that is not 
skewed towards legacy technologies; 
4. Transparent assumptions and changes to modeling sufficient to permit meaningful 
comparison of alternatives; 
5. Fixed and transparent decision date identified early in the process and adhered to; and 
6. Clearly identified decision authority if the stakeholder process does not produce 
consensus within the prescribed period. 
The objective should not be to circumvent stakeholder participation. It should be to prevent 
stakeholder process from becoming an indefinite substitute for decision-making.  
For transmission needs in particular, PJM’s analysis should be capable of comparing 
alternatives based not simply on initial capital cost, but on the amount of capacity delivered, time 
to deployment, lifecycle cost, electrical losses, reliability performance, constructability, use 
of existing structures and rights-of-way, and siting requirements.  Governance procedures 
should also ensure that PJM has access to the models and technical information necessary to 
accurately represent mature transmission technologies like carbon composite core advanced 
conductors. 
This is especially important for technologies that can materially increase the capability of 
existing infrastructure.  PJM should have governance and planning processes that allow advanced 
conductors to be evaluated quickly and on comparable technical and economic terms when they 
may provide a faster or less costly solution.

The Commission’s agenda specifically identified fast-path and time-bound review 
procedures for critical issues as a potential governance reform. Such a process would directly 
advance the objectives identified by the Commission in this proceeding: improving PJM’s 
responsiveness, transparency and ability to take necessary action in a timely manner.  
Other stakeholders raised, in their pre-technical conference comments, the need for greater 
oversight of PJM’s transmission planning. Some asked that PJM consider an Independent 
Transmission Monitor.3 Others asked that PJM implement tariff reforms that would require the 
immediate evaluation of alternative transmission technologies to maximize existing transmission 
system capacity.4 CTC broadly supports emphasizing the critical role of new technologies, 
including advanced conductors, in PJM governance, including the stakeholder process and before 
the PJM Board.  The actions necessary to rapidly increase PJM’s transmission capacity to meet its 
burgeoning need will not occur without greater inclusion of advanced transmission technologies 
in the solution.  
C.  The PJM Board's Collective Qualifications Should Include Transmission Planning, 
Grid Operations, and Technology Deployment Expertise 
CTC also believes that PJM’s Board qualification criteria should reflect the extraordinary 
operational and infrastructure challenges now facing the region.  This does not require every Board 
member to possess the same technical background, nor should it diminish the importance of Board 
independence. Rather, the Board should possess a mix of competencies sufficient to oversee one 
of the largest and most complex electric systems in the world.

That competency matrix should expressly include meaningful experience in areas such as

important in an environment where decisions involving transmission capability, resource

The Board should also receive regular, transparent reporting on PJM’s performance in 
addressing critical system needs. Relevant metrics could include the time between identification 
of a system need and a planning decision; the alternatives evaluated; anticipated capacity provided; 
projected cost; expected in-service date; and progress toward implementation. 
This would connect governance reform to measurable outcomes rather than treating 
governance structure as an end. 
II. 
Conclusion 
CTC supports reforms that make PJM more independent, responsive, transparent and 
capable of timely action. The ultimate measure of those reforms should be whether they improve 
PJM’s ability to solve the operational and market challenges confronting the region. 
PJM faces a period in which electricity demand and transmission needs are growing much 
faster than traditional infrastructure-development timelines can comfortably accommodate. 
Governance reform should therefore empower PJM to identify urgent needs, evaluate the full range 
of mature solutions, make timely decisions, and hold itself accountable for implementation. 
For transmission, that means not only planning new infrastructure where it is required but 
also maximizing the capability of the infrastructure already in place. Mature, advanced 
transmission technologies—including advanced conductors capable of substantially increasing 
capacity within existing transmission corridors—can be an important part of that effort. A
