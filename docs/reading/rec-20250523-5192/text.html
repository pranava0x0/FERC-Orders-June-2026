Pursuant to Rules 212 and 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the 
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC” or “Commission”), 18 C.F.R. §§ 
385.212 and 213 (2025), Public Service Electric and Gas Company (“PSE&G”)1 hereby 
submits this Motion for Leave to Answer and Answer to the initial and reply comments 
that have been filed in response to the Commission’s Show Cause Order issued on 
February 20, 2025 (the “Show Cause Order”).2   
In response to the Show Cause Order, a number of comments have been filed.  
Based on those comments, as well as the comments submitted in other dockets regarding 
co-located load arrangements over the past year, the Commission now has a very 
comprehensive, robust record before it upon which to establish just and reasonable rules

actual use of the transmission system would not comport with cost

I. 
MOTION FOR LEAVE TO ANSWER 
While Rule 213 of the Commission’s Practice and Procedure does not provide for 
answers to answers, the Commission permits such pleadings where, as here, the 
information provided will facilitate the Commission’s decision-making process and aid in 
the resolution of issues.3  There is good cause for the Commission to accept this Answer 
because it will clarify certain issues and assist the Commission with its decision-making.

A. 
Any Transmission Pricing Rules Governing Co-Location 
Arrangements Must Comply With Well-Established Cost Causation 
Principles.  
A bedrock ratemaking principle that the Commission and the courts have 
articulated and applied under the Federal Power Act over a number of decades is that 
“rates and charges must reflect to some degree the costs actually caused by the customer 
who must pay for them,”5 and that costs must be allocated in a manner that is “roughly 
commensurate” with benefits that entity receives.6  This is such a core ratemaking 
principle that is well understood and accepted such that the Court of Appeals for the 
Seventh Circuit has characterized it as really an “unremarkable principle.”  In a 2009 
decision, the Seventh Circuit instructed that under this cost causation principle, “FERC is 
not authorized to approve a pricing scheme that requires a group of  utilities to pay for 
facilities from which its members derive no benefits, or benefits that are trivial in relation

B. 
The Commission Should Ensure That Any Ruling Should Not Adversely 
Impact BTMG Customers, And Any Disparate Treatment of BTM Load 
Would Be Unjustified and Unduly Discriminatory. 
As PSE&G explained in its initial comments, there are a number of customers in 
PSE&G’s service territory and across the PJM region that have BTMG to meet a portion 
or all of their power needs, including universities, industrial customers, pharmaceutical 
companies and rooftop solar customers.13  Under the PJM Tariff, those customers are 
allowed to net their BTMG against their Network Load for purposes of paying 
transmission and other charges.  In approving these BTMG provisions, the Commission 
applied cost causation principles by recognizing that BTMG customers use the PJM grid 
to a lesser degree and should be responsible for paying transmission charges based on 
their actual or net use of the system.14  Importantly, the Commission explained that the

BTMG provisions in the PJM Tariff (and the netting approach) would encourage loads to 
reduce their use of the PJM transmission system and consequently the need for new 
transmission facilities.  The Commission’s longstanding policy is to encourage BTMG co-
location arrangements to reduce use of the transmission system and the need for new 
transmission facilities to be constructed.  This paradigm benefits all transmission 
customers.  In this proceeding, the Commission should ensure that customers who have 
BTMG or onsite generation are not negatively impacted by any new rules that the 
Commission may require.   
In addition, as relevant to this proceeding, co-located BTM load arrangements use 
the transmission system in the same way as BTMG customers.  Specifically, both 
arrangements involve load and generation co-locating at the same site.  This results in a 
similar reduced use of the transmission system and a reduced need for transmission 
upgrades.15  As such, based on the Commission’s comparability principles, both 
arrangements should be treated in the same manner and pay for transmission services 
based on their actual system usage (i.e., based on net use of the transmission system).  To 
treat BTM co-located load and BTMG differently would be improper, unjust and 
unreasonable and unduly discriminatory under the Federal Power Act.

III. 
CONCLUSION  
For the reasons discussed herein, the Commission should expeditiously issue a 
decision that provides timely and needed clarity with respect to co-located load rules and 
that leads to a result that is (i) consistent with cost causation principles; (ii) avoids 
harming impacting existing BTMG customers in PJM and treats BTM load in a non-
discriminatory manner; (iii) provides for regulatory flexibility and optionality to support 
data center development; and (iv) establishes a concrete schedule for the implementation 
of final co-location rules.
