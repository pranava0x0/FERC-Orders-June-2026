(“Allegheny”) respectfully tenders for filing to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or “Commission”) this Answer in the above- captioned proceeding concerning the treatment of large loads located at the same site as generating facilities (“Co-Located Load”) within the PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) footprint.  Allegheny submits this Answer pursuant to Rule 213 of the FERC Rules of Practice and Procedure1 and the Commission’s February 20, 2025 Order Instituting Proceeding Under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act and Consolidating with Other Proceedings (“Show Cause Order”).2  The Show Cause Order directs PJM as well as each of the PJM Transmission Owners (“TOs”) to address the treatment of Co-Located Load by PJM’s Open Access Transmission

Tariff, the Amended and Restated Operating Agreement of PJM, and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities in the PJM Region (collectively, “PJM Tariff”). Specifically, the Show Cause Order directs PJM and the TOs to either (1) show cause that the PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential without rates, terms, and conditions of service specific to Co-Located Load or (2) explain what changes to the Tariff would be necessary to address Co-Located Load.  In the Answer below, Allegheny acknowledges both the growth of interest in Co-Located Load and the importance of clarity in transmission rate and policies as to the treatment of Co-Located Load within the PJM Tariff. The Show Cause Order is the culmination of a number of contentious proceedings before the Commission addressing issues related to Co-Located Load.  In these proceedings, market participants have espoused a number of views as to (1) whether specific configurations of Co- Located Load may sufficiently sever itself from the transmission grid such that it receives no benefits from the grid, (2) whether Co-Located Load should be assessed transmission charges,

(3) what rates, terms, and conditions of transmission service would encourage or discourage Co- Located Load, and (4) whether the PJM Tariff should be revised to accommodate Co-Located Load.  Allegheny itself has taken no position on these questions, and it remains uncommitted as to whether the current PJM Tariff sufficiently addresses Co-Located Load and the more fundamental question of whether Co-Located Load should be allocated transmission costs. Ultimately, by the conclusion of this proceeding, the Commission will have a substantial record on which to determine whether to direct revisions to the PJM Tariff to accommodate Co-Located Load.  With the voluminous record in the consolidated proceedings and the burgeoning number of proceedings related to the issue of Co-Located Load, Allegheny is certain that the need for clarity from the Commission is both essential and urgent.

I. PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

This Answer is submitted pursuant to Rule 213 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Commission and the Commission’s February 20, 2025 Show Cause Order, which established March 24, 2025 as the date by which PJM and the TOs are required to submit answers in this Section 206 proceeding.

II. DESCRIPTION OF ALLEGHENY
Allegheny is a non-profit electric generation and transmission cooperative corporation (“G&T Cooperative”), incorporated under the Electric Cooperative Law of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, and it is the exclusive wholesale supplier of its 14 distribution cooperative members.  Similar to other G&T Cooperatives, Allegheny is owned entirely by its members.  Its Board of Directors, which governs Allegheny, is comprised exclusively of representatives of each of its members.  Thirteen of Allegheny’s members are located in Pennsylvania, and one is located in New Jersey.  Allegheny provides service to its members, which in turn serve over 600,000 rural consumer-members in Pennsylvania and New Jersey.

Allegheny is a PJM TO with transmission facilities included in the PPL Zone, the zone which also includes PPL’s transmission facilities.  Allegheny serves load in five PJM zones:  the PPL Zone, the Allegheny Power Zone, the Penelec Zone, the Met-Ed Zone, and the JCP&L Zone.  Allegheny is a ten-percent co-owner and Nuclear Regulatory Commission co-licensee of the Susquehanna Steam Electric Station, an approximately 2,600 megawatt, two-unit nuclear power plant located in Luzerne County, Pennsylvania.

III. BACKGROUND
The Commission has addressed issues related to Co-Located Load within PJM in a number of recent proceedings.

In Docket No. ER24-2172, the Commission considered amendments to a non-conforming Interconnection Service Agreement (“ISA”) among PJM, Susquehanna Nuclear, LLC and PPL Electric Utilities Corporation that would have increased the amount of Co-Located Load at the Susquehanna Steam Electric Station from 300 MW to 480 MW.  The proceeding attracted over 60 intervenors and drew comments from industry participants throughout the nation, weighing in on the questions raised in the opening paragraphs of this Answer.  On November 1, 2024, the Commission rejected the proposed amendments to the ISA, finding that PJM had failed to meet the burden of demonstrating that the non-conforming provisions of the ISA were necessary.3  In particular, the Commission stated that the proceeding raised questions as to whether PJM would offer the same terms and conditions of service to similarly situated Co-Located Load interconnection customers.4

In Docket Nos. ER24-2888 et al., on August 28, 2024, several subsidiaries of Exelon Company submitted for Commission approval proposed revisions to their respective transmission rate schedules contained in Attachment H of the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff to address Co-Located Load.  Specifically, the Exelon Company subsidiaries proposed to revise their transmission rate schedules to clarify that Co-Located Load must be designated as either Network Load or receive Point-to-Point Transmission Service and thus be allocated costs of the transmission grid.  On February 20, 2025, the Commission issued an order rejecting the

Accordingly, Co-Located Load configurations are attractive options to many new large loads. As demonstrated both by the litany of FERC proceedings identified above and the substantial number of intervenors in those FERC proceedings, the issue of the appropriate treatment of Co-Located Load has perplexed industry.  Yet, foundational, but ultimately technical, questions remain unanswered:  Does Co-Located Load sited with generation that is interconnected to the transmission grid benefit from the generator’s interconnection to the transmission grid?  What, if any, transmission services does Co-Located Load receive from the transmission grid under the various configuration models?  Can Co-Located Load ever sufficiently sever itself from the transmission grid so as not benefit from the transmission grid? Sophisticated technical experts and market participants have weighed the evidence in the FERC proceedings identified above and have reached entirely conflicting answers to these questions. In this proceeding, the Commission must layer legal and policy considerations atop the technical questions identified above.  Thus, it is important to recognize that the Commission is presented with these technical questions regarding the interconnection of new large loads at the same time that the industry is grappling with a dearth of capacity and, more broadly, ever- increasing transmission costs.  The loss of large baseload generation is having real-world impacts for load-serving entities.  As one example, in PJM, the 2025/2026 Base Residual Auction (“BRA”) produced a capacity auction clearing price nearly ten times that of the 2024/2025

Allegheny does not oppose Co-Located Load configurations but insists that the interconnection and treatment of Co-Located Load be accomplished in a manner that is fair to existing load and adheres rigidly to the Commission’s long-standing cost causation principle.  In short, if Co- Located Load benefits from the transmission system, it must be allocated the costs of those benefits to ensure that all users of the transmission system pay their fair share of the costs of maintaining and expanding the transmission system.

Ultimately, resolutions of the technical questions identified above will assist the Commission in answering the legal and policy questions presented by Co-Located Load.  With the record to be produced in response to the Show Cause Order, combined with the record in the Constellation complaint proceeding and in the FERC-convened technical conference, the Commission will be well-positioned to respond to an industry that is clamoring for certainty. Though a party to many of the proceedings identified above, Allegheny has taken no position on the foundational technical questions at issue in this proceeding related to whether Co-Located Load benefits from the transmission grid.  The Commission should make its determinations in this proceeding based on the record evidence as to technical, legal, and policy implications of interconnecting Co-Located Load.  Because Allegheny has no direct experience with interconnecting Co-Located Load, it respectfully defers to the findings of the Commission on these important questions but asks that the Commission act with expediency for the benefit of owners and users of the PJM transmission system, ensuring adherence to the Commission’s long- standing cost causation principle and keeping in mind that load-serving entities rely on the PJM transmission system to provide reliable and affordable service.

V. RESERVATION OF RIGHTS
Allegheny reserves the right to posit and address any issues that it or others may identify or raise herein that are developed in any other phase of this proceeding.

VI. CONCLUSION
The present proceeding presents the Commission with many legal and policy questions that should be answered by a robust review of the technical aspects of Co- Located Load.  Answering these questions is necessary to provide clarity to ensure that the United States retains technology-driven large load, including those related to data centers and AI, that is capable of locating elsewhere but is important for national security. Most importantly, resolving these outstanding questions will provide clarity to load- serving entities as to how they will meet their existing load obligations reliably and affordably at a time of diminishing generation resources.  Allegheny supports the Commission’s efforts to provide certainty on an expedited basis to the questions posed by the growth of Co-Located Load configurations in the PJM region.
