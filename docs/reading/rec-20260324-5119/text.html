MOTION FOR LEAVE AND SECOND ANSWER OF
CONSTELLATION ENERGY GENERATION, LLC
Constellation1 moves for leave and submits this second answer in response to the answer filed by PJM.2 Constellation sought clarification that generators serving non-firm electricity to Co-Located Loads, and that could be recalled during emergencies by both PJM and the

generator, do not have to delist and needlessly reduce the capacity available for existing PJM customers.3 PJM now opposes our request. We reiterate three discrete points in response.

I. Allowing Capacity Resources Serving Non-Firm, Recallable Co-Located Loads to
Remain Available to PJM is Good Policy
The Commission should encourage capacity resources serving non-firm, recallable Co-
Located Loads to remain committed to PJM to help meet the system’s resource adequacy needs.

This is good and sound policy. Indeed, there are several advantages of encouraging such
arrangements that align with the Commission’s4 and the Trump Administration’s priorities. First, it enhances reliability. Capacity resources serving non-firm Co-Located Loads will in fact be available to PJM to help meet resource adequacy needs. As the Commission found, large loads are “willing and able to limit [their] energy withdrawals from the transmission system.”5 Constellation specifically noted several available mechanisms to ensure Co-Located Loads are in fact interrupted when PJM calls upon the generator.6 Forcing the Co-Located Generating Facility to relinquish its capacity status needlessly takes megawatts out of the system without impacting reliability.

Second, it saves consumers money. If capacity is lost due to a delist, PJM will need to
procure additional capacity resources to make up for that loss. It is also possible that, if forced to delist, the capacity resource may choose not to serve the Co-Located Load. In that case, PJM would also have to procure additional capacity resources and complete more transmission

upgrades to serve the Co-Located Load.7 These otherwise avoidable costs would be shared

In short, PJM needs to stop finding ways to frustrate the Commission’s efforts to foster
innovative Co-Located Load arrangements that facilitate new large load while protecting
customers, and start looking for solutions. “Saying no to innovation is not a winning strategy.”17 Just as with non-firm exports, PJM could develop rules to accommodate service to non-firm Co- Located Loads. There are several options that the Commission and PJM may consider exploring, including an adjustment to energy market offer levels—either by quantity or price—to reflect the delivery of energy to the Co-Located Load as unavailable.

The Commission and PJM could also consider exempting capacity resources serving non-
firm Co-Located Load from the energy market must-offer rule. There is precedent for this
approach. The Commission recently approved PJM’s proposal to treat Reliability Must Run
(“RMR”) units as capacity for Reliability Pricing Model procurement purposes,18 but not impose the full suite of Capacity Performance obligations upon them. Specifically, the Commission

The Commission found that these RMR units served a “unique function in PJM” as they would avoid “PJM over-procuring capacity,” would be a “reasonable substitute for a capacity resource,” and would be “reasonably expected to be available for dispatch by PJM in expectation of any PJM emergencies and to perform to address emergencies.”20 Based on this function, the Commission found “it reasonable for PJM not to extend to qualifying RMR resources all the rights and obligations of capacity resources, including those [specifically] related to [energy market] must- offer requirements and the imposition of capacity performance penalties.”21

Capacity resources serving non-firm Co-Located Load also warrant an innovative solution, such as an exemption from the must-offer requirements. In fact, as compared to RMR units, capacity resources serving non-firm Co-Located Load are actual capacity resources that provide significant performance certainty and can be called upon to provide reliability support to the grid at any time. They are also subject to full testing, performance and penalty requirements of any capacity resource (which are far more stringent performance requirements than those applicable to the RMR resources), and just as with the RMRs, “excluding [them] from the capacity market supply stack would result in PJM over-procuring capacity.”22
