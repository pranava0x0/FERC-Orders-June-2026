Why PJM separated procurement from curtailment

PJM described the backstop and its forthcoming curtailment proposal as independent. It asked FERC to consider procurement even if the other tariff needed more review.

the two filings are independent of each other PDF p. 12
Filed comments: Reliability and curtailment

New capacity · PDF pp. 27–52

How a customer could reduce the target

The July proposal allowed newly contracted or self-supplied capacity to reduce the quantity PJM would buy. The customer had to report the capacity during the procurement window and provide evidence.

if reported to PJM within the RBP procurement window and supported by sufficient evidence PDF p. 38

Delivery risk · PDF pp. 53–80

What happens when promised capacity does not arrive

A project could retain its commitment through delays, but failing to supply any backstop capacity for three consecutive delivery years would cancel the balance of its 15-year commitment.

If the RBP Resource fails to provide any RBP UCAP MW after three consecutive Delivery Years PDF p. 68

Revenue settlement · PDF pp. 81–100

How the supplier would be paid

PJM proposed paying suppliers the shortfall after capacity-market revenue, subject to actual availability and clearing. Customers would fund the measured shortfall rather than a forecast.

Loads will fund the actual revenue shortfall, measured ex post PDF p. 88

Cost allocation · PDF pp. 101–120

Where state authority enters the bill

PJM distinguished the wholesale allocation it proposed from the decision retail regulators make about passing those charges on to individual customers.

allocation to retail customers, including data centers, is within the states’ and/or RERRAs’ purview PDF p. 111
