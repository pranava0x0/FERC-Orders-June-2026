POST-TECHNICAL CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF VISTRA CORP. 
 
Pursuant to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (FERC or Commission) July 
30, 2026 Notice Requesting Post-Technical Conference Comments, Vistra Corp. (Vistra) submits 
limited comments1 and full responses to the question in the Notice’s Appendix. 
Vistra is the second-largest independent power producer in the United States, owning and 
operating a diverse fleet of generation facilities totaling approximately 44,000 megawatts (MWs) 
of capacity in markets across the country.  Vistra is one of the largest independent power 
producers in PJM (with approximately 15,000 MW of generation), serves millions of retail 
customers as a competitive power supplier, and is an active participant in all aspects of the PJM 
stakeholder process.  Thus, while Vistra participates in the PJM stakeholder process via the 
Generation Owner sector, Vistra is also directly cognizant of the impacts PJM policies have on 
retail customers.  Vistra plans to participate constructively in the interest-based discussions 
convened by the Commission’s Dispute Resolution Service, with the goal of reaching consensus 
on impactful governance reforms that will improve PJM’s process and allow PJM and its 
stakeholders to return their focus to the critical market reforms that will sustain investment and 
resource adequacy well into the future.

questions, the issues being discussed all, at their core, relate to the core objectives of RTO/ISO 
governance: independence and responsiveness.  These are not competing objectives. Rather, they 
are companion principles the Commission has required of RTOs and ISOs since Order No. 2000, 
and reaffirmed and elaborated on in Order No. 719.2  Order No. 2000 defines independence as 
RTOs/ISOs having a decision-making process independent from any market participant or class 
of market participants and reaffirms the principle that “[a]n RTO needs to be independent in both 
reality and perception.”3  In Order No. 719, the Commission found that RTOs and ISOs must 
demonstrate responsiveness by “provid[ing] an avenue for customers and other stakeholders to 
present their views on RTO and ISO decision-making, and to have those views considered” and, 
critically, “ensure that no single stakeholder group can dominate.”4   The Commission should 
reject any position that treats independence and responsiveness as a zero-sum game, including 
proposals that would strengthen the Board’s hand only by weakening its obligation to hear from 
stakeholders, or that would elevate one stakeholder group over others. 
With respect to responsiveness, moving to a purely advisory stakeholder process, as PJM 
asks, will reduce PJM’s responsiveness to stakeholders writ large.  Stakeholder views are 
inherently weakened when an RTO/ISO can simply take them or leave them.  Moving to an 
advisory model also opens the door to capture by stakeholders who formally or functionally have 
the ability to influence the RTO behind the scenes.  It follows that responsiveness does not mean 
responsiveness to just one category of stakeholder.  PJM's independence (and the appearance of

Should Board members’ terms be extended 
to five or more years? 
X 
 
Comments:  Vistra believes that it could be helpful to extend Board members’ terms to up 
to five years.  That term would enhance continuity and build member experience over 
time.  However, we would be concerned with terms significantly longer than five years 
because it could reduce accountability. 
2 
Should there be a maximum number of years 
that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 
years)? 
X 
 
Comments: Vistra interprets this question to ask whether a Board member should be 
term-limited.  We believe that the ability to serve two five-year terms is reasonable, if the 
Board member is re-nominated and re-elected after their first term.  However, more than 
two terms could create stagnation in Board membership and limit the ability for new 
members to bring fresh experience and perspective. 
3 
Should the Board member qualification rules 
be revised to provide a preference for some 
Board members that reside in the PJM 
region? 
X 
 
Comments: Vistra thinks adding a preference for some Board members that reside in the 
PJM region is a reasonable change to address concerns raised by some stakeholders.  
However, we do not believe that residence in the PJM region should be a requirement for 
Board membership.  That approach would significantly limit the pool of potential Board 
members and exclude candidates that have valuable experience at other RTOs/ISOs. 
4 
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be 
revised to reduce the cooling off period for 
Board members from two years to one year? 
X 
 
Comments: Vistra believes that reducing the cooling off period from two years to one 
year strikes an appropriate balance between maintaining Board independence from any 
PJM stakeholder with broadening the pool of Board candidates. 
5 
Should restrictions on Board 
communications be relaxed to enable more 
communication between the Board and 
stakeholders? 
X 
 
Comments: Currently, the PJM Board meets with high-level representatives in each 
Sector once per year, and Board members are prohibited from privately speaking about 
any matter that is before, or may come before, the Board.  Vistra believes that both 
policies unnecessarily limit the Board’s ability to hear from stakeholders.  The Board 
should meet with Sector representatives several times per year, and not only with senior 
market participant officials.  Furthermore, the ex parte limitation, especially with respect

Should Board votes be made public or the 
Board be required to explain its reasoning? 
 
X 
Comments: It is certainly critical that the overall decisions of the Board be public, 
including the high-level reasoning behind those positions, as seen in the current Board 
Letter approach.  However, requiring that individual Board member votes and reasoning 
to be public is unnecessary because it is the cumulative action of the Board, rather than 
individual positions of Board members, that is relevant to PJM policy. 
7 
Should some Board meetings be open or 
available to the public? 
X 
 
Comments: Opening some Board meetings to the public would also be a common-sense 
way to increase transparency, but closed-doors business meetings are also important to 
allow candid discussions, particularly between the Board and PJM management and 
technical experts.  For example, the ISO-New England Board has an annual open meeting 
where it discusses certain regional strategies and takes public comment. 
8 
Should PJM governing documents explicitly 
state responsibility to serve the public 
interest? 
X 
 
Comments: Vistra does not object to including a reference to the public interest in PJM’s 
governing documents, to the extent it is consistent with PJM’s statutory obligations.  
PJM, as a public utility, is governed by the Federal Power Act’s mandate to provide 
jurisdictional services on terms that are just, reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or 
preferential.  Federal Power Act (section 201) also recognizes that the provision of 
transmitting and selling electric energy for ultimate distribution to the public is affected 
with a public interest and that the regulation of those activities is necessary in the public 
interest.   
9 
Should state representatives have at least one 
voting member on the Nominating 
Committee? 
X 
 
Comments: Vistra believes that having state representation on the Nominating Committee 
is a straightforward way to increase state participation in PJM governance.  However, 
Vistra cautions against including more than one state representative; no group should 
have an outsized role in the Nominating Committee process.  
10 
Should PJM adopt public performance 
metrics and tie management compensation to 
their achievement? 
 
X 
Comments: Performance metrics are an important component of strategic organization 
management.  However,  Vistra does not believe those metrics should be public.  Rather, 
such metrics should be utilized by the board to ensure performance of, and accountability 
from, PJM management.  Performance metrics also must not be so restrictive as to limit 
management’s flexibility to be responsive to changing circumstances and need.

Should the stakeholder process be advisory? 
 
X 
Comments: Advisory stakeholder processes make RTO/ISO Boards less responsive to 
stakeholders and create a situation where one stakeholder group can develop outsized 
influence over the RTO/ISO.  For example, certain stakeholder groups have inherent 
leverage over RTOs/ISOs (e.g, , Transmission Owners, whose membership is the 
foundation of the regional organization.)  Without a binding stakeholder process to 
ensure all stakeholders have a meaningful voice, inherent advantages can unduly 
influence Board decision-making. 
12 
Should PJM have unencumbered section 205 
filing rights over energy and ancillary 
services? 
 
X 
Comments: As noted in Vistra’s pre-conference comments, unencumbered filing rights 
would reduce PJM’s responsiveness to stakeholders. 
13 
Should PJM have unencumbered section 205 
filing rights over RTEP? 
 
X 
Comments:  Unencumbered filing rights would reduce PJM’s responsiveness to 
stakeholders. 
14 
Should OPSI have jump ball rights over 
certain filings? 
 
X 
Comments:  Jump ball rights can be a useful tool to increase RTO/ISO responsiveness 
and accountability to stakeholders.  However, no one stakeholder group should have 
sole jump ball rights.  That structure would provide one organization with outsize 
influence over what is filed.  The ISO-NE example is instructive: jump ball rights are 
held by the stakeholder body as a whole, which drives consensus building and ensures 
that no one group has undue influence in the process. 
15 
Should the Members Committee have jump 
ball filing rights over tariffs where PJM has 
205 rights? 
X 
 
Comments:  Vistra would be open to the Members Committee having jump ball filing 
rights over tariff where PJM has 205 rights.  If PJM’s 205 filing rights are expanded to 
include the energy and ancillary services markets, Vistra believes the Members 
Committee should have jump ball if that approach is the product of voluntary 
stakeholder consensus.  In ISO-New England, the jump ball process has historically 
driven stakeholder consensus and organizational accountability.  However, a key reason 
that process functions well in ISO-NE is because it is supported by dedicated 
stakeholder process infrastructure administered by NEPOOL, which enables 
stakeholders to independently reach consensus. Any consideration of a similar 
mechanism for PJM would need to ensure that all stakeholders are represented in the 
alternative filing process and build comparable supporting infrastructure.

Should the approval threshold in the 
Members Committee be lowered from two-
thirds sector-weighted voting? 
 
X 
Comments: Requiring more than a simple majority vote of stakeholders is key to 
preventing any one sector from dominating the voting on a proposed market reform.   
However, Vistra would support lowering the two-thirds requirement to a threshold 
between a simple majority and supermajority.  For example, a 60% sector weighted 
voting threshold would be a high enough bar to prevent one group from dominating but 
reduce the ability of a single group or sector  to veto a proposal.  
17 
Should affiliate vote restrictions apply to 
lower-level committees? 
X 
 
Comments: Consistent voting structures across committees would prevent the 
inefficiencies of initiatives that pass at lower committees failing at higher committees 
and being sent back.

Should there be a second state representative 
group? 
 
X 
Comments:   Vistra supports expanding OPSI to be an organization that reflects a 
broader spectrum of state voices and has the resources to robustly participate in the 
stakeholder process.  Providing more resources to OPSI could allow it to expand its 
membership to include governors’ offices or other state representatives and increase its 
staffing and expertise to engage in PJM’s complex and technical process. 
19 
Should state representatives have a formal 
voting role in the stakeholder process? 
 
X 
Comments:  Providing states with a formal voting role in the stakeholder process 
would be logistically complex and has the potential to dilute, rather than elevate, state 
voices.  PJM state representatives are diverse both within a state (e.g., governors’ 
offices and state utility Commissions, which may have different perspectives on the 
same issue) and among PJM states, which are heterogenous in many respects 
(including in their status as net sellers or net buyers of electricity and in state 
environmental and economic policies).  Clarity on individual state perspectives could 
be reduced by boiling these interests down to voting positions.  Further, states already 
have a significant voice in the stakeholder process through their advocacy and 
influence with individual stakeholders and in the larger stakeholder process.  Moreover, 
state utility commissions act as regulators, not jurisdictional wholesale market 
participants or (unlike consumer advocates) direct representatives of retail customers. 
20 
Should PJM be required to consult with 
OPSI before certain section 205 filings? 
X 
 
Comments:  PJM must be mindful of elevating any one stakeholder group over others, 
but a consultation requirement before certain section 205 filings could be a helpful way 
for PJM to hear from states on issues like transmission cost allocation while not 
blurring state/federal jurisdictional lines by providing states with separate section 205 
filing rights.
