Pursuant to Rule 2131 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission (“Commission”), NextEra Energy Transmission MidAtlantic, Inc. 
(“NEET MidAtlantic”) hereby submits this answer (“Answer”) to the Commission Order

with Other Proceedings (“Show Cause Order”) directing PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) 
and PJM Transmission Owners (collectively, “TOs,” and individually, each a “TO”) to either:

Restated Operating Agreement, and the Reliability and Assurance Agreement Among Load

preferential without provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, 
and conditions of service that apply to co-located load arrangements; or (2) explain what changes 
to the Tariff would remedy the identified concerns if the Commission were to determine that the 
Tariff has become unjust and unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential and, 
therefore, proceeds to establish a replacement Tariff.2  Specifically, NEET MidAtlantic submits 
this Answer to show why NEET MidAtlantic presently takes no position on the issue of whether 
the PJM Tariff is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential as it relates to 
provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of

II. 
BACKGROUND 
 
NEET MidAtlantic was formed on July 8, 2015, as a Delaware limited liability company 
under the name NextEra Energy Transmission MidAtlantic, LLC.3  NEET MidAtlantic is a 
wholly owned subsidiary of NextEra Energy Transmission Holdings, LLC, and an indirect, 
wholly owned subsidiary of NextEra Energy Transmission, LLC.   
 
 
NEET MidAtlantic was formed to develop, construct, finance, own, operate, and 
maintain high-voltage electric transmission facilities within the PJM region.  On October 29, 
2020, NEET MidAtlantic acquired certain limited transmission facilities, consisting of 
approximately 40 circuit miles (or approximately 20 line miles) of 345 kV electric power 
transmission lines and related power line poles from Commonwealth Edison Company of 
Indiana, Inc.4  NEET MidAtlantic Indiana and PJM subsequently made an FPA section 205 filing 
to incorporate NEET MidAtlantic Indiana’s formula rate into the PJM Tariff as Attachment H-

III. 
ANSWER 
 
As detailed in the Background section, NEET MidAtlantic currently owns a limited 
segment of transmission facilities within the PJM region, comprising only approximately 40 
circuit miles (20 line miles) of 345 kV electric power transmission lines and related 
infrastructure.  NEET MidAtlantic does not serve retail load, nor does it presently have any 
operational or planned co-located load arrangements within PJM that would necessitate revisions 
to or clarifications within the PJM Tariff. 
 
The Commission’s Show Cause Order specifically seeks explanations from PJM and the 
PJM TOs regarding the adequacy, clarity, consistency, and justness and reasonableness of 
provisions within the PJM Tariff addressing co-located load arrangements.  These provisions 
inherently pertain to TOs or market participants directly involved in or contemplating co-located 
load configurations.  Given NEET MidAtlantic neither currently participates in nor plans to 
establish such arrangements, the tariff provisions under scrutiny do not apply to its transmission 
facilities or operations. 
 
Consequently, any substantive commentary or proposed modifications by NEET 
MidAtlantic on the Tariff provisions concerning co-located load arrangements would not 
meaningfully assist the Commission in addressing the specific issues identified in the Show 
Cause Order.7   
 
Accordingly, NEET MidAtlantic takes no position on the issue of whether the PJM Tariff 
is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential as it relates to provisions 
addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, terms, and conditions of service that 
apply to co-location arrangements.
