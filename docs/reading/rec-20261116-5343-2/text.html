Section 4.19
Large Loads
Consistent with any Local Regulatory Authority requirements, Large Loads and Loads may participate in the CAISO Markets using any model where they are capable of complying with the CAISO Tariff requirements, including without limitation as Proxy Demand Resources, Reliability Demand Response Resources, Participating Loads, Load-Serving Entities, and Non-Generator Resources. Section 24
24. Comprehensive Transmission Planning Process
24.1
Overview
The CAISO will develop a comprehensive Transmission Plan and approve transmission solutions using the Transmission Planning Process set forth in this Section 24.  For purposes of this Section 24, transmission solutions include both entirely new transmission facilities and upgrades or additions to existing transmission facilities that are proposed, considered, and/or specified in the comprehensive Transmission Plan during Phase 2 to meet an identified need determined by the CAISO.  The CAISO will consider the use of alternative transmission technologies including dynamic line ratings, advanced power flow control devices, advanced conductors, and transmission switching when evaluating transmission solutions for new regional transmission facilities, Large Loads, upgrades to existing transmission facilities, and Long-Term Regional Transmission Facilities.  Alternatives to transmission solutions are referred to as non-transmission solutions.  Solutions to meet an identified need can be either transmission solutions or non-transmission solutions.  The CAISO will analyze the need for transmission solutions in accordance with the methodologies and criteria set forth in this Section 24, the Transmission Control Agreement, and the applicable Business Practice Manuals.  The comprehensive Transmission Plan will identify Merchant Transmission Facilities meeting the requirements for inclusion in the Transmission Plan and transmission solutions needed (1) to maintain System Reliability; (2) to satisfy the requirements of a Location Constrained Resource Interconnection Facility; (3) to maintain the simultaneous feasibility of allocated Long-Term CRRs; (4) as additional components or expansions to LGIP Network Upgrades are identified pursuant to Section 24.4.6.5; (5) to meet state, municipal, county and federal policy requirements and directives, including renewable portfolio standards policies; and (6) to reduce congestion costs, production supply costs, transmission losses, or other electric supply costs resulting from improved

PDF page 4

access to cost-effective resources.
Beginning in 2027, the CAISO will transition to two complementary transmission planning cycles: (a) a comprehensive transmission plan performed biennially (every two years) with the first biennial comprehensive transmission plan to be issued in 2030; and (b) a Long-Term Regional Transmission Plan performed every four years, with a plan first issued in 2030, and further described in Section 24.11. Additionally, the CAISO will perform reliability studies in each year a comprehensive Transmission Plan is not issued to ensure continued reliability of the CAISO controlled grid and to comply with Applicable Reliability Criteria, CAISO Planning Standards and state mandated resource adequacy study requirements. This is further described in Section 24.3.3.1.  For purposes of this Section 24, when referring to the comprehensive Transmission Planning Process, the term “the year X planning cycle” will refer to the comprehensive Transmission Planning Process initiated during year X-2 to complete a comprehensive Transmission Plan in year X.  For purposes of this Section 24, when referring to the Long- Term Regional Transmission Planning process, the term “the year Y planning cycle” will refer to the Long- Term Regional Transmission Planning process initiated during year Y-3 to complete a comprehensive Long-Term Plan in year Y.

Section 25
25. Interconnection of Generating Units, Large Loads, and Facilities
25.1
Applicability
This Section 25.1 applies to:
(a) each new Generating Unit that seeks to interconnect to the CAISO Controlled Grid;
(b) each existing Generating Unit connected to the CAISO Controlled Grid that will be modified with a resulting increase in the total capability of the power plant;

(c) each existing Generating Unit connected to the CAISO Controlled Grid that will be modified without increasing the total capability of the power plant but has changed the electrical characteristics of the power plant such that its re-energization may violate Applicable Reliability Criteria;

(d) each existing Generating Unit connected to the CAISO Controlled Grid whose total Generation was previously sold to a Participating TO or on-site End User but whose Generation, or any portion thereof, will now be sold in the wholesale market, subject to Section 25.1.2;

The owner of a Generating Unit described in Section 25.1 (a), (b), (c), or (g), or its designee, shall be an Interconnection Customer required to submit an Interconnection Request and comply with Appendix KK unless the Generating Unit will not inject Energy onto the CAISO Controlled Grid and instead serve a co- located End User, as permitted by the Local Regulatory Authority. Large Load Interconnections
End Users, including Large Loads, interconnect to the CAISO Controlled Grid pursuant to Participating TO and retail tariffs in accordance with Local Regulatory Authority requirements. The CAISO will not consider any Load a Large Load subject to the CAISO Tariff where the Load is operational or has a complete request with the Participating TO before February 16, 2027 unless and until the Load modifies or increases itself thereafter and would be a Large Load, as dictated by the Participating TO tariffs; or if the Load elects to become a Large Load subject to the CAISO Tariff.

Where such programs or schemes require disconnection or reduction of the Large Load, those requirements will supersede the ride-through requirements of this Section.

25.7.1.3 Post-Fault Active Power Recovery Requirements

PDF page 12

The Participating TO will require each Large Load to restore its active power consumption following a system fault or voltage disturbance. Following a fault or voltage disturbance, the Large Load will recover to at least ninety percent (90%) of its pre-disturbance active power consumption within three (3) seconds after the post-fault voltage at the point of interconnection recovers to 0.90 per unit or greater. Where a Remedial Action Scheme requires the disconnection or reduction of all or a portion of the Large Load following a fault, the minimum required active power recovery will be ninety percent (90%) of the remaining pre-disturbance active power consumption after accounting for the load reduction required by the Remedial Action Scheme. Compliance with this requirement will be evaluated based on the aggregate active power consumed from the transmission system by the Large Load at the point of interconnection. Following the initial recovery, sustained or repeated reductions in active power consumption attributable to the disturbance will not be permitted.

25.7.1.4 Ramp-Rate Limitations
The Participating TO will require each Large Load to limit changes in its active power consumption during normal operating conditions such that the average active power ramp rate, measured at the point of interconnection over a rolling ten (10)-minute interval, does not exceed twenty (20) megawatts per minute (MW/min).  The ramp-rate limitation will apply to both increases and decreases in active power consumption during normal operation, including startup, shutdown, routine operational changes, and restoration of load following outages. The ramp-rate limitation will not apply to automatic responses to system faults or disturbances that are required for equipment protection or compliance with this Section 25.7.1.

25.7.3.1
Large Load Scheduling Coordinator Requirements
Consistent with CAISO Tariff and Local Regulatory Authority requirements, Large Loads may participate in the CAISO Markets solely, or in aggregations with other resources, as their own Load Serving Entity, a Non-Generator Resource, or a Participating Load.  Such Large Loads may elect to comply with the requirements of Section 25.7.3 and participate in the CAISO Markets through their own Scheduling Coordinator in lieu of their Load Serving Entity Scheduling Coordinator.  A Large Load participating as a Proxy Demand Resource or Reliability Demand Response Resource will have the Load Serving Entity Scheduling Coordinator comply with Section 25.7.3 and submit Scheduled Demand including the Large Load, but may have the Demand Response Provider or other Scheduling Coordinator comply with Section 4.13.

Nothing in this Section 25 or the CAISO Tariff will affect the eligibility of an End-Use Customer to be treated as a Direct Access End-User, consistent with Section 2.2 of the CAISO Tariff. 25.7.4 Reliability Events

After a disturbance or other reliability event involving a Large Load, any associated Interconnection Customer, Scheduling Coordinator, and Participating TO must comply with any CAISO, Regional Entity, or Reliability Coordinator data request related to the event.

25.7.5 Energy Storage and Large Loads
Loads, Large Loads, and energy storage may be co-located. Consistent with Section 10.1.3.2, to avoid the commingling of wholesale and retail withdrawals, Interconnection Customers must ensure loads and Large Loads are metered separately and independently from any co-located energy storage participating in the CAISO Markets.  Loads and Large Loads nonetheless may net any withdrawals against non- participating generation and storage, as permitted by the Local Regulatory Authority.  Nothing in this Section will contradict the netting permitted by Section 10.1.3 of the CAISO Tariff. 25.7.6 Co-located Generating Units and Large Loads

The CAISO and Participating TOs will conduct all Interconnection Studies for Generating Units co- locating with Large Loads to account for their net impact on the CAISO Controlled Grid, as requested by the Interconnection Customer. 6.7.2.9
Co-located Load Modifications
The Interconnection Customer may request, and the CAISO and Participating TO will evaluate, the removal of co-located load from an Interconnection Request if the removal does not increase the requested Interconnection Service level. The CAISO and Participating TO must evaluate such modifications prior to deeming them a Material Modification, but only if the Interconnection Customer submits them prior to the return of the executed Interconnection Facilities Study Agreement by the Interconnection Customer to the CAISO.  Interconnection Customers seeking to add co-located load to an Interconnection Request after the commencement of the Cluster Study may request to do so pursuant to Section 6.7.2.3, including as a request to downsize the Interconnection Service level pursuant to Section 6.7.2.7.  For any co-located load addition or removal pursuant to the Participating TO tariff, if the CAISO and Participating TO determine that the change is a Material Modification or otherwise cannot be reflected in the Generating Facility’s Interconnection Study results, the CAISO and Participating TO will note the change in the GIA, but without modifying the Interconnection Study results.
