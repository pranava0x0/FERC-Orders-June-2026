REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION OF CALIBRANT ENERGY 
 
Pursuant to Rule 211 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (Commission) 
Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Calibrant Energy (Calibrant) respectfully submits this request 
for clarification of PJM Interconnection, LLC’s (PJM) proposed revisions to its Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (Tariff) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving 
Entities (“RAA”) to establish the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”). Calibrant seeks 
only a narrow clarification of proposed Tariff Attachment DD Section 18.2 (c) in order to 
eliminate confusion or ambiguity and the potential, if read wrongly, to result in unjust and 
unreasonable or unduly discriminatory barriers to the participation of resources that could 
otherwise contribute as part of the solution to the region’s resource adequacy challenges. 
Specifically, Calibrant seeks to clarify that bilateral contracts between loads and behind-the-
meter generation resources to support load response are sufficient evidence to support Qualifying 
RBP Offset UCAP eligibility under proposed Tariff Attachment DD Section 18.2 (c).    
Calibrant develops energy generation and storage assets throughout the United States, 
including in the PJM footprint. Calibrant participated in PJM’s stakeholder process to ensure that 
the behind-the-meter generation projects that it develops in the region, which are new “steel-in-
the-ground” projects capable of reliably serving the long-term capacity needs of the region with a 
faster development timeline than front-of-the-meter new capacity resources, are fully eligible to

I. 
REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION 
PJM’s Proposed Tariff Attachment DD Section 18.2 (c) sets forth the evidence that must 
be submitted during the RBP bid submission window in order for new capacity that will serve 
the region to be eligible as Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW. Three types of supporting 
evidence may qualify under this provision, each of which is subject to differing additional 
criteria. Under proposed Section 18.2 (c)(i), a signed bilateral contract may qualify; under (ii) a 
RERRA-approved or accepted integrated resource plan may qualify; and under (iii) a signed 
commitment with a Curtailment Service Provider or DER Aggregator may qualify. As a 
developer of behind-the-meter generation projects, Calibrant expects that it would provide as 
qualifying evidence pursuant to Section 18.2 (c)(i) a signed bilateral contract with an end-use 
customer to enable load response. Under this pathway, a contract would qualify if its term is for 
provision of Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW for at least five Delivery Years with a

commercial operation date for the project prior to the 2032/2033 Delivery Year, and matched 
with load indicating that it will come online in the 2027/2028 or 2028/2029 Delivery Year. 
Calibrant notes that nothing in the requirements under Section 18.2 (c)(i) or definition of 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW limit applicability of the bilateral contract evidentiary path to 
capacity that is front-of-meter rather than behind-the-meter generation that can support load 
response. 
In contrast, proposed Section 18.2 (c)(iii) appears to apply to the commercial relationship 
between curtailment service providers and flexible loads that may not be supported by physical 
assets, and incorporates additional limitations that would not appropriately apply to Qualifying 
RBP Offset UCAP MW provided by behind-the-meter generation projects. Specifically, Section 
18.2 (c)(iii) requires that the new load will either (a) participate directly as demand response 
starting immediately in the 2028/2029 Delivery Year Second Incremental Auction or (b) 
“purchase replacement Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW that are eligible generation or storage 
resources.” Such provisions make little sense in the context of a behind-the-meter generation 
project that contracts with the end-use customer to achieve a load reduction. Because behind-the-
meter generation projects require steel in the ground, they may, like front-of-the-meter projects 
and unlike load response unsupported by physical generation assets, have a commercial 
operation date that does not allow for participation in the 2028/2029 Delivery Year. But, as is the 
case for front-of-the-meter generation projects, that is not a basis to disqualify them entirely from 
providing capacity that could meet the region’s resource adequacy needs over the long-run. 
Likewise, the alternate option under Section 18.2 (c)(iii) to purchase replacement eligible 
generation or storage resources makes little sense in a context in which otherwise eligible 
capacity from a behind-the-meter generation project has already been procured by the new load,

but has simply not yet come online. These restrictions make more sense applied to load 
flexibility unsupported by physical assets than they do the load response enabled by behind-the-
meter generation in the types of configurations that Calibrant develops. 
In addition, Section 18.2 (c)(iii) requires evidence that the “new load(s)” subject to the 
signed commitment with a Curtailment Service Provider or DER Aggregator will participate “in 
a MW quantity equal to MW quantity of the new load(s)” as Annual Demand Resources or DER 
Capacity Aggregation Resources. As an initial matter, it is unclear whether this requirement 
attaches to the quantity of new load matched to Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW or the 
entirety of a customer’s load at a given site. But if a new load supported by behind-the-meter 
generation were required to participate as demand resource or to purchase replacement 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW at no less than 100% of their full load in order to constitute 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW, such a constraint would be unjust and unreasonable or 
unduly discriminatory. There is no reasonable basis to bar a behind-the-meter generation project 
from serving a new load’s capacity needs in part, particularly where front-of-the-meter 
generation projects face no equivalent obligation to match 100% of the new load in order to be 
eligible as Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW. Prohibiting smaller generation projects from 
partially enabling a larger load’s provision of load response would preclude them from helping to 
address a resource adequacy crisis in the region; Calibrant does not believe PJM intended 
Section 18.2 (c)(iii) to impose such a constraint on participation by behind-the-meter generation 
in RBP. 
Fortunately, the Tariff is broad enough to allow bilateral contracts between a behind-the-
meter generation project and a new load to support the resulting capacity’s qualification as 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW. This pathway is best suited to the particularities of behind-

II. 
CONCLUSION 
For the foregoing reasons, Calibrant respectfully requests that PJM or the Commission 
clarify that behind-the-meter generation projects can provide evidence that they are eligible as 
Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW under Proposed Tariff Attachment DD Section 18.2 (c)(i), 
and that such projects are not restricted to demonstration via Section 18.2 (c)(iii).
