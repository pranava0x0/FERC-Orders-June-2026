I. INTRODUCTION
Persistence Analytics Group LLC ("PAG") respectfully submits these limited supplemental comments
concerning the Interim Resource Adequacy Service ("IRAS") proposed by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. ("PJM").
PAG filed comments in this proceeding on August 13, 2026 addressing implementation assurance, the
Large Load Registry, revalidation of material assumptions, and the evidentiary basis for claimed IRAS load-
reduction capability.
Those comments emphasized a straightforward principle: a represented ability to reduce load is not
necessarily equivalent to demonstrated operational capability under the conditions in which PJM may rely
on that capability.
Subsequent filings have sharpened a related issue that warrants brief additional comment.
The Organization of PJM States, Inc. ("OPSI") requested additional time to consider the interaction among
PJM, Electric Distributors, retail regulators, IRAS implementation, and the associated compensation
mechanism. The Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, Google LLC, and East Kentucky Power Cooperative,
Inc. supported that request. The Commission denied the requested extension on September 2, 2026.
PAG does not address here the separate jurisdictional, retail-rate, or cost-recovery questions raised by those
parties.
PAG instead addresses a narrower evidentiary question:
Before compensation is assigned to claimed IRAS performance, what evidence is sufficient to
establish that the compensated capability was real, attributable, available when required, and
actually performed as represented?

The existence of a compensation mechanism increases, rather than decreases, the importance of decision-

II. COMPENSATION ELIGIBILITY SHOULD FOLLOW DEMONSTRATED
PERFORMANCE, NOT MERELY DECLARED FLEXIBILITY
IRAS necessarily depends on the ability of participating Large Loads to reduce consumption or otherwise
respond when PJM requires relief.
PAG's August 13 comments explained why a stated ability to reduce a quantity of load is not identical to
demonstrated capability under relevant timing, control, telemetry, fuel, permitting, and restoration
conditions.
When compensation is attached to that capability, the evidentiary consequence becomes greater.
A compensation obligation should not arise merely because a Large Load has represented that it possesses
flexibility or because an expected reduction has been incorporated into an operating arrangement.
Rather, the implementation framework should permit the relevant parties to determine whether the
claimed performance actually occurred and whether the amount attributed to the responding Large Load is
supported by evidence appropriate to the resulting payment.
Accordingly, PAG recommends the following principle:
Compensation eligibility should follow demonstrated capability and verified performance, not merely
declared flexibility.
This does not require the Commission to prescribe a single measurement methodology for every Large
Load.
Large Loads differ materially in configuration, metering, onsite generation, operational controls, customer
arrangements, and retail treatment.
The narrower point is that a compensation determination should be supported by an evidentiary chain
sufficient for the consequence of that determination.
III. THE PERFORMANCE RECORD SHOULD SUPPORT THE FULL CHAIN
FROM EXPECTED CAPABILITY TO COMPENSATED RESULT
Where IRAS performance results in compensation, the relevant implementation record should permit
authorized parties, consistent with applicable confidentiality protections, to understand the basis for the
compensated quantity.

Depending on the applicable implementation structure, that record should be sufficient to address material
elements such as:
the baseline or other reference quantity against which a claimed reduction is evaluated;
the quantity of load represented as available for reduction;
the PJM instruction or qualifying system condition giving rise to the response;
the timing of the response;
the actual reduction observed;
the duration of the reduction;
whether onsite generation or another mechanism contributed to the response;
material limitations affecting the claimed performance;
restoration or rebound conditions where relevant;
the basis for attributing the measured response to the participating Large Load;
the quantity ultimately treated as compensable; and
material changes that should require the capability or compensation eligibility to be revalidated.
The Commission need not resolve each implementation detail in this proceeding.
But the Commission should recognize that the evidentiary framework supporting compensation should be
capable of tracing the claimed service from represented capability through actual performance to the
compensated result.
Otherwise, a compensation mechanism could create a payment obligation before the underlying
performance claim has reached an equivalent level of evidentiary maturity.
A payment obligation should not mature faster than the evidence supporting the underlying
performance claim.

SUFFICIENT EVIDENTIARY CONTEXT FOR THEIR DISTINCT
RESPONSIBILITIES
The issues identified by OPSI underscore that IRAS compensation does not exist in isolation.

Electric Distributors and retail regulators may have responsibilities distinct from PJM's wholesale reliability
function, including questions concerning implementation, customer arrangements, compensation, and
retail treatment.
PAG does not take a position here on the allocation of legal authority among PJM, Electric Distributors, state
commissions, or other Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authorities.
However, regardless of which entity ultimately determines or administers a particular payment obligation,
the evidentiary integrity of the underlying performance claim remains important.
A state commission considering retail consequences may need to understand not merely that a Large Load
participated in an IRAS arrangement, but what evidence supports the quantity for which compensation is
claimed.
An Electric Distributor administering a load-reduction arrangement may similarly need information
concerning the baseline, actual response, duration, attribution, and material operating conditions.
The Large Load Registry and associated implementation processes should therefore preserve sufficient
provenance to allow authorized users to distinguish among:
represented capability;
capability supported by implementation evidence;
actual observed performance; and
the quantity ultimately used for compensation.
Those are related facts, but they are not interchangeable.
V. MATERIAL CHANGES SHOULD TRIGGER REVALIDATION OF
COMPENSATION-RELEVANT CAPABILITY
PAG's August 13 comments recommended identifiable revalidation triggers for execution-critical
assumptions.
That principle is especially important where the affected assumption supports future compensation
eligibility.
A load-reduction capability that was reasonable when initially documented may become materially different
if, for example, the facility's operating configuration changes, onsite generation becomes unavailable,
relevant permits change, telemetry or control arrangements change, customer operations materially
change, or the quantity and timing of available reduction differ from the conditions previously relied upon.
PAG does not recommend continuous requalification of unchanged capability.
Instead, the framework should identify material changes that require the affected performance assumption
to be revisited before continued reliance or compensation is based on an outdated condition.

The appropriate principle is:
Compensation should follow evidence, and continued compensation eligibility should follow
continued evidentiary support.
VI. CONCLUSION
PAG respectfully submits that the emerging compensation issues in this proceeding reinforce the
implementation-assurance principles identified in PAG's August 13 comments.
PAG does not ask the Commission to resolve state retail jurisdiction, prescribe a universal performance test,
or create a new approval layer.
PAG instead recommends that the Commission recognize that any IRAS compensation mechanism should
rest on evidence sufficient to connect:
represented capability → qualifying instruction or condition → actual response → measured
reduction → duration → attribution → compensable performance → revalidation.
In particular, PAG respectfully recommends that the Commission ensure, through PJM's tariff, Manuals,
implementation guidance, or other appropriate process, that:
compensation eligibility is supported by demonstrated capability and verified performance rather
than declared flexibility alone;
the basis for a compensated reduction is sufficiently traceable for authorized parties to understand
the relevant baseline, actual response, duration, attribution, and material conditions;
Electric Distributors and relevant retail regulators receive sufficient evidentiary context to perform
their distinct responsibilities; and
material changes affecting compensation-relevant capability trigger appropriate revalidation.
These principles would complement, rather than replace, PJM's operational authority and state regulatory
responsibilities.
They would also help ensure that a compensation obligation does not become more certain than the
evidence supporting the performance for which compensation is being provided.
