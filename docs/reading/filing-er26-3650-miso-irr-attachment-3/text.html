Q. 
HOW DID MISO ARRIVE AT THE PROPOSED DEFINITION OF LARGE 
1 
LOAD? 
2 
A. 
MISO developed the proposed Large Load definition through review of emerging industry 
3 
practices, consideration of the reliability needs within the MISO region, and stakeholder 
4 
feedback.  MISO reviewed approaches being developed or used by NERC and other RTOs, 
5 
including PJM, SPP, and ERCOT.  While those approaches vary, they generally recognize 
6 
that sufficiently large, concentrated load additions warrant additional visibility and 
7 
reliability consideration. 
8 
Based on that review, MISO selected a 50 MW threshold as a reasonable and 
9 
administratively clear starting point, consistent with industry precedent and sufficiently 
10 
significant to warrant application of MISO’s Large Load requirements.  The definition is 
11 
based on gross load rather than net load so that MISO has visibility into the full electrical 
12 
demand and characteristics of the facility. 
13 
Finally, MISO also defined Large Load at the facility or site level rather than solely at an 
14 
individual point of interconnection.  This allows loads under common ownership or 
15 
coordinated operational control at a single site to be considered together, including where 
16 
the site is served through multiple points of interconnection. 
17

18 
MISO REGION? 
19 
A. 
MISO determined that 50 MW is a reasonable threshold based on industry precedent, 
20 
including from PJM and SPP, and the need for a clear and administratively workable means 
21 
of identifying significant, concentrated loads that warrant additional reliability visibility.

16

17

Forecasting Requirements: Establishes short-term and other forecast information 
18 
needed to anticipate Large Load demand and operating changes. 
19 
•

20

performance verification.
