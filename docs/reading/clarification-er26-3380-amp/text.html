regarding the justness and reasonableness of PJM’s proposed cost

intended to make a final decision as to the justness and reasonableness of PJM’s as-filed cost allocation method that would be subject to a request for rehearing. B. Request for Rehearing
1. The Commission’s rejection of PJM’s RBP cost allocation
method was based on an apparent misunderstanding of that
method.
As discussed above, the Commission’s principal rationale for rejecting PJM’s proposed cost allocation method was that PJM’s reliance “on Load Adjustments to measure load growth may be inconsistent or not reasonably capture forecasted load growth or actual large load growth.”43 While the Commission reasonably determined that the cost causation principle requires RBP costs to be allocated based on “large load growth,” the Commission erred in concluding that PJM’s as-filed proposal would not accomplish this aim. The Commission’s conclusion appears to have been based, in significant part, on a misunderstanding of the PJM methodology. As a consequence, the Commission’s decision is not the product of reasoned decision making.44 The Commission expressed concern that, based on PJM’s discussion of its load forecasting process in a prior proceeding, “it appears some large loads may never have been accounted for as Load Adjustments because PJM accounted for them when calculating the initial load forecast.”45 This observation misapprehends the PJM Load Adjustment methodology used in allocating the Initial RBP Target. The Load Adjustments reflected in Table B9b, which was developed after PJM’s filing in Docket ER24-2447, are intended to include new large loads that might otherwise have been reflected in the underlying econometric forecast. PJM has described51 the basic methodology for Table B9b Load Adjustments to be as follows: First, PJM identifies zones with existing data center loads. Second, PJM removes monthly data center load from the commercial Energy Information Administration data underlying the load forecast. Third, PJM removes hourly data center load from historical loads used in PJM’s model estimation. Finally, total accepted load is added to the load forecast. Thus, PJM removes large loads from the underlying forecast data and adds large loads back into the forecast through the Load Adjustments.52 Table B9b is intended to capture the difference between the final forecast and what the final forecast would be without any large load, not just without Load Adjustment requests above the embedded value.

2. The objections raised by the NJBPU did not warrant rejection of
