SUPPORTING COMMENTS OF 
THE ENTERGY OPERATING COMPANIES AND CLECO POWER LLC 
 
Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure1, and 
the Commission’s October 2, 2026, notice in the above-captioned docket, Entergy 
Services, LLC, on behalf of the Entergy Operating Companies (“Entergy”)2 and Cleco 
Power, LLC hereby submit comments in support of the Midcontinent Independent 
System Operator, Inc.’s (“MISO’s”) filing to establish the Large Load Addition Resource 
Study (LARS).   
Entergy and Cleco Power LLC support MISO’s October 2, 2026, filing to 
establish the LARS process, and respectfully urges the Commission to accept it, as it will 
enable MISO to expeditiously review the interconnection needs of large loads over 200 
MW that have identified new generation resources to support their needs.  As a general 
principle, the LARS process should reduce overall interconnection-related costs to 
consumers.   
Large loads, including computational data centers, advanced manufacturing, and 
other energy-intensive industries, are arriving in MISO’s footprint at an increasing pace,

I. 
Background 
Both Entergy and Cleco Power LLC are load-serving entities with an obligation to 
serve the electricity needs of their customers.  The Entergy Operating Companies are 
generation and transmission-owning members of MISO and serve approximately 3.1 
million retail customers, 70% of which are large industrial or commercial businesses. As 
load serving entities, the Entergy Operating Companies procure both generation and 
transmission service for their retail customers, including large loads. The Entergy 
Operating Companies own or lease over 24,000 MW of generation assets producing 
electricity that is carried across more than 16,000 miles of high voltage transmission 
lines.  
Cleco Power LLC is an electric utility engaged principally in the generation, 
transmission, distribution, and sale of electricity within Louisiana.  Cleco Power LLC is a

transmission solutions and avoid the introduction of duplicative and expensive upgrades

II. 
The LARS Process is Just and Reasonable 
LARS should be accepted by the Commission because it is just and reasonable 
and delivers coordinated speed to power without sacrificing study integrity.  By studying 
load and its paired generation together, MISO can identify combined transmission 
solutions, avoid duplicative and costly upgrades, and produce a reliable outcome in 
approximately 120 days.  This is not currently achievable across the separate EPR, DPP, 
and ERAS processes and timelines that are now in place.5 Making LARS a permanent 
process is appropriate because it provides needed, long-term regulatory and financial 
certainty required to site and finance large load-and-generation pairings, and it includes 
structural limits that keep the process bounded in every cycle.6  As further highlighted in 
MISO’s filing, LARS aligns with and is directly responsive to the Commission’s Show 
Cause Order, as it offers an explicit pathway for MISO to “account for the operational 
dynamics of serving electrically proximate large loads.”7   
The Commission should evaluate LARS under the “independent entity variation 
standard” to determine whether MISO’s variation here is just and reasonable, not unduly 
discriminatory or preferential and accomplishes the purposes of Order No. 2003.8  First, 
the LARS project requirements are appropriately tailored with reasonable limitations to 
prevent unintended uses.  LARS limits the total number of projects to ensure that MISO
