The Show Cause Order commended SPP’s “bold and proactive steps” to
integrate large loads. It recognized that SPP’s High Impact Large Load (“HILL”) process already provides an enhanced study framework for Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of large loads.6 Notwithstanding this, the Commission preliminarily found “that SPP’s Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among transmission customers, which may result in unjust and unreasonable rates for transmission service.”7 The Commission expressed concern that there is no mechanism to help ensure that Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of large loads bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide transmission service, including the cost of Network Upgrades.8

To address this concern, SPP proposes the LSHL for Network Upgrades needed to add or modify load delivery points studied under the Tariff. The LSHL mitigates the risk of cost shifting associated with the connection of large loads by requiring the Transmission Customer to pay, and to secure with financial security, all delivery point Network Upgrade costs above a threshold—creating an obligation that correlates directly with the level of cost resulting from the delivery point request and does not depend on whether, when, or at what level the load materializes. The proposed limit of $500,000 per MW is informed by two data sets, which are attached to this transmittal letter as Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 2. Exhibit 1 is an affidavit from Natasha Henderson with an attached spreadsheet outlining the upgrade costs resulting from Notifications to Construct (“NTCs”) that have been issued by SPP through its delivery point study processes from 2012 to 2026, which includes for each load request (1) the load amount; (2) the total cost of the Network Upgrades identified;

(3) the cost per MW of load requested; (4) the approximate date when the Network Upgrades are put into service; and (5) the cost per MW percentile compared to the other requests included on Exhibit 1. Exhibit 2 is an affidavit from Don Frerking with an attached spreadsheet that includes transmission gross and net plant values, both in total and on a per-MW basis, for the SPP Region. Historical delivery point studies requiring Network Upgrades showed
approximate costs of $177,000 per MW at the median, $378,000 per MW at the 75th percentile, and $1.018 million per MW at the 95th percentile. The estimated investment in transmission facilities across the entire SPP Region is approximately $422,000 per MW on a net plant basis (reduced for depreciation) and $572,000 per MW on a gross plant basis. The value of the proposed per-MW limit falls between those system-wide investment measures and between the historical 75th and 95th percentiles for delivery point requests. The percentiles from this history of requests that required Network Upgrades suggest that the majority of future delivery point requests requiring such upgrades will remain under the proposed per-MW cost cap, thereby resulting in full Base Plan funding at an average cost level for delivery points which are not HILLs. Also, it should be noted that a large majority of delivery point requests do not require issuance of a Notification to Construct for any Network Upgrade. The proposed framework of a per-MW limit coupled with an aggregate limit ensures that the focus is on mitigation of cost risk. An aggregate cap is needed because a per-MW limit alone could leave the SPP Region, and its ratepayers, potentially exposed to very large cost burdens. Further, a per-MW limit without an aggregate limit could incentivize the submission of inflated delivery point load amounts in some situations. The proposed $25 million aggregate cap for the LSHL is calculated using the 50 MW HILL definition threshold57 for interconnections above 69 kV (i.e., 50 MW multiplied by $500,000) and provides a meaningful, regional funding level. The proposed LSHL results in a division of cost responsibility for the delivery point end of a transmission service path between a portion subject to regional cost allocation and a portion directly assigned to the requesting Transmission Customer. This is generally consistent with the Tariff’s current treatment of the resource end of the transmission service path (i.e., the existing Safe Harbor Provisions), which incorporates a division of cost responsibility between a portion subject to regional cost allocation and a portion assigned to the requesting Transmission Customer by means of the Safe Harbor Cost Limit and other qualifying criteria for regional funding as set forth in Section III.B of Attachment J. Those Transmission Customers who bear directly assigned costs for delivery point Network Upgrades as a result of the Load Safe Harbor Limit will be eligible for compensation through allocation of Candidate Incremental Long-Term Congestion Rights, as provided under Attachment Z2 of the Tariff.

mitigates the cost risks to existing Transmission Customers by requiring a minimum contribution by Transmission Customers taking transmission service on behalf of the large loads that impose costs above the LSHL. The per-MW component of the proposed LSHL is based on the level of SPP transmission service that is requested by the Transmission Customer and the aggregate cap component of the proposed LSHL is based on an amount corresponding to the 50 MW threshold for a HILL.
