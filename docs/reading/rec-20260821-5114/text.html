Introduction 
The PJM Legislators’ Collaborative and the National Caucus of Environmental Legislators 
(NCEL) are pleased to submit these post-conference comments and appreciate the opportunity to 
continue engaging with the Commission on these important issues. We welcome the discussion 
generated by the technical conference and look forward to participating in the dispute resolution 
process. We remain committed to working constructively with FERC, PJM, and other 
stakeholders. 
 
The comments below are organized into three sections. First, we provide an overview of the 
recommendations and priorities outlined in our initial filings. Second, we provide a more 
detailed discussion of our regional state committee proposal. Third, we respond directly to the 
questions the Commission posed in its post-conference request for comments. We offer these 
comments as a starting point for further discussion and are open to continued dialogue and 
negotiation with PJM, the states, FERC, and other stakeholders to develop a governance 
framework that reflects the interests and responsibilities of the states and the ratepayers they 
represent.  
PJM Legislators’ Collaborative and NCEL Governance and Stakeholder Reform 
Filing 
In recent comments submitted to FERC, the PJM Legislators’ Collaborative and NCEL outlined 
five key recommendations designed to improve outcomes for consumers across the region: 
●​ Establish a Public Interest Mission: Explicitly require PJM to legally balance reliability 
and affordability while serving the public interest in all major decisions. This public 
interest mandate should be incorporated into PJM’s Operating Agreement and Section 9 
of the Open Access Transmission Tariff.  
●​ Strengthen the Role of States: Create a regional state committee with meaningful 
authority to participate in market rule and cost allocation decisions. More details on this 
committee's structure are below.

●​ Increase Board Independence and Accountability: Give states a role in nominating 
Board members and ensure FERC holds the Board accountable to measurable 
performance standards.  
●​ Reform the Stakeholder Process: Extend weighted voting to prevent large firms and 
their affiliates from dominating voting, while providing consumer advocates with the 
resources to participate effectively.  
●​ Improve Transparency: Record, publish, and archive all stakeholder and Board 
meetings, votes, Board rationales, planning assumptions, and cost analyses. These 
proceedings influence billions of dollars in regional investments and require public 
oversight. Also allow media access to all stakeholder meetings.  
PJM State Committee Proposal 
Building on our original filings, the PJM Legislators’ Collaborative and NCEL propose creating 
a States Committee within PJM (“PJMSC”) to represent state interests in PJM governance and 
ensure state perspectives are reflected in rule and tariff changes PJM considers and files with 
FERC. Through structured, formal engagement with PJM staff and governance bodies, the 
PJMSC would represent the interests of ratepayers across the PJM region who depend on 
affordable and reliable electricity.  
Critically, the PJMSC must include state legislator representation. Much of the work needed to 
address PJM’s resource adequacy, reliability, and affordability challenges will require long-term 
partnership between PJM and the States. As FERC Commissioner David Rosner noted in a 
concurring opinion, solving these challenges “will take partnership with states given their 
authority over resource procurement and generation and transmission siting.” PJM leadership has 
made similar comments, with Executive Vice President Asim Haque noting in a Utility Dive 
op-ed that “it will require the entire industry to work in the solutions space, including those 
working within state frameworks, to successfully navigate through this supply/demand challenge 
and resulting affordability concerns.” 
State regulators and governors are essential partners in this process, and state legislators have a 
distinct and equally important role. Legislatures enact the laws that establish the authority, 
mandates, and policy direction under which the executive branch and regulators operate. They 
also play a critical role in shaping the broader policy framework, including resource 
procurement, siting, utility regulation, and consumer protection. State legislative action is 
frequently necessary to implement new policies, amendments, and changes to statutory authority 
that are key to solving PJM’s reliability and affordability challenges. Excluding state legislator 
representation from the PJMSC would create a critical governance gap and would undermine 
efforts to align communication and coordination between PJM and the states. 
A new state committee within PJM – with appointees from state legislatures, governors' offices, 
and regulators – would create a meaningful forum for advancing policy initiatives that integrate

state lawmaking into the regional energy framework. It would also give PJM a direct, explicit 
opportunity to engage lawmakers on regional issues that require legislative action, rather than the 
indirect and ad hoc engagement it currently relies on.  
The Legislators’ Collaborative and NCEL propose that the PJMSC have the characteristics 
outlined below: 
●​ Establishment and funding: The PJMSC would be established and funded through the 
PJM tariff at a level sufficient to maintain robust technical staff and expertise, 
independent of PJM management.  
●​ State representation: Each state would be permitted to appoint up to four delegates, 
with no more than one delegate appointed by each of the following: the Governor’s 
Office; the relevant state regulatory agency, such as the state utility commission the state 
House; and the state Senate. Each state fully within PJM would receive four votes. States 
only partially within PJM would receive two votes, with each delegate receiving one-half 
vote.1 Each individual casts their own vote, and consensus within each state is not 
required. 
●​ Leadership: The PJMSC would nominate and elect its own officers, including a 
President, Treasurer, and other leadership positions. 
●​ Shared filing rights: The PJMSC would have shared Section 205 filing rights over the 
PJM Tariff and Operating Agreement, using a “jump-ball” structure similar to those 
employed by other RTOs. Where the PJMSC supports a proposal different from that of 
the PJM Board, PJM would be required to file both proposals with FERC, provided the 
PJMSC proposal has received an internal simple majority of votes (if there is a tied vote, 
no action will be taken). FERC would then determine which proposal to accept. 
●​ Communication, data transparency, and information access: The PJMSC would have 
regular opportunities to meet directly with the PJM Board and engage with PJM staff, 
with the PJM Board required to respond to meeting requests within a timely period. To 
promote transparency and informed participation, the PJMSC and its staff would be 
provided timely access to relevant models, data, and analyses underlying proposed rule or 
tariff changes, including those related to load forecasting and resource accreditation. The 
PJMSC would also have explicit rights to commission independent studies on 
affordability, reliability, and emerging load trends.  
 
 
 
1 Rationale: The Collaborative and NCEL determined that weighting votes based on whether a state is 
fully or partially within the PJM footprint is important to reflect the differing levels of engagement and 
vested interest in PJM between these states. This approach is preferable to weighting votes based on the 
number of ratepayers in PJM, which could disproportionately diminish the voice of smaller states that are 
fully within the PJM footprint.

Stakeholder Process 
 
 
Questions/Comments 
Yes 
No 
16) 
Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-thirds 
sector-weighted voting? 
X 
 
 
Comments: The current two-thirds requirement allows relatively small coalitions of stakeholders to block 
proposals indefinitely. The Collaborative’s core recommendation is for FERC to "enhance the Board’s 
ability to act when stakeholder processes reach deadlock.” This would ensure the Board has the flexibility 
to prioritize the public interest even when the stakeholder process fails to reach the two-thirds consensus. 
17) 
Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior standing committees, 
also apply to lower-level committees? 
X 
 
 
Comments: Yes, affiliate votes can dominate lower-level committees.
