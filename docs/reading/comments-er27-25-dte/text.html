COMMENTS OF DTE ELECTRIC COMPANY 
 
Pursuant to Rules 212 and 214 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s 
(“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure, 18 CFR §§ 385.212 and 385.214 (2013), DTE 
Electric Company (“DTE Electric”, “DTE”, or the “Company”) hereby submits its comments 
regarding the filing submitted by Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. ("MISO") on 
October 2, 2026, in the above-captioned docket proposing to establish a Large Load Addition 
Resource Study ("LARS") process.1 DTE Electric supports MISO's proposal and believes the 
LARS framework represents a reasonable approach to evaluating the transmission, operational, 
and resource adequacy impacts associated with significant new load additions and associated 
generation resources within the MISO footprint. 
I. 
Background 
In this proceeding, MISO proposes revisions to its Tariff to establish the LARS framework. 
MISO states that the proposal is intended to facilitate the timely and reliable integration of 
significant new load additions and associated generation resources through a coordinated study 
process that evaluates transmission, operational, and resource adequacy impacts. Under the

II. 
COMMENTS  
DTE Electric supports the LARS filing and respectfully requests that the Commission 
accept the filing as proposed. As the load-serving entity responsible for serving approximately two 
million customers in Michigan, DTE Electric has a direct interest in ensuring that significant new 
load additions are integrated through processes that appropriately account for associated 
generation, transmission needs, and effects on other customers. 
A. The LARS filing is a just and reasonable solution to integrate new large loads 
while maintaining resource adequacy and reliability and supports MISO's 
compliance with the Commission's Show Cause Order. 
The LARS Filing directly responds to the Commission’s directive to create “a just and 
reasonable framework for addressing these concerns and ensuring the timely, efficient, and reliable 
integration of large and co-located loads onto the transmission system.”2 By allowing large loads 
and associated generation resources to be studied together, the proposal recognizes that jointly 
planned and electrically proximate resources may produce materially different system impacts than 
traditional stand-alone studies. This coordinated approach provides MISO with a more complete 
assessment of the net impacts of load and generation pairings and supports more informed planning 
and operational decision-making.

The proposal also contains meaningful safeguards designed to ensure participation is 
limited to qualifying projects and to discourage speculative requests. Participating projects must 
satisfy applicable deposit, minimum size, site control, and commercial operation requirements. In 
addition, load and generation pairings must demonstrate a qualifying contractual relationship, 
remain within the same Local Resource Zone, and maintain a long-term commitment to remain 
paired. These requirements provide reasonable assurance that participating projects represent 
durable load-generation relationships and help mitigate potential impacts to the transmission 
system and other customers. 
The LARS filing also preserves an open and non-discriminatory framework by permitting 
the interconnection customer responsible for the generation component to be any eligible entity. 
This approach maintains flexibility for loads, generators, and load-serving entities while 
preserving the technical, financial, and operational safeguards necessary to protect reliability and 
other customers. 
Taken together, these provisions establish a targeted and balanced framework that 
facilitates the efficient integration of significant new loads while preserving appropriate customer 
and reliability protections. Accordingly, DTE Electric respectfully requests that the Commission 
approve the LARS Filing as proposed.
