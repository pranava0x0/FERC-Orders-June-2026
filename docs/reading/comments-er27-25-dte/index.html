ER27-25-000 · 2026-10-23 · DTE Electric

Comments of DTE Electric Company

DTE Electric, which serves about two million Michigan customers, comments in support of MISO's October 2 LARS filing and asks FERC to accept it as proposed, citing its entry requirements and its role in answering the show cause order.

Asks FERC to: Accept MISO's LARS filing as proposed.

Answers the order. Supports MISO's LARS filing as compliance with the E-8 show cause order, which DTE says directs a just and reasonable framework for integrating large and co-located loads.

E-8 MISO: Show cause or propose tariff changes in 60 days (p. 5)

Key points

  1. DTE Electric supports LARS as a reasonable way to evaluate load additions and paired generation.
    DTE Electric supports MISO's proposal and believes the LARS framework represents a reasonable approach to evaluating the transmission, operational, and resource adequacy impacts PDF p. 1
  2. Studying load and generation together can show different impacts than stand-alone studies.
    jointly planned and electrically proximate resources may produce materially different system impacts than traditional stand-alone studies. PDF p. 2
  3. DTE points to deposit, size, site control and pairing requirements as safeguards against speculative requests.
    Participating projects must satisfy applicable deposit, minimum size, site control, and commercial operation requirements. PDF p. 3
  4. DTE notes any eligible entity may be the interconnection customer for the generation.
    permitting the interconnection customer responsible for the generation component to be any eligible entity PDF p. 3

What the document says

PDF p. 2

Supporters say studying load and generation together removes duplicative upgrades and lowers costs.

By allowing large loads and associated generation resources to be studied together, the proposal recognizes that jointly planned and electrically proximate resources may produce materially different system impacts PDF p. 2

PDF p. 3

MISO says LARS answers the Show Cause Order and leaves the generator queue untouched.

The LARS filing also preserves an open and non-discriminatory framework by permitting the interconnection customer responsible for the generation component to be any eligible entity. PDF p. 3
