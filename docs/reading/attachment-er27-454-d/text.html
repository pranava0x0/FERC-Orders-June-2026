Initial RBP Target  
4. 
PJM proposes to update the Initial RBP Target using the lesser of the projected 
shortfall established from the latest load forecast and planning parameters for the 
2028/2029 Delivery Year that will be available at the time the RBP is conducted, 
and the aggregate quantity of Large Load growth reported to PJM, rather than

3 
relying solely on the shortfall quantity established through the Base Residual 
Auction for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year, held in July 2026.    
5. 
First, utilizing the latest information allows PJM to identify the most accurate 
estimate of the reliability need for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year based on the latest 
load forecast of demand that will need to be served and the expected supply.  
Specifically, because this updated information will be available prior to conducting 
the RBP, it is appropriate to use the most up-to-date information to produce an 
Initial RBP Target that represents the latest expected reliability shortfall and avoid 
either under-procurement or over-procurement of resources. 
6. 
Second, applying the “lesser of” calculation using the aggregate quantity of Large 
Load growth reported to PJM for the RBP will mitigate the potential over-allocation 
of RBP Charges to a zone/area relative to the forecasted Large Load growth.  PJM 
believes this scenario is unlikely based on currently available information.  
Specifically, PJM’s 2026 Load Forecast indicates approximately 9,000 MW of large 
load growth, while the projected Regional Transmission Organization (“RTO”) 
shortfall for the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction is approximately 6,800 MW.  
Although these values may change as updated forecasts become available in 2027, 
PJM expects the relationship between Large Load growth and the projected 
shortfall to remain generally consistent.  Nevertheless, including the “lesser of” 
calculation in the Initial Target provides an important safeguard by accounting for 
the possibility that previously reported Large Load agreements may be canceled 
between the collection of data for the 2027 Load Forecast and the establishment of 
the RBP.  By ensuring that RBP cost allocations do not exceed 100% of the Large 
Load growth reported for the applicable zone/area, the proposed procurement target 
and cost allocation framework better align cost responsibility with the principles of 
cost causation and beneficiary pays. 
7. 
Further, since PJM plans to run the RBP in March 2027, by that time PJM will have 
conducted the 2029/2030 Base Residual Auction, scheduled for December 2026.  
That auction may produce a shortfall, and of different quantity than for the 
2028/2029 Delivery Year.  Nevertheless, PJM proposes to maintain the 2028/2029 
Delivery Year shortfall as the basis for the RBP.  This approach is consistent with 
the underlying intent of the RBP to address the nearer-term reliability need while 
allowing market fundamentals to address the longer-term resource adequacy.  
8. 
Additionally, adopting a different procurement target timeframe would necessitate 
broader revisions to the overall design, including resource eligibility requirements 
and commitment terms.  Because market participants have already planned and 
made business decisions based on PJM conducting the RBP for the 2028/2029 
Delivery Year, maintaining the existing design is prudent.  Doing so promotes 
certainty, preserves stakeholder expectations, and avoids unnecessary disruption to 
the market. 
9. 
The RBP is intended to serve as a bridge mechanism to address immediate 
reliability concerns while PJM and stakeholders continue to pursue longer-term

market design reforms aimed at improving investment and retention signals to 
support resource adequacy.  In support of those efforts, PJM has initiated its 
Powering Reliability Through Market Design initiative with stakeholders and 
continues to work with the Charles River Associates to facilitate bilateral matching 
efforts. 
10. 
The shortfall MW calculation that will set the Initial RBP Target will be calculated 
using the updated information from the 2027 Load Forecast and associated updated 
planning parameters and submitted Large Load MW values from the Electric 
Distributors.  More specifically, PJM will calculate the Initial RBP Target MW as:  
The lesser of: 
(1) the PJM Region Reliability Requirement for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year 
minus the UCAP-equivalent of the ICAP quantity of Capacity Resources that 
cleared in the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction 
Where the PJM Region Reliability Requirement for the 2028/2029 Delivery 
Year will be calculated as the RTO Region Peak Load Forecast for the 
2028/2029 Delivery Year from the 2027 Load Forecast times the 2028/2029 
Forecast Pool Requirement from the updated planning parameters published 
in 2027 prior to the commencement of the RBP, less the sum of all updated 
Unforced Capacity Obligations of FRR Entities in the PJM Region.  
And where the UCAP-equivalent of the ICAP quantity of Capacity 
Resources that cleared in the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction, where the 
UCAP-equivalent values will be determined by the Capacity Resources’ 
updated capacity accreditation factors for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year that 
will be available in the updated planning parameters available just prior to 
the commencement of the RBP. 
And  
(2) the UCAP equivalent of the aggregate region-wide Large Load MW quantity 
reported by the Electric Distributor 
Where the UCAP equivalent of the aggregate region-wide Large Load MW 
is the aggregate region-wide Large Load MW times the most recent 
2028/2029 Forecast Pool Requirement. 
11. 
The RBP will function as a safety net to address the shortfall MW for the 2028/2029 
Delivery Year that has not been addressed by new Large Load bringing their own 
new capacity through bilateral agreements or otherwise.  The RBP is a stop-gap, 
one-time measure aimed specifically at addressing the resource adequacy shortfall 
in the PJM Region, an effort designed to work in part with the load that has 
voluntarily brought supply to the PJM Region—independently or with support of 
ongoing PJM bilateral matching efforts—and a return to market fundamentals to 
support the expected load growth over the next decade.

RBP Cost Allocation Methodology 
12. 
The cost allocation structure begins with the Initial RBP Target based on the Large 
Load growth between the 2026 summer peak load and the forecasted Large Load 
MW in each Electric Distributor’s zone/area for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year.  The 
Initial RBP Target is then adjusted by the quantity of Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP 
MW and Peak Shaving Adjustment opt-out to reach the Final RBP Target.  Each 
Electric Distributor’s zones/areas with resulting forecasted Large Load growth 
between 2026 and the 2028/2029 Deliver Year will be allocated a pro rata share of 
the Final RBP Target.1  The MW quantity assigned to each Electric Distributor’s 
zone/area will be further allocated to Load Serving Entities (“LSE”) based on the 
RBP Obligation (“RBO”), which permits states to determine the appropriate cost 
allocation to specific LSEs.  Should the Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory 
Authority(s), through the relevant Electric Distributors, decline to provide an RBO 
MW, PJM has proposed to apply a backup default cost allocation where costs would 
be allocated to all of the LSEs in the zone/area based on their percent share of the 
zone/area’s Daily Unforced Capacity Obligation.  
Step 1: 
Collecting the Forecasted Large Load Growth Between 2026 
Through the 2028/2029 Delivery Year Subject to RBP Cost 
Allocation  
13. 
Seven days prior to the commencement of the RBP, PJM will receive all Large 
Load growth in the Electric Distributor zone/area from the 2026/2027 Delivery 
Year through the 2028/2029 Delivery Year.  The Large Load growth will be 
submitted based on firm commitments, as supported by electric service obligation, 
construction commitment, or equivalent agreements, and measured based on 
summer peak values.  These submissions are required to align with the Large Loads 
submitted to PJM for the 2027 load forecast.  Any discrepancies between the 
Electric Distributors’ submissions for RBP and data submitted to PJM for the 2027 
load forecast must be accompanied by written documentation of the change from 
the Electric Distributor.  
14. 
The aggregate Large Loads growth will be submitted within a zone/area, along with 
identification of the underlying Large Loads.  This will result in PJM having the 
aggregate value to determine the total Large Load MW for the zone/area and the 
summation of all zone/areas to determine the total Large Load MW for the RTO 
Region.  The underlying Large Loads will be used to facilitate the Qualifying RBP 
Offset UCAP MW process.

The Bounds of Qualifying Offsets 
23. 
Large Load can be removed from the Large Load MW used to determine RBP cost 
allocation by bringing Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW.  A Large Load can bring 
up to their capacity obligation (in UCAP), determined as peak MW times the 
Forecast Pool Requirement.  MW from supply resources in excess of that value will 
not be considered to decrement the Large Load growth in a zone/area or decrement 
the Initial RBP Target value.   
24. 
A zone/area cannot reduce its zone/area share of the Initial RBP Target beyond the 
capacity obligation of the total Large Load MW in that zone/area (floored at zero).  
Further, a zone/area must provide Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW equal to 
capacity obligation of the total Large Load MW in that zone/area to be fully 
removed from the RBP cost allocation.
