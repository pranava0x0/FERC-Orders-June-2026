POST-TECHNICAL CONFERENCE JOINT STATEMENT OF CURRENT 
AND FORMER SECTOR REPRESENTATIVES AT THE  
PJM NOMINATING COMMITTEE 
The undersigned Current and Former Sector Representatives at the PJM Nominating Committee 
(the “Representatives”) are submitting this Joint Statement to assist the Commission and 
participants in this proceeding with an understanding of the Nominating Committee process and 
their experience on the Nominating Committee.  Nothing in this Joint Statement is intended to 
provide a response or opinion on the questions the Commission has circulated in preparation for 
Settlement discussions in September.1 
Governing Documents: Section 7.1 of the Operating Agreement establishes the Nominating 
Committee.  This Section prescribes Board qualification requirements and Nominating Committee 
deadlines2.  The PJM Manual 34 on the Stakeholder Process restates the Operating Agreement’s 
provisions.  The Manual notes that the Nominating Committee has its own Charter and Code of 
Conduct. 
The Nominating Committee Charter and Formation of the Committee 
The Members Handbook contains the Nominating Committee Charter.  Of particular importance 
is the composition of the committee: Three Board Members who are not up for re-election, of 
which one member cannot vote but chairs the committee.  In addition, each of the five sectors in 
PJM elects a representative annually to the committee.  The PJM President may also participate as 
an ex officio non-voting member.  Finally, the Secretary of PJM shall attend to serve as the 
secretary of the Nominating Committee, and the Assistant to the President and PJM Board shall 
attend for the purpose of scheduling meetings.  
The Nominating Committee’s mission is to identify and nominate qualified Board of Managers 
candidates for election by the Members Committee, and to report annually to the Members on the 
nomination process.  A quorum is necessary for a vote to be successful.  Votes are by blind ballot.

Core duties: Developing/reviewing Board qualification criteria; at its option, retaining an

distributing nominee information to the Members Committee at least 30 days before the Annual 
Meeting.3 
The Nominating Committee Code of Conduct. 
Prior to the commencement of the annual work of the Nominating Committee, each member of 
the Nominating Committee and each ex officio participant in the Nominating Committee executes 
a certification that he or she has read and understood the content, spirit and intent of the 
Nominating Committee Code of Conduct and agrees that he or she will fully abide by its terms.  
Although the Code of Conduct provides the process by which candidates are identified, 
interviewed and then elected, the two most salient points are the confidentiality provisions and the 
requirement that the Representatives set aside their sector interests for the greater needs of PJM: 
“1. The members of the Nominating Committee shall undertake and fulfill their duties in 
furtherance of the best interests of PJM without regard to the individual or affiliated 
company interests of any member of the Nominating Committee and without regard to 
the interests of any other PJM member or group of PJM members.” 
“2. All information that the members of the Nominating Committee receive in undertaking 
and performing their duties shall be deemed confidential information of PJM and shall 
not be disclosed in any manner to any other member of PJM or any other person, except 
as specifically permitted by this Code of Conduct.”4 
The Process.  The Nominating Committee meets as frequently as necessary to accomplish their 
mission.  It has been the course of conduct that an outside consultant is hired to assist in identifying 
and vetting applicants.  The only exception to the confidentiality provisions governing the 
Nominating Committee is that the consultant is privy to confidential information and is required 
to sign confidentiality agreements. 
The Representatives would like to highlight the confidentiality that is critical in this process.  Many 
candidates are applying while employed and would leave their employment if chosen.  Other 
candidates want their privacy to be respected in the event they are not chosen.  The Representatives 
believe that the pool of candidates could diminish if this strict adherence to confidentiality were 
not maintained.  Additionally, the Code of Conduct explicitly states at the outset that “it is the 
policy of PJM to attract the best qualified candidates to serve on the PJM Board.  In order to fulfill 
that goal, it is the policy of PJM to employ nominating procedures that strictly maintain the

confidentiality of the identity of potential Board candidates and that ensure that those involved in 
the nomination process strictly follow procedures that maintain such confidentiality.” 
Representatives may choose to meet with their sector members to discuss the open Board seats, 
what type of candidate would be a good fit, and to encourage stakeholder nominations as well.  
With respect to a Board member who could run for re-election, the Representatives may receive 
input from others but must abide by the confidentiality provisions of the Code of Conduct. 
Stakeholders may provide input with respect to an individual board member whose term is up for 
renomination.  The Representatives may not disclose whether a Board member is seeking re-
election and may not disclose any non-public information.   
The usual practice has been that the consultant will manage the vetting of candidate’s resumes, 
which may be submitted by anyone, including a Member of PJM, a state regulator, or even a 
stakeholder with an interest in PJM.  In fact, the public notice of the initiation of the Nominating 
Committee with information about the qualifications and experience being targeted includes 
information for how resumes may be provided to the consultant.  The consultant identifies potential 
candidates, the Nominating Committee asks questions to understand the background, experience 
and potential conflicts of those potential candidates.  Ultimately, the Nominating Committee 
conducts a blind vote on the list of potential candidates provided by the consultant indicating which 
candidates the Nominating Committee desires to further consider.  The consultant then narrows 
the list down based on the candidates that received the most votes.  At this point, the candidates 
are discussed, and a decision is made about whether a candidate should proceed to the next step of 
evaluation.  In recent years, best efforts have been made to ensure candidates are interviewed by 
the entire Nominating Committee. 
Representatives evaluate candidates based on the candidates’ qualifications and whether they meet 
the requirements to fill the open Board seat.  They consider each candidate’s career, integrity, 
personality fit with the Board, and, most importantly, whether or how the candidate would help 
further the best interests of PJM.  Although the Member Representatives are selected by their 
respective sectors, their role on the nominating committee is not to advocate for the interest or 
preferred outcome of those sectors.  In evaluating candidates, representatives are expected to set 
aside sector-specific commercial and policy interests and exercise independent judgement based 
on the qualifications established in the Operating Agreement, the needs of the Board as a whole, 
and the best interest of PJM.  Sector affiliation provides the Committee with a diversity of 
perspectives, but it does not determine which candidate a Representative supports.  Consistent with 
that responsibility, the Committee focuses on identifying candidates who are likely to form their 
own opinions that are supported by expertise in fields necessary for PJM to function, and question 
PJM staff, PJM management, the Independent Market Monitor and stakeholders.  The most 
challenging discussions occur with respect to which candidate has the best qualifications to fill a 
seat when candidates could satisfy various qualifications identified in the Operating Agreement.  
After a candidate has been nominated by the Nominating Committee, the consultant goes through 
typical background checks.  Under Section 9 of the Code, the President then informs the candidates 
of their selection, as well as the Board and the Members Committee.

Finally, a Members Committee vote on the Board nominations takes place at the PJM Annual 
Meeting held in the Spring.  If the Members Committee does not achieve a quorum or an 
affirmative vote to approve the Nominating Committee’s recommendations, then the process starts 
anew.  While there have been sector votes against specific nominees, it is rare that the majority of 
the Members Committee does not approve the Nominating Committee’s recommendations, as 
occurred in 2025.  Overall, the Nominating Committee process has generally resulted in Board 
candidates receiving the necessary support and has served as an important component of PJM’s 
governance for decades.  The membership has a high degree of confidence and respect for the 
integrity of the Nominating Committee process that has supported a decades-long track record of 
success for PJM notwithstanding the current resource adequacy and market challenges.   
Again, the Representatives appreciate the opportunity to provide insight into their experience on 
the Nominating Committee in hopes of aiding the Commission in its deliberations of PJM 
governance.
