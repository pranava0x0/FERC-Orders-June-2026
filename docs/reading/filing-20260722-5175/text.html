MOTION TO RESCIND SHOW CAUSE ORDER AS TO
ALLEGHENY ELECTRIC COOPERATIVE, INC.

Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Allegheny Electric Cooperative, Inc. (“Allegheny”) hereby submits this motion to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or “Commission”) to rescind the show cause order (“PJM Show Cause Order”)2 directed to Allegheny and issued on June 18, 2026, in the above captioned proceeding.

I. DESCRIPTION OF ALLEGHENY
Allegheny, the owner of transmission facilities turned over to the operational control of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) and thus a PJM transmission owner (“PJM TO”), is a non-profit electric generation and transmission cooperative (“G&T Cooperative”) corporation, incorporated under the Electric Cooperative Law of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.  It is the exclusive wholesale supplier of its 14 electric distribution cooperative members located in Pennsylvania and New Jersey.  Like other G&T Cooperatives, Allegheny is owned entirely by its members.  Each of Allegheny’s members either receives financing under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936 or sells less than 4 million megawatt hours of electricity per year.

For this reason, Allegheny is not a public utility subject to FERC jurisdiction, pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act.3

II. RELEVANT BACKGROUND
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the PJM Show Cause Order, thereby initiating a proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act.  The PJM Show Cause Order found that PJM’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential for failure to include specific provisions addressing the interconnection of data centers, manufacturing facilities, and other large energy users.4  The PJM Show Cause Order directed PJM and certain of the PJM TOs either to explain how the Tariff remains just and reasonable without specific provisions for the interconnection of large load or to develop such provisions.5

On the same day, the Commission initiated similar Federal Power Act Section 206 show cause proceedings against five other regional transmission organizations/independent system operators (“RTOs/ISOs”) and their transmission owners.6

III. JUSTIFICATION FOR RESCISSION
The Commission directed the PJM Show Cause Order to PJM and to those PJM TOs that are public utilities, and it expressed an intention to exempt those PJM TOs that are not subject to FERC jurisdiction.7  The Commission specifically indicated that it did not address the PJM Show Cause Order to the following PJM TOs because they are not public utilities pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act:  AMP Transmission, LLC; City of Cleveland,

Department of Public Utilities, Division of Cleveland Public Power; City of Hamilton, Ohio; East Kentucky Power Cooperative, Inc.; and Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative, Inc.8 The Commission, however, noted that it retains the authority to review each of these entities’ tariff provisions as a component of PJM’s Tariff in an order to show cause directed towards PJM, and it also noted that PJM is obliged to respond regarding these entities’ tariff provisions.9 As demonstrated above, Allegheny is not a public utility pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act.  Accordingly, like each of the PJM TOs that the Commission identified as non-public utilities, Allegheny should have been exempted from the PJM Show Cause Order. Allegheny believes that the Show Cause Order directed to it was issued in error.  In requesting rescission of the Show Cause Order as to it, Allegheny acknowledges the Commission’s authority to review the Allegheny-specific provisions of the Tariff and pledges to cooperate with PJM, to the extent that PJM requests assistance from Allegheny, in crafting its response. Allegheny submits this request for recission consistent with the Commission’s guidance to transmission owners that are not subject to its jurisdiction located in other ISO/RTO regions. For instance, the Commission directed the CAISO Show Cause Order to CAISO transmission owners that are not public utilities despite their non-jurisdictional status “because their Transmission Owner Tariffs on file with the Commission detail the wholesale load interconnection process in CAISO.”10  The Commission advised that, “[i]f a non-public utility wishes to be removed from this proceeding, we will rescind the show cause order against them at their request … ”11  Allegheny’s motion for rescission is thus consistent with this guidance.
