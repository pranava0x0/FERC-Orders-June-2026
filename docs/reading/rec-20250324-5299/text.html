TO THE ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE  
 
Data centers have vast importance for economic development and national security 
in the United States.  To foster data center development and to maintain U.S. leadership in 
this transformative industry, the Commission should act expeditiously to reduce any 
regulatory barriers that may hinder their development.  As relevant to this proceeding, data 
centers should have the option to select a configuration that best fits their particular 
circumstances and needs – whether this is in front of the meter or a co-location 
arrangement.  While there has been significant litigation regarding co-location 
arrangements in recent months, the fact is that co-locating load with generation is not 
something new, but has been available in PJM for over two decades based on the 
Commission’s recognition that locating load in close proximity to generation resources 
will reduce system costs and benefit customers.  Within the Public Service Electric and 
Gas Company (“PSE&G”) service territory and elsewhere in PJM, there are numerous 
customers, such as universities, industrial customers, pharmaceutical companies, and 
rooftop solar customers, that have behind the meter generation (“BTMG”) resources to

First, the Show Cause Order appropriately recognizes that (i) there are different 
co-location load configurations available; (ii) their use or impact on the power grid is

Accordingly, it logically follows that transmission charges, if imposed, must be based on 
usage of the power grid and should not be imposed on a blanket, one-size-fits-all basis.  
To comply with cost causation principles, which courts have described as adding “flesh to 
the bare statutory bones of the just and reasonable requirement” under the Federal Power 
Act,8  any charges imposed must reflect the use or benefit of the specific co-located load 
arrangement.  A transmission pricing methodology that reflects cost causation would also 
provide the appropriate incentives for data centers to locate in close proximity to 
generation resources to reduce transmission costs.

between co-located loads and other types of arrangements where load and generation share

BACKGROUND 
A. 
Description of PSE&G  
PSE&G is a regulated electric and gas utility and owns 1,600 circuit miles of 
electric transmission lines within the PJM region.  PSE&G also has 2.4 million electric 
customers in its franchised service territory and an obligation to reliably serve those 
customers.  Many customers on the PSE&G system are large load customers, such as 
universities, government agencies, manufacturers, and pharmaceutical companies, who 
rely on solar, cogeneration, reciprocating engine, gas turbine generation, combined heat 
and power (“CHP”), and other types of generation resources on a behind the meter basis to 
meet all or some of their power needs.  In fact, according to the New Jersey Board of 
Public Utilities’ (“NJBPU”) Office of Clean Energy, there are currently over 200,000 
behind the meter solar projects in New Jersey.11  Of this number, there are approximately 
90,000 behind-the-meter solar installations in PSE&G’s service territory.  For 23 
consecutive years, PSE&G has won reliability awards in the Mid-Atlantic region and has a 
proven record of providing reliable and affordable service to customers.12

In determining what transmission service charges, if any, should be imposed in the 
context of different co-located configurations, the Commission should simply apply well-
established cost causation principles that courts have recognized as informing the just and 
reasonable standard. 24 A bedrock ratemaking principle is that all FERC-jurisdictional 
rates and charges must “reflect to some degree the costs actually caused by the customer 
who must pay for them,” and that costs must be allocated in a manner that is “at least 
commensurate with the benefits that entity receives.”25  Under this principle, which has 
been the foundation of the Commission’s transmission pricing policies, transmission 
charges to be assessed based on a party’s “actual use” of the transmission system.  
Commission precedent in the co-location context teaches that in circumstances in which 
certain customers “rely to a lesser degree on the PJM integrated transmission system” than 
others, the transmission charges must reflect the parties’ actual use of the transmission 
system because this would ensure that “PJM allocates its transmission charges to those

imposed or benefits drawn by that party.”28  Additionally, the court stressed that “the cost-
causation principle, by allocating project costs consistent with project benefits, creates the

campus needs for electricity.44  With multiple campuses located on PSE&G’s system, 
Rutgers owns and operates a number of onsite generating facilities.45  Charging 
transmission based on a gross load approach would have broad application and adverse 
impacts on entities like Rutgers and on the customers of QFs across the PJM footprint, and 
would disrupt the settled expectations of these entities that they need not take transmission 
service when relying on their on-site generation resources.  Accordingly, any replacement 
rate that the Commission may adopt must not adversely impact existing customers with 
BTMG and their ability to net and reduce the use of the transmission system.

government, including this agency, should be doing very best they can to

In recognition of the importance of data center for economic development and 
national security issues, the Commission should expeditiously take action in this 
proceeding to provide much needed clarity and certainty as to the “rules of the road” for 
co-located load arrangements.  In so doing, the Commission should maintain optionality 
so that customers have the ability to select the best configuration for their particular 
circumstances and needs.  In particular, the Commission should continue to keep in mind

of providing the proper pricing incentives to encourage entities to reduce their usage of the 
transmission system.  This means allocating costs based on the co-located load’s actual

CONCLUSION  
WHEREFORE, for the reasons discussed herein, the Commission should 
expeditiously issue an order that provides timely and needed clarity with respect to co-
located load rules and that leads to a result that is (i) consistent with cost causation 
principles; (ii) avoids adversely impacting existing BTMG customers in PJM;  and (iii)
