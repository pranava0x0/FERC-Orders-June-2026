4. Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”)
New Large Loads may avoid or reduce their IRAS exposure by contracting with qualifying new capacity. BYONC is defined as new megawatts of Unforced Capacity (“UCAP”) that meet specified “gating criteria” to ensure that the capacity is genuinely new – including new-build generation, qualifying uprates, repowered deactivated resources, qualifying fuel conversions, and certain new demand-side resources.27 To ensure that the capacity benefits are durable, BYONC must participate as a price taker ($0/MW offer) in RPM Auctions for ten consecutive Delivery Years, subject to all capacity market rules.28 A New Large Load’s potential IRAS obligation is inversely proportional to the amount of BYONC and Allocated RBP UCAP MW it has secured.29

5. VRR Curve Exclusion
Beginning with the 2029/2030 Delivery Year, PJM proposes to exclude incremental New Large Loads from the VRR Curve to the extent that they do not have sufficient BYONC or Allocated RBP UCAP MW arrangements.30 Once a New Large Load’s associated BYONC is offered into the applicable RPM Auction, the corresponding load would be reflected in the demand curve.31 This measure is intended to protect residential customers from capacity price increases attributable to large-load additions that have not secured their own capacity.32 It also has the effect of limiting BYONC to only those resources that clear simultaneously with the New Large Load.

B. Elements of PJM’s IRAS Proposal Are Not Just and Reasonable.

Resource adequacy is a forward-looking investment and planning function
20. Resource adequacy planning ensures that sufficient resources will be available to serve forecast load and maintain reliability standards. Because generation resources often require years to permit, finance, construct and interconnect, utilities and other load-serving entities make resource development and procurement decisions well before a resource enters service. Reliability outcomes depend on investment decisions, contractual commitments, and development activities that occur years before commercial operation.

21. Utilities routinely commit capital and organizational resources to projects long before those projects satisfy market qualification requirements. Site acquisition, permitting, equipment procurement, financing, engineering, construction, and interconnection activities all occur during the development process. These actions represent material commitments that determine whether a resource ultimately reaches commercial operation. As a result, resource commitment and market qualification represent distinct milestones. Resource commitment reflects a decision to develop or procure a resource to meet future system needs, whereas market qualification determines whether PJM recognizes that resource under its tariff for

For example, a resource may enter service during a Delivery Year and provide energy and ancillary services to the grid, yet not have cleared as a capacity resource in an RPM auction for that Delivery Year.

22. Practically, resource adequacy is enhanced when investment and development decisions result in resources entering service and becoming available to support system reliability. Under the proposed IRAS framework, New Large Loads remain subject to curtailment until PJM recognizes associated BYONC resources. However, rather than linking BYONC recognition to a resource’s operational in-service date and its contribution to resource adequacy, PJM conditions recognition on compliance with specified RPM qualification and participation requirements. As a result, differences between RPM administrative timelines and resource in-service schedules may directly affect whether a New Large Load remains subject to curtailment under IRAS during a Delivery Year.
