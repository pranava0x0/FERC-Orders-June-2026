Commenter respectfully urges the Commission, in evaluating the responses to its Show Cause Orders, to

II. The Binding Contingency Has Shifted 
Reserve adequacy and frequency-response planning have historically been sized against an enumerated 
largest credible contingency — typically the loss of the single largest generating unit — treated as 
physical and independent. Large computational load introduces a contingency of a different character: a 
set of geographically dispersed loads that, through a shared orchestration or control plane, can reduce 
demand in near-simultaneity, behaving as a single correlated block. 
This block is invisible to topology-based N-1 analysis, which scores dispersed loads as independent low-
probability events. Measured at the interconnection boundary, the same loads exhibit correlated ramp 
events at machine speed. Where the coordinated-drop magnitude of the block exceeds the loss-of-largest-
unit value, the largest credible contingency is no longer a generator — it is the load block. A reserve 
adequacy computed against the former, at representative system conditions, is measuring the wrong event 
at the wrong condition. 
III. The Frequency-Response Absorption Standard (Minimum-Inertia Condition) 
A load drop pushes system frequency upward. The immediate rate of change of frequency (RoCoF), 
before primary frequency response (PFR) engages, is governed by the swing equation: 
df/dt  =  − ΔP / (2 H · S_base) 
where ΔP is the net imbalance from the coupled-block drop, H is the system inertia constant, and S_base 
is the system base. Stability requires two conditions simultaneously: the initial RoCoF must remain below 
the protection threshold at which relays trip generation, and the frequency excursion before PFR arrests it 
must remain within the trip band. Both depend on H. 
System inertia is not constant. As synchronous generation retires and is displaced by inverter-based 
resources, H declines, reaching its lowest values under high non-synchronous output and low 
synchronous commitment — conditions that recur on a dispatch-driven schedule. The consequence is a 
compounding exposure: available inertia (H) shrinks while the coordinated-drop magnitude (ΔP) grows, 
both driving RoCoF upward at once. Evaluation at average or representative inertia systematically 
understates the risk, because the binding failure occurs at the minimum, not the mean. 
Commenter therefore respectfully submits that the absorption of any behaviorally-coupled load block 
must be evaluated at a credible minimum-inertia condition — the lowest inertia state expected to recur 
within the planning horizon at a stated low exceedance probability, adjusted for scheduled synchronous 
retirements and forecast resource mix, and crediting only firmly committed grid-forming or synchronous-
condenser contribution. Expressed as a checkable interconnection requirement, a coupled block is 
absorbable only if, at that minimum-inertia condition, its resulting RoCoF remains below the protection 
threshold and deliverable primary frequency response within the arrest window is at least equal to the 
block magnitude. Where neither holds, there exists a coupled-load penetration beyond which further 
interconnection is not physically feasible without defined, committed grid-strengthening measures. This is 
a limit set by physics, not by policy; the standard’s function is to locate it before it is discovered by a 
coordinated-drop event.

IV. The Coupling-Discrimination Protocol 
A rigorous standard must distinguish the true common-mode risk from legitimate distributed flexibility, 
because both produce correlated load movement. Two loads may move together because a shared control 
plane commanded them (orchestration-coupling — the contingency), or because each independently 
responded to the same public signal, such as price or system frequency (environmental correlation — the 
demand-response behavior the system relies upon). A metric based on aggregate correlation alone cannot 
separate them and would erroneously penalize legitimate response. 
The protocol discriminates on signatures observable at the boundary, without knowledge of internal 
cause: the dispersion of ramp-event onset times (a shared command arrives within a narrow machine-
speed window; independent responses disperse over each participant’s own reaction time); the 
congruence of ramp waveform shape (a command replicates one instruction; independent responses vary 
in shape); and, decisively, the correlation that survives conditioning on common environmental signals. 
Only co-movement that remains after removing the price-, frequency-, dispatch-, and weather-explained 
components counts toward the coupling coefficient: 
ρ_coupling  =  corr( residualᵢ , residualⱼ )  after conditioning on common environmental signals 
A set of loads all responding to a price signal shows high raw correlation but near-zero residual 
correlation — correctly cleared as environmental. A set sharing a scheduler shows correlation that 
survives conditioning — correctly identified as orchestrated. Only genuinely orchestration-coupled load 
enters the block magnitude tested under Section III. This resolves each participant into a measured 
position — coupled and subject to the absorption test, environmentally correlated and cleared, or 
independent — so that no party is swept into a contingency obligation by mere correlation, and none 
escapes one by disputing intent. The classification is reproducible from boundary data. 
V. Remediation Is Intrinsic to the Measurement, Not Appended to It 
This section states an engineering property of the architecture, not a policy position. In the settlement 
measure, the quantity that identifies unaccounted-for load and the quantity that reassigns its cost are the 
same quantity. The measure computes the difference between declared and delivered load; that difference 
is, simultaneously and by the same arithmetic, the measured extraction and the cost-causation basis that 
reassigns it to the load that caused it. There is no intermediate discretionary step — no point at which an 
operator elects whether to reallocate — because the reallocation is not a subsequent decision applied to 
the measurement; it is the measurement, read as a ledger entry. Detecting the extraction and correcting it 
are one operation. Value recovered is thereby directed, by the arithmetic itself, to those who bore its cost, 
to the grid infrastructure its presence stressed, and to the structural correction of the conditions that 
permitted the misallocation, so that it does not recur. 
Because the two are one operation, accountability is not a discretionary layer that any institution is asked 
to honor by declaration; it is a property of the invariants, such that the measurement and the reassignment 
cannot be performed independently of one another. This removes the discretion through which 
unaccounted-for costs are ordinarily socialized: a system that performs the measurement has, in the same 
step, performed the reallocation. Stated in the Commission’s terms, the protection of the ratepaying public 
here is not an aim asserted alongside the technical work — it is a defined output of the equations, present 
or absent as a measurable quantity rather than as a declared posture. The specific mechanism for
