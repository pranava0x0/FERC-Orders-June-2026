Backstop procurement and shortage service

PJM put the procurement rules and curtailment rules into the same stakeholder process. A large customer without enough new supply could have to cut demand before existing customers.

Connecting before transmission upgrades

PJM was considering contractual load reductions as a way to connect some customers sooner. The planning question was who could order a reduction and how that obligation would be divided.

would allow certain large load customers to connect to the grid prior to completion of necessary transmission upgrades PDF p. 5

Resource adequacy · PDF pp. 6–8

Three choices for the capacity market

PJM put three paths on the table: long-term procurement, different reliability targets within the region, or greater reliance on energy and reserve revenue. They were alternatives for discussion.

either through mandatory LSE hedging or through centralized long-term procurement PDF p. 7

Reserve pricing

PJM wanted reserve prices to reflect the cost of ramping generators when demand or supply turns out differently than forecast.

recalibrate reserve products and update reserve demand curves to better account for ramping and uncertainty PDF p. 8

PJM was studying whether generators in the third phase of its queue could operate before every study finished, subject to safety and reliability.
