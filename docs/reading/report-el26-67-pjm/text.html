The CIFP addressing service during periods of insufficient resource adequacy was 
convened to focus on developing  a solution to both incentivize investment in generation and 
manage the reliability challenges during the transitional period in which new load additions are 
outpacing the additions of new capacity in the PJM system.  This framework will provide PJM 
operators with a tool to maintain reliability while allowing all large loads to connect to the system. 
Large loads that do not bring sufficient supply resources to the system to cover their requirements 
should not negatively impact reliability for other load, and therefore these large loads may be 
required to reduce their withdrawals from the transmission system before PJM needs to curtail 
other load.7  Because these issues have linkage, the PJM Board moved to consolidate them into 
one CIFP.8

i. Increasing the Pace of Adding Generating Capacity Through Markets 
1. Powering Reliability Through Market Design 
Following the PJM Board’s January 16, 2026 Decisional Letter on the CIFP for Large Load 
Additions directing staff to undertake a holistic review of the PJM capacity market design and 
investment incentives,20 PJM released a report on Powering Reliability Through Market Design.21  
This report delineated three potential paths forward:  (1) stabilizing PJM’s long-term market; (2) 
allowing for differential reliability between regions within the PJM Region; and (3) pursuing a 
transition from reliance on the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) for resource adequacy to the 
energy and Ancillary Services markets.22  The first solution path preserves the existing capacity

2. Reserve Certainty Reforms 
As highlighted in PJM’s report, the RCSTF is developing enhancements to recalibrate 
reserve products and update reserve demand curves to better account for ramping and uncertainty 
and better align reserve pricing with operational practice.29  The PJM proposal put forward in the 
RCSTF is designed to provide more efficient economic incentives through enhanced products for 
resources participating in PJM’s Ancillary Services markets which should also help increase the 
pace of adding new generating capacity to the PJM Region through improved market signals.  
Stakeholder discussion of this issue at the RCSTF concluded on June 25 and voting concluded on 
July 10.30  The proposal will be discussed at the August 24 MRC and voted on at the September 
MRC, with a final vote scheduled to take place at the October MC.31  If a proposal is endorsed by 
stakeholders, PJM plans to file that proposal with the Commission by the end of 2026.32

ii. Increasing the Pace of Adding Generating Capacity Through

1. Expedited Interconnection Track 
On June 9, 2026, the Commission issued an order accepting PJM’s proposed Expedited 
Interconnection Track (“EIT”) proposal.33  In so doing, the Commission found that PJM’s proposal 
“accomplishes the purposes of Order Nos. 2003 and 2023 by helping to ensure that interconnection 
customers are able to interconnect to the transmission system in a reliable, efficient, transparent, 
and timely manner” and that the “EIT proposal will help address PJM’s near-term resource 
adequacy needs by establishing a separate, time-limited, expedited interconnection process for a 
limited number of resources that are able to bring significant capacity onto the system in the near-
term.”34   
The Commission found that the eligibility requirements proposed “are tailored to 
addressing near-term resource adequacy needs and appropriately limit EIT to capacity resources 
that are more likely to timely contribute to meeting the region’s needs”35 thereby increasing the 
pace of adding generating capacity in the PJM Region.  The EIT was developed through the 
Critical Issue Fast Path – Large Load Addition (“CIFP-LLA”) stakeholder process36 which 
culminated in a Special MC meeting on November 19, 2025.  During that process, PJM and 
stakeholders proposed multiple packages that included concepts around an expedited

2. Expansion of Provisional Service  
PJM’s Interconnection Process Subcommittee (“IPS”) is currently exploring an issue 
charge to expand provisional service by identifying under what circumstances generation projects 
that advance to Phase III of a Cycle may be permitted to safely and reliably begin generating prior 
to their completion of certain Cycle studies without impacting the timing or fundamentally altering 
the Generation Interconnection Procedures.38  The IPS intends to report the final results of polling 
related to this stakeholder effort to the Planning Committee in September 2026 for further 
consideration.39  PJM is also assessing the impact of the Show Cause Order on this topic.
