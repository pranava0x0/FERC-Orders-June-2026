As directed by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission”) Order Instituting Proceeding Under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”) (“Show Cause Order”),1 PJM submits this informational report to update the Commission on the current status of proposals under consideration in the stakeholder process to address the issue of resource adequacy to serve new large loads and other ongoing stakeholder processes that aim to increase the pace of adding generating capacity in the PJM Region.

I. INFORMATIONAL FILING
PJM has been working proactively to address the issue of resource adequacy for new large loads and to increase the pace of adding generating capacity in the PJM Region through myriad stakeholder processes.  As further explained below, this effort is most directly evidenced by the Critical Issue Fast Path (“CIFP”) for the development of a reliability backstop procurement (“RBP”) and a framework for service during periods of insufficient resource adequacy.2  These 1 PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. et al., 195 FERC ¶ 61,211, at P 104 (2026) (“Show Cause Order”).

The CIFP addressing service during periods of insufficient resource adequacy was
convened to focus on developing  a solution to both incentivize investment in generation and manage the reliability challenges during the transitional period in which new load additions are outpacing the additions of new capacity in the PJM system.  This framework will provide PJM operators with a tool to maintain reliability while allowing all large loads to connect to the system. Large loads that do not bring sufficient supply resources to the system to cover their requirements should not negatively impact reliability for other load, and therefore these large loads may be required to reduce their withdrawals from the transmission system before PJM needs to curtail other load.7  Because these issues have linkage, the PJM Board moved to consolidate them into one CIFP.8

2. Reserve Certainty Reforms
As highlighted in PJM’s report, the RCSTF is developing enhancements to recalibrate reserve products and update reserve demand curves to better account for ramping and uncertainty and better align reserve pricing with operational practice.29  The PJM proposal put forward in the RCSTF is designed to provide more efficient economic incentives through enhanced products for resources participating in PJM’s Ancillary Services markets which should also help increase the pace of adding new generating capacity to the PJM Region through improved market signals. Stakeholder discussion of this issue at the RCSTF concluded on June 25 and voting concluded on July 10.30  The proposal will be discussed at the August 24 MRC and voted on at the September MRC, with a final vote scheduled to take place at the October MC.31  If a proposal is endorsed by stakeholders, PJM plans to file that proposal with the Commission by the end of 2026.32

ii. Increasing the Pace of Adding Generating Capacity Through
Interconnection Reforms
1. Expedited Interconnection Track
On June 9, 2026, the Commission issued an order accepting PJM’s proposed Expedited Interconnection Track (“EIT”) proposal.33  In so doing, the Commission found that PJM’s proposal “accomplishes the purposes of Order Nos. 2003 and 2023 by helping to ensure that interconnection customers are able to interconnect to the transmission system in a reliable, efficient, transparent, and timely manner” and that the “EIT proposal will help address PJM’s near-term resource adequacy needs by establishing a separate, time-limited, expedited interconnection process for a limited number of resources that are able to bring significant capacity onto the system in the near- term.”34

The Commission found that the eligibility requirements proposed “are tailored to addressing near-term resource adequacy needs and appropriately limit EIT to capacity resources that are more likely to timely contribute to meeting the region’s needs”35 thereby increasing the pace of adding generating capacity in the PJM Region.  The EIT was developed through the Critical Issue Fast Path – Large Load Addition (“CIFP-LLA”) stakeholder process36 which culminated in a Special MC meeting on November 19, 2025. 2. Expansion of Provisional Service
PJM’s Interconnection Process Subcommittee (“IPS”) is currently exploring an issue charge to expand provisional service by identifying under what circumstances generation projects that advance to Phase III of a Cycle may be permitted to safely and reliably begin generating prior to their completion of certain Cycle studies without impacting the timing or fundamentally altering the Generation Interconnection Procedures.38  The IPS intends to report the final results of polling related to this stakeholder effort to the Planning Committee in September 2026 for further consideration.39  PJM is also assessing the impact of the Show Cause Order on this topic.
