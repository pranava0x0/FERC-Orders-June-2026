B. The RBP distorts the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) price signals and unduly discriminates against LSEs serving large loads.

The RBP is a fundamental departure from how the RPM operates. The RPM is designed to procure capacity for all customers using a market structure where the price signals sent by the auction’s outcome encourage generation to enter (or exit) the market in response to demand.21 The RPM is operated “to ensure that enough capacity is procured, at a reasonable cost, to maintain reliable grid operations.”22 The RPM is intended to allow generators providing capacity an opportunity to recover their fixed costs while giving customers the opportunity to procure the least-cost option for capacity as a service. Thus, the auction clearing price (“ACP”) varies from year to year based on the supply present in the market for the series of auctions for each year, the demand from load, and the costs of providing the service, which are a blend of the clearing prices for the auctions in each year’s series. Generators do not receive a firm price from year to year, but neither do capacity buyers. All are exposed to the potential for significant price

NOVEC is also compelled to reiterate that the rest of PJM market participants will benefit from RBP Resources funded by NOVEC and other LSEs serving large loads in a more direct sense. Requiring RBP Resources to bid into the market as price-takers suppresses the capacity price. The suppressed capacity prices benefit other capacity customers who benefit from a reduced capacity price. The suppressed capacity price can also harm RBP Buyers, who are responsible for a contract for difference: because RBP Buyers have to fund (or receive) the difference between the RPM clearing price and the fixed RBP price, a suppressed capacity price means they may pay more when they are forced to true-up the RPM ACP to the RBP, and they may get a lower payment in years where the RPM exceeds the RBP price. Essentially, NOVEC and other RBP Buyers would be subsidizing the rest of the capacity market, a result that is not consistent with the cost-causation principle. And after the first 15-year period, the generation will remain available to the capacity market – a clear benefit, even if difficult to quantify at this time.
