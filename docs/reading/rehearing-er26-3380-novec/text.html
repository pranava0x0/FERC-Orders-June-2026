STATEMENT OF ISSUES AND SPECIFICATIONS OF ERROR 
NOVEC submits the following Statement of Issues and Specifications of Error pursuant 
to Rules 713(c)(1) and 713(c)(2)6 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure: 
1. The Commission erred by not considering and requiring the voluntary, stakeholder-
endorsed subscription model. FERC must engage in reasoned decision-making. See 
Emera Me. v. FERC, 854 F.3d 9, 27 (D.C. Cir. 2017). Reasoned decision-making 
requires FERC to “respond to objections and address contrary evidence in more than 
a cursory fashion.” Trans. Agency of N. Cal. v. FERC, 628 F.3d 538, 543-44 (D.C. 
Cir. 2010) (“TANC”). Further, FERC is required to consider “reasonable alternatives”

the adoption of the voluntary framework as an alternative to the RBP filed by PJM. 
 
2. The Commission erred by not rejecting the as-filed RBP framework because the RBP 
distorts market signals that the capacity market needs to operate and unduly 
discriminates against Load Serving Entities (“LSE”) serving new large loads by 
imposing a capacity surcharge on them. The fact that PJM presumes or expects that 
the LSEs will, in turn, pass these charges through to their data center customers does 
not alter the fact that PJM’s tariff would impose charges on some – but not all – LSEs 
based on the nature of the retail load they are serving. PJM has no authority to base its 
tariff rates and charges on the character of retail service provided by LSEs. As a 
result, the Commission’s decision is arbitrary and capricious, unsupported by record 
evidence, and inconsistent with the FPA and the Commission’s regulations. See 5 
U.S.C. § 706; Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass’n of U.S., Inc. v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. 
Co., 463 U.S. 29, 43 (1983) (quoting Burlington Truck Lines v. United States, 371 
U.S. 156, 168 (1962)); ANR Storage Co. v. FERC, 904 F.3d 1020, 1024 (D.C. Cir. 
2018).

the RBP are within the scope of the paper hearing and that NOVEC is not foreclosed

not addressing NOVEC’s arguments with respect to cost allocation, see Trans. 
Agency of N. Cal. v. FERC, 628 F.3d 538, 543-44 (D.C. Cir. 2010). By approving an 
approach for cost allocation that does not recognize other causes of the capacity

B. 
The RBP distorts the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) price signals and unduly 
discriminates against LSEs serving large loads. 
 
The RBP is a fundamental departure from how the RPM operates. The RPM is designed 
to procure capacity for all customers using a market structure where the price signals sent by the 
auction’s outcome encourage generation to enter (or exit) the market in response to demand.21 
The RPM is operated “to ensure that enough capacity is procured, at a reasonable cost, to 
maintain reliable grid operations.”22 The RPM is intended to allow generators providing capacity 
an opportunity to recover their fixed costs while giving customers the opportunity to procure the 
least-cost option for capacity as a service. Thus, the auction clearing price (“ACP”) varies from 
year to year based on the supply present in the market for the series of auctions for each year, the 
demand from load, and the costs of providing the service, which are a blend of the clearing 
prices for the auctions in each year’s series. Generators do not receive a firm price from year to 
year, but neither do capacity buyers. All are exposed to the potential for significant price

NOVEC is also compelled to reiterate that the rest of PJM market participants will 
benefit from RBP Resources funded by NOVEC and other LSEs serving large loads in a more 
direct sense. Requiring RBP Resources to bid into the market as price-takers suppresses the 
capacity price. The suppressed capacity prices benefit other capacity customers who benefit from 
a reduced capacity price. The suppressed capacity price can also harm RBP Buyers, who are 
responsible for a contract for difference: because RBP Buyers have to fund (or receive) the 
difference between the RPM clearing price and the fixed RBP price, a suppressed capacity price 
means they may pay more when they are forced to true-up the RPM ACP to the RBP, and they 
may get a lower payment in years where the RPM exceeds the RBP price. Essentially, NOVEC 
and other RBP Buyers would be subsidizing the rest of the capacity market, a result that is not 
consistent with the cost-causation principle. And after the first 15-year period, the generation 
will remain available to the capacity market – a clear benefit, even if difficult to quantify at this 
time.
