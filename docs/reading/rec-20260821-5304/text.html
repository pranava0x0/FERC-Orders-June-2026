COMMENTS OF BLUE RIDGE POWER AGENCY 
Pursuant to Rules 211 and 212 of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and 
Procedure1 and the Commission’s notice of filing of August 3, 2026, Blue Ridge 
Power Agency (“Blue Ridge”) submits the following comments on the filing by 
PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) of proposed revisions to the PJM Open 
Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement 
Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish a Reliability Backstop 
Procurement (the “RBP Proposal”).2
As set forth below, Blue Ridge is concerned that the lack of specificity in 
the RBP Proposal as to cost allocation to load-serving entities may result in unjust 
and unreasonable rates to Blue Ridge’s members.  
A.
Blue Ridge

B.
Comments 
In its transmittal letter, PJM describes the RBP Proposal as “a one-time 
procurement of new capacity resources intended to address previously identified 
resource adequacy challenges in the PJM Region arising from insufficient 
commitments of capacity in the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Reliability Pricing 
Model (“RPM”) Auctions.”3 More specifically, PJM asserts that its RBP Proposal 
“is a just and reasonable means to address the influx of large loads while 
maintaining resource adequacy, reliability, and affordability in a manner that 
respects the divide between state and federal authorities and avoids disrupting 
existing commercial arrangements.”4
Blue Ridge shares PJM’s concerns and objectives and recognizes the need 
for interim measures to address them. But Blue Ridge is concerned that the cost-
allocation methods described by PJM are not set forth with enough specificity in

Blue Ridge agrees with PJM’s general proposal to allocate RBP costs 
within a zone/area in accordance with each load-serving entity’s “RBP 
Obligation” (or “RBO”) as determined by a state or Relevant Electric Retail 
Regulatory Authority (RERRA),5 provided that the legal authority of a municipal 
RERRA (e.g., a city council or utility board) over retail electric service in the 
municipal service area is fully respected by PJM. Moreover, given the 
unprecedented 15-year term of PJM’s proposed capacity procurements under the 
RBP Proposal, Blue Ridge is concerned that a one-time allocation of costs may 
become unjust and unreasonable over this 15-year period. Finally, Blue Ridge is 
concerned that the RBP Proposal does not have enough specificity to ensure that 
PJM allocates RBP costs to each area/zone in the PJM region in a manner roughly 
commensurate with cost causation. For example, PJM’s discretion to make load 
adjustments or “Qualifying RBP Offsets” is not adequately specified.6 The 
problem created by this lack of specificity is exacerbated by PJM’s “backup” cost-
allocation method, applicable when an RBO is not determined, which socializes

C.
Conclusion 
PJM’s RBP Proposal does not adequately address the cost-allocation 
concerns set forth above. Blue Ridge suggests that the Commission demand more 
specificity from PJM, or, alternatively, set this filing for hearing and settlement 
procedures.
