Pursuant to section 313 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”)1 and Rule 713 of the 
Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure,2 East Kentucky Power Cooperative, Inc. 
(“EKPC”) respectfully requests rehearing of the Commission’s order accepting, subject to refund 
and additional proceedings, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM”) revisions to its Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities 
(“RAA”) establishing the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) .3 
The Commission erred in accepting aspects of the RBP Proposal without adequately 
addressing EKPC’s cost-causation objections. The Commission did not explain why the existing 
self-supply portfolio of a load-serving entity (“LSE”) may be disregarded in determining the LSE’s 
RBP exposure, or why qualifying offsets need not reduce the obligations of the LSE or the load 
that supplied them. Nor did it explain whether it accepted PJM’s default allocation to all LSEs in 
a zone/area when no state- or Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authority (“RERRA”)-
determined allocation is reported or reserved that distinct issue for further proceedings. The 
Commission rejected EKPC’s Fixed Resource Requirement (“FRR”) comparability argument 
without adequately addressing the relevance of comparable resource-planning actions by

C. 
The Commission’s Order Accepting the RBP Proposal Subject to 
Additional Proceedings 
In the Order, the Commission accepted the RBP for filing, suspended it for five months to 
become effective February 28, 2027, subject to refund, and established paper hearing procedures 
and an FPA section 206 proceeding in Docket No. EL26-108-000.35 The Commission identified 
three hearing topics: cost allocation, Transmission Owner exit rules, and LSE collateral 
requirements.36 It stated that “the other aspects of PJM’s RBP proposal are just and reasonable.”37 
On cost allocation, the Commission questioned reliance on Load Adjustments, directed briefing 
on updated load forecast information, and “decline[d] to further address arguments raised in the 
protests related to PJM’s proposed cost allocation method at this time.”38 It separately upheld the 
FRR exclusion and rejected the comparability argument concerning RPM self-supply LSEs.39 On 
collateral, it upheld seller collateral, questioned LSE collateral, and expressly requested responses 
on “(1) what initial RBP Credit Rate is appropriate and (2) whether 45 days before the start of the 
delivery year is sufficient time for LSEs to post collateral.”40 Those questions do not clearly 
establish the treatment of the independent allocation and collateral-methodology objections raised 
here.

III. Request for Rehearing 
Under the Administrative Procedure Act and federal appellate court precedent, the 
Commission’s decisions will be set aside on appeal if they are “arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of 
discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law,” or “unsupported by substantial evidence.”46 
For the Commission to have engaged in reasoned decision-making, it must have examined the 
relevant data and provided a rational connection between the facts found and the choices made.47 
If the Commission “does not provide a rational explanation for its action based on relevant data, 
then that action is arbitrary and capricious.”48 Here, EKPC, a party aggrieved by the Commission’s 
Order,49 seeks rehearing of the Order because it is arbitrary and capricious, an abuse of discretion, 
not based upon substantial evidence in the record, and otherwise contrary to law. 
The FPA’s just-and-reasonable standard incorporates the cost-causation principle, under 
which all approved rates must “reflect to some degree the costs actually caused by the customer 
who must pay them,” and costs must be allocated in a manner at least roughly commensurate with
