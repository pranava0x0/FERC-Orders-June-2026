B. Constellation Would Have the Commission Improperly and Prematurely
Make a Broad Jurisdictional Determination in this Proceeding.
Under FPA section 201(b)(1), FERC has jurisdiction over “the transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce and . . . the sale of electric energy at wholesale in interstate commerce.”17  Except as otherwise provided for under the FPA, carved out from FERC’s jurisdiction are “facilities used for the generation of electric energy[,] . . . facilities used in local distribution or only for the transmission of electric energy in intrastate commerce, . . . [and] facilities for the transmission of electric energy consumed wholly by the transmitter.”18 The National Association of Regulatory Utility Commissions (“NARUC”) recognizes in a recent resolution that “the ANOPR asserts FERC jurisdiction over the interconnection of large loads to the transmission system” and asks FERC to “make clear that it is affirmatively not asserting jurisdiction over end-use sales, which falls squarely within the exclusive jurisdiction of state retail energy regulatory authorities.”19

C. Constellation’s Preferred Outcome Raises Significant Affordability and
Reliability Concerns.
What Constellation proposes would result in large loads avoiding their share of the cost of the transmission services that they rely upon.  But the services are not free.  If the large loads do not pay for the transmission system and related services that they use, someone else must pay instead.  Constellation’s continued push to subsidize co-located load would result in significant cost-shifts to other customers.24

While transmission and ancillary service charges are the primary issue in this proceeding, load impacts should certainly be taken into consideration.  PJM’s independent market monitor, Monitoring Analytics, LLC, warned in its recent 2025 Quarterly State of the Market Report that rapid data center growth is driving tight supply-demand conditions, higher capacity prices, and

D. Nothing Has Changed: The Standard Under Federal Power Act Section
206 Has Not Been Met.
As a legal matter, nothing has changed since the commencement of this proceeding.  As discussed in the prior submissions of the Indicated TOs, the existing forms of transmission service in PJM—Network Service and Point-to-Point Transmission Service (“PTP Service”)—continue to be just and reasonable methods of providing transmission service to loads in PJM, including loads co-located with generation facilities.

Constellation continues to ignore a fundamental part of FPA section 206:33 only after a successful showing that the existing rate is unjust and unreasonable can the Commission order a replacement rate.34  No such showing has been made.  The PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable, and nothing in the record of this proceeding proves otherwise.  “The burden of proving an unjust or unreasonable rate rests with the party that initiated the proceeding—that is, the Commission or the third-party complainant.”35  Neither the Commission nor any of the parties supporting the initiation of this FPA section 206 proceeding have demonstrated that the existing PJM Tariff forms of transmission service are unjust and unreasonable.  The indisputable fact is that there is a path under the existing PJM Tariff for all customers to get service in a timely fashion at reasonable, non-preferential rates.

III. Conclusion
There are important issues to consider such as possible reforms to the interconnection queue process to minimize unjustified delays.  That is where the problem is—not with the type of transmission services offered.  Constellation wants to avoid paying for Network Service and asks for quick action to establish a new form of service in PJM to accommodate its desires while the Commission simultaneously considers related national policy direction and solutions.  There is no basis for that and the standard under FPA section 206 has not been met to require such a change to the PJM Tariff.  For the reasons stated above, FERC should reject Constellation’s Motion. Should FERC decide to act in this proceeding prior to resolution of the ANOPR proceeding, the complaint and show cause proceedings should be terminated, and FERC should find that the PJM Tariff remains just and reasonable.
