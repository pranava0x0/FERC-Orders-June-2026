I. 
KEY DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS AND RATIONALE 
4. 
PJM is aiming to commence a procurement of new capacity resources through the 
RBP1 on September 30, 2026.  PJM will set a procurement target for the RBP, 
calculated as the shortfall MW from the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction 
(“BRA”), as adjusted to reflect voluntarily contracted new capacity procured by 
load coming on the PJM system in the 2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years 
and any supported load that opts-out of the procurement.  The shortfall MW from 
the 2028/2029 BRA was 6,831.3 MW of UCAP below the Regional Transmission 
Organization (“RTO”) Reliability Requirement. 
5. 
The RBP will function as a safety net to address the shortfall MW from the 
2028/2029 BRA that has not been addressed by new load bringing their own new 
capacity through bilateral agreements or otherwise.  The RBP is a stop-gap, one-
time measure aimed specifically at addressing the resource adequacy shortfall in 
the PJM Region, an effort design to work in part with the load that has voluntarily 
brought supply to the PJM Region—independently or with support of ongoing PJM 
bilateral matching efforts—and a return to market fundamentals to support the 
expected load growth over the next decade.  The RBP seeks to obtain “RBP 
Resources,” a new, defined term in the Tariff, for a fixed, 15-year period, which 
will be set from the 2028/2029 Delivery Year to the 2042/2043 Delivery Year.  The 
commitment term of the RBP Resources will vary by each resource’s commercial 
operation date.  Those RBP Resources with a commercial operation date later than 
2028/2029 will have a shorter commitment, and the RBP commitments will not 
extend beyond the 2042/2043 Delivery Year.  
6. 
PJM will run the procurement mechanism as a pay-as-bid, capacity-only structure, 
with a MW-weighted average levelized procurement cost maximum willingness to 
pay equal to $555/MW-day UCAP across all Delivery Years.  PJM will commit

II. 
REGION-WIDE PROCUREMENT OF RBP RESOURCES 
7. 
The RBP is being driven by the resource adequacy shortfall at the RTO level.  
Therefore, the RBP aims to pool the supply and demand at the RTO level to address 
this shortfall.  PJM does not propose to match specific supply to specific demand 
or to apply locational constraints in the central procurement.  
8. 
PJM recognizes there are locational considerations at large for resource adequacy, 
and these constraints are modeled in the Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”).  RPM 
is an annual three-year forward framework, which allows PJM to model the 
necessary locational parameters—Capacity Emergency Transfer Objectives 
(“CETO”) and Capacity Emergency Transfer Limits (“CETL”)—to accurately 
capture the locational needs in the capacity market.  By contrast, PJM does not 
project and calculate these parameters 15-years out, which is the eligible term of 
the RBP, because PJM does not have the information needed to determine such 
locational parameters for 15-year term capacity commitments.  
9. 
PJM’s interconnection process ensures the initial deliverability of new capacity to 
the RTO.  Going forward, the PJM Regional Transmission Expansion Plan 
(“RTEP”) process annually studies and addresses any transmission upgrades 
needed to ensure the ongoing deliverability of capacity resources.  Load 
deliverability is also assessed as part of the RTEP process, which will identify 
transmission needs as load patterns change.

A. 
Supply Eligible to Reduce Initial RBP Target 
12. 
For supply to qualify as Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW, counterparties would 
be required to demonstrate the existence of commercial bilateral agreements or self-
supply and show that the associated resources result in net new UCAP being made 
available to the PJM system.  The following is a list of resources that could 
demonstrate net- new UCAP and would be considered in this case: 
a. New build including new resources that obtain Capacity Interconnection 
Rights (“CIR”) transferred from deactivated resources or resources that 
have announced deactivation as of April 10, 2026; 
b. Uprates (incremental Installed Capacity (“ICAP”)/CIR only); 
c. Repowering of deactivated generators that have retired as of April 10, 2026; 
d. Surplus interconnection (incremental only); 
e. Resources that undergo fuel-switching to another more efficient fuel type 
that results in a higher UCAP and CIR uprate (incremental only); and 
f. New Load Management (Emergency or Pre-emergency Demand Resources 
and Distributed Energy Resource (“DER”)) Capacity Aggregation 
Resources.  
13. 
PJM is defining this criterion broadly to capture all meaningful sources of new 
UCAP serving new load that contribute to resource adequacy and prevent double 
procuring for new load that is bringing new capacity to the system. 
B. 
Supply Eligible to Participate as RBP Resources in the RBP 
14. 
Resources eligible to participate as RBP Resources in the RBP, if accepted, will be 
procured up through the end of a fixed, 15-year capacity commitment.  PJM 
believes additional requirements are necessary to ensure that resources permitted 
to participate in the RBP align with the underlying objectives of the procurement 
to obtain new physical capacity for the system and demonstrate a high likelihood 
of successfully entering service and remaining available throughout the 
commitment period. 
15. 
The RBP Resource eligibility criteria are comparatively more restrictive than the 
criteria for Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW, and specifically in addition to 
requiring new UCAP be brought to the system, there is a requirement that there is 
new ICAP and Maximum Facility Output (“MFO”) being brought to the system, 
and new or transferred CIRs.2  These additional requirements remove the eligibility 
of two defined sources of new UCAP that may be Qualifying New UCAP MW and

eligible to reduce the Initial RBP Target.  More specifically, the following are not

a. Surplus interconnection (incremental only); and

16. 
These more restrictive eligibility requirements are intended to ensure that resources

17. 
The exclusion of Surplus Interconnection Service (“SIS”) resources from supply 
eligible to serve as RBP Resources was driven by the fact that these projects do not 
bring any additional MFO or CIRs to the system.  This is a defined requirement for 
resources entering through the SIS-defined Tariff provisions.  Resources that do 
interconnect and operate under SIS do not have any rights on their own to the PJM 
system.  By design, a surplus resource can only operate while the existing resource 
is in service, or no more than one year after the deactivation of the existing resource, 
and when the existing resource is not operating and using the interconnection 
service.  Given the 15-year term, resources need to be able to operate independently 
to provide certainty of deliverability for a 15-year term.  Further, these projects, 
while increasing UCAP in a hybrid configuration, do not have clear contributions 
of UCAP between the existing resource and surplus resource creating uncertainty 
and the need for sensitivities or estimates in both the increase system capacity and 
the RBP settlements. 
18. 
This is not a complete barrier to participation of surplus resources in RBP.  As 
mentioned above, these resources are able to participate as Qualifying New UCAP 
MW to reduce the Initial RBP Target.  Further, these sited projects would still have 
an opportunity to offer into the RBP and co-locate with the existing resource and 
be studied by PJM for increased CIRs and MFO at the facility through the 
interconnection process.  
19. 
The exclusion of resources that undergo fuel-switching to another more efficient 
fuel type that results in a higher UCAP and CIR uprate (incremental only) is also 
necessary to provide the certainty of deliverability for a 15-year term expected from 
an RBP Resource.  Fuel switching does not bring new ICAP or new MFO on to the 
system.  There is an anticipated UCAP increase resulting in the resource Effective 
Load Carrying Capability (“ELCC”) class change and resulting accreditation, 
however the potential new UCAP captured from this resource would require some 
estimate of the incremental UCAP value, which may require sensitivity analyses, 
to determine the delivered incremental UCAP and RBP payment, that cannot be 
based on any direct increase in ICAP for the resource.  This creates uncertainty in 
what capacity will be available to the system in the 15-year term and should be 
settled under the RBP.

Additionally, PJM has proposed more stringent requirements on Demand

IV. 
PAY-AS-BID STRUCTURE 
21. 
PJM generally supports a single-clearing-price market design and continues to view 
uniform clearing prices as the most efficient market outcome.  Uniform pricing 
promotes transparent price discovery, facilitates forward pricing, and supports 
competitive market outcomes and the PJM Region sees the benefits of that design 
across its existing markets. 
22. 
However, the proposed RBP differs significantly from PJM’s standard market 
constructs and warrants a different settlement approach which is why PJM is 
proposing a pay-as-bid structure for several reasons unique to this procurement. 
23. 
First, the RBP is intended as a one-time procurement mechanism designed to 
address near-term resource adequacy challenges while PJM continues to focus on 
long-term market fundamentals and supporting continued commercial bilateral 
contracting.  Because this procurement is not designed to be a durable or repeatable 
procurement, its pricing outcomes are less likely to serve as incentive market 
signals. 
24. 
Second, much of the longer-term capacity that the RBP framework seeks to secure 
is expected to be obtained through bilateral arrangements outside of PJM’s 
organized markets.  A pay-as-bid structure more closely aligns with the economics 
and settlement structures of bilateral contracts and reduces the likelihood that RBP 
outcomes will distort or influence those private contracting activities.   
25. 
Finally, the proposed procurement is not acquiring a uniform commodity.  
Although the RBP will seek a fixed, 15-year period of time for procurement and 
cost allocation of RBP Resources, those resources may differ based on contract 
duration (dependent on the projected in-service date of selected resources), 
commercial operation dates, and other factors such as differences in ELCC 
accreditation and varying UCAP across the full term of the commitment.  These 
differences create distinct products rather than a single homogeneous offering.  
Establishing a uniform clearing price across such varied products would require 
PJM to normalize offers through potentially complex and subjective assumptions.

--- PAGE 8 ---
7 
A pay-as-bid structure avoids those challenges while providing a straightforward 
and transparent procurement framework.

MAXIMUM WILLINGNESS TO PAY OF $555/MW-DAY UCAP 
26. 
PJM believes it is necessary to identify, for the RBP, a maximum willingness to 
pay because it is not reasonable to assume that load should be obligated to pay an 
unlimited amount for supply.  As with other PJM procurement mechanisms, an 
upper bound helps balance reliability needs with customer cost protections. 
27. 
In this procurement, PJM is not receiving direct buy bids or an explicit expression 
of loads’ maximum willingness to pay, and thus PJM is proposing an administrative 
maximum willingness to pay that PJM believes will appropriately balance 
reliability needs and customer cost protections. 
28. 
The RPM relies on the Variable Resource Requirement (“VRR”) curve, which is 
derived from the Cost of New Entry, to establish the value PJM is willing to pay 
for resource commitments in a given Delivery Year.  Specifically, for the 
2028/2029 Delivery Year BRA, absent the temporary price collar, Point 1 on the 
VRR curve is $555MW-day UCAP.  Because the RBP is intended to address a 
specific resource shortfall identified in the 2028/2029 BRA, PJM is proposing to 
use Point 1 of the applicable BRA VRR curve as the basis for establishing the 
procurement maximum willingness to pay for the RBP.  
29. 
PJM is proposing an RBP maximum willingness to pay to be a MW-weighted 
average of the levelized procurement cost of all selected RBP Sell Offers to be set 
at $555/MW-day across all Delivery Years.  This results in the cost to load being 
allocated for RBP Charges to not exceed $555/MW-day, on average, over the 15-
year term.  Using this average/budget approach to the maximum willingness to pay 
(in comparison to providing a price cap in each Delivery Year or a direct price cap 
on offers) allows more flexibility in supply sell offers, to the extent that a 
$555/MW-day maximum willingness to pay may not capture every resource’s costs 
and eliminates a potential barrier to supply participation.  
VI. 
SELECTION PROCESS 
30. 
PJM is proposing to calculate a levelized procurement cost for each RBP Sell Offer 
to determine the selection.  The levelized cost of capacity is an industry standard 
evaluation metric used in competitive resource procurements across the country.  
The levelized cost calculation for evaluation addresses the fundamental issue of a 
simple MW-weighted average cost by accounting for the time value of money, 
which is critical when evaluating contracts spanning 15-year terms, as PJM is 
proposing in the RBP.  Levelizing properly discounts future cost streams to a 
common basis, giving us a true economic comparison across bids.  In Exhibit 1, 
appended to my affidavit, I provide examples, with step-by-step explanations, 
which illustrate how PJM’s proposed selection process will work.

--- PAGE 9 ---
8 
31. 
The calculation PJM will use is:  Levelized Cost ($/MW-day) = Net Present Value 
(RBP Sell Offer price * UCAP offered)/Net Present Value (UCAP offered), with a 
discount rate equal to 9.5% after-tax average weighted average cost of capital 
(“ATWACC”).  The proposed discount rate is The Brattle Group-estimate 
calculated value used in the 2025 Periodic Review of VRR Curve Shape and Key 
Parameters.3  The purpose of discounting is not to forecast inflation or market 
prices, but to reflect the time value of money and the opportunity cost of invested 
capital.  The levelized cost is intended to represent the cost per UCAP unit needed 
to recover investment and operating costs over the offer term, and the ATWACC 
represents the economically appropriate discount rate.  ATWACC represents the 
minimum expected return required by debt and equity holders and is therefore the 
standard rate used to annualize long-lived infrastructure costs.  
VII. 
MARKET MITIGATION 
32. 
PJM is not proposing market power screening or mitigation measures as part of the 
RBP.  The RBP is designed as a pay-as-bid procurement in which each selected 
resource is compensated based on its individual offer rather than a market-clearing 
price established by the marginal resource.  As a result, the traditional incentive and 
ability to exercise market power are substantially reduced.  
33. 
While the pay-as-bid design on its own does not eliminate market power concerns, 
the overall proposed RBP design further mitigates market power concerns.  This is 
driven by the one-time nature of the procurement, the voluntary participation 
model, the limited scope and bilateral process, and the maximum willingness to 
pay.  In a pay-as-bid approach, unlike a uniform clearing price construct where 
sellers are expected to offer a marginal cost, sellers are more likely to offer the 
expected clearing price.  Given offers will likely have less variation and information 
underlying offers it can be difficult and ineffective to determine offers exhorting 
market power. 
34. 
The procurement is voluntary, meaning suppliers can simply choose to participate 
or not, and then further PJM is proposing a maximum willingness-to-pay cap, 
which provides a direct limit on procurement costs even in the absence of offer 
mitigation.  The illustrative example below demonstrates the inability for one 
resource to impact the clearing price of any other resource in a pay-as-bid structure.

37.

price stability to both the buyer (load) and seller (supply).  It is structured around a 
“strike price” where the settlement or CfD is based on a difference between the 
market price and a committed strike price.  If the strike price is higher than the 
market price, the buyer must pay the difference between the strike price and market

PJM is proposing to settle the RBP with a CfD-like structure, where the strike price

39. 
PJM is proposing the CfD will only be assessed for MW that are committed in the 
RPM and delivered and will be capped at RBP committed MW. This is to say there 
will not be a CfD on RBP Resource committed MW that are not delivered in the 
applicable Delivery Year.  This is a deviation from the purely financial CfD where 
the settlement would be on the committed amount, not what was physically 
delivered.  PJM’s design choice of not designing a fully financial CfD was driven 
by the underscoring goal of the RBP supporting reliability and resource adequacy, 
and intended to preserve market participation while creating a more stable capacity 
revenue stream for the RBP Resource. 
IX. 
RBP COST ALLOCATION METHODOLOGY  
40. 
As a wholesale market operator, PJM’s role is to design and administer a 
procurement mechanism that addresses regional reliability needs in a manner 
consistent with our regulatory framework.  Cost allocation decisions should 
therefore be grounded in principles that appropriately assign costs to beneficiaries 
(demand) while respecting the jurisdictional boundaries between wholesale market 
operations and retail rate design.  This jurisdictional reality was also recognized in 
the Statement of Principles Regarding PJM signed by the National Energy 
Dominance Council and the 13 governors of the PJM states, issued on January 16, 
2026, where PJM’s 13 governors agreed to:  
[u]se their Authorities to Allocate Costs to Data Centers and Protect 
Residential Customers:  Use all available authorities to ensure that 
their state public utility commissions design rate class structures to 
ensure that their states’ LSEs allocate their share of the cost to 
procure new capacity through the Reliability Backstop Auctions to 
new data center loads that have not otherwise procured capacity or 
agreed to be curtailable.5 
41. 
The cost allocation structure is designed around first allocating the Final RBP 
Target to Electric Distributor zones/areas with forecasted load adjustment, and then 
further allocating the charges to LSEs based on the RBO, which permits states to 
determine the appropriate allocation of RBP UCAP MW to specific LSEs and/or 
end-use customers, thus allowing Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authorities 
(“RERRA”) to appropriately assign costs to their respective retail customers, 
including data centers.  Should the relevant RERRA(s) decline to communicate

Examples Demonstrating RBP Resource Procurement Process 
The following examples are intended to demonstrate how RBP Resources will be procured 
based on a maximum willingness to pay, defined as a “MW-weighted average of the levelized 
procurement cost” for the RBP, and selected through a “budget approach.”1  
Each RBP Resource Sell Offer will include an offer price and MW quantity pair for each 
Delivery Year beginning with the first year the resource is expected to reach commercial operation.  
PJM will then determine the “levelized procurement cost” for this resource to use in the selection 
process.2  This will be done with the Levelized Cost equation, with a discount rate equal to 9.5% 
after-tax average weighted average cost of capital (“ATWACC”).  
Levelized Cost ($/MW-day) = Net Present Value (RBP Sell Offer price x UCAP 
offered)/Net Present Value (UCAP offered)

In the example below, Unit A offers at a price and a quantity for each Delivery Year of the 
15-year fixed term, beginning in Year 1 and ending in Year 15.  This Offer has a levelized 
procurement cost of $101.50/MW-day UCAP and a levelized MW value of 69.3 MW.
