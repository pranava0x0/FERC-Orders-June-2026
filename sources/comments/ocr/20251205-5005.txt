They recognize this issue is especially complex in light of the myriad efforts being made by state

AG in their initial comments dated November 21, 2025, the State of Oklahoma is addressing
several complicated regulatory changes: growing demand from large loads; requirements for
integrated resource planning; requirements for All-Source Request for Proposals; statutory
provisions for preapproval of generation projects; newly enacted behind-the-meter legislation;
recent regulatory action to create a large load tariff by July 1, 2026; newly enacted legislation for
transmission projects to attain a certificate of authority; and the Southwest Power Pool's ("SPP")
response to growing demand from large loads. The above list only addresses Oklahoma, and the
Oklahoma AG and OCC echo the State Entities' comment that any Commission rulemaking here
should not impede that progress or prevent other regions from making similar progress.

Last, the Oklahoma AG supports the State Entities' comment addressing ratepayer
protections. At this time, the OCC has not had a meaningful opportunity to make findings of facts,
conclusions of law, tariff or rule changes that fit Oklahoma's unprecedented load growth. However,
maintaining the jurisdictional boundaries will allow Oklahoma to explore the protections described
in Section 2 "Ratepayer Protections," as well as the recent regulatory actions by Oklahoma's
neighbors that share jurisdictional utilities with Oklahoma. Along with the State Entities, the
Oklahoma AG and OCC believe that the items described in Section 2 should reduce the cost and
reliability impact of large loads connecting directly to the transmission system and can serve as
key protections for ratepayers in the wake of current and anticipated future load growth. Likewise,
the Commission should consider expanding the scope of this rulemaking to consider additional
load flexibility mechanisms that can similarly alleviate the peak demand burden on ratepayers.
CONCLUSION
The Oklahoma AG and OCC would like to thank the Commission for the detailed
explanation about the need for "potential reforms to ensure the timely and orderly interconnection
of large loads to the transmission systemạẠạ The Oklahoma AG and OCC appreciate the
Commission's attention to this matter and its solicitation of public input on the development of
rules and processes to facilitate interconnections of large loads to the transmission system.
