II. 
MOTION TO INTERVENE 
 
On August 13, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) filed, pursuant to section 205 
of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”), 16 U.S.C. § 824d, proposed revisions to the PJM Open Access 
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving 
Entities (“RAA”) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”) and a Large Load 
Registry. PJM proposes that IRAS be available beginning with the 2027/2028 Delivery Year 
during periods in which system conditions indicate insufficient resources to reliably meet demand 
in real-time operations. PJM also requests that the Commission accept the proposed Tariff and 
RAA revisions effective October 12, 2026. 
OELC is a not-for-profit trade association that represents its members’ interests in utility 
and ratemaking matters in Ohio. OELC advocates for fair, transparent, and reasonable utility tariffs 
and rates so that Ohio remains an attractive place for businesses and enterprises to remain, grow, 
and invest. OELC’s members include energy-intensive manufacturing, industrial, educational, 
institutional, information-technology, and retail business customers in Ohio. Collectively, OELC’s 
members annually consume many billions of kilowatt-hours of electricity and are major 
contributors to Ohio’s economy and employment sector. 
OELC has a direct, immediate, and substantial interest in this proceeding. OELC’s 
members include large commercial and industrial energy customers in Ohio that are served in the 
PJM Region. PJM’s proposed IRAS framework would establish a new mechanism for real-time 
load-reduction actions in Electric Distributor zones or areas during specified emergency 
conditions. Under the proposal, PJM may direct an Electric Distributor zone or area to reduce load 
up to the capacity quantity attributable to Eligible New Large Loads in that zone or area. The 
proposed Large Load Registry, related definitions of Large Load and New Large Load, 
requirements concerning new capacity for New Large Loads, and proposed revisions affecting the

Variable Resource Requirement Curve may also affect the reliability, rates, terms, and conditions 
of electric service received by OELC’s members. 
OELC’s interests include ensuring that the Commission’s disposition of PJM’s proposed 
IRAS and Large Load Registry protects existing commercial and industrial customers through just, 
reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory rates, terms, and conditions; that the proposed 
framework does not result in inappropriate cost shifts; and that it appropriately accounts for the 
reliability and affordability effects of the proposed revisions on Ohio customers. These interests 
directly implicate OELC’s mission to promote customer-driven policies supporting adequate, 
reliable, and efficient delivery and supply of energy at competitive, reasonable, and transparent 
rates. 
OELC’s participation is in the public interest and will assist the Commission in developing 
a full and complete record. OELC and its members have substantial experience with electric-utility 
rates, tariffs, resource adequacy, reliability, infrastructure planning, and the economic effects of 
electricity costs on Ohio businesses and institutions. OELC can provide the Commission the 
perspective of large commercial and industrial customers that depend on reliable and competitively 
priced electricity service and may be affected by the proposed IRAS framework and related Tariff 
and RAA revisions. 
No other party can adequately represent OELC’s interests. OELC’s members have distinct 
interests as large energy consumers in Ohio and as customers potentially affected by the proposed 
IRAS framework, including its load-reduction mechanisms, resource-adequacy requirements, 
Large Load Registry, and related Tariff and RAA revisions. This motion is timely, will not unduly 
delay or prejudice any party, and satisfies the requirements for intervention under 18 C.F.R. § 
385.214.
