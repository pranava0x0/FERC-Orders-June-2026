I. 
ANSWER 
 
The Joint Parties’ Motion asks that the Commission find the PJM Interconnection, L.L.C 
(“PJM”) Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) to be unjust and unreasonable, stay the 
consolidated proceeding, and establish settlement judge procedures to develop a replacement rate.3 
The Commission should deny the Motion and find that the PJM Tariff is just and reasonable and 
does not require revisions. 
 
The Motion presupposes not only that the PJM Tariff is not just and reasonable, but that 
revisions to the PJM Tariff that provide for unique treatment to co-located load would be just and 
reasonable and not unduly discriminatory. The first assumption is incorrect. Co-location can be 
effectively accommodated within the existing PJM Tariff through established transmission service 
structures as Network Load or Point-to-Point Service.4 Every co-location arrangement that has 
been proposed, aside from true physical islanding, involves reliance on the transmission system 
for ancillary services and backup supply. This is why, as the Indicated PJM Transmission Owners 
explained, the PJM Tariff provides that co-located customers need to acquire firm transmission 
service just like every other customer.5 The fact that the rules do not produce the Joint Parties’ 
preferred outcome does not mean that the rules are unclear or unable to accommodate co-location 
arrangements. Viable and fair options exist under the current Tariff. There is no ambiguity, only 
the desire for a different result. 
 
The second assumption is also incorrect. The Motion implies that co-location arrangements 
should not be subject to a “one-size-fits-all” solution.6 To the contrary, a uniform approach is

appropriate because, like other load, co-location arrangements rely on the transmission system. 
Prospective co-location arrangements still seek to maintain a connection to the transmission 
system, despite claims that these arrangements will not use or rely on the transmission system. 
NOVEC and NREMC believe that all load should be served but cannot agree that certain new and 
privileged large loads should receive preferential treatment at the expense of all other rate-paying 
transmission customers and the load they serve.  
 
Because the Motion’s threshold presuppositions are incorrect, the relief the Motion 
requests should not be granted. Staying the proceeding is unnecessary as the Commission should 
dismiss the consolidated proceeding on the record before it as the existing PJM Tariff is just and 
reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential. A settlement process would not be 
productive because there is no ambiguity to resolve by settlement. If load chooses not to 
interconnect to the transmission system, then it does not have to pay transmission costs. If, through 
any configuration, load interconnects to the transmission system, it must contribute to the costs of 
the transmission system on which it relies – which can be adequately resolved through existing 
structures in the PJM Tariff. NOVEC and NREMC also agree with the reasoning underlying the 
Independent Market Monitor for PJM’s (“IMM”) opposition to resolving this proceeding through 
settlement.7 As the IMM contends, these issues should be “resolved objectively, transparently and 
in the public interest.”8  
II. 
CONCLUSION 
WHEREFORE, for the foregoing reasons, NOVEC and NREMC respectfully request that 
the Commission deny the Motion.
