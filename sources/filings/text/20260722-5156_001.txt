INTRODUCTION & INTEREST OF THE COMMENTER 
Nichole Gerber/Owner of MoonDoggie LIVERee LLC./ Conserve Ohio Committee Member / 
Adams County, OH, submits these public comments in response to the Federal Energy 
Regulatory Commission  (FERC) Notice of Technical Conference regarding PJM 
Interconnection, L.L.C. ("PJM") Governance and Stakeholder Reforms under Docket No. 
AD26-7-000. 
As a constituent and small business owner in Adams County, OH and as a Conserve Ohio 
committee member on a statewide Constitutional Amendment initiative, banning data centers 
larger than 25MW,  within the PJM footprint in the Great State of Ohio, we witness firsthand 
how PJM current governance structure ISOLATES LOCAL COMMUNITIES and state leadership 
while PRIORITIZING PRIVATE,MULTIBILLION-dollar corporate INTERESTS.  
PJM decision-making frameworks directly impact our local electric reliability, regional water 
security, and monthly utility bills, especially in the heavily targeted Ohio rural Appalachian 
region for proposed data centers development.  
II. COMMENTS ON PANEL  
2: STAKEHOLDER PROCESS, TRANSPARENCY, AND DECISION-
MAKING 
1.
Codify an Explicit Public-Interest Mandate
--- PAGE 2 ---
PJM  current five-sector voting model was designed for a slower-moving energy transition, not 
for hyper-accelerated, speculative load growth from hyperscale data centers and artificial 
intelligence infrastructure. PJM board and stakeholder processes currently operate with a bias 
toward legacy utilities and private corporate developers, leaving residential and agricultural 
rate-paying communities out of the equation for hike we are footing the bill for infrastructure 
For proposed deals, rate increases, and future grid strains SOLELY IMPACTING US! 
  
•
Requested Reform: FERC must order PJM to amend its governance documents to 
explicitly include a Public Interest Mandate. PJM must be legally bound to prioritize 
ratepayer affordability, grid reliability, and state environmental/resource policies over 
corporate capital expenditure goals. 
2. Eliminate Corporate Gridlock and "Squeaky Wheel" Lobbying 
The current PJM stakeholder process routinely degenerates into procedural gridlock, where 
well-funded private actors stall necessary reforms to defend hyper-specific business models. 
Conversely, when regional state coalitions or consumer advocates push for ratepayer 
protections such as forcing large-load developers to pay for 100% of their own transmission 
and substation upgrades corporate interests use procedural maneuvers to veto consensus.  In 
Ohio protections are being proposed such as the ratepayer protection act and HB 970 but no 
action has been taken as they sit on the table.   
•
Requested Reform: Establish time-bound, transparent decision-making pathways for 
large-load interconnects that prioritize Ratepayer Insulation. Speculative data center 
projects must not be granted queue access or capacity allocations without proving they 
bring their own generation capacity and bear 100% of their infrastructure footprint.  Make 
sure permits include that behind the meter power generation is provided before they 
power on and help to keep current clean air safeguards in place for the American People 
as you can.  
3. Formalize State and Local Government Access 
State legislatures, county commissions, and consumer advocates are currently forced to react 
to PJM decisions from the "outside looking in." When local counties face sudden grid 
instability, industrial noise pollution, or depleted aquifers caused by uncoordinated large-load 
deployments, PJM offers zero direct mechanisms for local public accountability. 
•
Requested Reform: Grant the Organization of PJM States, Inc. (OPSI) and regional 
local official coalitions formal, elevated status within PJM board governance. If state utility 
regulators and local county governments broadly oppose a PJM market rule or 
transmission cost-allocation proposal, FERC should establish a "jump ball" mechanism 
allowing state leadership to submit alternative proposals directly to FERC alongside PJM 
filings. 
4. Mandatory Public Board Meetings and Decisional Transparency 
PJM Board of Managers currently makes key regional determinations behind closed doors 
without providing detailed roll-call votes, comprehensive meeting minutes, or clear evidentiary 
orders justifying their actions. 
•
Requested Reform: Mandate that all PJM Board meetings involving wholesale market 
rules, transmission expansion planning, or large-load queue management be open to

public observation. The Board should be required to publish formal Decisional Orders 
explaining the factual and public-interest basis for every major policy decision. 
III. CONCLUSION 
PJM is currently failing the 67 million residents who rely on its grid for safe, affordable power. 
The status quo” corporate entities dictate regional grid terms while shifting billions in 
transmission costs onto rural and working-class families” is completely untenable. 
We urge the Commission to use Docket No. AD26-7-000 to order structural, binding 
governance reforms that enforce public transparency, protect residential ratepayers, and hold 
PJM accountable to the communities it serves.
