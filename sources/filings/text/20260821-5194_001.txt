POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF DUQUESNE LIGHT COMPANY 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Duquesne Light Company (“Duquesne Light”) respectfully submits the following 
comments in response to the Notice Requesting Post-Conference Comment issued by the 
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or “Commission”) on July 30, 2026, 
following the July 23, 2026 Technical Conference on PJM Governance and Stakeholder 
Reforms.1 The Technical Conference discussed important issues around how PJM 
governance works and the ability of the PJM stakeholders to work within that process to 
development consensus solutions for the betterment of the PJM region. Duquesne Light 
commends the Commission for holding the technical conference.  
II. 
Duquesne Light Company’s Interest in the Proceeding 
 
Duquesne Light is a FERC-jurisdictional Transmission and Distribution only utility 
that serves over 600,000 customers and operates in parts of Allegheny and Beaver 
Counties, located in Western Pennsylvania, including the city of Pittsburgh. Duquesne

A. 
PJM Governance Reform is Needed 
 
The PJM stakeholder process was developed over 20 years ago during a 
time of excess supply, flat load growth and with far fewer stakeholders. In 2006, 
the PJM stakeholder process had fewer than 400 members.2 To meet the needs of 
the members and the environment at the time, a stakeholder process was built for 
slow, deliberate changes. The stakeholder process was not built to make quick 
changes or to be able to respond to an environment that is rapidly changing. It was 
built to allow significant time for all parties to consider issues, engage in lengthy 
discussion, conduct significant analysis and to ultimately reach consensus. One 
example is that it typically takes four months to hold the votes necessary to pass a 
typical proposal. Any proposal must go through a first read, before an

standing committees. For example, if a proposal comes through the Planning

month two, there would be an endorsement vote at the Planning Committee. If 
passed at the Planning Committee, in month three, there would be a first read at

endorsement vote at the Markets and Reliability Committee. A Members

Reliability Committee. Therefore, if the Members Committee vote were to happen 
following the Markets and Reliability Committee, the process could take six 
months, with the Members Committee first read happening in month five and the 
endorsement vote in month six. This process, while extremely effective at 
ensuring there is sufficient review and opportunity for feedback, is not built to be 
nimble. By design the stakeholder process is slow and methodical, built for 
deliberate consideration and consensus building. That design is at conflict with the 
challenges PJM is facing today. 
Today, PJM membership includes over 1,100 members.3 Membership has 
more than doubled in the last 20 years. In addition, the landscape is drastically 
different from when the stakeholder process was designed. The explosive growth

The PJM stakeholder process raises concerns regarding accountability and 
control. Vince Duane and Tony Clark aptly described the problem well in their 
2021 whitepaper, “Who Owns the RTO?” 
“RTOs are organized such that their customers, in the broadest 
sense imaginable, are also their owners, with ownership rights 
varying in practical degree and in all cases ambiguously stated 
legally. These customers exercise shared control over RTO 
decisions and RTO management – notwithstanding the supposed 
independence of RTOs. The peculiar RTO corporate structure is 
akin to the organization of a cooperative corporation, to the extent 
that each member typically exercises the same voting control, 
without regard to the member’s respective financial commitment 
and interest in the RTO. The structures have always been 
bureaucratic, but they also dissipate any locus of control, blur 
ultimate accountability and not surprisingly, often fail to reach 
consensus, leaving important questions of market design and 
operational protocol to be resolved by FERC through 
administrative litigation.”4 
 
This lack of accountability is a key flaw in PJM's governance. PJM’s 
stakeholder process constrains PJM’s ability to effectuate change, leaving 
outcomes largely dependent on stakeholders who “lack a shared purpose and 
vision when it comes to the RTO’s”5 and whose individual interests are far from 
aligned. Expecting this stakeholder body to compromise and reach consensus

B. 
PJM as the RTO Should be the Entity Held Accountable. The 
Stakeholder Process Should be Advisory. 
 
Stakeholders play an important role in PJM's governance by contributing 
their expertise and helping inform solutions across a range of interests. However, 
stakeholders should not have the ability to prevent PJM from fulfilling its 
responsibilities as the RTO, including submitting filings pursuant to Section 205 
of the Federal Power Act (“Section 205” or “205”).  
As the RTO PJM should be the entity accountable to FERC and the region 
for fulfilling RTO responsibilities, including making Section 205 filings related to 
its markets and regional planning processes. Currently, the stakeholder process 
inhibits PJM’s ability to make Section 205 filings at FERC to address issues or 
update its core functions, thereby limiting its ability to act and fulfill its role as an 
RTO.  
All stakeholders and states have the ability to make filings pursuant to 
Section 206 of the Federal Power Act (“Section 206” or “206”) at the Commission 
or to protest any filing made by PJM, providing an appropriate balance. As the 
RTO, PJM should be the entity submitting proposals and defending them before

the Commission. Those proposals should consider and incorporate feedback from 
PJM stakeholders through the stakeholder process. If consensus is not reached or 
stakeholders disagree with the decision of the RTO, they can take their arguments 
to FERC, either in response to a filing by PJM or by initiating a 206 filing. FERC 
is then able to make an informed decision based on the record and is the 
appropriate entity to do so. 
Given this, PJM’s stakeholder process should become advisory. PJM 
should have the Section 205 filing rights over the Tariff and Operating Agreement. 
PJM should continue to maintain its stakeholder process, but the ultimate 
conclusion of it would be advisory.  
Transitioning to an advisory stakeholder process would also enable PJM to 
move more expeditiously to address emerging reliability, market, and planning 
needs when necessary. While there are many options for how this could work, 
PJM would be able to define a process that meets a moment, allowing it to move 
quickly to address emergent issues without being hamstrung by a lengthy 
stakeholder process, or vulnerable to rejection by stakeholders based on a 
procedural issue. 
C. 
States Should Have an Increased Role in the PJM Stakeholder Process  
 
State input is critical to the success of the RTO; therefore Duquesne Light 
supports providing states a larger role at PJM, including a seat on the Nominating 
Committee and the ability to cast advisory votes.
--- PAGE 7 ---
7 
 
Allowing one collective seat for all PJM state entities on the Nominating 
Committee is appropriate. The Nominating Committee consists of “one 
representative elected annually from each sector of the Members Committee 
established under Operating Agreement, section 8.1 and three voting Board 
Members (provided that one such Board Member shall serve only as a non-voting 
member of the Nominating Committee).”6 Each sector has a single representative 
and only the PJM Board has an additional voting representative. Therefore, it is 
appropriate for the states to have a single representative collectively representing 
all state interests. The PJM Board would continue to be the only entity with more 
than one voting representative on the committee, which is appropriate because it 
bears ultimate responsibility and accountability for PJM's performance and 
governance and is therefore best positioned to identify the skills, expertise, and 
experience needed in future Board candidates. 
States having the ability to cast advisory votes in the stakeholder process is 
also appropriate. In line with the recommendation above that the stakeholder 
process should become advisory; state votes should also be advisory and should be 
considered by the PJM Board to inform their decision making. States’ input is 
critical and should be allowed to inform the PJM Board for its ultimate decisions.  
Duquesne Light encourages an approach to increase state participation in 
PJM in a coordinated fashion, including using the existing Organization of PJM
