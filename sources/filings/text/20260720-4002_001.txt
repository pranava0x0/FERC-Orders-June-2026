FERC Technical Conference on PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms 
Docket No. AD26-7-000 
Overview of Potential Changes to PJM Governance 
Delaware Division of the Public Advocate 
Jameson Tweedie, Public Advocate for Delaware 
 
Embed affordability into PJM governing documents 
• Reason for change: Affordability is central concern across the region, part of FERC’s 
mission, and a valid consideration under Supreme Court precedent. Yet PJM and 
numerous stakeholders repeatedly reject its relevance.  
• Specific change: Through adoption of pending AARC proposal (or equivalent process), 
affordability should be directly embedded into PJM governing documents, including 
Operating Agreement sections 7.7(i) and 10.4 and PJM’s Mission Statement. 
 
Update voting structure at lower-level PJM committees to match MC 
• Reason for change: At senior committees (MRC, MC), voting is sector weighted allowing 
more meaningful input from consumer interests. However, affiliate voting at the lower-
level committees limits the diversity of proposals that reach the senior committees.  
• Specific change: Amend PJM governing documents (ex. Manual 34, sec. 8.4) so that all 
levels of PJM stakeholder voting are sector-weighted and exclude affiliate voting, with 
public reporting of voting.   
 
Formalize the role of the States 
• Reason for change: PJM’s decisions impact the 67 million customers in its region, yet 
state input is not a formalized part of PJM decision-making. Establishing a formal vehicle 
for state input would create a clear and meaningful venue for the interests of States and 
the customers PJM serves. 
• Specific changes:   
o Formally establish the Governors Collaborative to meaningfully participate in 
PJM’s processes, including necessary funding.   
o Establish a sixth voting sector for the State entities (including the 14 consumer 
advocate offices, and – if they choose – designees of the Governors of each State 
and the Mayor of DC, and representatives of OPSI). 
o Establish a regional State Committee for the State entities (prior bullet), with the 
rights to:  
 propose problem statements and issue charges directly to the PJM Board. 
 recommend proposals to the PJM Board when no proposal receives 
a supermajority vote at the MC.

Improve accountability, transparency 
• Reason for change: PJM’s decisions affect 67 million Americans across the largest RTO 
in the country. Operating in private undermines confidence among the public and policy 
makers and transparency should be the norm.   
• Specific changes:   
o Make all PJM Board meetings and stakeholder meetings public and recorded, 
publish all votes, and require minutes with decision rationale (unless for any of 
these there is a specific reason for confidentiality). 
o More regular engagement between the PJM Board and the sectors. 
o Improve access of all members to modeling and underlying data. 
o Establish an Independent Transmission Monitor, similar to the Independent 
Market Monitor.
