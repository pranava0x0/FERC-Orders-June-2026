UNITED STATES OF AMERICA 
 
FEDERAL ENERGY REGULATORY COMMISSION 
 
PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms  
Docket No. AD26-7-000 
COMMENTS OF THE NATIONAL CAUCUS OF ENVIRONMENTAL LEGISLATORS 
 
July 20, 2026 
 
We appreciate FERC addressing critical governance issues at PJM and welcome the opportunity 
to comment. As state lawmakers, we believe it is crucial that FERC prioritize reforms that 
ensure states can engage meaningfully in PJM processes and have access to sufficient 
information. Doing so will allow our states to more effectively fulfill our obligations and 
exercise our authority under the Federal Power Act. 
 
The Federal Power Act recognizes states' authority over key energy policy issues, most notably 
in the realm of resource choice, siting, permitting, generation, and retail cost allocation. PJM’s 
role in transmission planning, energy markets, and resource adequacy decisions — and the lack 
of a state voice in these processes — directly impacts our ability to fully and appropriately 
execute these duties while achieving state policy objectives. 
 
Creating meaningful opportunities for state participation is a crucial step for improving PJM 
governance. We believe this can be accomplished by drawing on the lessons and precedents 
from other regions, including the New England States Committee on Electricity (NESCOE) and 
the Southwest Power Pool’s (SPP) Regional State Committee (RSC).   
 
We urge FERC to take action on the following: 
●​ Establish a formal governance role for a new or existing organization 
representing state interests. This role could be based on the “direction to file” and 
regional policy functions exercised by state entities in other regions, such as the RSC in 
SPP or ISO-NE’s NESCOE.  
●​ Provide this new or existing organization with the ability to submit alternative 
proposals to FERC for consideration when stakeholder processes — or work among 
the stakeholders, states, and PJM — produce competing results. This mechanism would 
enable FERC to evaluate more than one option and function similarly to the New 
England Power Pool's “jumpball rights.”  
●​ Provide a seat on the Nominating Committee for the PJM Board to a representative 
from the states. 
●​ Modify the Board makeup requirements to provide two additional seats, including a 
seat to represent the interests of states and a seat to represent the interests of end-use 
customers. 
●​ Amend PJM’s foundational operating agreement to codify an explicit public 
interest mandate that requires PJM to balance system reliability and market efficiency 
with public interest objectives, explicitly including ratepayer cost containment and state 
public policy alignment. 
●​ Strengthen transparency by allowing designated representatives of PJM state 
governments to attend Liaison Committee meetings.

Additionally, some practical modifications and adjustments to the PJM stakeholder process 
would generally improve the process and make decision-making more effective and 
transparent:​
 
●​ Record all PJM Stakeholder Meetings and make these recordings publicly available.  
●​ Implement sector-weighted voting in the Standing Committees to align committee 
work with the ultimate stakeholder decision-making process.  
●​ Publish and disseminate records of voting results taken in all PJM committees, task 
forces, and similar standing, ad-hoc, or issue-specific stakeholder groups. 
●​ Allow media to be present in all stakeholder process meetings. 
●​ Require cost impact analyses to be presented during the First Read at the Senior 
Standing Committees.  
●​ Establish an Independent Transmission Monitor to help control rising transmission 
costs and efficiently move forward with needed projects. 
 
The governance reforms identified above would strengthen alignment between PJM 
decision-making and state policy objectives, while also improving transparency and 
accountability in the system. Energy affordability concerns and the need for a modernized grid 
are two issues that no single entity can solve. We look forward to continuing to work with PJM 
and FERC in our efforts to reduce costs and deliver customer reliability.
