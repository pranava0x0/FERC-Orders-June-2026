2026, North Carolina Electric Membership Corporation (“NCEMC”)2 submits the 
following comments on the filing by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) 
proposing revisions to its Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and 
Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to 
establish a Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP Proposal”).3
PJM states that the RBP Proposal will be a one-time procurement of new 
capacity resources intended to address previously identified resource adequacy 
challenges that have arisen as a result of insufficient capacity commitments in the 
2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) Capacity Market 
Auctions. NCEMC shares the reliability concern that PJM is attempting to 
address; however, PJM’s proposed fix gives rise to a key cost-allocation concern

I.
NCEMC 
NCEMC is a generation and transmission cooperative responsible for the 
full or partial power supply requirements of its 25 members throughout the state of 
North Carolina. Those 25 distribution cooperatives, in turn, supply electricity to 
approximately one million homes, farms, and businesses in which more than 2.5 
million North Carolinians live and work. NCEMC distribution cooperative loads 
are located throughout the service areas of three investor-owned public utilities: 
Duke Energy Carolinas, LLC, and Duke Energy Progress, LLC, both subsidiaries 
of Duke Energy Corporation, and Virginia Electric and Power Company, doing 
business as Dominion Energy Virginia in Virginia and as Dominion Energy North 
Carolina in North Carolina (hereinafter, Dominion).  
NCEMC is a member of PJM, which operates transmission facilities owned 
by Dominion and other electric utilities. NCEMC purchases transmission services 
from PJM over Dominion’s transmission facilities to serve the loads of its six 
member cooperatives that distribute power at retail in the Dominion transmission 
zone of the PJM region. NCEMC is a member of PJM’s Electric Distributor 
sector, a load-serving entity, and a participant in PJM’s energy, ancillary services, 
capacity, and financial transmission rights markets.

II.
The RBP Proposal Allocates Costs to Non-Cost-Causers, including 
NCEMC and its Member Cooperatives. 
NCEMC shares EKPC’s concern that “[t]he PJM RBP Proposal does not 
adequately define who is responsible for the charges (which affects how much of 
the zone/area assigned costs any individual LSE [Load-Serving Entity] may 
receive) or how the charges are tied to incremental cost causation.” EKPC Protest 
at 19. EKPC points out that “PJM has failed to provide ‘clear and transparent 
procedures’ to allow an LSE to determine the amount of costs it may be allocated 
under the PJM RBP Proposal.” Id. at 17 (citation omitted). EKPC explains the 
uncertain cost allocation that LSEs face:  
Most concerning, PJM’s proposal for a default 
allocation methodology, should the Electric Distributor 
not report an RBO [“RBP Obligation”], is that all 
LSEs in the zone/area will be held accountable to pay 
the costs PJM assigns to the zone/area, regardless of 
whether they serve any large load or contributed to the 
need for backstop procurement. The uncertainty of 
being assigned costs, as well as the magnitude of the 
costs that may be assigned, should the large loads not 
materialize in each zone/area as PJM anticipated in 
establishing the procurement target, will itself impact 
whether large loads materialize as forecasted. It is 
nearly impossible for an LSE, based on the 
information available to it in PJM’s RBP Proposal, to 
determine the amount of costs it may be allocated.  
Id. at 17–18 (citation omitted).

PJM could and should clearly identify entities which will not bear costs under the 
RBP Proposal.4 With regard to NCEMC, PJM has not done so. EKPC provided the 
following helpful example:  
It is also unclear whether PJM is properly using the 
information from Table B9b from the 2026 PJM Load 
Forecast to ensure Electric Distributor zone/areas 
receive correct allocations from PJM. For example, 
[NCEMC] is located in the Dominion Energy zone. 
But, the NCEMC area is not currently reflected as “0” 
in PJM’s Initial Allocation to Electric Distributor 
zone/area table. Therefore, as initially presented by 
PJM, NCEMC could be subject to some kind of cost 
allocation if the cross-state cost allocation is not 
appropriately determined.

PJM should be required to clearly identify entities which will not bear costs 
under the RBP Proposal’s primary cost-allocation methodology as well as under 
its alternative “default methodology.” EKPC’s Protest explains the deficiency of 
PJM’s proposed default methodology:  
PJM’s proposal to use a default allocation based on 
Daily Unforced Capacity Obligation if the more 
targeted allocation process does not produce an LSE-
specific result is also unjust and unreasonable. When a 
state or RERRA [Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory 
Authority] has not determined an RBO for LSEs 
within a zone/area, PJM defaults to allocating costs 
based on each LSE’s Daily Unforced Capacity 
Obligation ratio share. This backup methodology 
socializes RBP costs across all load within the zone—

Conclusion
