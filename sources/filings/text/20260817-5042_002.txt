COMMENTS OF PERSISTENCE ANALYTICS GROUP LLC REGARDING PRE-
PROCUREMENT ASSUMPTION VERIFICATION AND REVALIDATION
Persistence Analytics Group LLC ("PAG") respectfully submits these comments in response to 
PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.'s July 31, 2026 filing proposing a Reliability Backstop Procurement 
("RBP") in Docket No. ER26-3380-000.
PAG's central point is narrow: a reliability procurement should not convert a modeled shortfall 
into long-duration financial obligations until the material assumptions producing that shortfall 
have been revalidated against the best currently available evidence.
I. THE COMMISSION SHOULD TREAT ASSUMPTION REVALIDATION AS A THRESHOLD 
DECISION-ASSURANCE ISSUE
PJM states that the proposed RBP is intended to address a 6,831 MW shortfall identified for the 
2028/2029 Delivery Year. PJM also states that the procurement could secure new resources for 
terms of up to 15 years, subject to a maximum willingness to pay equal to a MW-weighted 
average of $555/MW-day, and that the initial procurement target would be reduced by qualifying 
new resources reflected in executed bilateral contracts or otherwise bringing new capacity to the 
system. See PJM, "PJM Reliability Backstop Proposal Outlines Steps To Secure New Supply 
and Maintain Reliability" (July 31, 2026).
Those features make the quality of the underlying evidence especially consequential. A 
procurement quantity is not simply a mathematical output. It is the result of assumptions 
concerning accredited supply, seasonal capability, outage risk, load growth, project timing, 
bilateral commitments, interconnection status, and other inputs. When those inputs materially 
change, the decision supported by the model may also need to change.
The relevant decision chain is: forecast demand + accredited supply -> modeled shortfall -> 
evidence status -> sensitivity testing -> revalidation -> procurement quantity -> contract 
obligation -> cost allocation -> actual load -> realized ratepayer outcome.
The Commission need not determine in this proceeding that any competing model is correct. It 
should determine whether PJM's proposed procurement quantity is sufficiently evidenced, 
transparent as to material assumptions, and capable of being resized when those assumptions 
materially change before commitments become fixed.
II. CURRENT SUPPLY-SIDE EVIDENCE SHOULD BE REVALIDATED BEFORE THE PROCUREMENT 
QUANTITY IS FIXED
A recent external analysis published by SemiAnalysis challenges aspects of PJM's resource-
adequacy modeling, including the treatment of winter thermal capability and post-Storm Elliott

winterization. The authors estimate that those modeling choices may materially affect the 
amount of reliable capacity credited to the existing fleet and, in turn, the size of the perceived 
shortfall. See Robert Boswall et al., SemiAnalysis (Aug. 16, 2026).
PAG does not ask the Commission to adopt SemiAnalysis's reconstructed model, its dollar 
estimate, or its conclusions as established fact. The authors themselves acknowledge limitations 
arising from unavailable information and describe their reconstruction as an approximation. The 
significance of the analysis for this docket is therefore procedural and evidentiary: it identifies 
specific, potentially material assumptions that can be tested before the proposed procurement 
creates long-duration obligations.
At minimum, the Commission should require a current evidentiary record addressing whether 
material changes in seasonal resource capability, winterization, operating limits, demonstrated 
performance during recent cold-weather events, and other relevant supply-side conditions are 
reflected in the procurement quantity. Where an assumption is based substantially on historical 
performance, the record should explain whether subsequent physical changes or demonstrated 
operating performance warrant revalidation rather than mechanical continuation of the historical 
assumption.
Historical evidence is not necessarily current evidence. A planning assumption can remain 
statistically embedded after the physical system has changed. If a material system change has 
occurred, the appropriate question is whether the old assumption remains decision-grade for the 
procurement now before the Commission.
III. DEMAND-SIDE MATURITY AND DURABILITY SHOULD BE TESTED WITH THE SAME 
DISCIPLINE
The RBP is also expressly tied to resource adequacy challenges associated with large-load 
growth. That makes demand-side evidence equally important. Forecast load should not be treated 
as a single undifferentiated category when the economic consequences of the procurement 
depend on whether that load actually becomes executable, energized, and persistent.
For large-load planning, the Commission should distinguish among at least the following 
evidentiary states: announced -> requested -> contracted or financially committed -> executable 
-> energized -> persistent.
A reported MW value may be appropriate for one planning purpose yet insufficient for another. 
A long-duration procurement and associated cost-allocation structure warrant a clear explanation 
of the evidence supporting the load assumptions being relied upon, the project milestones that 
increase or decrease confidence, and the triggers that require revalidation if projects are delayed, 
resized, self-supply, secure bilateral arrangements, move to another region, or do not materialize.
This is not an argument to exclude legitimate forecast demand. It is an argument to preserve the 
distinction between a forecast and the evidentiary maturity necessary to support a consequential 
financial commitment.

IV. THE COMMISSION SHOULD REQUIRE DEFINED REVALIDATION TRIGGERS BEFORE AND 
DURING THE PROCUREMENT WINDOW
PJM has already recognized that the initial procurement target should be reduced for qualifying 
bilateral contracts and other new capacity. That is a form of revalidation. PAG respectfully 
submits that the same principle should be applied systematically to all material decision inputs.
Before the procurement quantity becomes fixed, and again before final awards become 
irrevocable, PJM should identify material revalidation triggers. Those triggers should include, as 
applicable: significant changes in accredited or seasonal supply capability; verified winterization 
or resilience improvements; executed bilateral supply arrangements; material changes in large-
load commitments; interconnection delays or withdrawals; project cancellations or resizing; 
changes in expected in-service dates; and other developments that materially alter the shortfall or 
the parties expected to bear the resulting costs.
For each trigger, the record should identify the responsible decision owner, the evidence 
required, the effect on the procurement quantity or cost allocation, and the disposition of 
unresolved exceptions. The objective is not perpetual reopening of the model. It is disciplined 
change control for assumptions that are material enough to affect a multi-year obligation.
V. THE COMMISSION SHOULD REQUIRE A DECISION-GRADE EVIDENTIARY RECORD, NOT 
MERELY A MODEL OUTPUT
A system of record is not the same as a system of verification. Likewise, a model output is not 
self-validating simply because it was produced through an established process. For consequential 
reliance, the evidentiary record should make clear what assumptions are being relied upon, what 
evidence supports them, what material uncertainties remain, and what changes would require the 
decision to be revisited.
A useful classification for material assumptions is: SUPPORTED / CONDITIONAL / 
UNSUPPORTED / REVALIDATION REQUIRED.
Such a framework need not displace PJM's modeling, engineering judgment, tariff authority, 
stakeholder process, or the Commission's statutory responsibilities. It simply makes the 
evidentiary status of decision-critical assumptions visible before they are converted into 
contractual obligations and ratepayer exposure.
VI. REQUESTED COMMISSION ACTION
PAG respectfully requests that, in acting on PJM's filing, the Commission require or direct PJM 
to provide a pre-procurement revalidation record that:
• identifies the material supply-side and demand-side assumptions producing the procurement 
quantity;
• states the current evidence supporting each material assumption and any material uncertainty;
• explains how recent changes in seasonal capability, winterization, operating performance, 
bilateral commitments, large-load maturity, and interconnection status have been evaluated;
--- PAGE 4 ---
• defines objective triggers for revalidation and resizing before the procurement quantity and 
awards become fixed;
• documents how material changes affect procurement quantity, cost allocation, or continued 
reliance; and
• preserves an auditable decision record showing why the final procurement quantity remained 
supportable at the time commitments were made.
The Commission does not need to resolve every technical disagreement about PJM's resource-
adequacy model to impose this discipline. It need only ensure that the assumptions driving a 
long-duration backstop procurement are current, evidenced, and subject to defined revalidation 
before the resulting obligations are allowed to harden.
VII. CONCLUSION
PJM is confronting a genuine reliability challenge in an environment of rapid load growth and 
constrained supply. That makes speed important. It also makes evidence quality more important, 
not less. The greater the duration and financial consequence of the procurement, the stronger the 
case for verifying that the assumptions producing the procurement quantity remain supportable 
when the decision is made.
Trust the model. Verify the assumptions. Revalidate before commitment.
For the foregoing reasons, PAG respectfully requests that the Commission require a transparent 
pre-procurement assumption-verification and revalidation record as part of its disposition of 
PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal.
