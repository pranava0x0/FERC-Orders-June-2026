UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
BEFORE THE FEDERAL ENERGY REGULATORY COMMISSION

PJM Governance and Stakeholder Reforms
Docket No. AD26-7-000

POST-CONFERENCE COMMENTS OF THE SENECA CENTER FOR ENERGY AND CRITICAL MINERALS POLICY
August 21, 2026

I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY OF POSITION
Pursuant to the Commission's July 30, 2026 Notice, The Seneca Center for Energy and Critical Minerals Policy ("Seneca Center") submits these comments on PJM governance. Writing from the generation side of the PJM footprint (the Appalachian and Ohio Valley corridor), we emphasize that governance reform is only valuable if it changes physical outcomes--specifically, ensuring firm, dispatchable, fuel-secure capacity is built, retained, and dispatched. Procedural elegance that leaves the region short by 6,623 MW for the 2027/2028 delivery year, and 6,831 MW for 2028/2029, is a failure.

Our core propositions:

The sole test for any reform is whether it improves resource adequacy.

Board independence must be permanently tethered to accountability.

State regulators require a formal, structurally strengthened role.

Transferring Section 205 filing rights warrants conditional support at most.

PJM must measure whether existing firm capacity is being dispatched before procuring more.

Structural remedies, including breaking up PJM, must remain a backstop.

II. STATEMENT OF INTEREST AND COMMUNICATIONS
The Seneca Center is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit researching electricity market design, resource adequacy, and dispatch economics. We are not a market participant. Our interest is the reliability and affordability of electric service and the integrity of PJM's governance.

III. THE STANDARD THE COMMISSION SHOULD APPLY
Chairman Swett and Deputy Secretary Danly accurately diagnosed PJM's grave legitimacy crisis, a physical problem with a procedural cause. We propose one evaluative standard for every reform: Does it make it more likely that firm, dispatchable capacity gets built, retained, and run? Speed to a bad decision solves nothing.

IV. BOARD AUTHORITY, INDEPENDENCE, AND ACCOUNTABILITY
We support strengthening board independence through six-to-nine year staggered terms. However, independence without accountability creates a referee chosen by the players but answerable to no one. Expanded board authority must include:

A codified public interest mandate in governing documents running directly to retail consumers.

Published annual reliability metrics (e.g., reserve margins, cold-weather outage rates, undispatched capacity).

An independent nominating process to prevent regulatory capture.

V. SECTION 205 FILING RIGHTS AND DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE
PJM proposes moving Operating Agreement Schedules 1 and 6 to the Tariff, granting it unilateral Section 205 authority. Given PJM already holds Section 205 authority over the capacity construct--where the region's shortfall has occurred--we offer conditional support only. This transfer must be strictly paired with:

The codified public interest mandate.

A formal state counterfiling right.

A mandatory reliability impact statement for any Section 205 filing, reviewed by the Independent Market Monitor.

VI. THE ROLE OF THE STATES
The central governance defect in PJM is a federalism problem: states bear responsibility for resource adequacy and retail rates but lack structural power. We support:

Nominating Committee: Three state seats on PJM's Nominating Committee. Furthermore, removing a sitting board member should require the supermajority concurrence of PJM states via OPSI.

Counterfiling Right: A formal, Commission-enforceable right for a supermajority of states to file an alternative proposal alongside a PJM Section 205 filing. We reject informal, non-binding agreements, which failed to enforce the January 2026 statement of principles.

Funding: Tariff-based funding for OPSI and a governors' designee group to ensure independent technical capacity.

VII. STAKEHOLDER PROCESS DESIGN, VOTING, AND TRANSPARENCY

Voting Alignment: We support conforming lower-committee voting to senior committee sector weighting and ending affiliate multi-voting.

Committee Consolidation: We oppose wholesale elimination of lower committees. A task force meeting 1.17 times per month during a resource crisis is a scheduling failure, not a structural one. We recommend a strict shot clock on critical issues.

Work Plan: Codify the Manual 34 annual work plan into the Tariff to make it Commission-enforceable.

Advisory-Only Voting: We oppose converting to an advisory model unless the state governance package is adopted simultaneously. We also support increasing the sector weight for state consumer advocates.

Recorded Positions: We strongly support Commissioner LaCerte's proposal to circulate a formal, public ballot on reform proposals to establish accountability.

VIII. THE MISSING MEASUREMENT: UTILIZATION OF EXISTING CAPACITY
No PJM committee measures whether existing firm, accredited capacity is being dispatched before the region procures replacement capacity. In the Ohio Valley, fully depreciated, low-marginal-cost coal units often sit idle while utilities recover high replacement power costs from ratepayers.

We recommend the Commission direct PJM to produce an annual Capacity Utilization and Dispatch Prudence Report, subject to Independent Market Monitor review. It must identify economically available, undispatched firm capacity during peak load events and the cost of replacement energy procured in its place.

IX. STRUCTURAL SCOPE, EXIT RIGHTS, AND THE ENFORCEABLE BACKSTOP
PJM coordinates two divergent economies: an Atlantic seaboard of data-center load growth and an Appalachian generation corridor absorbing the costs.

Exit Rights: We adamantly oppose restricting member withdrawal rights. The right to exit is the only functional market discipline currently operating on PJM.

A Date Certain: The Commission must establish March 31, 2027, as the absolute deadline for a Section 205 reform package. Failure to file must trigger a Section 206 proceeding examining if PJM's footprint and governance remain just and reasonable--keeping RTO division explicitly on the table.

X. SUMMARY OF RECOMMENDATIONS

Codify an Order No. 2000 public interest duty to retail consumers.

Adopt six-to-nine year staggered board terms.

Require OPSI supermajority concurrence for board member removal.

Provide three state seats on the Nominating Committee.

Fund OPSI and a governors' designee group through the Tariff.

Establish a formal state counterfiling right on Section 205 filings.

Condition Section 205 transfers on a public interest mandate, counterfiling right, and reliability impact statement.

Require published annual reliability performance metrics.

Order an annual Capacity Utilization and Dispatch Prudence Report.

Apply senior committee sector-weighted voting at lower committees.

Codify the Manual 34 work plan in the Tariff.

Impose resolution shot clocks rather than eliminating committees.

Increase the consumer advocate sector weight.

Circulate a recorded, public stakeholder ballot before ADR commences.

Reject any restrictions on member withdrawal rights.

Set March 31, 2027, as a Section 206 backstop deadline.

XI. CONCLUSION
The Appalachian and Ohio Valley communities will measure the outcome of this proceeding against one question: whether the lights stay on and what it costs. We stand ready to participate in the Alternative Dispute Resolution process commencing September 1, 2026.
