I.
INTRODUCTION 
Old Dominion Electric Cooperative (“ODEC”) appreciates this opportunity to provide 
comments pursuant to the Notice issued by the Commission in the above-referenced proceeding 
on July 30, 2026. As discussed herein, ODEC generally supports the existing PJM governance and 
stakeholder process. However, ODEC also understands that the Commission has undertaken a 
review “with a particular focus on identifying and evaluating actionable reforms to improve PJM’s 
ability to address system needs in a timely and efficient manner.”1 With this focus in mind, ODEC 
offers comments on the issues discussed at the Technical Conference held on July 23, 2026. ODEC 
also provides in this pleading answers to the questions posed in the Commission’s July 30, 2026 
Notice. 
ODEC believes PJM’s governance model has generally served the region well and should 
not be fundamentally restructured. The stakeholder process remains one of PJM’s defining 
strengths and continues to provide valuable technical expertise, stakeholder engagement, and 
accountability. At the same time, ODEC believes targeted reforms are warranted to improve 
transparency, strengthen Board accountability, modernize certain governance requirements, and 
enhance the effectiveness of the stakeholder process. Targeted reforms to PJM’s governance and 
stakeholder process should also result in improved involvement and coordination with the PJM 
states.

II.
COMMENTS 
A. Overview of ODEC 
ODEC is a not-for-profit power supply electric cooperative, organized and operating under 
the laws of Virginia and subject to FERC jurisdiction.  ODEC supplies capacity and energy to its 
eleven electric distribution cooperative members in Delaware, Maryland and Virginia, all of which 
are located within the PJM control area. ODEC is a network transmission customer of PJM, as 
well as a PJM Transmission Owner. ODEC is also a generation owner in PJM. ODEC takes 
transmission service under the PJM Tariff.  ODEC is also a Market Seller of energy and capacity 
under the PJM Tariff. Thus, ODEC participates in each market operated by PJM and also 
participates extensively in the PJM stakeholder process as a member of the Electric Distributor 
(“ED”) sector.

B. Board Independence 
ODEC supports maintaining an independent Board of Managers and believes Board 
independence remains an essential component of effective regional governance. The Board must 
also possess the expertise, transparency, accountability, and perspective necessary to maintain 
stakeholder confidence and effectively respond to the increasingly complex reliability, market, and 
transmission challenges facing the PJM region. Accordingly, ODEC supports consideration of 
several targeted reforms to strengthen the Board’s effectiveness and legitimacy while preserving 
its independence and oversight role. Those reforms include the following: 

Increasing the term of PJM Board members from three years to five years, and 
eliminating term limits; 

Reducing the “cooling off” period from two years to one year; 

Increasing transparency into PJM Board decision-making through the publication of 
individual Board voting records and expanded use of public Board meetings; 

Recognizing the public interest as a responsibility of the PJM Board, provided that the 
concept of “public interest” is clearly defined and tied to PJM’s core mission, with the 
addition of affordability; and  

Adding state representation on the PJM Nominating Committee. 
In addition to these reforms, there are several aspects of PJM’s Board composition and process 
that ODEC believes should be retained. For example, the existing restrictions against ex parte 
communication with the PJM Board should not be relaxed. ODEC also does not support imposing 
a mandatory residency requirement for members of the PJM Board of Managers. Familiarity 
and/or expertise regarding the PJM region is helpful, but residing in the region is not critical. 
Instead of a residence requirement, the Board member qualification rules should be revised to

C. Section 205 Filing Rights 
Section 205 filing rights are among the most consequential authorities within PJM’s 
governance framework. Within PJM, the allocation of Section 205 filing authority is not 
straightforward. While certain filing rights are expressly granted to PJM, Transmission Owners, 
or other entities, the ability to propose reforms is often influenced by where the relevant provisions 
appear in PJM’s governing documents and the approval processes applicable to those documents. 
As a result, discussions regarding “who holds a Section 205 filing right” frequently refer not only 
to legal filing authority before FERC but also to which entity’s approval is required for a proposal 
to be advanced. 
Consequently, proposals to move a provision from the Operating Agreement to the Tariff, 
or to transfer filing authority from one entity to another, are often about more than filing rights 
alone. Such reforms can alter the role of stakeholders in the development of PJM rules, affect the 
degree of discretion afforded to PJM or the Board of Managers, and ultimately change the balance 
of authority within PJM’s governance structure. For this reason, the location of a provision within 
PJM’s governing documents can be nearly as important as the filing right itself when evaluating 
governance reform proposals. ODEC generally believes governance reforms should focus less on 
preserving existing filing-right allocations and more on ensuring that any reallocation of authority 
maintains appropriate checks and balances among the PJM Board, and stakeholders.

ODEC conditionally supports granting the PJM Board independent Section 205 filing 
rights over the Energy Market and Ancillary Services Markets, as well as over RTEP matters, 
provided stakeholders retain the ability to advance competing proposals through a Members 
Committee “jump ball” filing process. ODEC believes PJM should possess sufficient authority to 
advance reforms to core Energy Market and Ancillary Services Market constructs when it 
determines such reforms are necessary to maintain reliability, improve market outcomes, or 
address emerging challenges facing the region. Similarly, PJM should possess sufficient authority 
to advance transmission planning reforms when it determines such reforms are necessary to 
maintain reliability, accommodate load growth, facilitate resource development, and/or improve 
the efficiency of the transmission planning process. Providing PJM with independent Section 205 
filing authority would allow PJM to bring proposals directly to FERC for consideration without 
first obtaining supermajority stakeholder approval. ODEC does not support further revisions to 
FPA Section 205 filing rights at this time. 
At the same time, ODEC believes meaningful stakeholder participation remains an 
essential component of PJM governance. The Energy Market and Ancillary Services Markets 
directly affect generators, load-serving entities, electric distributors, transmission owners, 
consumers, and other market participants throughout the PJM footprint. Stakeholders provide 
valuable technical expertise and practical experience that improve market design and help identify 
unintended consequences associated with proposed reforms. 
In addition to the “jump ball” filing process, it will be critical for the Commission to ensure 
that the PJM stakeholder process continues to require PJM to thoroughly vet changes with 
stakeholders. Also, as the Commission determined in rejecting PJM and the PJM Transmission 
Owners’ prior attempts to revise the governing documents regarding PJM FPA section 205 filing

D. Stakeholder Process 
PJM’s stakeholder process is one of the organization’s defining characteristics and serves 
as the primary mechanism for developing changes to PJM’s markets, planning procedures, 
operations, and governing documents. The process brings together representatives from all PJM 
sectors to provide technical expertise, identify potential impacts, and build consensus around 
proposed reforms. 
While recent governance reform discussions have raised concerns regarding the speed and 
effectiveness of the stakeholder process, ODEC believes meaningful stakeholder participation 
remains one of PJM’s greatest strengths and should be preserved. At the same time, ODEC 
recognizes that certain reforms are warranted to improve efficiency, reduce procedural barriers, 
and ensure the process remains capable of addressing the increasingly complex challenges facing 
the PJM region. 
One such change that ODEC supports in order to improve efficiency is to introduce a  
sector-weighted vote to approve proposals in PJM’s lower-level committees and senior task forces. 
Requiring sector-weighted vote in lower-level committees would help ensure that proposals 
brought forward to the Members Committee reflect greater consensus and have received 
meaningful consideration across stakeholder sectors. ODEC also supports eliminating affiliate 
voting at PJM’s lower-level committees and senior task forces. The purpose of lower-level 
committees is to develop proposals, identify stakeholder concerns, and build consensus among 
affected parties. Allowing affiliated entities to exercise multiple votes at this stage can create the

E. State Participation  
States have an important interest in PJM governance given their responsibility for 
overseeing retail electric service and protecting consumer interests. As PJM’s decisions 
increasingly affect reliability, affordability, transmission investment, and resource adequacy, 
many stakeholders have proposed expanding the role of states within PJM governance. ODEC 
supports meaningful state engagement in PJM governance. However, any reforms should preserve 
clear lines of accountability, maintain PJM’s regional character, and recognize that individual 
states may have different positions on issues. Consistent with these principles, ODEC supports 
exploring opportunities to better coordinate and consolidate state participation within PJM 
governance but does not support creating additional state-focused organizations and forums. The 
existence of multiple state-focused organizations and forums can create uncertainty regarding 
which entity is speaking on behalf of the states, particularly in instances where different 
organizations within a single state may not agree. Multiple state-focused organizations will also 
make it difficult to determine whether a particular position reflects a consensus view or the 
perspective of a subset of states. ODEC also supports the use of non-binding advisory or 
informational voting mechanisms for states to communicate their views and the addition of state 
representation to the Nominating Committee. However, ODEC does not support granting states 
formal voting rights within PJM’s stakeholder process. As discussed in the response to the

Commission’s questions, below, states initially opted against becoming Members of PJM. 
Moreover, states have the ability to communicate with and advise the PJM Board freely and would

uncertainty regarding how state votes would be allocated and exercised.

Comments: Board members will have an initial period of time where they learn PJM 
substantive matters and process (e.g., stakeholder, governance processes). Board

balance.
