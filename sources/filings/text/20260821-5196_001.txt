Shell Energy North America (US), L.P. (“Shell Energy”)1 submits these comments on the 
Staff-led technical conference held on July 23, 2026, in the above-referenced docket.2 Shell Energy 
appreciates this opportunity the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or 
“Commission”) has created for stakeholders to collaborate on enhancements to PJM’s governance 
and stakeholder process. PJM has made meaningful progress over the past year in enhancing 
transparency and stakeholder engagement.  Further reforms should be evaluated based on whether 
they improve decision-making, allow appropriate checks and balances, and support the regulatory 
certainty needed to promote investment and maintain reliability at just and reasonable rates.

II. 
APPENDIX RESPONSES 
The responses below reflect these principles and focus on reforms that improve decision-
making and preserve stakeholder accountability, market transparency, and confidence in PJM’s 
long-term reliability objectives and market framework. 
PJM Board Independence 
1. Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years?  
Governance research and board governance best practices support periodic board 
renewal and recognize that long tenure can diminish independence, critical oversight, and the 
introduction of new perspectives. While no single tenure limit has been shown to be universally 
optimal, many organizations establish an outer limit of approximately six to nine years of 
continuous board service. 7 Multi-year staggered terms, like those in effect in PJM, are preferred 
over a single lengthy term.8 Staggered renewable terms provide periodic opportunities to assess 
engagement, performance, and continued fit. They also promote board continuity through 
staggered turnover and reduce the risk of multiple directors departing simultaneously.

2. Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 10 
years)?  
Industry practice generally supports term limits, while allowing limited discretion in 
exceptional circumstances to justify service beyond term limits (e.g. term limit waivers for 
MISO Board members Barbara Krumsiek and Todd Raba in 2025). PJM’s current governance 
framework achieves this balance through staggered three-year terms and a five-term 
maximum (15 years). As discussed in Response 1, regular re-election cycles provide 
opportunities to assess performance, engagement, independence, and continued fit, while 
preserving valuable institutional knowledge and continuity. As a result, PJM’s existing 
approach appropriately balances board refreshment with long-term effectiveness.  
3. Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for some 
Board members that reside in the PJM region?  
While familiarity with the PJM region is beneficial, expertise is not contingent on 
current residence. The primary qualifications for Board service should remain independence, 
expertise, and governance capability. Residency within the footprint may be considered a 
positive factor but should not be a mandatory qualification that could unnecessarily limit the 
pool of potential candidates.  
4. Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for Board 
members from two years to one year?  
This change would potentially increase the pool of candidates. Prospective Board 
members should remain subject to PJM’s conflict-of-interest and independence requirements, 
including restrictions on financial interests in and business relationships with PJM Member 
companies and their affiliates.

5. Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more communication 
between the Board and stakeholders?  
The current ex parte communication framework appropriately protects the Board’s 
independence and helps prevent undue influence. PJM has made progress in expanding 
opportunities for Board engagement through forums such as at the end of Members 
Committee meetings. Additional engagement should be pursued within the existing 
framework.  
6. In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be 
required to explain its reasoning?  
PJM has made significant progress on transparency through Board letters and other 
communications. The Board should continue to enhance transparency by explaining the 
rationale for its decisions, including insight into differing views considered during 
deliberations, and why its views may have switched over time. Public disclosure of individual 
Board member votes raises separate concerns. It could increase the risk of lobbying, undue 
influence, or retaliation directed at individual members, making robust ex parte protections 
even more important. Board performance should be assessed based on the integrity of its 
process, decision-making, and engagement; not on whether stakeholders agree with a 
particular outcome.  
7. In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available to the 
public?  
Select Board meetings or meeting segments should be open to the public to increase 
transparency and demonstrate that stakeholder and public concerns are being considered. 
However, confidential or market-sensitive discussions should remain closed to ensure 
effective governance.

8. Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the responsibility of 
PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest?  
PJM and its Board advance the public interest by ensuring reliability, just and 
reasonable rates, and well-functioning competitive markets. PJM members invest in and 
provide the infrastructure and risk management necessary to help achieve these outcomes. 
Any such revision should clearly define the term and avoid creating ambiguity that could 
allow PJM’s core responsibilities to be deprioritized in favor of particular stakeholder or 
policy preferences.  
9. Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the Nominating 
Committee?  
Consumer interests are already represented by the End Use Consumer sector’s voting 
seat, so the purpose and responsibilities of a state representative seat must be clearly structured 
to support PJM’s overall interests rather than those of any individual state or policy agenda. 
Because the Nominating Committee Code of Conduct requires its members to act in the best 
interests of PJM, the selection criteria for any state representative should be transparent, 
objective and designed to promote independence from political considerations.  
10. Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management compensation to their 
achievement?  
Transparent, publicly reported performance metrics that are tied to management 
compensation would enhance accountability and align management incentives with PJM’s 
core responsibilities.  
Section 205 Filing Rights 
11. Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the Board?  
An advisory stakeholder process may improve PJM’s ability to act more quickly, but

Page 7 of 8 
 
it should not be adopted if PJM also receives exclusive Section 205 filing rights. The 
stakeholder process serves important functions beyond producing votes, including vetting 
proposals, identifying unintended consequences, improving filings, and encouraging 
compromise before issues reach FERC. If stakeholder votes are merely advisory and PJM has 
sole authority to file under Section 205, stakeholders may have little incentive to invest in 
consensus-building and instead reserve their efforts for privately lobbying of PJM staff and 
litigation before FERC. 
The MISO Resource Availability and Need (RAN) proceeding illustrates this concern. 
In 2021, stakeholders overwhelmingly supported a motion requesting that MISO delay its 
filing and implementation schedule. MISO ultimately chose not to follow the stakeholder 
recommendation and proceeded with its filing after only a limited delay.9 This example 
demonstrates how an advisory process can leave stakeholders with influence, but little 
practical ability to shape the outcome when management disagrees. 
Accordingly, stakeholder voting should become advisory only if stakeholders are 
provided a meaningful “jump-ball” right to bring alternative proposals before FERC. 
Providing meaningful jump-ball rights helps ensure that stakeholder engagement continues to 
improve proposals, identify unintended consequences, and produce more durable market 
reforms.  
12. Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary services 
unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members 
Committee?

PJM should have unencumbered Section 205 filing rights over the energy and ancillary 
services markets provided stakeholders are afforded meaningful “jump ball” rights that allow 
FERC to consider stakeholder-supported alternative proposals. This approach supports timely 
decision-making, creates an important governance check, and maintains incentives for 
stakeholders to build consensus. As reforms across capacity, energy, and ancillary services 
markets are considered as part of PJM’s Holistic Market Review, a consistent legal framework 
would improve regulatory certainty and investment across the full revenue stack.  
13. Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion Planning 
(RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the Members 
Committee?  
As with energy and ancillary services, PJM should have unencumbered Section 205 
filing rights over RTEP provided stakeholders have meaningful “jump ball” rights that allow 
alternative proposals to be presented to FERC. Otherwise, exclusive filing authority would 
concentrate authority in PJM and reduce an important check and balance within PJM’s 
governance framework. 
The Commission recently considered related issues in EL24-119, ER24-2336, and 
ER24-2338. While FERC rejected the specific proposal before it, the proceedings highlighted 
the importance of carefully balancing PJM independence, stakeholder participation, and 
governance accountability. As discussed in Response 11, the 2021 MISO Resource 
Availability and Need (RAN) proceeding further illustrates the importance of preserving 
meaningful stakeholder checks. Having a mechanism for stakeholders to advance alternative 
proposals to FERC would help maintain confidence in the stakeholder process and encourage 
continued investment in consensus building.

14. Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over resource 
adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of OPSI agrees on 
an alternative proposal?  
OPSI should not receive separate jump-ball rights. If additional state participation is 
warranted, OPSI could advance proposals through the stakeholder process on the same footing 
as other stakeholder proposals considered by the Members Committee. 
15. Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the portions of the tariff 
where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders approves an 
alternative proposal?  
The Members Committee should have meaningful jump-ball rights when a 
supermajority of stakeholders approves an alternative proposal in areas where PJM has 
Section 205 filing authority. Similar to the NEPOOL framework in ISO-NE, the Members 
Committee should be able to advance stakeholder supported alternative proposals to FERC, 
which would allow PJM to independently file the proposal it believes is necessary to fulfill 
its responsibilities. 
Jump-ball rights promote stakeholder engagement, encourage consensus-building, and 
provide an important governance check without granting stakeholders veto authority over 
PJM filings. They also ensure that FERC can evaluate competing proposals on their merits 
when consensus cannot be reached. 
As PJM considers coordinated reforms across the capacity, energy, and ancillary 
service markets, reserving meaningful jump-ball rights will help maintain confidence in the 
stakeholder process while ensuring that broader market reforms benefit from a robust record 
before the Commission.

Stakeholder Process 
16. Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-thirds 
sector-weighted voting?  
Any reduction in the Members Committee approval threshold should be limited to 
60% sector-weighted voting (three sectors). This would improve efficiency while preserving 
the need for broad stakeholder support and maintaining an important governance check on 
PJM’s Section 205 authority. To the extent PJM has unilateral Section 205 filing rights over 
portions of the Tariff or Operating Agreement, the approval threshold is arbitrary. 
17. Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior standing committees, 
also apply to lower-level committees?  
Applying affiliate voting restrictions consistently across all committee levels would 
improve the consistency and perceived fairness of the stakeholder process. While no voting 
structure perfectly aligns voting power with stakeholder interests and deployed capital, this 
could allow removal of layers of committees that often encumber the stakeholder process. 
PJM could also consider a representative voting model like those used in ERCOT and MISO, 
where elected sector representatives vote on behalf of their sectors at the highest levels of the 
stakeholder process. Such an approach could encourage greater consensus-building within 
sectors and improve the efficiency of stakeholder decision-making.  
State Participation 
18.  In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting of 
governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ representatives? 
Rather than creating a second state representative organization, FERC should consider 
whether OPSI could be expanded to incorporate additional state perspectives. OPSI already 
provides a coordinated regional forum for state utility regulators within the PJM footprint.

Creating a separate organization for governors’ representatives and/or legislators could lead 
to duplicative processes, conflicting recommendations, and additional governance 
complexity.  If FERC concludes that broader state participation is warranted, a more effective 
approach may be to expand OPSI’s role in a manner similar to NESCOE in New England. 
Such a structure could provide a single forum for utility regulators, governors’ offices and 
other state energy policymakers to coordinate and engage with PJM, while preserving each 
state’s independent authority to advocate for its own positions before PJM and FERC. 
19. Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process (limited to 
certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost allocation) in 
addition to the consumer advocate votes? 
Allowing state voting rights to a subset of topics would create unnecessary complexity 
and likely lead to disputes over which issues qualify for state participation. If additional state 
involvement is warranted, strengthening OPSI’s consultative role, similar to NESCOE's role 
in New England, would provide a clearer and more effective mechanism for state engagement 
than creating a new voting class within the stakeholder process. 
20. Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 205 filing related to 
resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation? 
PJM should consult with OPSI before making Section 205 filings on these issues. Such 
consultation would ensure state regulators have a formal opportunity to provide input, like the 
role stakeholder consultation plays through the Members Committee. New England’s 
experience with NESCOE demonstrates the value of structured state engagement while 
preserving RTO and state independence.

III. 
CONCLUSION  
Shell Energy appreciates the opportunity to provide these comments. As PJM considers 
coordinated reforms across capacity, energy, and ancillary services, governance changes should 
prioritize timely decision-making, meaningful stakeholder engagement and appropriate checks and 
balances. A governance framework that balances efficiency, accountability, and transparency will 
be better positioned to support investment, maintain confidence in PJM markets, and enable 
durable market reforms that support reliability over the long term.  
Ultimately, governance reform should be evaluated based on whether it helps PJM 
maintain reliability, support just and reasonable rates, and advance a transparent, durable and 
investable market framework.
