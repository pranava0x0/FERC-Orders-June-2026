SUMMARY OF FILING 
The Show Cause Order commended SPP’s “bold and proactive steps” to 
integrate large loads. It recognized that SPP’s High Impact Large Load (“HILL”) 
process already provides an enhanced study framework for Eligible Customers taking 
transmission service on behalf of large loads.7 The Commission nonetheless 
preliminarily found that “SPP’s Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because 
it does not include transmission services that reflect Eligible Customers taking 
transmission service on behalf of flexible large loads that are willing and able to limit 
their use of the transmission system under certain conditions.”8  The Commission 
directed:

behalf of Eligible Load, or propose appropriate Tariff revisions.

With this filing, SPP is proposing the addition of PALS to the Tariff to address 
the Commission’s specific concern that the Tariff “does not include transmission 
services that reflect Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of 
flexible large loads that are willing and able to limit their use of the transmission system 
under certain conditions.” PALS is a flexible transmission service that allows 
Transmission Customers to limit their use of the Transmission System. Under PALS, 
a PAL’s withdrawals are governed by the price-sensitive PAL Energy Bids11 its Market 
Participant submits, which are subject to a uniform $25/MWh cap that protects other 
customers from substantially high energy prices and from utilizing resources for PALS 
loads that are otherwise intended to serve firm load. PALS is available for renewable 
terms of one to ten years and is not tied to the construction of Network Upgrades. PALS 
is SPP’s regional variation on the Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission service 
described in the PJM Co-Location Order, tailored to SPP’s Integrated Marketplace and 
SPP’s transmission services. Like the Commission’s Non-Firm Contract Demand 
transmission service, PALS limits service to a specified MW quantity (the PALS 
Capacity). It is curtailed before firm service, imposes penalties for unreserved use, 
carries no resource adequacy obligation, and requires control technologies or protection 
systems that keep withdrawals within approved levels. 
  
This filing is one part of SPP’s comprehensive response to the Show Cause 
Order. The collective filings addressing the Show Cause Order’s remaining directives 
are identified in Section V. Section III.B.11 explains how PALS avoids disrupting 
existing commercial arrangements, as the Show Cause Order requires.

and accommodate new large loads. PALS is a novel approach to non-firm transmission 
service intended to serve loads that do not seek a bridge to firm service but have a 
business case for a non-firm transmission product. Unlike CHILLS, PALS is not 
limited to HILLs and is not tied to the completion of Network Upgrades or the addition 
of Designated Resources. Instead, PALS is designed for flexible, price-sensitive loads. 
PALS does not require supporting Resources, will not cause Network Upgrades to be 
identified or constructed, and uses a rate design based on the rates for hourly Non-Firm 
Point-To-Point Transmission Service.

III. 
DESCRIPTION OF AND JUSTIFICATION FOR PROPOSED TARIFF 
REVISIONS  
 
A. 
Overview 
 
During the development of the CHILLS, there was stakeholder desire for a 
long-term transmission service product that would provide flexibility in how and when 
transmission customers could use the SPP Transmission System. CHILLS, although a 
non-firm, long-term transmission service product, requires the Transmission Customer 
to obtain and pay for transmission service based on a MW amount of reserved 
transmission capacity. SPP and its stakeholders developed the PALS to allow loads, 
not just HILLs, to obtain a long-term, non-firm dispatchable load service and 
 
regulatory agency input on matters of regional importance related to the development 
and operation of bulk electric transmission. The SPP RSC is comprised of retail 
regulatory commissioners from agencies in Arkansas, Arizona, Colorado, Iowa, 
Kansas, Louisiana, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Mexico, North 
Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, Utah and Wyoming. The RSC’s approved 
the following motion: “The Regional State Committee approves the revisions to 
Business Practice 8100 proposed in RR791 - Price Adaptive Load Service. The 
Regional State Committee recommends the Board of Directors support policies to 
reliably maximize utilization of the transmission system and consider the 
recommendation of the Cost Allocation Working Group relating to RR791 - Price 
Adaptive Load Service.”

SPP proposes new definitions for PAL, PALS, PALS Capacity, PALS 
Operating Agreement, PAL Upper Operating Tolerance, PALS Usage, and PALS 
Usage Limit. Similar to CHILL and CHILLS, PALS is the transmission service, and a 
PAL is the load that takes the service. The PALS Capacity is the maximum amount of 
Energy SPP agrees to transmit, the PALS Usage is the amount delivered, and the PALS 
Usage Limit reflects the four different paths possible for limiting withdrawals by PALs.  
 
PALS is designed to accommodate flexible, price-sensitive loads that are 
willing to withdraw from the Transmission System only when the transmission 
capacity is available. PALS is a new transmission service product, similar to CHILLS, 
that will help expedite the connection of new loads on the Transmission System. As 
described and justified below, the PALS proposal is just and reasonable and not unduly 
discriminatory or preferential.

non-firm service that serves the same purpose as the non-firm contract demand (“Non-

regional variations appropriate to SPP. SPP addresses interim NITS and firm contract 
demand service in the separate filings identified in Section V.

PALS Will Not Disrupt Existing Commercial Arrangements 
 
The Show Cause Order directs that any Section 205 filing explain how the 
proposal will avoid disrupting existing commercial arrangements and identify a 
reasonable effective date that accommodates them.157 PALS satisfies this requirement. 
PALS is a new, voluntary service option. It does not modify any existing service 
agreement, transmission service reservation, or pending study request, and it does not 
alter the terms of existing transmission service processes (i.e. NITS, Point-To-Point 
Transmission Service, CHILLS, HILLS). No existing customer is required to convert 
to PALS, and Eligible Customers with agreements nearing completion are free to 
proceed under existing Tariff process. Because PALS is an as-available service with 
priority equal to hourly Non-Firm Point-To-Point Transmission Service, it does not 
reduce the Available Transfer Capability available to firm customers or CHILLS. As 
discussed in Section VI, PALS will apply prospectively once SPP implements the 
necessary system changes, currently targeted for the second quarter of 2028.

requirements, and consequences for deviations from SPP’s instructions.

SPP proposes to cap the high end of PAL Energy Bid Curves at $25/MWh.168

The proposed fixed $25 bid cap is just and reasonable. Because PALs represent 
a new load participation model in the Integrated Marketplace, no historical operational 
experience or market data currently exists to support a more dynamic or formula-based 
approach. Under these circumstances, a fixed bid cap provides transparency, certainty,  
and administrative simplicity as SPP integrates PALs into market operations and gains 
experience with their performance and impacts. 
 
To ensure the bid cap remains appropriate over time, SPP will review the bid 
cap, at minimum, annually.169 As operational experience and market data become 
available, SPP will evaluate whether modifications to the bid cap are warranted, 
including the potential adoption of a formula-based methodology in the future.

several different interests. SPP sought to balance protection of load from high LMPs, 
protect reliability, increase transmission revenues, prevent excessive wear from

allow PALs to have a load factor that allows them to be viable. Using historical SPP
