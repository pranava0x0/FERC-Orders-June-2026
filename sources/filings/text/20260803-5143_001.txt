Controllable Load Framework: A Reliability Opportunity

The electric grid is entering a new era. For more than a century, planning assumed that demand simply consumed electricity while generators produced it. That assumption is no longer entirely true.
The rapid growth of artificial intelligence data centers has introduced a new class of electric load. Unlike traditional industrial facilities, these computational loads can often be scheduled, shifted, paused, or reduced in seconds through software. They are not simply consumers of electricity; they are programmable electric resources.

Today's planning and reliability frameworks largely continue to treat these facilities as conventional load. That overlooks an engineering opportunity.

The Controllable Load Framework (CLF) proposes that qualified large programmable loads be recognized as reliability resources alongside generation, transmission, storage, and conventional demand response. Their flexibility should be measured, verified, and incorporated into planning and system operations where appropriate.

This is not a substitute for dispatchable generation. Reliable electric systems will continue to require sufficient generation, transmission, and reserves. Rather, controllable computational load should become another engineering tool available to system operators.

For NERC
NERC's work on emerging large loads appropriately focuses on protecting Bulk Electric System reliability. That effort should also examine how programmable computational loads can contribute to reliability.

Future reliability standards could establish performance expectations for qualified controllable loads, including:

verified response capability,
communications and telemetry,
predictable operating characteristics,
response during frequency and voltage events,
testing and validation procedures.
Recognizing controllable loads as measurable reliability resources would strengthen planning while maintaining system security.

For PJM Governance
PJM's governance challenge is not simply making decisions faster. It is ensuring that planning evaluates all available engineering solutions.

Governance should require that planning studies evaluate controllable large loads alongside generation, transmission, storage, and demand response whenever significant new load is proposed.

This would allow stakeholders to compare engineering alternatives using objective reliability metrics rather than relying solely on traditional assumptions about electric demand.

Better governance is not merely faster governance. Better governance is governance that consistently considers the full range of technically viable solutions.

Looking Forward
The electric grid has always evolved when technology changed.

Today, the change is not only new generators or new transmission lines. It is the emergence of software-controlled electric demand capable of supporting grid reliability.

Planning frameworks should evolve to recognize that reality.

The Controllable Load Framework does not replace existing reliability tools. It expands the engineering toolbox available to planners and operators.

As computational load continues to grow across North America, the question should no longer be whether programmable demand exists. The question should be how best to integrate it into a reliable, resilient electric system.
