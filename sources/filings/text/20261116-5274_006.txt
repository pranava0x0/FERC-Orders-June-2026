I.
BACKGROUND 
SunZia Transmission is an indirect, wholly-owned subsidiary of Pattern Energy Group 
LP and has developed, constructed and recently placed into commercial operation, an 
approximately 552-mile, bi-pole, high voltage, direct current (“HVDC”) 500 kV transmission 
line (the “Transmission System”) with a transmission capacity of 3,021 MW in New Mexico and 
Arizona that connects generation resources in the Southwest with markets and customers in 
California and Arizona.7  SunZia Transmission commenced construction of the Transmission 
System in 2023 and placed the Transmission System into commercial operations in April, 2026.  
The Transmission System has been developed as a merchant transmission project with negotiated 
rate authority contingent on the allocation of its transmission capacity pursuant to an open 
solicitation process reviewed and approved by the Commission.8
As a result, the entire capacity of the Transmission System is committed to two 
Subscribers, SunZia Wind South LLC and SunZia Wind North LLC (collectively, the “SunZia 
Wind Companies”), both of which own and operate wind-powered electric generating facilities 
located in Lincoln, Torrance, and San Miguel Counties, New Mexico (the “SunZia Wind 
Projects”).  SunZia Transmission will provide to the SunZia Wind Companies firm point-to-
point transmission service for the entire rated capacity of the Transmission System over a thirty-
year term9 from the Point of Receipt at the Pete Heinrich 345 kV AC Switchyard to the Point of 
Delivery at the Salt River Project Agricultural Improvement and Power District’s (“SRP”) Pinal 
Central 500 kV Substation pursuant to Amended and Restated Transmission Service Agreements 
7 
SunZia Transmission and the New Mexico Renewable Energy Transmission Authority (“RETA”) have entered 
into a lease arrangement pursuant to which RETA owns the physical assets of the Transmission System.  
SunZia Transmission and RETA are both responsible for the co-development of the Transmission System, 
while SunZia Transmission is responsible for the construction and financing of the Transmission System and is 
the owner of the capacity rights created by the Transmission System.  RETA is a state authority established by 
the New Mexico legislature in 2007 and charged with implementing the New Mexico Renewable Energy 
Authority Transmission Act, NMSA 1978 § 62-16A-1, et seq. (the “Act”).  As an authority or instrumentality 
of the State of New Mexico, RETA is not a “public utility” within the meaning of Section 201 of the FPA.  See 
16 U.S.C. § 824(f) (2019).  Under the Act, RETA is authorized to plan, finance, develop and acquire 
transmission and storage projects (transmitting or storing at least 30% renewable energy) in order to promote 
the development of renewable energy in New Mexico.  For purposes of this filing, SunZia Transmission is 
considered the Transmission Owner in interest of the Transmission System pursuant to the lease arrangement 
described above.

SunZia Transmission is in a unique position with respect to its compliance with the 
Commission’s directives in the Order to Show Cause for several reasons.  First, SunZia 
Transmission does not directly serve load and, accordingly, there are no provisions in its TO 
Tariff that address the interconnection of the load to the SunZia Transmission System.  As a 
result, the SunZia Transmission TO Tariff is silent with respect to load interconnections and 
there is no need to modify the Tariff to address the interconnection of large or co-located loads. 
Second, the SunZia Transmission System is not located within the service territory of a load 
serving participating transmission owner within the CAISO market that is subject to the Order to 
Show Cause and that will be submitting tariff changes in response to the Order to Show Cause.  
The SunZia Transmission System is embedded within the service territories of Arizona and New 
Mexico load-serving entities that are not subject to the Order to Show Cause or any of the other 
Orders to Show Cause issued to regional transmission operators or independent system operators 
throughout the country.  Finally, Section 8 of the SunZia Transmission TO Tariff states that all 
requests for interconnection to the SunZia Transmission System must be directed in the first 
instance to CAISO with notice of such request being provided to SunZia Transmission.  Thus, 
CAISO is responsible for managing the generator interconnection process for the SunZia 
Transmission System with SunZia Transmission supporting that process through the undertaking 
of studies on the impact of the interconnection on the System. Any changes to the generation 
interconnection process to facilitate the interconnection of co-located load to the transmission 
system must be set forth in the CAISO Tariff, in which case SunZia Transmission will propose 
conforming changes to its TO Tariff to remain consistent with the CAISO’s changes. 
II.
DESCRIPTION OF FILING 
Consistent with the description above as to SunZia Transmission’s unique and limited 
role in the interconnection process overall, Section 8 of the TO Tariff has been amended to 
clarify that the CAISO Tariff will continue to control with respect to the interconnection of 
Generating Units, energy storage co-located with Large Load and Co-located Generating Units 
with Large Loads.  In proposing these limited changes to its TO Tariff, SunZia Transmission 
reserves the right, following review of the proposed tariff changes filed by the other Participating 
Transmission Owners (“PTOs”) that are subject to the Commission’s Order to Show Cause, to 
amend this filing to propose additional changes to ensure consistency among all similarly-
situated PTOs.   
Finally, consistent with its comments made during the stakeholder process, SunZia 
Transmission continues to support the need to apply these enhanced Large Load interconnection 
procedures to Electrically Proximate Loads.  CAISO has explained that the need for such 
changes within the CAISO footprint in California will be addressed following the submission of 
the Large Load compliance filing.  SunZia Transmission supports the commencement of that 
effort as soon as possible because the value proposition and the question surrounding how to 
study the interconnection of electrically proximate large load and generation in tandem (similar 
to co-located load and generation) are unique when applied to the SunZia Transmission System 
in New Mexico and Arizona.

3.13
SunZia Transmission System.  The SunZia Transmission System is an approximately 
552-mile, bi-pole, high voltage direct current transmission line in New Mexico and Arizona 
with a nominal transmission capacity of 3,021 MW (“SunZia Transmission System”).  The 
SunZia Transmission System consists of a 345 kV alternating current switching station in 
New Mexico referred to as the Pete Heinrich Switching Station along with voltage source 
converter stations at a nominal direct current voltage of ±525 kV with 2156 kcmil 
“Bluebird” conductors arranged in a three-conductor bundle for each of the two (2) pole 
conductors traversing the approximately 552-mile transmission line length.  Additionally, 
the transmission towers include dedicated metallic return conductors insulated at 85 kV 
which are electrically bundled as two (2) 1590 kcmil “Lapwing” conductors but 
geographically separated on the towers (i.e., split bundle).  The SunZia Transmission 
System includes two (2), 1,500 MW dynamic braking resistors in New Mexico and two 
~0.75-mile 500kV AC transmission lines to connect the SunZia Transmission System to 
the Pinal Central substation in Pinal County, Arizona.

For the period commencing on the grant of operational control of the SunZia Transmission
System to CAISO and the subsequent two-year period following the Generation COD Date and
in accordance with the CAISO Tariff, the Non-Subscriber Usage Rate for both import and export
transactions over the SunZia Transmission System shall be set at $0.00 per MWh.  For the
one-year period beginning on the second anniversary of the Generation COD Date and in
accordance with the CAISO Tariff, the Non-Subscriber Usage Rate for both import and export
transactions over the SunZia Transmission System shall be set at $2.0775 per kW/month (which
increases annually by one-half of one percent (0.5%) commencing on the date on which
operational control of the SunZia Transmission System is granted to CAISO) converted to an
annual rate (i.e., multiplying by twelve) and then divided by 8,760 hours to derive an hourly
off-peak rate for use of the SunZia Transmission System by Non-Subscribers.  Commencing on
the third anniversary of the Generation COD Date and in accordance with the CAISO Tariff, the
Non-Subscriber Usage Rate for both import and export transactions over the SunZia
Transmission System shall be the current monthly point-to-point transmission rate charged by
SunZia Transmission for use of the SunZia Transmission System by Subscribers in the TSA, as
amended, supplemented, or restated from time to time and as accepted by the Commission
(which increases annually by one-half of one percent (0.5%) commencing on the date on which
operational control of the SunZia Transmission System is granted to CAISO), converted to an
annual rate (i.e., multiplying by twelve) and then divided by 8,760 hours to derive an hourly
off-peak rate for use of the SunZia Transmission System by Non-Subscribers.  In no event shall
the Non-Subscriber Usage Rate for the use of the SunZia Transmission System exceed the
applicable CAISO Regional Access Charge in effect at the time service is provided to the
Non-Subscriber.

Palo Verde) – As of the Grant of Operational Control to CAISO:

Arizona Transmission Rights shall be the weighted average (weighted by the amount of firm
point-to-point transmission service held as an Arizona Entitlement by SunZia Transmission) of

the Open Access Same-Time Information System (“OASIS”) of each transmission provider,

derive an hourly off-peak rate for use of the Arizona Transmission Rights by Non-Subscribers
plus the actual Schedule 1 and 2 charges imposed by WAPA, SRP and TEP.  In no event shall
the Non-Subscriber Usage Rate for the Arizona Transmission Rights exceed the applicable
CAISO Regional Access Charge in effect at the time service is provided to the Non-Subscriber.
