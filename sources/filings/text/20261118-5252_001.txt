II. 
BACKGROUND 
A. 
The HILL Process  
 
On January 14, 2026, the Commission accepted SPP's Tariff revisions 
establishing the HILL study process in Attachment BA and the HILLGA process in 
Attachment BB.11 Those revisions (1) defined HILLs as a new category of load; (2) 
established enhanced study requirements for HILLs, including the HDPS, to enable 
SPP to assess the reliability impacts of a HILL on the Transmission System; and (3) 
imposed ongoing operational requirements on HILLs to ensure the continued reliable 
operation of the Transmission System. Those operational requirements, in Attachment 
BA, Section 2.2, include: compliance with non-conforming load requirements; real-
time telemetry; remote disconnect capability; a ramp-rate limitation; phasor 
measurement unit installation; ride-through requirements; and a prohibition on 
withdrawals without appropriate transmission service.12 The Commission found the 
HILL process to be “a just and reasonable approach for integrating transmission 
customers’ new large loads onto SPP’s transmission system in a reliable manner.”13 
 
B. 
Generator Interconnections to Facilities Outside the Transmission 
System  
 
Attachments V and AY of the Tariff govern interconnection to SPP’s 
Transmission System.14 Those Attachments also include Affected System Study 
procedures for proposed interconnections to other transmission systems that may affect 
SPP’s Transmission System.15 Some generation is Behind-The-Meter Generation, co-
located on-site, or connected to distribution or other facilities that are not part of the 
Transmission System. SPP currently addresses that type of generation through 
Business Practice 7250, which uses "non-jurisdictional" terminology. The Tariff does 
not define that term or use it consistently. As a result, the Tariff does not clearly state 
when such generation will be studied for its impact on the Transmission System, by 
whom, or through which study process. Nor does it identify the responsible party when 
such generation fails to register in the Integrated Marketplace and is not subject to an 
interconnection agreement with the Transmission Provider or a Transmission Owner.

III. 
DESCRIPTION OF AND JUSTIFICATION FOR THE LARGE LOAD 
REVISIONS 
 
The Large Load Revisions respond to the gaps the Commission identified and 
build on a framework the Commission has already found just and reasonable. They 
apply uniformly to all similarly situated customers and advance the Commission’s 
stated objectives of protecting consumers from unnecessary costs, safeguarding 
reliability, and improving transparency and consistency for customers, Transmission 
Owners, Market Participants, and federal and state regulators. III.A through III.C 
describe the HILL Transmission Service Revisions, Section III.D describes the Non-
Transmission Facility Revisions, and Section III.E identifies the directives SPP is 
addressing in separate filings. 
 
Under FPA Section 205, SPP must show that its proposal is just and reasonable. 
It need not show that the proposal is the only reasonable approach or that it is more 
reasonable than the alternatives.36 As Commissioner LaCerte observed, FPA Section 
205 provides a “relatively capacious standard” under which the Commission may 
accept a broad range of proposals shown to be just and reasonable and not unduly 
discriminatory or preferential.37 The Large Load Revisions meet that standard. For each 
concern the Commission identified, SPP: 
 
1. places in the Tariff the binding obligation the Commission found missing; 
2. ties that obligation to an existing process the Commission has already accepted 
(the HDPS, the pro forma service agreements, the STEP, the Affected System 
Study framework, and the Attachment AE registration requirements) instead of 
creating a parallel process; and 
3. places implementation details, such as technology-specific evaluation methods 
and size- and impact-based study thresholds, in Business Practices that are 
developed and revised through SPP's stakeholder process.

This structure follows the Commission's rule of reason. Under that policy, a 
tariff must include practices that significantly affect rates, terms, and conditions of 
service, are realistically susceptible of specification, and are not so generally 
understood in contractual arrangements that stating them would be superfluous. 
Implementation details that do not meet that test may be placed in business practices.38 
The Large Load Revisions divide the material along that line. The Tariff sets out each

standard in the Show Cause Order itself, focusing on responsibilities that “may

(1)(a), First Component) 
 
 
1. 
Commission Concern 
 
The Commission preliminarily found that the Tariff lacks clear and consistent 
provisions requiring the evaluation of alternative transmission technologies as potential 
solutions to accommodate a request for transmission service on behalf of a large load.40

customers may bear a portion of those costs.41 The Commission directed SPP to address 
provisions that would (1) require the evaluation of alternative transmission 
technologies “in all instances, without the need for a request from the Eligible 
Customer”; and (2) if traditional Network Upgrades are selected, include in the study 
report a sufficiently clear demonstration of why those technologies are not feasible or
