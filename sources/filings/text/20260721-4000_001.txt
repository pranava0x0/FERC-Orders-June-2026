Pre-Conference Comments of 
Kelsey A. Bagot, Chair, Virginia State Corporation Commission 
 
The issues facing regional power grids, including the one operated by PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. 
(“PJM”), have become more complicated in recent years.  As the PJM stakeholder process has failed 
to drive meaningful and timely outcomes for the most critical of issues facing PJM, trust among (and 
between) stakeholders has eroded.  
There are three overarching concerns that challenge the ongoing efficacy of PJM as an independent 
regional transmission operator as compared to other RTOs/ISOs: 
(i) 
the PJM Board is not sufficiently independent, undermining stakeholder confidence in the 
impartiality and durability of Board decision-making;  
(ii) 
the PJM stakeholder process is prone to delay and gridlock, impeding PJM’s ability to 
take timely action or requiring PJM to pursue less-optimal solutions; and,  
(iii) 
State representatives feel their concerns are neither acknowledged nor sufficiently 
addressed by voting Members or PJM, resulting in states using blunt measures to force 
outcomes regardless of how disruptive they may be to the markets PJM operations or to 
achieving resource adequacy. 
Addressing these challenges requires solutions that enable PJM to engage in independent, 
comprehensive decision-making, with the benefit of all stakeholder input, and without creating more 
complexity or gridlock.  Reforms should be targeted, purposeful, and – where appropriate – iterative 
or incremental.  These objectives can be achieved by consideration of the following reforms, which 
fall within two broad, and interrelated, categories: (1) establishing greater PJM Board independence 
and (2) defining calibrated, appropriate roles for the States.   
1. 
Ensuring Board Independence  
States are not currently voting Members of PJM – an intentional decision made at the time of PJM’s 
creation.  However, the input of state regulators, like that of voting Members and all other 
stakeholders, serves an essential purpose: educating PJM and the PJM Board on the operational, 
financial, legal, and consumer trade-offs of action contemplated by the Board.  While Members 
naturally advance solutions that protect their interests, state elected officials and regulatory 
commissions are directly accountable to the retail consumers they are mandated to protect and are 
uniquely positioned to understand the challenges facing their particular jurisdictions.  Moreover, 
actions taken by PJM can be undermined or made less effective if states are unclear, unable, or 
unwilling to fully enable PJM’s intended outcomes.  
The appropriate system of governance would balance PJM’s access to the knowledge, experience, 
and innovation of all its stakeholders, including the States, on an advisory basis, against the authority 
of the Board to take action, independent from undue influence of any of its stakeholders. To get 
closer to this balance, given the present challenges, the following recommendations merit 
consideration:

• 
Section 205 Filing Rights: Section 205 filing authority (with certain limited exceptions, e.g., 
transmission rate-setting or cost allocation) should rest firmly with the PJM Board. The 
Board’s decision-making should be advised by outcomes of the stakeholder process but 
should not be constrained by it.  This will prevent procedural gridlock when decisive action is 
required and allow the Board to pursue the most optimal solutions without unnecessary legal 
hurdles.  Currently, the Board is often unable to move forward with critical reforms because 
one or more elements require changes to the Operating Agreement, requiring a degree of 
Members Committee support that is not always achievable.  Allowing the Board to tailor 
reforms, considering the guidance received from the stakeholder process but not beholden to 
specified stakeholder proposals, would enable the Board to act comprehensively and 
independently where it is now prevented from doing so.  
 
• 
Board Terms & Candidate Pool: Board members’ terms should be extended so the Board can 
engage in long(er)-term decision-making and benefit from institutional expertise and 
experience gained over time.  At the same time, prohibitions on serving consecutive terms 
should be considered to allow the Board to take independent action without regard toward 
potential renomination.  Additionally, at least one or more candidates with regulatory 
experience, preferably at the retail level, should be given preference to ensure that the Board 
and the actions it takes benefit from the perspective of an individual familiar with striking a 
balance that fairly accounts for the interests of the retail end-user.  Finally, preference should 
be given to candidates with professional experience in the PJM region.  
 
• 
Nominating Committee: State interests should be adequately represented on the Board 
Nominating Committee.  Specifically, the States should have the ability to appoint three new 
Nominating Committee members.  For a Board candidate to move forward, the candidate 
should be supported by at least two of the three new state-appointed members.  
 
2. 
Crafting Appropriate Roles for State Leadership and Regulators  
Reforms must recognize the distinct roles and perspectives of state elected officials (e.g., Governors) 
versus utility commissions (e.g., PUCs/OPSI).   
• 
State Elected Officials (Governors): Governors hold a distinct political mandate. Their input 
would be well leveraged, for example, through holding/appointing seats on the Nominating 
Committee and/or selecting state representation on the Board.  The Governors’ 
representatives should also have a seat on the Strategic Steering Committee discussed below.  
 
• 
State Utility Commissions & OPSI:  The States have long advocated for “jump-ball” 205 
filing rights over resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation issues at PJM.  Under 
the current regime, where the PJM Board lacks sufficient independence or authority to take 
timely action, Section 205 filing rights would enable OPSI to force action when state 
perspectives are not reflected in PJM proposals or when the PJM stakeholder process has 
stagnated.  To the extent concerns about Board independence and authority are adequately 
addressed, Section 205 filing right for OPSI may no longer be desirable or necessary. (This 
position differs from OPSI’s position, which views increased PJM Board independence and 
OPSI Section 205 filing rights as complementary reforms.)

That said, the durability and efficacy of many PJM outcomes require meaningful 
consideration of state perspectives.  As a result, the following additional reforms should be 
considered:  
- 
Strategic Steering Committee: OPSI (and potentially the Governors) should meet 
annually with the Board and other Members as the Strategic Steering Committee to 
collaborate on developing and approving PJM’s annual strategic plan. OPSI’s voice in 
this process should have equal weight as other Member representatives.  At present, the 
plan is developed without direct participation by OPSI. 
 
- 
Pre-205 Filing Consultation Requirement: To the extent such a process would not result 
in undue delays, PJM and the PJM Transmission Owners should be required to engage in 
a pre-filing consultation with OPSI to solicit OPSI feedback and collaboration.  This 
process should (i) provide adequate time for OPSI to review and provide meaningful 
feedback on any proposed filing, and (ii) require the filing entity (PJM/TOs) to explain in 
the body of the filing why it rejected any recommendations made by OPSI in the 
consultation process.  To hold OPSI accountable, any such process should be clearly 
defined and time-limited. 
- 
Non-Tariff Process Enhancements: Today, PJM is already enabled to improve state 
engagement through efforts that require no 205 filing at FERC to implement.  For 
example, PJM can serve as a technical resource to the States in working through 
proposed reforms.  State engagement processes should be standardized and more clearly 
defined to allow PJM to (i) help enable consensus among the States, where possible, (ii) 
elucidate areas where continued refinement of reforms might result in additional 
cooperative action among the States, and/or (iii) highlight where State policies create 
impediments to cooperative action.  Under new leadership, PJM has already 
demonstrated its increased commitment in working with OPSI and other state 
representatives.  Continued improvements should not be held up awaiting other potential 
governance reforms.
