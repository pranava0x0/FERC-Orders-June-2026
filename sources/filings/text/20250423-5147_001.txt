COMMENTS AND INTERVENTION OF THE  
DELAWARE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION 
 
The Delaware Public Service Commission (“DEPSC”) expresses its appreciation to the 
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission” or “FERC”) for instituting this show 
cause proceeding pursuant to Section 206 of the Federal Power Act (“FPA”). Many of the questions 
asked by the Commission are of particular interest to Delaware including, in particular, issues 
around maintenance of adequate transmission and generation necessary to ensure local and 
regional resource adequacy in light of the unprecedented increase in load forecasts related to 
mostly data centers and some other large load additions. The issue of co-located loads is front and 
center to the DEPSC’s interests in ensuring that the energy needs of Delaware are met. 
I. 
BACKGROUND AND CONTEXT 
More recent events in the PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) region give the DEPSC 
great concern, and the DEPSC agrees with the Commission that the status quo tariff provisions in
--- PAGE 3 ---
PJM will likely not maintain resource adequacy and reliability despite the best efforts of PJM.  
PJM is to be commended for seeking to accelerate its interconnection queue process for new 
generation, reforming its Surplus Interconnection Service (“SIS”) provisions, and implementing a 
Reliability Resource Initiative (“RRI”) to advance certain generation projects to enhance resource 
adequacy.  While all of these initiatives are likely to improve the ability of resources to connect to 
PJM’s system over the status quo, it is far from certain that resource adequacy will be maintained 
under current and future Base Residual Auction (“BRA”) timelines, given the unprecedented 
increases in demand related to co-located and non-co-located large loads, mainly new data centers. 
More specifically, PJM’s most recent 2025 forecast is projecting load increases averaging 
6 GW per year in the summer peaks from 2024 through 2032, and 6.5 GW per year in the winter 
peaks over the same forecast period.  This forecast growth is almost entirely related to the addition 
of data centers to the grid, with some other minor industrial and manufacturing load adjustments.  
In short, predominantly very large data center load forecast adjustments are driving unprecedented 
increases in PJM load growth forecasts. These are the types of facilities that are co-locating behind 
generation and are the focus of this proceeding. At the same time, PJM is projecting significant 
retirements of generation during this same forecast period. Between the period 2022 and 2030, 
PJM projected an average of approximately 4.4 GW per year of generation retirements. In order 
to maintain resource adequacy, PJM would then need to interconnect roughly 10.4 GWs of 
unforced capacity (“UCAP”) of new generation every year.  
As a reference point, in PJM’s last 2025/2026 BRA, PJM procured only 0.1103 GW of 
capacity from new generation and 0.7538 GW from uprates to existing or planned generation. The 
quantity of new generation is down from the previous BRA, in which only 0.3285 GW of new 
generation was procured.  The 2025/2026 BRA procurement results were low despite an increase
--- PAGE 4 ---
in the Rest-of-RTO capacity clearing price from $28.92/MW UCAP to $269.92/MW UCAP.  Most 
concerning, however, was that only 1.022 GW of Effective Load Carrying Capability (“ELCC”) 
adjusted MWs did not clear in the 2025/2026 BRA. Thus, there is almost no buffer to accommodate 
future load additions and generation retirements. New demand must be met from new generation 
and additional Demand Response (“DR”).  It should be noted that some recent PJM market reforms 
will provide some very temporary boosts to supply, because Reliability Must-Run (“RMR”) units1 
that meet certain criteria, and all units (including intermittent and battery units) with Capacity 
Interconnection Rights (“CIRs”), will now be required to offer their generation into the BRA. 
As PJM procures resources two or more BRAs out (Delivery Years 2027/2028 and later), 
PJM will need to rely on new generation resources, much of which will need to be in the current 
interconnection queue.  Based on PJM’s queue of “active projects” as of December 31, 2024, even 
if every “active project” comes online, new generation would still be insufficient to meet PJM’s 
forecasted increase in large load additions, even at today’s ELCC ratings.2  There is almost zero 
chance that all “active projects” will come on-line, as only a relatively small number of projects 
actually makes it to the post-construction, in-service phase.   
At recent OPSI meetings, many stakeholders identified barriers such as siting, permitting, 
and continuing supply chain constraints, particularly for large equipment like transformers and 
turbines, as key drivers in the delays and outright termination of interconnection queue positions.  
Further, it is unlikely that DR would fully meet any remaining resource adequacy gap, as the day-
to-day operational activation of DR would significantly increase, likely causing DR capacity 
market participation to decline. Further, this analysis assumes a fixed ELCC value for wind, solar,

outright unlikely, for the existing PJM queue, comprised mostly of new intermittent, limited 
duration resources, combined with future DR resources, to meet the growing demand associated 
with large load additions.  
II. 
COMMENTS 
In response to the Commission’s inquiry, the DEPSC expresses support for the Comments 
of OPSI in this proceeding as it relates to resource adequacy, transparency, consumer protection, 
cost allocation, and jurisdiction.  In addition, the DEPSC draws particular attention to the concerns 
around resource adequacy, underscored by the well-documented tightening of supply and future 
supply shortage conditions that are rapidly approaching. These tightening conditions should be 
addressed immediately, as the status quo tariff provisions fail to address the cumulative impacts of 
large load additions via co-located and non-collocated configurations. More to the point, as 
disruptive as it seems to subject new large load additions to further reliability analysis under 
conditions of resource adequacy shortages, the failure to adequately plan for and address these 
resource adequacy shortages will most certainly result in system load curtailments and extreme 
shortage prices, the impacts of which will be devasting to low and middle income customers, small 
businesses, and electricity price-sensitive industrial customers. To avoid these highly disruptive 
impacts, the DEPSC supports solutions that can effectively mitigate future resource adequacy 
shortages and operational challenges related to the real-time adequacy of energy and reserves.   
In its response to the Commission’s show cause Order of February 20, 2025, PJM outlined 
three potential options that focused acutely on additional supply and operational flexibility:  Option
--- PAGE 6 ---
6 (co-located load that elects to be Network Load and leverages provisional interconnection service 
or a State Agreement Approach), Option 7 (co-located load that takes a new non-capacity backed 
service that could be curtailed in advance of emergency procedures), and Option 8 (co-located 
participates as Demand Response).   The DEPSC is inclined to support these types of frameworks, 
provided that they can effectively mitigate the risk of resource adequacy shortages and sustained 
shortage pricing events.   
However, none of these solutions, on its own, may be adequate to address the fundamental 
problem of PJM and others reacting to large load additions rather than addressing large load 
additions proactively.  To date, many Electric Distribution Companies (“EDCs”) have enabled 
large load additions without ensuring that adequate on-site supply or adequate deliverable supply 
was available to meet the energy needs of the large load additions. Rather, the large load is being 
interconnected and permitted to go into service, forcing PJM, PJM stakeholders, this Commission, 
and others into a reactive mode, hurriedly scrambling to identify and implement solutions.  The 
proverbial cart appears to be dragging the proverbial horse.  Consequently, in addition to the PJM 
options that appear feasible, as identified above, the DEPSC would recommend that this 
Commission direct the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) to undertake 
an accelerated process to establish reliability and resource adequacy standards for new large load 
additions that would apply to Electric Distribution Companies (“EDCs”), to generators involved 
with co-located large load configurations, and other entities subject to NERC standards, to ensure 
that regional and local operational reliability and resource adequacy will be maintained as large 
load additions interconnect to the grid or interconnect in a co-located configuration. As it stands 
today, reliability and resource adequacy gaps exist in the current regulatory construct. To help fill 
this gap, the combination of this Commission and NERC could effectively empower and obligate
--- PAGE 7 ---
all relevant entities involved with large load interconnections to ensure either that large load 
additions are accompanied with their own adequate supply arrangements or that large load 
additions can be met with deliverable supply before the large load additions are placed into service.  
This Commission can and should put the horse back in front of the cart.

IV. 
CONCLUSION 
The DEPSC is very concerned about the tightening supply/demand balance and urges this 
Commission to require PJM to file provisions to ensure that resource adequacy and operational 
reliability are maintained in light of unprecedented large load additions, anticipated large 
generation retirements, and current new generation development barriers. These large load 
provisions should apply to both co-located and other large load additions. In addition, the 
Commission should direct NERC to establish standards to ensure that large load additions have 
access to their own supply or to deliverable supply as a condition to grid interconnection. A sense 
of urgency is necessary given the alignment of all these factors, and the DEPSC commends this 
Commission for proactively identifying these issues and seeking solutions to address them.
