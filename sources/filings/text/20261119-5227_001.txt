I. 
SUMMARY OF FILING  
The Show Cause Order commended SPP’s “bold and proactive steps” to 
integrate large loads. It recognized that SPP’s High Impact Large Load (“HILL”) 
process already provides an enhanced study framework for Eligible Customers taking 
transmission service on behalf of large loads.6 Notwithstanding this, the Commission 
preliminarily found “that SPP’s Tariff appears to be unjust and unreasonable because 
it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost shifting among transmission 
customers, which may result in unjust and unreasonable rates for transmission 
service.”7 The Commission expressed concern that there is no mechanism to help 
ensure that Eligible Customers taking service on behalf of large loads bear the risk and 
are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide transmission service, including 
the cost of Network Upgrades.8  
To address this concern, SPP proposes the LSHL for Network Upgrades needed 
to add or modify load delivery points studied under the Tariff. The LSHL mitigates the 
risk of cost shifting associated with the connection of large loads by requiring the 
Transmission Customer to pay, and to secure with financial security, all delivery point 
Network Upgrade costs above a threshold—creating an obligation that correlates 
directly with the level of cost resulting from the delivery point request and does not 
depend on whether, when, or at what level the load materializes. The LSHL preserves 
a defined level of rate support for Network Upgrades approved by SPP (i.e., “Base Plan 
funding”) for load delivery points while directly assigning costs above that level to the 
requesting Transmission Customer. Because the LSHL operates through the Tariff and 
SPP’s existing Commission-accepted cost allocation framework, it applies uniformly 
to every delivery point request, ties the customer’s obligation to the actual cost of the 
Network Upgrades identified in SPP’s studies, and preserves SPP’s existing regional

The Commission’s large load Show Cause Order emphasizes mitigation of the

sufficient financial security to reduce cost shifting risk when forecasted load does not 
develop as planned. This proposal directly addresses those concerns by retaining a basic 
level of load interconnection service that is funded by the SPP Region and directly 
assigning costs exceeding that level, backed by financial security, to the customer 
whose transmission service request causes the upgrades. This proposal establishes a 
direct link to cost causation, with the entity for whom the Network Upgrades were built 
bearing any excess costs.  
The proposed limit of $500,000 per MW is informed by two data sets, which 
are attached to this transmittal letter as Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 2. Exhibit 1 is an affidavit 
from Natasha Henderson with an attached spreadsheet outlining the upgrade costs 
resulting from Notifications to Construct (“NTCs”) that have been issued by SPP 
through its delivery point study processes from 2012 to 2026, which includes for each 
load request (1) the load amount; (2) the total cost of the Network Upgrades identified; 
(3) the cost per MW of load requested; (4) the approximate date when the Network 
Upgrades are put into service; and (5) the cost per MW percentile compared to the other 
requests included on Exhibit 1. Exhibit 2 is an affidavit from Don Frerking with an 
attached spreadsheet that includes transmission gross and net plant values, both in total 
and on a per-MW basis, for the SPP Region. The transmission plant per-MW values 
were derived from: (1) gross and net plant values in individual Transmission Owner 
formula rate template updates (effective as of August 2026); and (2) transmission 
revenue requirement and zonal load information sourced from the August 2026 
Revenue Requirements and Rates (“RRR”) file.54 
Historical delivery point studies requiring Network Upgrades showed 
approximate costs of $177,000 per MW at the median, $378,000 per MW at the 75th 
percentile, and $1.018 million per MW at the 95th percentile. The estimated investment 
in transmission facilities across the entire SPP Region is approximately $422,000 per 
MW on a net plant basis (reduced for depreciation) and $572,000 per MW on a gross 
plant basis. The value of the proposed per-MW limit falls between those system-wide 
investment measures and between the historical 75th and 95th percentiles for delivery 
point requests. The percentiles from this history of requests that required Network 
Upgrades suggest that the majority of future delivery point requests requiring such 
upgrades will remain under the proposed per-MW cost cap, thereby resulting in full 
Base Plan funding at an average cost level for delivery points which are not HILLs. 
Also, it should be noted that a large majority of delivery point requests do not require 
issuance of a Notification to Construct for any Network Upgrade. In addition to 
protecting the region from bearing excess costs associated with load interconnection 
requests, the proposed per-MW limit provides an incentive for the requesting 
Transmission Customer to select cost effective interconnection locations, which is

ensures that the focus is on mitigation of cost risk. An aggregate cap is needed because

1,000 MW request could qualify for $500 million of Base Plan funding if there were 
no aggregate cap. Further, a per-MW limit without an aggregate limit could incentivize 
the submission of inflated delivery point load amounts in some situations.

The proposed LSHL results in a division of cost responsibility for the delivery 
point end of a transmission service path between a portion subject to regional cost 
allocation and a portion directly assigned to the requesting Transmission Customer. 
This is generally consistent with the Tariff’s current treatment of the resource end of 
the transmission service path (i.e., the existing Safe Harbor Provisions), which 
incorporates a division of cost responsibility between a portion subject to regional cost 
allocation and a portion assigned to the requesting Transmission Customer by means 
of the Safe Harbor Cost Limit and other qualifying criteria for regional funding as set 
forth in Section III.B of Attachment J. This is a means to recognize the benefits that 
Network Upgrades can provide to other Transmission Customers in the Zone, 
Subregion, or even across the SPP Region while still applying the cost-causation 
principle through direct assignment of any cost above the LSHL.   
Those Transmission Customers who bear directly assigned costs for delivery 
point Network Upgrades as a result of the Load Safe Harbor Limit will be eligible for 
compensation through allocation of Candidate Incremental Long-Term Congestion 
Rights, as provided under Attachment Z2 of the Tariff.     
C. 
The Load Safe Harbor Limit Resolves the Show Cause Order’s Cost 
Recovery Directive 
As required by the Show Cause Order, the proposed LSHL specifically 
mitigates the cost risks to existing Transmission Customers by requiring a minimum 
contribution by Transmission Customers taking transmission service on behalf of the 
large loads that impose costs above the LSHL. The per-MW component of the proposed 
LSHL is based on the level of SPP transmission service that is requested by the 
Transmission Customer and the aggregate cap component of the proposed LSHL is 
based on an amount corresponding to the 50 MW threshold for a HILL. As a result, the

Page 14 
 
amount of potential contribution for large loads varies directly with the cost created by 
the load request, a cost level which is driven primarily by the MW amount requested.58 
The proposed framework also requires Transmission Customers taking transmission 
service on behalf of large loads to  provide a sufficient contribution to ensure that large 
loads interconnecting to the SPP Transmission System are not unduly burdening other 
Transmission Customers with costs caused by such interconnection.59 Lastly, the Load 
Safe Harbor Limit Revisions require the Transmission Customer taking service on 
behalf of a large load to provide sufficient security to back its obligation to pay the 
amounts that are owed.60 The directly assigned obligation is a fixed payment that does 
not depend on whether, when, or at what level the load is energized. Payments begin 
when the Transmission Owner establishes a revenue requirement for the upgrade, 
addressing the timing concern in Paragraph 65 of the Show Cause Order; and directly 
assigned amounts are excluded from the Base Plan Upgrade revenue requirements 
charged to other customers. This is the kind of “other just and reasonable method[]” 
the Commission invited.61 
Under FPA Section 205, SPP must show that its proposal is just and reasonable. 
It need not show that the proposal is the only reasonable approach or that it is more 
reasonable than the alternatives.62 The LSHL meets this burden. As described above, 
the LSHL provides for a just and reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory, approach 
for satisfying the Commission’s concern outlined in the Show Cause Order, and the 
Commission should accept it.
