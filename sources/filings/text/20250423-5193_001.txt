NREMC appreciates the Commission’s initiative in launching this inquiry and commends

I. 
INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND 
The Show Cause Order is the culmination of multiple proceedings focused on the issue of 
co-location. The Commission consolidated this new proceeding, Docket No. EL25-49-000, with 
Docket Nos. AD 24-11-000 and EL25-20-000.3 Notably, the Show Cause Order is specific and 
limited to PJM. In its Show Cause Order, the Commission directed the respondents to:  
(1) show cause as to why the [Open Access Transmission Tariff], the Amended 
and Restated Operating Agreement of PJM, and Reliability Assurance Agreement 
Among Load Serving Entities in the PJM Region [(collectively, the “PJM 
Tariff”)] remains just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential 
without provisions addressing with sufficient clarity or consistency the rates, 
terms, and conditions of service that apply to co-location arrangements; or  
 
(2) explain what changes to the Tariff would remedy the identified concerns if the 
Commission were to determine that the Tariff has in fact become unjust and 
unreasonable or unduly discriminatory or preferential and, therefore, proceeds to 
establish a replacement Tariff.4 
 
The Commission then invited interested entities to address either or both of: “(1) whether the 
Tariff remains just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential; and (2) if not, 
what changes to the Tariff should be implemented as a replacement rate.”5 
NREMC provides these comments from the perspective of a non-profit, member-owned 
electric distribution utility organized and existing pursuant to the laws of the State of Indiana

II. 
COMMENTS 
A. Existing Tariff Framework is Sufficient 
NREMC agrees with PJM the Indicated TOs that co-location can be effectively 
accommodated within the existing PJM Tariff through established transmission service structures 
– as Network Load or Point to Point Service.8 PJM outlined three options currently available 
under the Tariff for serving co-located load: (1) Option 1 – Load with a separate point of 
interconnection that is Network Load; (2) Option 2 – Co-located load that is at the same point of 
interconnection but separately metered that is Network Load; and (3) Option 3 – Existing Behind 
the Meter Generation where load is the customer.9 Under both existing Options 1 and 2, the load 
uses and pays for transmission services, ancillary services, energy, and capacity.10

However, Option 3 was not designed to accommodate large-scale loads. The existing 
Non-Retail Behind the Meter Generation (“BTMG”) rules are capped annually, as these 
resources do not maintain reserves equivalent to the installed reserve margin.11 Large co-located 
loads, such as data centers paired with behind the meter generation, present significantly greater 
operational risks than traditional BTMG arrangements because they lack adequate reserves.12 
Furthermore, this arrangement would require these loads to co-locate with generation through the 
same point of interconnection (unless an EDC consented to serve) thereby concentrating large 
amounts of risk from both the load and generation sides of the meter.13  In emergencies or 
outages, their reliance on the grid could severely impact resource adequacy and system 
reliability.14 PJM correctly noted that the BTMG model does not apply well to co-location 
scenarios involving large loads.15 It was originally developed for much smaller and distributed 
installations – not for high consumption, single point facilities like data centers.16 Additionally, 
simple netting of generation against load could allow these facilities to avoid most, if not all, 
costs by netting out to zero as the entire load is supplied by the co-located generator, despite the 
co-located loads reliance and benefit from the grid.17 This would shift costs and burdens unfairly 
to other users, violating core principles of just and reasonable ratemaking. 
Nevertheless, viable and fair options exist under the current Tariff for co-located load as 
Network Load in Options 1 or 2 and Point to Point Service. The PJM Tariff continues to be just 
and reasonable as it requires all load – regardless of usage consistency – to pay for grid access

and services, in accordance with Order No. 888 and the Federal Power Act.18 A fundamental 
ratemaking principle establishes that “the costs assessed against a party must bear some 
resemblance ‘to the burdens imposed or benefits drawn by that party.’”19 Maintaining the 
existing Tariff provisions for co-located load to interconnect as Network Load or Point to Point 
Service ensures that costs are equitably shared in line with this principle.20 Creating carve-outs 
that exempt co-located load from standard transmission charges would undermine this principle 
and unlawfully shift costs onto other users – an outcome that would be unjust and 
unreasonable.21  
NREMC fully supports the Indicated TOs’ position that the PJM Tariff should apply to all 
who seek transmission service within the region.22 This ensures that every customer contributes 
fairly to the shared infrastructure and services from which they benefit.23 Co-location 
arrangements can be readily accommodated within the existing Tariff framework—either through 
retail service from a local distribution company or by registering as a Network Customer in PJM 
(where permitted by state jurisdiction).24 In either case, the co-located load is treated like all 
other users and pays its fair share.25

range of Transmission and Ancillary Services. This remains true even when the load is only 
connected via the distribution system, since both transmission and distribution operators depend 
on ancillary services to maintain grid reliability, stability, and power quality.27 Essential services

physical and synchronous connection to the grid.28 Given this reliance, NREMC co-located load 
should be designated as Network Load and be responsible for its share of transmission and 
ancillary service costs. The current PJM Tariff appropriately reflects this framework and remains 
just and reasonable. 
C. Co-located Load Should be Treated as Network Load & Pay Fair Share 
Even if a load connects to the grid indirectly—such as through facilities connecting a 
generator to the grid—it still requires the ability to be served from the transmission network, as 
stated by the Indicated TOs.29 As a result, the transmission system must be planned, built, and 
maintained to accommodate and support that load.30 The intermittent nature of co-located load 
does not lessen the need for ongoing investment in infrastructure or the operational costs 
incurred by transmission owners to ensure reliability.31 Because co-located load is interconnected 
and relies on the transmission system—it both uses and benefits from it, NREMC agrees with 
PJM that co-located load must contribute its appropriate share of the associated costs.32

III. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, NREMC respectfully requests that the Commission find the 
current PJM Tariff is just, reasonable, and non-discriminatory or preferential as it already 
accommodates co-location arrangements through Network Service and Point to Point Service, 
which uphold equitable cost allocation for the transmission and grid services co-location 
arrangements benefit from.
