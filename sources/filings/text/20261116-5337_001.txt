I. 
BACKGROUND  
A. Horizon West 
 
Horizon West is a Non-LSE PTO in the CAISO region.  Horizon West is a public utility 
and wholly owned direct subsidiary of NextEra Energy Transmission, LLC, formed in 2014 to 
develop, construct, finance, own, operate, and maintain electric transmission facilities in the 
CAISO region.  Horizon West currently owns and operates the 230 kV +300/100 MVAr Suncrest 
Reactive Power Project, which is designed to accommodate reactive power for high-voltage 
transmission.  The Estrella Substation Project, a new 230/70/12 kV substation, new 230/70 kV 
transformers, and reconductoring and looping at the existing transmission line are still under 
development.  More recently, Horizon West was selected by CAISO to fund, develop, construct, 
own, and operate: (1) the North Gila—Imperial Valley #2 500 kV Line Project, a new, roughly 
80-mile 500 kV transmission line spanning a portion of Yuma County, Arizona and southern 
Imperial County designed to improve access to renewable resources for customers in Southern 
California; and (2) the proposed Imperial Valley—North of San Onofre Nuclear Generating 
Station 500 kV Line and 500/230 kV Substation Project, consisting of a 140-mile 500 kV 
transmission line and related substation, which was identified as necessary to meet the future 
needs of the region’s electric grid.  Horizon West recovers its transmission revenue requirement 
(“TRR”) through a Commission-approved formula rate under the Horizon West Tariff, which is 
invoiced and collected through the CAISO Access Charge rates under Section 26 of the CAISO 
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“CAISO Tariff”).  As a Non-LSE PTO under the CAISO Tariff 
and Transmission Control Agreement,7 Horizon West serves no retail load.8

A. Large Load Interconnection Process 
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission made a preliminary finding that the CAISO 
Tariff and PTO Tariffs appear to be unjust and unreasonable because they lack clear and 
consistent provisions with respect to the application and study process for Eligible Customers 
seeking transmission and interconnection service on behalf of Large Loads.17  The Horizon West 
Tariff establishes, in Sections 8.1 and 10, a defined and time-limited interconnection process for 
Large Loads with specific application, readiness, study, and operational requirements, and it 
clarifies how a Large Load interconnected to Horizon West’s facilities obtains transmission 
service over the CAISO Controlled Grid.  Elements of this proposal include the following tariff 
improvements for the integration of Large Loads. 
1. 
Definition of Large Load   
Commission Concerns:  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission made a preliminary 
finding that the CAISO Tariff and PTO Tariffs appear to be unjust and unreasonable because they 
lack a definition of Large Load sufficient to capture loads that present the unique challenges 
described in the Show Cause Order.18 
Horizon West Solution:  In response to the Commission’s finding, Horizon West 
proposes to define a Large Load as “[a]n End-User located at a single site behind one or more 
Points of Interconnection to the CAISO Controlled Grid, and that has a peak load of 50 MW or 
greater.”19 This definition is identical to the definition CAISO proposes to add to Appendix A of 
the CAISO Tariff, and adopts the 50 MW threshold the Commission identified as reasonable.20

2. 
Application and Study Procedures 
Commission Concerns:  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission made a preliminary 
finding that the CAISO Tariff and PTO Tariffs appear to be unjust and unreasonable because they 
lack clear and consistent provisions addressing the application process and study procedures for 
Eligible Customers to request transmission service on behalf of Large Loads.21  The Commission 
indicated that such processes should clarify that applications be accepted on a rolling basis; 
require a non-refundable application fee as well as staged readiness requirements; and specify 
the information and data requirements to be submitted with an application.22 
Horizon West Solution:  The proposed tariff revisions leverage Horizon West’s existing 
application and study processes while identifying the additional procedures applicable to Large 
Loads due to their unique operational requirements and reliability impacts on the transmission 
system.  Horizon West submits that this approach promotes transparency and administrative 
efficiency by providing a single, integrated framework for Large Load interconnection requests 
under the Horizon West Tariff.  This is responsive to the Show Cause Order’s concern regarding 
the lack of specificity for Large Loads and their unique operational characteristics and 
requirements. 
Horizon West’s proposed tariff revisions establish the applicable provisions for Eligible 
Customers requesting service on behalf of Large Loads (as defined in the Horizon West Tariff).  
First, revised Section 8.1 explicitly extends Horizon West’s obligation to interconnect to entities 
eligible to request interconnection to pursuant to FPA section 210(a) and Eligible Customers, 
including Large Loads owned or served by a third party.  Because Horizon West is a Non-LSE 
PTO with no retail customers, a Large Load connecting directly to Horizon West’s facilities may 
not qualify as an “Eligible Customer” in certain instances.  Horizon West therefore proposes, in 
new Section 10.2, a voluntary offer of unbundled retail transmission service, where consistent 
with state law, so that Large Loads can request Interconnection and be studied under the Horizon 
West Tariff as the Show Cause Order contemplates.23 Second, revised Section 10.1 requires that 
a Large Load Interconnection be requested pursuant to Section 10 of the Horizon West Tariff and 
comply with Section 25.7 of the CAISO Tariff.24  Horizon West affirms that under the Horizon 
West Tariff, applications are accepted on a rolling basis, and revised Section 10.4 requires 
Horizon West to timestamp each Large Load Interconnection Application to establish study 
priority on a rolling basis.

Study Process Timeline 
Commission Concerns:  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission made a preliminary 
finding that the study processes could culminate in a report to the Eligible Customer describing

good-faith estimate of the cost and the time to construct such facilities, which should take no

Horizon West Solution:  Horizon West proposes the adoption of revised Section 10.12 
and 10.12.1 to its tariff requiring that a System Impact Study for a Large Load provide a non-
binding, good-faith estimate of the cost of Direct Assignment Facilities and Network Upgrades 
needed to accommodate the request and a similar good-faith time estimate required to construct 
them.  Following the System Impact Study, pursuant to Section 10.16 the Facilities Study will 
refine those estimates leading into the Interconnection Agreement process.40  The report will be 
available following the System Impact Study, consistent with the Show Cause Order directive.  
Horizon West also recognizes the need for timely progression of required studies.  
Accordingly, the proposed Horizon West Tariff revisions shorten the timeline for interconnection 
studies at each step of the process to the maximum extent achievable (Sections 10.8 - 10.12).  
Under the proposed Large Load study procedures, Horizon West must notify the applicant 
whether a System Impact Study is required within ten (10) Business Days of the Completed 
Application Date and must tender a System Impact Study Agreement within ten (10) Business 
Days of receipt of the Completed Interconnection Application.  The applicant must execute that 
agreement within ten (10) Business Days and, for a Large Load Interconnection, remit a study 
deposit together with financial security drawable to cover System Impact Study costs and, if 
necessary, Facilities Study costs.  Horizon West must complete the System Impact Study no later 
than ninety (90) Calendar Days after the Completed Application Date.  This 90-day timeline runs 
from the Completed Application Date, not from execution of the System Impact Study 
Agreement, and therefore includes the time for the System Impact Study Agreement and deposit 
steps.  The result of this proposed schedule is the initiation and completion of a System Impact 
Study within the 60-90 calendar day range that the Commission identified as reasonable.

Revenue Requirement.  This ensures the Eligible Customer pays the full costs of their Network 
Upgrades.  Horizon West will provide a separate workpaper in the informational filing detailing 
the accounting treatment and crediting for the Annual Required Revenue Commitment.
