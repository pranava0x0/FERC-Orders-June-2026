INTRODUCTION 
 
The unprecedented number of new and forecasted large load additions, mostly data 
centers, in the PJM footprint has outstripped new supply, and PJM’s capacity auctions have 
cleared short of the region’s reliability requirement for two consecutive auctions despite hitting 
auction price caps in both auctions.3 These shortfalls increase the risk of loss-of-load events and 
sustained high-capacity prices for existing ratepayers who are not responsible for these new 
shortfalls. Moreover, PJM expects that capacity shortfalls may continue due to the rapid growth 
of data centers and other large loads that PJM cannot currently serve reliably.  
The IRAS Filing is an attempt by PJM to ensure that future shortfalls do not create 
reliability risks and to avoid shifting costs to residential or other existing ratepayers. PJM 
proposes four changes to its governing documents. First, PJM will require that a Load Serving 
Entity (“LSE”) must bring new capacity to offset or exceed the amount of peak load from new 
large loads.4 If an LSE fails to bring such new capacity, it will be subject to load reduction 
requirements prior to Pre-Emergency Load Management Response.5 Second, PJM will establish 
a Large Load Registry, which will allow PJM to identify how much new capacity an LSE is 
required to bring to offset new large loads.6 Third, PJM will define Large Loads, New Large 
Loads, and Eligible New Large Loads.7 Fourth, PJM will exclude New Large Loads that do not 
Bring Your Own New Capacity (“BYONC”) from the Variable Resource Requirement (“VRR”)

PJM’s consent to modification is a further signal that it is committed to the most effective 
IRAS Filing possible. Rather than present a take-it-or-leave-it package, PJM “consents to the 
Commission’s exercise of authority to modify and/or sever any element of the proposed Tariff 
and RAA language” so long as any modification “is consistent with PJM’s overarching 
objectives in this filing.”13 PJM invites stakeholders to “comment as they feel necessary and

modifications that the Commission can prescribe in an order approving the IRAS Filing. 
PJM’s consent is bounded by its “overarching objectives in this filing,” yet the IRAS 
Filing never explicitly defines the objectives.15 Fairly read, however, the IRAS Filing pursues 
three objectives: 
1. Promote reliability by requiring new large loads to be curtailable until they bring new 
capacity to meet their needs. 
2. Promote affordability by requiring new large loads to pay for their own new capacity 
and avoid shifting costs to residential and other existing ratepayers. 
3. Provide states and LSEs with sufficient information to ensure that new large loads, 
and not existing customers, are curtailed when new large loads fail to BYONC and 
that new large loads pay for their own new capacity. 
The IRAS Filing fails to fully satisfy the first two objectives. This limited protest 
identifies four narrow but important modifications that will help PJM advance these objectives 
with the IRAS Filing.

First, the IRAS Filing allows resources that do not BYONC to exit IRAS without first 
bringing new generation. The IRAS program “effectively goes to sleep” after three consecutive 
delivery years of meeting PJM’s Reliability Requirement, even though the Reliability 
Requirement excludes load subject to IRAS.16 PIOs propose that the Commission either require 
PJM to never put the IRAS program to sleep, or else to clarify that the trigger for the IRAS 
program being put to sleep is the capacity market clearing for three consecutive years with 
sufficient supply to meet all demand including new large loads subject to IRAS . 
Second, the IRAS Filing does not have any penalty provisions for non-compliance with 
IRAS curtailment requirements. The Commission should require PJM to create a penalty applied 
to LSEs that fail to reduce load when PJM makes an IRAS Action and require PJM to provide 
LSEs and states with sufficient information to enable retail regulators to ensure that any such 
penalties are borne by loads that fail to curtail as instructed, rather than by other consumers.  
Third, the IRAS Filing provides compensation to unmatched large loads that curtail under 
the IRAS program. And the IRAS filing lacks a cap on the total amount of compensation IRAS 
resources may receive in the aggregate.17 The Commission should remove this compensation 
because the ability to pursue speedy interconnection prior to bringing their own capacity 
resources already provides IRAS resources with sufficient consideration for their commitment to 
curtail when required. Moreover, because the purpose of the proposed compensation for IRAS 
Actions is to make up for any capacity bills that large loads may still be required to pay, the 
Commission should require PJM to cap the compensation at no more than the amount the

Fourth, the IRAS Filing does not include a locational requirement for the new BYONC

and they fall squarely within PJM’s consent. These modifications will help the IRAS Filing 
ensure that PJM maintains reliability without imposing undue costs on residential and other 
existing ratepayers.

PJM is facing resource adequacy and affordability concerns as it struggles to meet 
data center load growth. 
 
PJM’s capacity market, the RPM, worked relatively well at assuring resource adequacy at

I. 
The IRAS Framework is just and reasonable and not unduly discriminatory. 
Overall, the IRAS proposal presents a just and reasonable approach to protect reliability 
and fairly allocate costs among PJM customers and zones. Without the IRAS proposal, PJM 
customers will be doomed to bear ever-rising costs and degrading reliability as large loads 
consistently outpace new supply. This reality warrants new rules. The IRAS framework is an 
essential course-correction that will help rectify years of public subsidization of large load

PIOs strongly support the overarching IRAS framework.  
As described above, the IRAS Filing has distinct components which all must operate 
together to form a comprehensive solution. PJM proposed to establish a Large Load Registry, 
require LSEs to ensure that New Large Loads BYONC, implement IRAS load reduction requests 
prior to Pre-Emergency Load Management Response, and remove New Large Loads that do not 
BYONC from the VRR Curve in the capacity auction. These comments will refer to New Large 
Loads that do not BYONC as “unmatched large load.” The interplay between these components 
is essential to the function of the IRAS proposal. Together, these components can be referred to 
as the “IRAS Framework.” 
The IRAS Framework is not unduly discriminatory because it recognizes that large load 
customers are differently situated from other customers due to their outsized impacts on other 
customers’ costs, resource adequacy, and reliability risks. Similarly, the IRAS Framework is 
jurisdictionally sound because it respects the roles of PJM, states, end-use customers, and 
Electric Distributors (“EDs”) outlined in the Federal Power Act. Finally, the IRAS Framework 
ensures just and reasonable rates by removing unmatched large loads from the capacity auction, 
ensuring that new large loads are responsible for their own capacity costs.

Large load customers are differently situated from other customers. 
Large load customers are differently situated than traditional residential and commercial 
customers and warrant new rules and obligations. The IRAS Filing correctly recognizes this 
distinction and applies new responsibilities to large loads, through their EDs, that will protect 
other customers from unjust costs and reliability risks.

C. 
Removing New Large Loads from RPM is just, reasonable, and not unduly 
discriminatory. 
 
Removing New Large Loads that do not BYONC from the VRR Curve in RPM is the 
lynchpin of the IRAS filing and is essential to comply with cost-causation principles.69 This 
provision is an urgent course-correction after three consecutive auctions that resulted in 
degrading reliability margins and massive public subsidies for power needed to serve large loads. 
The IRAS Framework does not undermine market fundamentals; rather, it correctly recognizes 
that one single customer class is driving unjust and unreasonable outcomes, and suggests fair 
changes to preserve competitive results for all ratepayers in PJM. Similarly, the provision does 
not block market access for large load customers—it simply clarifies commercial conditions. It is 
clear beyond reasonable doubt that, without this solution, the trend of high clearing prices, cross-
subsidization, and degrading reliability would continue for the foreseeable future.  
Removing new large loads that do not BYONC from the VRR Curve observes principles 
of cost causation because it prevents the RPM from forcing other ratepayers to subsidize  
capacity on behalf of new large loads. As described in the previous section, large loads have 
increased the clearing price for the last three consecutive capacity auctions as they outpace new 
supply. These clearing prices are spread across all customers and resulted in significantly higher 
utility bills for residential and commercial customers.70 Leaving unmatched large loads in the

III. 
Modification 1: The Commission must ensure that the IRAS filing effectively 
removes new large loads from the capacity market until associated new capacity is 
online and deliverable. 
 
PJM proposes to suspend the entire IRAS Framework if the PJM capacity auction meets 
the Reliability Requirement—not including large loads subject to IRAS—for three consecutive 
delivery years, but this proposal risks creating a volatile market that traps prices at the cap and 
invites gaming by large load customers. If this risk occurs, PJM will treat all customers the same: 
there will be no differentiation between large loads and other customers; no customers will have 
an obligation to demonstrate BYONC; there will be no IRAS load; and all load will be included 
in the demand curve for the capacity auction.100 In fact, even the definition of New Large Load 
and BYONC—the foundation of the IRAS Framework—will be ineffective as long as the IRAS

B. 
Cost Causation Principles Support a Zero-Dollar Rate or a Capacity-Charge 
Credit. 
 
The Federal Power Act’s just-and-reasonable standard incorporates cost causation. Rates 
must be roughly commensurate with the costs imposed or the benefits received by any given 
ratepayer.141 PJM’s proposed default rate does not satisfy that standard. 
The customers—including residential customers—who would be charged to pay large 
loads under the IRAS process did not cause the capacity need associated with an unmatched 
large load’s decision to interconnect before bringing online the capacity it requires. Nor does the 
curtailment transform the load into a new capacity resource that those customers procured. 
Existing customers already pay for reliable service through the RPM. IRAS curtailment does not 
provide existing customers with a service, it simply restores the reliability that would exist 
absent the demand from unmatched large loads. 
Nor can it be said that customers who must pay the Eligible New Large Loads for 
curtailing receive a benefit for not being curtailed themselves. Forcing them to pay for this 
putative benefit is more akin to the extortion of a mob protection racket than receiving security 
from an outside threat. Even if the Commission characterizes emergency avoidance through a 
pre-emergency calling of Eligible New Large Loads as a regional benefit, PJM must show that 
the resulting charges are roughly commensurate with that benefit, which it has not. PJM has 
neither substantiated the need for the charge nor explained how it should be calculated and 
assessed that it is roughly commensurate with the alleged benefits received. PJM’s failure to 
make that showing supports a zero-dollar IRAS rate. PJM has not established that a capacity

VI. 
Modification 4: The Commission should require BYONC supply to be fully 
deliverable to new large loads by considering transmission limits between zones.  
 
PJM’s proposed BYONC requirements do not consider the location of the New Large 
Load nor the associated supply. This jeopardizes reliability and risks socializing costs to other 
customers in violation of the objectives of the IRAS Framework. Without a locational 
requirement, PJM risks relying on new capacity that will not actually be able to serve the zone in 
which the sponsoring new large load resides, thus failing to achieve reliability objectives.149 
Similarly, allowing distant load and supply arrangements could result in significant regional 
transmission needs, which shift costs to other ratepayers, failing objective 2. Because PJM’s 
capacity and energy markets recognize locational constraints, new supply that is transmission 
constrained will not deliver cost relief. The Commission should direct PJM to require BYONC to 
be located only in zones that are not constrained with respect to the zone in which the new large 
load resides.

CONCLUSION 
 
PJM’s IRAS Filing generally presents a just and reasonable mechanism to ensure large 
loads are responsible for their own new capacity and protect other customers from unintended 
reliability consequences while respecting state jurisdiction over retail rates. However, to ensure 
that the IRAS Filing achieves its objectives, the Commission must make the modifications 
described above to: (1) ensure IRAS and BYONC requirements are permanent for all new large 
loads or else clarify that the trigger for turning IRAS off requires the capacity market to clear for 
three consecutive years with sufficient supply to meet all demand including all large loads 
subject to IRAS; (2) require penalties for LSEs that fail to comply with IRAS Actions; (3) either 
eliminate compensation for IRAS resource curtailment or require PJM to cap the compensation 
awarded to IRAS loads at no more than the amount the new large load paid in capacity charges 
for the relevant delivery year; and (4) require BYONC supply to be fully deliverable to new large 
loads by considering transmission limits between zones. Accordingly, PIOs 
respectfully request that the Commission approve the IRAS Filing with the 
modifications described above.
