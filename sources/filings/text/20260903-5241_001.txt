I.
INTRODUCTION 
On August 13, 2026, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) submitted revisions to the PJM 
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among 
Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) for a proposed Interim Resource Adequacy Service (“IRAS”). 
IRAS, as described by PJM, is a “new supplemental capacity product”1 through which certain New 
Large Loads may be directed to reduce their load when PJM determines necessary to address 
potential capacity emergencies or Interconnection Reliability Operating Limits (“IROL”) 
violations. New Large Loads that do not have sufficient capacity of their own will qualify as 
Eligible New Large Loads under IRAS.  
The IRAS filing also includes the following “foundational revisions”2 in order to facilitate 
IRAS: (1) clarification in the RAA that load serving entities (“LSEs”) are obligated to bring 
sufficient new capacity to cover New Large Loads or be subject to load reductions during 
Emergencies;3 (2) establishment of a Large Load Registry that PJM will maintain based on 
information to be provided by LSEs and Electric Distributors;4 and (3) revisions to the Tariff and 
RAA to include definitions for Large Load and New Large Load. Finally, PJM proposes, beginning 
with the 2029/20230 Delivery Year, to exclude certain New Large Loads from the Variable

elements of the IRAS proposal require further clarification or explanation, including the definition 
of New Large Load, compensation for IRAS load reductions, and the treatment of Bring Your 
Own New Capacity (“BYONC”).

II.
COMMENTS 
A. PJM’s proposed Large Load Registry requires LSEs to provide information they 
might not be able to obtain and might warrant enhanced confidentiality 
protection.  
PJM proposes to establish a Large Load Registry that will be used to facilitate 
implementation of IRAS, among other purposes.9 The Large Load Registry is a key component of 
PJM’s IRAS proposal, since the Large Load Registry will be used by PJM “to determine the 
quantity of New Large Loads by zone/area and allocate IRAS responsibility to each zone/area 
accordingly . . .”10 ODEC agrees with the concept of a Large Load Registry to provide transparency 
and consistent, verified information regarding New Large Loads. ODEC also agrees that the Large 
Load Registry is generally consistent with the Commission’s June 2026 Order to Show Cause 
regarding large loads, where the Commission determined that PJM’s Tariff appeared to be unjust 
and unreasonable absent provisions “that would require PJM and the Transmission Owners to 
publicly post and regularly update data that details (1) aggregate amounts of proposed large load 
additions in PJM’s footprint. . .”11
As an initial matter, PJM should clarify whether its proposed RAA revisions would require 
LSEs to provide the Large Load Registry information. The IRAS transmittal letter states that “PJM 
is proposing that LSEs submit information to PJM about the Large Loads they serve”12 and that 
PJM may obtain information from Electric Distributors and Transmission Owners. PJM also states 
that it proposes to hold LSEs primarily responsible for providing information for the Large Load

In addition to these requirements, PJM proposes to require LSEs to promptly inform PJM 
of any change in the above-mentioned information.21 Here again, LSEs may not receive such 
notification and may not have ready access to changes in the required information. The obligation 
and risk imposed on LSEs by PJM’s proposal is significant. Not only does PJM place the 
responsibility on LSEs to ensure aspects of PJM’s IRAS that are foundational to the success of 
IRAS,22 but PJM emphasizes in its filing that LSEs’ reporting of information for the Large Load 
Registry subjects LSEs to the full extent of the Commission’s enforcement authority for the duty 
of candor and the prohibition against false or misleading statements.23 To the extent LSEs are 
acting in good faith and simply unable to provide information that they cannot obtain, LSEs should 
not be subject to the threat of an enforcement action. While PJM’s need for complete and accurate 
information is justifiable, placing the full burden on LSEs to ensure access to information that they 
simply may not be able to obtain, under threat of enforcement actions, is not. Instead, PJM should 
change its RAA to (1) require LSEs to provide the information that they are able to obtain with 
reasonable efforts or due diligence; (2) require LSEs to promptly identify information that they are 
not able to obtain; and (3) change the provisions regarding PJM’s possible coordination with 
Transmission Owners and Electric Distributors as needed.24

B. PJM should revise its IRAS load reduction framework. 
PJM proposes to require Electric Distributors to execute PJM’s directives to reduce load 
during Interim Resource Adequacy Service Actions.25 PJM’s IRAS does not consider how the load 
reductions will need to be carried out.26 Electric Distributors may not have the necessary 
equipment on site with all New Large Loads in order to require the load reductions and/or verify 
the load reductions are executed as directed. For example, where colocated loads are served behind 
a generator’s point of interconnection. Therefore, while having the Electric Distributor responsible 
might provide the necessary system stabilization, it will not be possible where the Electric 
Distributor does not have equipment to execute the load reduction. An alternative would be to 
require the assistance of Transmission Owners in executing IRAS load reductions. However, load 
reductions at the higher voltage levels (e.g., 230 kV) will result in larger MW reductions of load, 
which could destabilize the transmission system if multiple New Large Loads need to be reduced 
simultaneously. 
As a reasonable alternative to PJM’s proposal to hold Electric Distributors solely 
responsible for executing New Large Load reductions for an Interim Resource Adequacy Service 
Action, PJM should consider its role and authority to initiate Interim Resource Adequacy Service 
Actions, as such is “an ‘Operating Instruction, as defined by NERC.’”27 PJM should use its existing 
authority as defined by NERC, as well as authority that may be developed as part of NERC’s 
computational loads/large loads standard in development, to determine PJM’s ability to coordinate 
with Electric Distributors and/or relevant state entities (perhaps RERRAs) to execute Interim

Further, PJM’s filing addresses the reliability and other benefits of load reductions.

C. PJM should provide additional clarifications regarding its proposed IRAS. 
In addition to the changes discussed above, certain elements of PJM’s IRAS should be 
further clarified so that the Commission can ensure the proposal is just and reasonable, and so that 
market participants have certainty regarding the IRAS.  
First, the definition of Large Load should be clarified. PJM proposes to define Large Load 
as follows: 
end-use customer(s) load that has a cumulative peak load of 50 megawatts or 
greater at a single electrical site behind one or more points of interconnection, 
where for configurations involving multiple affiliated load facilities or multiple 
points of interconnection with affiliated load facilities behind them, all affiliated 
load facilities within a one-mile radius will be considered a single electrical site as 
further described in the PJM Manuals. 
ODEC agrees that the definition should include affiliated load facilities. However, PJM’s 
filing does not provide how affiliation will be determined for purposes of the one-mile radius 
measurement. For example, where facilities are owned by separate legal entities within the same 
corporate family, does common control deem the entities affiliates?

Second, PJM’s Tariff provisions for IRAS compensation should clarify that such 
compensation will be limited to Eligible New Large Loads that are incurring capacity costs. PJM 
explains that its IRAS compensation will be implemented by Electric Distributors, in conjunction 
with applicable state authorities and New Large Loads.28 PJM further explains that “the IRAS rate 
is intended to be conjoined with retail rates and ultimately paid to Eligible New Large Loads that 
reflect a discount on capacity costs commensurate with the impact of Interim Resource Adequacy 
Service on addressing resource adequacy issues.”29 The proposed Tariff provisions, however, do 
not explicitly state this condition that compensation will only be paid to Eligible New Large Loads 
that are charged capacity costs. PJM should provide such clarification in its Tariff provisions. 
Third, PJM’s proposal for BYONC appears to allow a New Large Load in one zone to be 
supported by BYONC located in another zone. However, PJM has not explained how BYONC 
will impact RPM locational deliverability and market modeling assumptions, including CETL, 
CETO, LDA demand curves or capacity price separation. PJM should provide such clarification. 
III.
CONCLUSION 
WHEREFORE, for the foregoing reasons, ODEC requests that the Commission consider 
these comments in its determination of the PJM IRAS proposal and if the Commission accepts 
PJM’s filing, direct PJM to (1) modify the Large Load Registry as provided in section II.A. above; 
(2) modify the IRAS load reduction framework as discussed in section II.B. above, and (3) clarify 
the definition of Large Load, IRAS compensation and treatment of BYONC, as discussed in
