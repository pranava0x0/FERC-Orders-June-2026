I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY 
1. PJM asks the Commission to accept a wholesale rate that has no value and a cost 
allocation that has no allocator. 
2. The rate for Interim Resource Adequacy Service ("IRAS") is not a number. Under 
proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, § X(a), it is any point on a continuum between 50% of the 
Non-Performance Charge Rate and zero dollars, and which point governs is decided not by this 
Commission, not by the tariff, and not at the time of filing, but by a retail election made later, in 
a state forum, under state law. No customer reading PJM's tariff can determine what the rate is. 
Neither can a State. The Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission has already told the 
Commission it cannot read the provision, finding "ambiguity due to discrepancies between PJM's
--- PAGE 2 ---
transmittal letter and the red-lined tariff amendments proposed on this issue of 0-50% 
compensation." 
3. PJM's own cited authority forecloses this. In footnote 315 of its transmittal, PJM 
invokes Electric District No. 1 v. FERC, 774 F.2d 490, 493 (D.C. Cir. 1985), for the proposition 
that "[p]urchasers of electricity cannot plan their activities unless they know the cost of what 
they are receiving," and that "providing the necessary predictability is the whole purpose of the 
well-established filed rate doctrine," quoting Arkansas Louisiana Gas Co. v. Hall, 453 U.S. 571, 
577 (1981). PJM deploys that principle to demand that the Commission prescribe with precision 
any language the Commission itself revises. The principle does not run in one direction only. A 
rate that cannot be ascertained from the tariff, and whose value awaits a state proceeding that no 
one is obliged to open, supplies precisely the predictability the filed rate doctrine exists to 
guarantee — and PJM has not supplied it. 
4. The cost allocation fails for the same reason and more starkly. PJM states that "the 
entities to be paid the rate and the loads to be charged to support such compensation must be 
determined at the state level," and then concedes that its "proposal imposes no obligation on any 
state to do anything." IRAS Filing, transmittal at 104, 106. PJM's framework therefore identifies 
no charged party in any State that has not acted. In the companion Reliability Backstop 
Procurement filing, PJM at least proposed a "backup default cost allocation" for that 
contingency. Here it proposes nothing. 
5. Delaware is the proof. The Delaware proceeding that would supply PJM's missing 
terms — Public Service Commission Docket No. 25-0826 — has been suspended since April 
14, 2026, and Delaware has adopted no large-load rate structure of any kind. Delaware is also
--- PAGE 3 ---
restructured, which by PJM's own footnote 281 is the harder case, in which the Electric 
Distributor is not the Load Serving Entity — the case PJM identifies and then declines to 
analyze. Against that, PJM's 132-page transmittal never once mentions Delmarva Power & Light 
Company, the Electric Distributor on which the entire scheme would operate in Movants' zone. 
"Delaware" appears a single time, in a footnote listing the thirteen Governors who signed a 
voluntary pledge. 
6. Meanwhile the tariff is precise about one thing. Proposed Attachment DD-4, § X(b)(iii) 
provides that "any default shall be assessed to the Electric Distributor." PJM has drafted with 
care to ensure that no uncollected IRAS obligation reaches PJM or its membership. It has drafted 
nothing to keep that obligation from reaching Delmarva's captive retail customers, who are not 
New Large Loads, caused none of the shortfall IRAS addresses, receive no IRAS compensation, 
and are already absorbing a 21.8% increase in Delaware Docket No. 25-1555. 
7. Under section 205 of the Federal Power Act, the burden of proving that this rate is just 
and reasonable rests on PJM and on no one else. 16 U.S.C. § 824d(e). PJM has not carried it. The 
Commission should not accept proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, § X as filed.

14. The tariff names the entity that will bill them. Proposed Attachment DD-4, §

Customer's supply is procured and repriced annually, and DPL's Rider "SOS" § B provides that 
the SOS Rate "will include the costs for energy, capacity and ancillary services as reflected in the 
awarded electric supply prices for specific services in each year." The Delaware Public Service 
Commission's own consultant has confirmed the mechanism: "[b]idders use $/MW-day capacity 
auction prices to create a capacity component (in $/MWh) of their SOS bid prices," and "[l]ower 
load factor customer classes . . . feel a greater impact from capacity prices than high load factor . 
. . customers since they have fewer MWh over which to spread their demand-based capacity 
charges."

III. THE FILING 
18. On August 13, 2026, PJM submitted a filing under section 205 of the Federal Power 
Act proposing to establish IRAS and a Large Load Registry, requesting an effective date of 
October 12, 2026. IRAS Filing, transmittal at 8. 
19. IRAS is a load-reduction service. When PJM lacks sufficient available economic real-
time supply to maintain reserves or remain within Interconnection Reliability Operating Limits, 
it may direct load reductions by New Large Loads that have neither entered into "bring your own 
new capacity" ("BYONC") arrangements nor received Allocated RBP UCAP MW. PJM may 
call on IRAS reductions "prior to calling on Pre-Emergency Load Management Reductions 
Actions" — that is, ahead of resources that bid into a market and hold a capacity obligation. Id. 
at 28. "New Large Load" means "an end-use customer load that is either (a) a Large Load that 
goes into service after June 1, 2027, or (b) incremental load in-service after June 1, 2027, that 
increases the existing end-use customer load at a single electrical site that, prior to such 
incremental load, did not previously meet the definition of Large Load." Proposed Tariff, 
Definitions — New Large Load.
--- PAGE 7 ---
20. PJM has severed this filing from its Reliability Backstop Procurement ("RBP") filing 
in Docket No. ER26-3380-000, stating that "the two filings are independent of each other" and 
that "IRAS can and should be accepted and made effective as requested." IRAS Filing, 
transmittal at 2 n.3. PJM must therefore defend this filing on its own terms, without borrowing 
support from a proceeding it has disclaimed. 
IV. PROTEST 
A. PJM has not filed a rate. It has filed a range and deferred its value to a proceeding that 
may never occur. 
21. PJM proposes a rate "equal to 50% of the Non-Performance Charge Rate." IRAS 
Filing, transmittal at 102. The tariff does not stop there. The rate drops below 50% "if the 
Eligible New Large Load accepts in its retail rate a sum less than 50% of the Non-Performance 
Charge Rate," and "a rate of zero dollars for IRAS would apply where Eligible New Large Loads 
waive in their retail rates receipt of compensation." Id. The proposed tariff makes the wholesale 
rate follow the retail election: it "shall reflect the accepted sum less than 50% of the Non-
Performance Charge Rate or if compensation is waived entirely, the rate shall be zero dollars." 
Proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, § X(a). 
22. The filed wholesale rate is therefore every value between zero and 50% of the Non-
Performance Charge Rate. Which value governs depends on a retail election, made in a state 
forum, under state law, at a time no one can specify — and, in a State with no proceeding in 
which to make it, never made at all. 
23. This is not a drafting quibble raised by lay customers. The Pennsylvania Public 
Utility Commission, a State commission with a standing energy bar, told this Commission on 
September 1, 2026 that it finds "some ambiguity due to discrepancies between PJM's transmittal
--- PAGE 8 ---
letter and the red-lined tariff amendments proposed on this issue of 0-50% compensation," and 
that while PJM's redlines "identify EDC compensation obligations and collection thereof from 
jurisdictional customers," the "tariff does not expressly recognize state authority to prohibit, 
approve, or modify such compensation and jurisdictional recovery." Answer of the Pennsylvania 
Public Utility Commission to Motion for Extension of Time, Docket No. ER26-3515-000, at 2–3 
(Sept. 1, 2026) (Accession No. 20260901-5028). When the regulator of PJM's largest state 
cannot determine what the tariff does, the tariff has not given notice. 
24. PJM has told the Commission the governing standard, in its own transmittal. Footnote 
315 quotes Electric District No. 1: "[P]urchasers of electricity cannot plan their activities unless 
they know the cost of what they are receiving, 'providing the necessary predictability is the 
whole purpose of the well-established filed rate doctrine.'" 774 F.2d at 493 (quoting Ark. La. Gas 
Co. v. Hall, 453 U.S. 571, 577 (1981)). PJM adds that "mere 'direction' without 'sufficient detail 
on each element [of the replacement rate] that [customers/market participants] could determine 
when their transactions would be subject to [the new rule]'" would "violate the filed rate 
doctrine." PJM invokes this to constrain the Commission. It applies with at least equal force to 
PJM's own submission: a tariff that states a range and locates the operative number in an 
unscheduled state proceeding gives market participants no way to "determine when their 
transactions would be subject to" what rate. 
25. In Delaware today, the wholesale IRAS rate is unknowable. It is not zero — zero is 
what results from an affirmative waiver election that Delaware provides no forum to make. It is 
undetermined, and PJM asks the Commission to accept it in that condition. 
B. PJM concedes that its framework cannot function without state action, and supplies no 
default when that action does not come.
--- PAGE 9 ---
26. PJM's transmittal states the concession plainly: 
"That said, PJM's proposal imposes no obligation on any state to do anything. 
States are free to design retail rate regimes that facilitate the recovery and 
allocation of wholesale IRAS rates through generally applicable retail rate tariffs. 
Alternatively, if states decline to act generically, they could negotiate specific 
retail filed rates with affected entities. Further still, if states decline to act, Eligible 
New Large Loads could initiate regulatory proceedings to develop retail pathways 
to recover wholesale IRAS rates. Or, if states decline to act and Eligible New 
Large Loads decline to initiate regulatory proceedings to recover through retail 
rates the wholesale IRAS rate, they would effectively waive a right to 
compensation." 
IRAS Filing, transmittal at 106–07. 
27. PJM presents this as flexibility. It is an admission that the filing is incomplete. The 
passage answers one question — who gets paid when no State acts — and leaves three that 
belong to this Commission because they concern the operation of a federal tariff: 
(a) The service is still compelled. Nothing conditions PJM's authority to direct IRAS load 
reductions on the existence of a state-approved compensation mechanism. PJM takes the 
reliability benefit in a non-acting State on identical terms. 
(b) The Electric Distributor's obligations still attach. Under the proposed tariff the Electric 
Distributor administers the program, identifies the beneficiaries, and absorbs the billing default 
— in a State that has adopted no plan enabling it to do the first two. 
(c) The tariff supplies no charged party. PJM's answer that compensation may be "effectively 
waive[d]" addresses only payment. It says nothing about who is charged, and it leaves § 
X(b)(iii)'s default assignment fully operative.
--- PAGE 10 ---
28. PJM knows how to solve this, because it did so in the companion docket. There PJM 
proposed a "backup default cost allocation" governing precisely the case in which a Relevant 
Electric Retail Regulatory Authority ("RERRA") declines to act. Here PJM proposes none, while 
relying on the same assumption of state action. 
29. The regulators on whom that assumption rests have told the Commission they do not 
yet understand it. OPSI sought additional time so that "the retail regulators in the PJM region" 
could "consider the interaction between the rights and responsibilities of PJM, Electric 
Distributors, and retail regulators related to the implementation of Interim Resource Adequacy 
Service and the associated compensation mechanism." Notice Denying Extension of Time at 1. 
Google LLC, the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, and East Kentucky Power 
Cooperative supported that request; no party opposed it. PJM's response to the fact that the 
region's retail regulators have not worked out the handoff is to ask that the framework be 
accepted anyway, effective October 12, 2026. 
C. Delaware refutes PJM's premise. 
30. PJM tells the Commission that some States "have begun proceedings to evaluate how 
to best facilitate coordination with PJM on implementing IRAS," and that "PJM welcomes all 
such efforts." Delaware began one. It stopped. 
31. On October 14, 2025, the Delaware Public Service Commission opened Docket No. 
25-0826 to establish a large load tariff, to ensure that "large load facilities, like data centers, do 
not shift costs to other ratepayers." That is the proceeding in which Delaware would adopt the 
"state-approved load reduction plans and other state-approved programs, rates, and rules" on 
which PJM's framework depends. IRAS Filing, transmittal at 104.
--- PAGE 11 ---
32. On April 14, 2026, by Order No. 10998, the Hearing Examiner suspended the 
procedural schedule in Docket No. 25-0826. No Hearing Examiner's Report issued on the 
scheduled date. No Commission deliberation occurred on the scheduled date. Nearly five months 
later the docket remains suspended, and Delaware has adopted no large-load rate structure. 
There is no state-approved load reduction plan in Delaware, and no state-approved program, rate, 
or rule under which Delmarva could identify either the beneficiaries of an IRAS reduction or the 
loads to be charged for it. PJM's framework, applied to Delaware on October 12, 2026, would 
operate with both terms blank. 
33. The loads are already at the door. Order No. 10998 records that Delmarva is "working 
with seven developers on large load projects seeking a total of 2,455 MW," that five of the seven 
are data centers "seeking 82% of the total load request," and that one proposed project is 1.2 GW 
against a Delaware summer peak load of 2.7 GW. These are the very loads that would qualify as 
New Large Loads, in the very zone where no retail mechanism exists. 
34. Delaware is also the case PJM identified and then abandoned. Footnote 281 states: 
"PJM understands that in traditional, non-restructured states, the Electric Distributor also doubles 
as the LSE for the New Large Load, while in restructured states, the LSE may be an independent 
entity, not affiliated with the Electric Distributor." IRAS Filing, transmittal at 105 n.281. 
Delaware is restructured. Delmarva is the Electric Distributor and is not the LSE for a 
competitively supplied large load. PJM assigns administration, identification, and default risk to 
an entity whose compensation obligations run through LSEs it does not control, notes the 
problem in a footnote, and never returns to it.
--- PAGE 12 ---
35. The absence is not confined to analysis. "Delmarva" appears zero times in PJM's 
132-page transmittal letter. "DPL" appears zero times. "Delaware" appears once — in a 
footnote listing the thirteen Governors who signed the Statement of Principles Regarding PJM of 
January 16, 2026. "RERRA" appears twenty-three times. PJM has built a framework that 
operates through Electric Distributors and RERRAs without examining what happens at the one 
it never names, in the State it mentions only as a signature on a voluntary pledge that binds no 
one and that no order of this Commission can enforce. 
D. Section X(b)(iii) shifts uncollected wholesale obligations onto Delmarva's captive 
customers. 
36. PJM drafted protection for itself with precision. The indemnification language exists 
"to make clear that neither PJM nor the PJM membership, generally, is financially responsible 
for any potential defaults that may arise in association with the billing of this compensation." 
IRAS Filing, transmittal at 108. If "a LSE does not pay as required . . ., the default will not pass 
to the PJM membership." Id. Where PJM bills at the Electric Distributor's request, "such billing 
will be viewed as a bilateral transaction between the Electric Distributor and the other party and 
any default shall be assessed to the Electric Distributor." Id. at 109; proposed Tariff, 
Attachment DD-4, § X(b)(iii). 
37. The provision fixes where a default lands and is silent on who ultimately pays it. In a 
restructured State with no state-approved allocation mechanism, that silence has an obvious 
answer: the Electric Distributor's captive retail customers. 
38. Delmarva is at this moment seeking a distribution base rate increase before the 
Delaware Public Service Commission in Docket No. 25-1555 that includes a 21.8% increase for 
the MGS-S class — the Customer's class — with interim rates already in effect and collecting
--- PAGE 13 ---
subject to refund. A wholesale default assessed to Delmarva under § X(b)(iii) is an amount 
Delmarva will seek from that same customer base: customers who are not New Large Loads, did 
not cause the shortfall IRAS addresses, and receive no IRAS compensation. 
39. A provision is not just and reasonable merely because it protects the PJM 
membership. PJM has shown where the cost stops being its problem. It has not shown that the 
place it comes to rest is lawful. 
E. The Large Load Registry cannot perform the function PJM assigns it. 
40. Every allocation in this framework depends on knowing which loads are New Large 
Loads and when they entered service. PJM would maintain that information in the Large Load 
Registry and rely on States and Electric Distributors to act on it. A State cannot allocate costs to 
loads it cannot identify, and an Electric Distributor cannot bill them. 
41. A State commission that supports PJM has told the Commission the predicate fails. 
The Illinois Commerce Commission stated that "expecting the states to make such 
determinations for PJM and the Commission may be unreasonable," and that "PJM would seem 
to be in a better position and have access to the appropriate data to make an assessment of the 
cost causers and even beneficiaries." Comments of the Illinois Commerce Commission, Docket 
No. ER26-3380-000, at 6 (Aug. 19, 2026) (Accession No. 20260819-5134). On the Registry 
itself, the ICC warned that "[s]tates have different rules that can limit their access to confidential 
data," that the ICC "would not be in a position to sign the pro forma non-disclosure agreements 
to access confidential data from PJM as it reads at this time," and that it "is crucial that any large 
load registry be accessible to ICC staff in order to fulfill any state's cost allocation obligations." 
Id. at 7.
--- PAGE 14 ---
42. That is the assessment of an engaged, supportive State commission with resources 
and an active docket. PJM's framework fares worse, not better, in a State whose large-load 
proceeding is suspended. 
F. The VRR Curve exclusion raises capacity prices for every customer in the zone, and 
rests on a pledge no one can enforce. 
43. Beginning with the 2029/2030 Delivery Year, PJM would exclude from the VRR 
Curve the MW of New Large Loads that have not entered BYONC arrangements and hold no 
Allocated RBP UCAP MW, above the level in the 2028/2029 load forecast — and would do so 
"regardless of whether PJM is experiencing a shortfall that triggers IRAS in the relevant 
Delivery Year." IRAS Filing, transmittal at 110. Removing load from the demand curve raises 
the capacity clearing price for every load in the zone, including residential and MGS-S Standard 
Offer Service customers who are nowhere near the New Large Load threshold and receive no 
IRAS compensation. 
44. PJM's justification is the Ratepayer Protection Pledge — a voluntary commitment 
that appears in no tariff, binds no signatory, and cannot be enforced by this Commission or by 
any party. A demand curve is a price-formation instrument. PJM has offered no tariff 
consequence if the pledge on which the modification rests goes unhonored. 
V. RELIEF REQUESTED 
45. The Commission should reject proposed Tariff, Attachment DD-4, § X as filed. PJM 
bears the burden under FPA section 205 and has not carried it as to either the rate or its 
allocation.
--- PAGE 15 ---
46. In the alternative, the Commission should accept the IRAS compensation framework 
only as conditioned on the following, each of which PJM has consented in advance to have the 
Commission prescribe. PJM asks that the Commission "expressly prescribe the revised rate or 
rule in its order," and confirms that "the proposal put forth here is not a stakeholder compromise 
package," so no bargain is disturbed. IRAS Filing, transmittal at 122–23. 
47. First, PJM must state on the face of Attachment DD-4, § X the wholesale IRAS rate 
and the responsible collecting entity that apply in an Electric Distributor zone where the RERRA 
has not established a mechanism identifying the entities to be paid and the loads to be charged. 
PJM proposed exactly such a backstop in the companion RBP filing. A federal filed rate must be 
ascertainable from the tariff in every zone to which it applies. 
48. Second, the Commission should condition acceptance of § X(b)(iii) so that a default 
assessed to an Electric Distributor may not be recovered, directly or indirectly, from retail 
customer classes containing no Eligible New Large Loads and receiving no IRAS compensation. 
49. Third, PJM must make the Large Load Registry information necessary to identify 
New Large Loads within a zone — at minimum identity, location, in-service date, and 
cumulative peak load quantity — available to the RERRA and the Electric Distributor for that 
zone on terms each is legally able to accept, so that the allocation function PJM assigns to the 
States can actually be performed. 
50. Fourth, the Commission should not permit the VRR Curve exclusion to take effect 
without a tariff provision specifying the consequence if the Ratepayer Protection Pledge 
commitments on which it rests are not performed.
--- PAGE 16 ---
51. Any modification the Commission adopts should be expressly prescribed in its order, 
as PJM has requested. IRAS Filing, transmittal at 122. 
VI. CONCLUSION 
52. PJM asks the Commission to approve a rate no one can compute and an allocation no 
one is required to make, and to make both effective on October 12, 2026. Its answer to the State 
that has not acted is that compensation is waived — an answer that resolves nothing about who is 
charged, and leaves a tariff provision routing every uncollected dollar to the Electric Distributor 
and from there to captive retail customers. Delaware has been in that position since April 14, 
2026, and appears in PJM's 132-page filing exactly once, as one of thirteen signatures on a 
pledge that binds no one. The Commission should require the tariff to answer, in its own text, 
what PJM has left to a forum that may never convene: in a zone where the State has not acted, 
what is the rate, who is charged, and who bears the default. 
53. For the foregoing reasons, Movants respectfully request that the Commission grant 
this motion to intervene and the relief requested herein.
