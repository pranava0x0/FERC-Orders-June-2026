DUKE ENERGY OHIO, INC. AND DUKE ENERGY KENTUCKY, INC.  
 
 
On July 23, 2026, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC” or the 
“Commission”) convened a technical conference in this docket to discuss PJM Interconnection, 
L.L.C. (“PJM”) governance and stakeholder processes, with a particular focus on identifying and 
evaluating concrete, actionable reforms to improve PJM’s ability to address operational and market 
needs in a timely and efficient manner. On July 30, 2026, the Commission issued a notice inviting 
interested parties to file post-conference comments, including responding to a list of questions 
posed by the Commission regarding potential PJM governance reforms. Duke Energy Kentucky, 
Inc. and Duke Energy Ohio, Inc. (“collectively, “Duke Energy’) hereby submit comments to the 
questions posed by the Commission.1  Duke Energy Kentucky, Inc. and Duke Energy Ohio, Inc. 
are transmission-owning utilities within PJM and are PJM members in the Transmission Owner 
sector. Duke Energy provides electric service to 920,000 customers in Kentucky and Ohio. Duke 
Energy appreciates the Commission’s initiative and active engagement to investigate reforms that 
can improve PJM’s ability to meet the needs of the moment and ensure the independence and 
reliable operation of PJM in the long-term.  
 
Listed below are the questions posed by the Commission and Duke Energy’s responses:

PJM Board Independence 
1. Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years? 
No. Duke Energy supports a three-year board term, with the opportunity for 
renomination, and opposes extending board terms to five years or more because shorter 
terms promote responsiveness to industry changes, facilitate replacement of directors 
when concerns arise, and align with prevailing RTO/ISO governance standard practice 
among RTOs/ISOs. 
 
2. Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 
10 years)? 
 
Yes. Term limits reinforce both independence and accountability while preserving a 
board’s ability to benefit from experienced leadership. We recommend three, three-year 
terms for the maximum term of nine years. However, limited extensions can preserve 
needed expertise while maintaining the discipline of a term-limit framework. 
Consequently, Duke Energy supports stakeholder approval for exceeding the maximum 
nine-year term to no more than two, three-year terms.  
 
3. Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for 
some Board members that reside in the PJM region? 
 
Yes. Duke Energy believes there is value in having a portion of the PJM Board reside 
within the PJM footprint. Regional residence can provide practical familiarity with the 
states, communities, reliability concerns, and consumer impacts within the PJM region. 
However, residency should be treated as a preference rather than a mandatory 
qualification. A strict requirement could unnecessarily limit the pool of qualified, 
independent candidates and should not outweigh the need to select Board members with 
the appropriate expertise, independence, and judgment to oversee PJM effectively. 
 
4. Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for 
Board members from two years to one year? 
 
Yes. Duke Energy supports reducing the cooling-off period from two years to one year. A 
one-year period provides meaningful separation from prior employment or stakeholder 
affiliations while avoiding an unnecessarily restrictive barrier to qualified candidates. 
Given the complexity and pace of change in PJM’s markets, reliability obligations, and 
transmission planning responsibilities, the Board benefits from access to individuals with 
current and relevant experience. Any remaining conflict concerns can be appropriately 
addressed through robust disclosure, recusal, and ongoing conflict-of-interest safeguards.

5. Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more 
communication between the Board and stakeholders? 
 
No. Duke Energy believes that PJM’s restrictions should remain in place because they 
preserve the independence and impartiality of the Board’s decision-making process. 
Permitting unrestricted communications between individual stakeholders and Board 
members could create the perception that certain interests receive preferential access or 
influence over Board deliberations. The current restrictions promote fairness, 
transparency, and stakeholder confidence that Board decisions are based on the record 
through PJM’s open stakeholder processes rather than private advocacy. However, PJM 
could adopt a “Board Reception” approach similar to other RTOs/ISOs where 
stakeholders are allowed to speak to Board members at events such as the PJM Annual 
Meeting concerning any issue of interest for up to 3 hours.  
 
6. In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board 
be required to explain its reasoning?  
 
Yes, with some exception. Duke Energy supports increased transparency into Board 
voting and reasoning where appropriate, as doing so can enhance accountability and 
stakeholder confidence in PJM governance. However, any requirement to make Board 
sessions or explanations public should preserve the ability of the Board to maintain 
confidentiality for matters that are appropriately addressed in executive sessions, 
including personnel, compensation, legal, privileged, commercially sensitive, 
cybersecurity, or other confidential matters. This approach balances the value of 
transparency with the need for candid Board deliberation and responsible governance 
 
7. In order to increase transparency, should some Board meetings be open or available 
to the public? 
 
Yes, with some exception. Duke Energy supports making appropriate portions of some 
Board meetings open or publicly available to enhance transparency and stakeholder 
confidence. However, public access should not extend to matters appropriately addressed 
in executive sessions, including personnel, compensation, legal, privileged, commercially 
sensitive, cybersecurity, or other confidential matters. This strikes a balance between 
visibility into Board governance while preserving the Board’s ability to deliberate 
candidly and responsively on sensitive issues.  
 
8. Should PJM’s governing documents be revised to explicitly state that the 
responsibility of PJM and its Board is to serve the public interest? 
 
No. Duke Energy does not believe additional language is necessary. PJM’s governing 
documents already require the Board to oversee PJM in a manner consistent with the safe 
and reliable operation of the PJM Region, and the creation and operation of robust, 
competitive, and nondiscriminatory electric power markets. Those obligations are core 
expressions of public interest in the RTO context. Adding a separate, generalized public

interpretations of that term, without improving PJM’s existing obligations to reliability,

9. Should state representatives have at least one voting member on the Nominating 
Committee? 
 
No. While Duke Energy believes that input from the states is important, we do not 
support giving state representatives a voting member on PJM’s Nominating Committee. 
PJM’s Nominating Committee is structured around representatives elected from the 
Members Committee sectors. Creating a voting role for state representatives would 
require a broader change to PJM’s sector-based governance structure and could disrupt 
the balance among existing stakeholder sectors. It could also blur the independence of the 
Board nomination process by creating the perception that Board selection is influenced 
by governmental or state policy priorities. State input is important and necessary, but it 
should continue to be provided through OPSI, stakeholder engagement and FERC 
proceedings rather than through a formal vote on Board nominations.  
 
10. Should PJM adopt public performance metrics and tie management compensation 
to their achievement? 
 
Yes. Duke Energy supports the development of PJM management performance metrics 
and believes stakeholder awareness and input are important. However, Duke Energy does 
not support posting those performance metrics broadly on PJM’s public website. Instead, 
PJM should circulate the proposed metrics to the Members Committee sectors for 
comment and input before the metrics are finalized, while preserving appropriate 
confidentiality for compensation-related and other sensitive information. 
 
Section 205 Filing Rights 
 
11. Should the stakeholder process be advisory, i.e., stakeholder votes only inform the 
Board? 
 
Yes. While a deliberative stakeholder process is important to understanding various 
perspectives and enabling coordinated regional decision-making, under the current PJM 
governance structure, the stakeholder process can too often forestall action if the matter is 
financially significant to vested stakeholder interests, operationally sensitive, or 
otherwise polarizing. PJM needs to be able to take more timely and decisive action. 
Converting the stakeholder process to an advisory process would balance the need for 
stakeholder input with PJM’s need for independence and ability to take decisive action on 
critical issues.

12. Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over the energy market and ancillary 
services unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-thirds of the 
Members Committee? 
 
Yes. Duke Energy supports PJM having Section 205 filing rights over the energy and 
ancillary services market rules. Energy and ancillary services market operation and 
design are core to PJM operations, and PJM should have the unilateral authority to take 
timely and decisive action, informed by stakeholder input.  
 
 
 
13. Should PJM have section 205 filing rights over Regional Transmission Expansion 
Planning (RTEP) unencumbered by the requirement to obtain approval of two-
thirds of the Members Committee? 
 
Yes, with important limitations. Duke Energy supported the agreement between PJM and 
PJM transmission owners to provide PJM section 205 filing rights over the RTEP as a 
package with other revisions and updates to the Consolidated Transmission Owners.2  
While that agreement and related filings were rejected by the Commission,3 Duke Energy 
continues to support PJM having Section 205 filing rights over the RTEP, as long as other 
key revisions and updates to the Consolidated Transmission Owners Agreement are also 
made.  
 
14. Should the Organization of PJM States Inc (OPSI) have “jump ball” rights over 
resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation filings when a supermajority of 
OPSI agrees on an alternative proposal? 
 
No. Duke Energy does not support granting OPSI “jump ball” rights over resource 
adequacy or transmission cost allocation filings, even where a supermajority of OPSI 
supports an alternative proposal. Resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation are 
core tariff matters that require coordinated regional decision-making, stakeholder input, 
and independent PJM judgment subject to Commission review. Providing OPSI with 
separate filing rights on these issues would risk elevating state policy preferences over 
the balanced, sector-based stakeholder process and could undermine PJM’s independence 
in administering its FERC-approved responsibilities. OPSI’s perspectives are important 
and should continue to inform PJM through existing consultation, stakeholder processes, 
and participation in Commission proceedings, but those perspectives should not create a 
parallel filing pathway that could complicate or delay timely regional reforms.

15. Should the Members Committee have “jump ball” filing rights over the portions of 
the tariff where PJM has 205 filing rights when a supermajority of stakeholders 
approves an alternative proposal? 
 
No. Duke Energy does not support granting the Members Committee “jump ball” filing 
rights over portions of the tariff where PJM has section 205 filing rights, even where a 
supermajority of stakeholders supports an alternative proposal. Where PJM is assigned 
section 205 filing authority, PJM must be able to exercise that authority independently 
and efficiently to carry out its FERC-approved responsibilities for reliable operations, 
market administration, and timely regional reforms. Creating a parallel Members 
Committee filing right over the same tariff provisions would undermine the purpose of 
assigning those filing rights to PJM, risk duplicative or competing filings, and could 
delay needed reforms. Stakeholder input remains essential and should continue to inform 
PJM’s decision-making through the stakeholder process and Commission proceedings, 
but that input should not displace PJM’s independent filing authority in areas where the 
tariff assigns PJM section 205 rights. 
 
Stakeholder Process 
 
16. Should the approval threshold in the Members Committee be lowered from two-
thirds sector-weighted voting? 
 
No. Duke Energy does not support lowering the Members Committee approval threshold 
from the current two-thirds sector-weighted voting standard. The existing threshold helps 
ensure that significant stakeholder-supported changes reflect broad consensus across 
PJM’s diverse sectors rather than the preferences of a narrow majority or a single sector 
coalition. Maintaining the two-thirds sector-weighted vote protects the balance embedded 
in PJM’s stakeholder governance structure, encourages compromise among sectors with 
different interests, and reinforces confidence that proposals advancing through the 
Members Committee have substantial cross-sector support. Lowering the threshold could 
weaken that consensus-based model and increase the risk that contested reforms move 
forward without sufficient stakeholder alignment. 
 
17. Should the voting rules barring affiliate votes, which apply to senior standing 
committees, also apply to lower-level committees? 
 
Yes. Duke Energy supports applying affiliate voting restrictions not only to senior 
standing committees but also to lower-level committees. Although lower-level 
committees may not make final decisions, they often shape, refine, and advance 
proposals that ultimately move through stakeholder process. Allowing affiliates to cast

State Participation 
 
18. In addition to OPSI, should there be a second state representative group consisting 
of governors’ representatives and/or state legislators’ representatives? 
 
No. Duke Energy does not support creating a second state representative group consisting 
of governors’ representatives or state legislators’ representatives. OPSI already provides 
an established mechanism for state regulatory perspectives to be communicated to PJM 
and stakeholders. Creating an additional governmental organization could duplicate 
existing channels, create uncertainty regarding which entity speaks for a state, and add 
complexity to an already extensive governance and stakeholder process. While state input 
is important, it should be provided through clear and existing channels in a manner that 
preserves PJM’s independence, supports timely decision-making, and avoids introducing 
political considerations into technical reliability and market governance matters. 
 
19. Should state representatives have a formal voting role in the stakeholder process 
(limited to certain topics, such as resource adequacy and regional transmission cost 
allocation) in addition to the consumer advocate votes?  
 
No. Duke Energy does not support granting state representatives a formal voting role in 
the PJM stakeholder process. State input is important and should continue to be 
considered through OPSI, FERC proceedings, stakeholder engagement, and other 
existing channels. However, providing state representatives with formal voting rights 
could blur the distinction between state policy advocacy and PJM’s independent 
responsibility to administer reliable operations and nondiscriminatory markets under its 
FERC-approved governing documents. It also could disrupt the balance of PJM’s sector-
based stakeholder process and add complexity to already challenging market, resource 
adequacy, and transmission cost allocation reforms. 
 
20. Should PJM be required to consult with OPSI prior to making a section 205 filing 
related to resource adequacy and transmission cost allocation? 
 
No. We are concerned that such a requirement would impede PJM’s independence. OPSI 
has opportunities to provide feedback through the current Stakeholder process. Additional 
consultation requirements could create delays and impediments to PJM taking decisive 
action when needed.

Conclusion
