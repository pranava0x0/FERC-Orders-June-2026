Post-Technical Conference Comments of NRG Energy, Inc. 
NRG Energy, Inc. respectfully submits these comments regarding potential reforms to the 
governance and stakeholder processes of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. ("PJM"). Governance 
reform should be guided by several core principles: broad stakeholder participation, 
transparency, accountability, independence in decision-making, regulatory stability, and support 
for investment and innovation. The PJM region faces unprecedented reliability, affordability, and 
resource adequacy challenges. Meeting those challenges will require substantial investment in 
generation, demand response, advanced technologies, transmission infrastructure, and innovative 
market solutions. Governance reforms should therefore be evaluated not only on whether they 
improve decision-making processes, but also on whether they strengthen the region's ability to 
attract and retain capital necessary to maintain reliability and support economic growth. 
I. Stakeholder Engagement Remains Paramount  
A defining strength of PJM has long been its ability to bring together a diverse set of 
market participants, state officials, consumer advocates, suppliers, transmission owners, demand 
response providers, and emerging technology developers. These varied perspectives have 
improved PJM's outcomes and enhanced stakeholder acceptance of policy choices. Accordingly, 
any governance reforms should ensure that participation remains accessible and meaningful. 
Barriers to participation should remain reasonably low while reforms should preserve 
opportunities for stakeholders to identify concerns, develop alternatives, and contribute 
expertise. Stakeholder engagement should not be viewed as an obstacle to progress. Rather, it is 
an important mechanism for improving market design and identifying unintended consequences 
before they occur. The long-term success of PJM depends upon maintaining confidence that 
diverse viewpoints continue to receive fair consideration. 
II. Governance Reform Must Not Impede Necessary Investment and Timely Action  
While stakeholder participation is essential, PJM's governance structure must also be 
capable of producing timely decisions. Investors evaluating opportunities in generation, demand 
response, storage, advanced technologies, large-load solutions, and other infrastructure require 
regulatory certainty and confidence that critical market and reliability issues will be resolved 
within reasonable timeframes. Governance structures that create persistent stalemates, indefinite 
delays, or fragmented decision-making undermine investment signals and can ultimately threaten 
reliability. To that end, further reforms should seek to improve efficiency without sacrificing 
inclusiveness. In particular, governance structures should encourage clear decision-making

timelines, provide transparency regarding how stakeholder input informs final decisions, reduce

III. Solutions Should Enhance Adoption of Innovations  
The electricity sector is undergoing rapid transformation. New and emerging 
technologies, novel business models, and large flexible loads are changing how electric systems 
operate. PJM's governance framework and the actions taken as part of that framework should 
welcome these developments rather than unintentionally discourage them. Governance reforms 
should be evaluated through the lens of whether they promote competitive outcomes, avoid 
barriers to entry, and allow new business models to compete on a level playing field. 
Competitive wholesale markets have succeeded because they enable innovation to emerge from 
market participants rather than from a central planner. Governance reforms should reinforce that 
principle.  
IV. Board Independence Remains a Critical Element of PJM’s Success  
Board independence has been a defining feature of PJM governance and should remain 
so. The PJM Board has the challenging task of balancing competing stakeholder interests while 
exercising sound judgment regarding reliability and market integrity to drive efficient outcomes 
that benefit the region’s stakeholders. An independent Board can and should remain accountable, 
transparent, and informed by stakeholder input. Reforms in this space should focus on improving 
communication and transparency. More significant changes to the nature of the Board would 
require a more deliberative exploration of the appropriate role of the Board relative to the role of 
PJM’s executives.  
V. Reforms Should Avoid Unnecessary Disruption  
The Commission should be cautious about reforms that fundamentally restructure PJM 
governance absent a compelling showing that such disruption is necessary. Major structural 
changes may create unintended consequences, increase uncertainty, and divert attention from the 
urgent reliability, market-design, and infrastructure challenges confronting the region. 
Incremental improvements that strengthen transparency, accountability, stakeholder engagement, 
and decision-making efficiency are often preferable to wholesale institutional redesign. In short, 
stakeholder efforts should seek targeted reforms that address identified shortcomings while 
preserving those aspects of PJM's governance framework that have contributed to decades of 
reliable system operations and competitive market development.

PJM Board Independence 
1 
Should Board members’ terms be extended to five or more years? 
No 
Comments 
NRG does not object to consideration of alternative Board member terms, but further 
exploration of the benefits and detriments of alternative term lengths should be 
explored before determining the outcome. 
  
2 
Should there be a maximum number of years that a Board member may serve (e.g., 
10 years)? 
Yes 
Comments 
It is NRG's understanding that there are guidelines in practice today that limit any 
individual Board members' term of service today. 
  
3 
Should the Board member qualification rules be revised to provide a preference for 
some Board members that reside in the PJM region? 
Yes 
Comments 
So long as this is a preference, and not a requirement, NRG can support this. The PJM 
Board, its Members, and its stakeholders should have a strong preference for the best 
qualified candidates regardless of residence.  
  
4 
Should the conflict-of-interest rules be revised to reduce the cooling off period for 
Board members from two years to one year? 
Yes 
Comments 
NRG supports this revision as it will expand the pool of prospective Board candidates 
with relevant experience. 
  
5 
Should restrictions on Board communications be relaxed to enable more 
communication between the Board and stakeholders? 
No 
Comments 
While NRG is open to different frameworks for Board communication, the existing 
framework provides significant transparency to all stakeholders regarding Board 
communications while avoiding issues regarding preferential or unduly discriminatory 
access. 
  
6 
In order to increase transparency, should Board votes be made public or the Board be 
required to explain its reasoning? 
Yes 
Comments 
NRG supports revisions that enhance transparency into the Board's reasoning for 
major decisions.  NRG also notes in recent matters of particular significance (e.g., the 
various CIFP decisions), the PJM Board has articulated its reasoning for the benefit of 
stakeholders.  
  
7

to the public? 
Yes 
Comments 
NRG supports this revision with the caveat that matters of litigation, personnel 
matters, and other confidential or sensitive matters should be addressed in executive 
session.
