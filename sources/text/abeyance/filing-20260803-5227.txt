I.
MOTION FOR ABEYANCE
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, thereby initiating a
proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act.  The Show Cause Order found that the
Open Access Transmission Tariff of the California Independent System Operator Corporation
(“CAISO”) and/or the Transmission Owner (“TO”) Tariffs of each of the CAISO Participating
TOs appear to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or preferential to the extent that
they do not include provisions establishing the rates, terms, and conditions for the
interconnection of and service to certain large and co-located loads.4  The Show Cause Order
therefore directed the CAISO and its Participating TOs to either explain how their respective
tariffs remain just and reasonable without specific provisions for the interconnection of large and
co-located loads or to develop such provisions.5
As explained in the Motion to Rescind, each of the Six Cities is a non-public utility that is
exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act
(“FPA”).6  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission acknowledged that some of the

Participating TOs in the CAISO are not subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction and explained

the CAISO “respond[s] to this order regarding the non-public utility’s CAISO Tariff provisions

Because the Commission has yet to act on the Motion to Rescind, the Six Cities
conditionally request a 90-day abeyance of the 60-day deadline to respond to the Show Cause
Order.  In the Show Cause Order, the Commission explained that it would consider requests “to
hold, for up to 90 days, all or certain aspects of this FPA section 206 proceeding in abeyance,
including the deadline to respond to this order.”8  The purpose of the abeyance, according to the
Commission, is to allow sufficient time for the CAISO and Participating TOs to develop FPA
section 205 filings proposing revisions to their respective TO Tariffs to address the issues in the
Show Cause Order.9
Good cause exists for the Commission to grant the Six Cities’ conditional request for
abeyance.  As an initial matter, this abeyance request will be moot in the event the Commission
grants the Motion to Rescind.  Recission is appropriate because each of the Six Cities is a non-
public utility exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant to Section 201(f) of the
FPA.10

If the Motion to Rescind is not granted, the Six Cities will need an additional 90 days to
prepare responses to the Show Cause Order, develop potential revisions to each of their TO
Tariffs to address any modifications that are determined to be needed, and coordinate both their
responses to the Show Cause Order and proposed tariff revisions with the CAISO (which has
advised that it intends to conduct a stakeholder proceeding to develop responsive policies and
tariff changes) and the other Participating TOs.  Moreover, the Six Cities’ facilities and
Entitlements in the CAISO generally represent either shares of jointly-owned facilities with, in
some cases, entities that are not CAISO Participating TOs and/or are also not Commission-
jurisdictional, or contractual rights to transmission service on other parties’ systems governed by
those parties’ tariffs and/or bilateral agreements.11  Therefore, the Six Cities may potentially
need to coordinate their responses to the Show Cause Order with these parties as well.  Finally,
each City will need to evaluate whether proposed revisions to their TO Tariffs to address the
Show Cause Order require review and approval by the City’s Public Utility Board or the City
Council (or both) and obtain any necessary authorizations from those regulatory authorities.
Given that the issues raised in the Show Cause Order are both important and novel, it is
critical that the Cities have adequate time to fully consider and prepare responsive filings if the
Show Cause Order is not rescinded.  For these reasons and in light of the circumstances
described above, the Six Cities conditionally seek a 90-day abeyance of the 60-day response
deadline if and to the extent that the Commission does not grant the Six Cities’ pending Motion
to Rescind.

CONCLUSION

this motion is moot.
