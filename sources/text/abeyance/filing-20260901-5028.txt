ANSWER OF THE PENNSYLVANIA PUBLIC UTILITY COMMISSION TO
MOTION FOR EXTENSION OF TIME

Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s
(“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure, 18 C.F.R. § 385.213(a)(3), Intervenor
Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission (PA PUC) submits this Answer in Support of
the Motion of the Organization of PJM States (OPSI) for a two-week extension of time to
submit comments in response to PJM’s August 13, 2026 filing proposing revisions to the
Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement
Among Load Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service
(“IRAS”), among other things.1  PA PUC supports and joins in OPSI’s request that the
Commission extend the time for comments in this docket from September 3, 2026 to
September 14, 2026.

PJM proposes a compensation mechanism for new large loads (NLLs) that provide
a load reduction service, i.e. curtailment, which creates new obligations for Electric
Distributors. There is also an opportunity for NLLs to waive this compensation, and ways
that states may be able to modify this compensation at the retail level. PA PUC also
requests time to examine PJM’s filing and seek clarification regarding the complicated
issue of how IRAS service may or should be compensated at the retail level.  We find
some ambiguity due to discrepancies between PJM’s transmittal letter and the red-lined

tariff amendments proposed on this issue of 0-50% compensation.  PA PUC sees some
potential jurisdictional conflicts arising from the proposed tariff amendments and may
file comments as to what role States should have in controlling the compensation rate via
state-level retail tariffs.   The curtailment protocol was not developed with PA PUC’s
input, and it may impinge upon our jurisdiction over retail rates.  Specifically, PJM’s
tariff redlines prescribe compensation obligations for Electric Distribution Companies.
The redlines for Tariff section X.b.i. identify EDC compensation obligations and
collection thereof from jurisdictional customers (See page 105 of PJM’s filing).  The
tariff does not expressly recognize state authority to prohibit, approve, or modify such
compensation and jurisdictional  recovery.  No party will be prejudiced should FERC
grant OPSI’s motion, nor is the motion made solely for the purpose of delay.  It is in the
public interest to grant OPSI and the PA PUC an additional two weeks to review the PJM
filing and develop comments in response.

CONCLUSION

WHEREFORE, the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission respectfully requests
that the Commission grant OPSI’s motion and extend the deadline for answers to  PJM’s
IRAS filing to the close of business on Monday, September 14, 2026.
