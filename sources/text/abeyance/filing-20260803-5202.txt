I.
WAPA BACKGROUND
WAPA is a power marketing administration of the U.S. Department of Energy.  WAPA
was created in 1977 by section 302(a)(1)(E) and (F) of the Department of Energy Organization
Act.  WAPA’s mission is to market and deliver reliable, renewable, cost-based federal
hydroelectric power, transmission, and related services within a 15-state region of the central and
western United States.  WAPA was established to assume the marketing functions of power
generated by the various dams owned and operated by the U.S. Department of Interior, Bureau of
Reclamation (Reclamation), and the U.S. Army Corp of Engineers.  WAPA is engaged in,
among other things, marketing, transmitting, and arranging for delivery of power to its customers
– which include Reclamation’s project-use loads and WAPA’s other electric service customers.
WAPA markets power to preference entities who are generally nonprofits including public utility
districts, municipalities, water and irrigation districts, federal and state agencies, rural electric
cooperatives, and Native American Tribes.  WAPA also owns, operates, and maintains high
voltage transmission systems to facilitate delivery of its power to its customers, and sells excess
transmission capacity.
WAPA’s Sierra Nevada Region (WAPA-SN) sells power at wholesale to preference
customers in California from multiple hydroelectric powerplants of the Central Valley Project
(CVP) and market purchases, using the CVP transmission system.  The great majority of WAPA-
SN’s system operates as a sub-balancing authority area under the Balancing Authority of

II.
RELEVANT BACKGROUND
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, thereby initiating a
proceeding under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act.  The Show Cause Order found that the
CAISO’s Open Access Transmission Tariff (“CAISO Tariff”) and/or the TO Tariffs of each of
the CAISO Participating TOs appear to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly discriminatory or
preferential to the extent that they do not include provisions establishing the rates, terms, and
conditions for the interconnection of and service to certain large and co-located loads.  The Show
Cause Order therefore directed the CAISO and the CAISO Participating TOs to either explain
how their respective tariffs remain just and reasonable without specific provisions for the
interconnection of large and co-located loads or to develop such provisions.3

III.
JUSTIFICATION FOR RESCISSION
WAPA is a non-public utility that is exempt from the Commission’s jurisdiction pursuant
to Section 201(f) of the Federal Power Act.4  The Commission acknowledged that the Show
Cause Order included Participating TOs that are not subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction and
explained that it would “rescind the show cause order” against such entities at their request,
provided that the CAISO “respond[s] to this order regarding the non-public utility’s CAISO
Tariff provisions on file with the Commission.”5  Accordingly, as a non-public utility, WAPA
requests that the Commission rescind the Show Cause Order against it.
WAPA has and will continue to coordinate and cooperate with the CAISO in responding
to the Show Cause Order, to the extent that the CAISO requests assistance from WAPA and
other non-public utilities.  WAPA has conferred with the CAISO in advance of the filing of this
motion. In accordance with WAPA policy and practice, if in the course of WAPA’s continued
coordination with the CAISO that it determines changes to its Tariff are needed to address the
issues in the Show Cause Order, WAPA will file proposed revisions to its Tariff with the
Commission in due course.
