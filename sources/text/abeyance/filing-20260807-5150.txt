COMMENTS OF THE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION OF THE STATE OF
MISSOURI IN SUPPORT OF THE JOINT MOVANTS’ REQUEST FOR ABEYANCE

Pursuant to Rule 20081 and the Notice Establishing Answer Period issued in this docket
on August 4, 2026, by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (Commission or FERC), the
Public Service Commission of the State of Missouri (Missouri PSC) hereby submits these
comments in support of the request for abeyance filed by the Southwest Power Pool, Inc. (SPP)
and various transmission owners (collectively, Joint Movants) on August 3, 2026.
I.  Summary of Comments

The Commission should grant the Joint Movants’ request for a 95-day abeyance in order
to accommodate SPP’s stakeholder process.  Although the Commission said it will disfavor a
request for an abeyance exceeding 90 days, SPP’s Board of Directors meeting is scheduled for
November 17, 2026—one day outside the 90-day window.  The Joint Movants’ requested 95-day
abeyance will allow SPP to complete its stakeholder and approval process for its compliance
filing in this docket, including input from state regulators.  The requested abeyance will allow
SPP’s stakeholder community adequate time to address important issues highlighted by the
Commission and represents only a de minimus extension beyond the Commission’s 90-day
timeline.  Therefore, the Joint Movants demonstrated good cause for the requested abeyance and
it should be granted.

III.  Background

The Missouri PSC is a “state commission” as defined in Section 1.101(k) of the
Commission’s Rules of General Applicability.2  Missouri law authorizes the Missouri PSC to
regulate rates, charges and terms of service for the sale and distribution of electricity by public
utilities to consumers in the state.3  The Missouri PSC is an active member of the SPP Regional
State Committee (RSC).

The Commission issued its Order Instituting Proceeding Under Section 206 of the
Federal Power Act (Show Cause Order) in this docket on June 18, 2026.4  The Commission
made a preliminary finding that SPP’s existing tariff appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or
unduly discriminatory or preferential in that it lacks certain provisions.5  The Commission
recognized and applauded the steps SPP and its stakeholders have already taken to address the
addition of large and co-located loads to the transmission system.6  The Commission also noted,

In the Show Cause Order, the Commission said it will consider requests from SPP and/or

On August 3, 2026, the Joint Movants filed a request for abeyance.10  The Joint Movants
asked to hold the proceedings in abeyance for 95 days, up to and including November 20, 2026.
The additional five days is necessary to accommodate SPP’s full stakeholder process,
culminating in a Board of Directors meeting scheduled for November 17, 2026.
IV.  Comments of the Missouri PSC

The Joint Movants have demonstrated good cause for the requested 95-day abeyance.
Commission Rule 2008 provides that “the time by which any person is required… to act under
any… order may be extended by the decisional authority for good cause, upon a motion made
before the expiration of the period prescribed… .”  On April 14, 2025, for example, the
Commission granted SPP a 12-month extension of time to submit filings in compliance with the
Commission’s Order No. 1920 to accommodate its comprehensive stakeholder process.11
Similar good cause exists to grant the much more limited 95-day abeyance requested here.

The issues identified by the Commission involve numerous interrelated aspects of SPP’s
tariff.  Developing revisions that meet the Commission’s requirements will need careful technical
analysis and input from the various members and stakeholder groups affected by these changes.
The requested 95-day abeyance will allow the stakeholders to refine the proposals and present
them to the SPP Board for full consideration while maintaining the Commission’s expedited
timeline.
Issues related to transparency and the avoidance of cost shifting12 are particularly
important to the Missouri PSC and other state regulators who bear the responsibility of setting
retail rates that include FERC-jurisdictional transmission costs.  The Missouri PSC is an active
participant in the RSC13 and the Cost Control and Allocation Review and Evaluation (CARE)
Team, both of which will play a role in analyzing and refining SPP’s compliance tariff revisions.
The CARE Team in particular will evaluate potential responses to the Commission’s concerns
regarding multiple potential approaches to cost mitigation and cost shifting.14  The requested
abeyance will allow the CARE Team time to complete this important work and present its
recommendations to the Board.
Along with these important benefits, the requested 95-day extension represents only a
minor extension of the Commission’s preferred 90-day extension timeline.  As the Commission
noted in its Show Cause Order, SPP and its stakeholders have been national leaders in
developing new approaches to integrating large loads into the transmission system.  Allowing
SPP and its stakeholders additional time to fully evaluate and approve the remaining work will
