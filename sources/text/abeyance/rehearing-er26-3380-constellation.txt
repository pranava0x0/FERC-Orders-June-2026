B.
The Commission Did Not Justify Mandatory Price-Taker Treatment
1.
The Commission Failed to Engage in a Reasoned Balancing Analysis
In its Order, the Commission rejected the arguments of Constellation and other commenters
objecting to the RBP Proposal’s price taker requirement, stating that “the Commission has
consistently found that Regional Transmission Organizations and Independent System Operators
can reasonably require resources that are compensated outside of the capacity market because they
are needed to maintain reliability to offer as price-takers in capacity auctions to avoid double
procurement, even at the risk of price suppression.”26
Notably, as support for its finding, the Commission cited to its RMR precedent, without
providing any additional analysis. The Order’s reliance on RMR precedent does not supply the
missing analysis.27  Unlike in the RMR proceedings, in the Order the Commission failed to engage
in a reasoned balancing analysis to determine whether it is just and reasonable in these
circumstances to require out-of-market resources to offer as price takers.  Any such meaningful
analysis would find that on balance the facts presented here, unlike in the RMR proceedings, weigh
in favor of finding that the RBP Proposal’s price taker requirement is unjust and unreasonable
given the material and potentially long-lived effect it may have on RPM price formation for years
to come.

2.
The Order Did Not Address the Matched Isolation Mechanism,
Which Was Supported by Expert Testimony
PJM justified mandatory price-taker treatment as necessary to prevent RPM from procuring
capacity already secured through the RBP. Paragraph 53 accepted that rationale without adequately
considering the countervailing market and long-term resource adequacy impacts of such approach
or addressing the mechanism Constellation proposed.
PJM suggested that RBP resources must be offered into RPM as a price-taker to ensure
load does not “double pay” for capacity. But as Constellation explained in previous filings, the
price-taker requirement will undermine RPM as a credible price-forming market and resource
adequacy will be hindered. A mandatory $0/MW-day offer is not just or reasonable, and does more
harm than good, because it will signal excess supply if anticipated load is delayed or does not show

As Constellation explained in its Limited Protest, these results can be avoided, and load
can still be protected from the double payment, if instead PJM isolates the RBP supply and

Constellation did not propose removing RBP supply while leaving the full associated
demand requirement in RPM. For each applicable auction, Constellation’s proposed mechanism
would exclude Available RBP UCAP from the price-forming supply stack and reduce the
applicable reliability requirement by the lesser of Available RBP UCAP and RBP-Related Load
reflected in that auction’s Planning Parameters. The mechanism therefore prevents duplicate
procurement for the matched quantity by adjusting both supply and demand, while avoiding the
one-sided price-forming effect of mandatory zero offers.

Shanker’s supporting analysis and Constellation’s recommended proposal to isolate RBP supply

In its Order, the Commission found that the proposed RBP Transmission Owner exit rules
may be unjust and unreasonable, and set the issue for paper hearing.  Constellation seeks
clarification that the Order neither established a presumption that LSEs in an exiting Transmission
Owner’s zone must bear RBP exit charges nor predetermined the allocation of such charges. To
the extent the Order intended otherwise, the Commission acted arbitrarily and capriciously and
rehearing is warranted.
The fact that an LSE may have benefited from the original RBP procurement does not
establish that it caused every incremental cost arising from a Transmission Owner’s later
withdrawal. The Transmission Owner controls the withdrawal decision; the affected LSEs
generally cannot prevent, predict, or hedge that event. An exiting Transmission Owner should bear
its appropriate cost burden of generation and load investments made under the assumption that the
Transmission Owner would remain in PJM.46 At minimum, costs attributable to the withdrawal
decision should not be shifted automatically to LSEs merely because those LSEs benefited from

LSE responsibility, rehearing is warranted.
