MISO’S LARGE LOAD AND COMPUTATIONAL LOAD DEFINITIONS ARE JUST AND
REASONABLE.

1.
The 50 MW Large Load threshold is reasonable, administratively
clear, and consistent with emerging industry practice.

The Commission should accept MISO’s Large Load definition as just and reasonable. As
explained below, the 50 MW threshold — measured at Points of Withdrawal for facilities located
at a single substation— (1) provides an objective, administrable screen for Large Loads; and (2)
reflects specific characteristics of MISO’s transmission system; and (3) ensures necessary
visibility through Section 37A; and (4) does so without a requiring a separate voltage criterion
because applicability is already defined at the Transmission System boundary.
First, MISO proposes to define a Large Load as Load with Demand of 50 MW or more
associated with a facility or aggregation of facilities located at a single substation behind one or
more Points of Withdrawal from the Transmission System and owned, operated, or controlled by
a single entity or a coordinating group of entities; Load used exclusively for charging an Electric
Storage Resource is excluded.28  MISO determined 50 MW was the appropriate threshold based
on emerging industry practices, the reliability needs of the MISO region, and stakeholder
feedback.29  For example, Southwest Power Pool’s (“SPP”) Commission-accepted HILL
framework uses a 50 MW threshold to evaluate reliability impacts and operational requirements

The IRR Proposal should be accepted as filed.  It responds directly to the Commission’s
identified operational-reliability concerns, establishes enforceable Tariff obligations for visibility,
