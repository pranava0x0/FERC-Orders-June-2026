Dear Ms. Reese:

PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”), pursuant to section 205 of the Federal Power
Act (“FPA”), 16 U.S.C. § 824d, hereby submits revisions to the PJM Open Access
Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”) and the Reliability Assurance Agreement Among Load
Serving Entities (“RAA”) to establish the Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”)—a
one-time procurement of new capacity to address resource adequacy challenges in the PJM
Region1 arising from insufficient commitments of capacity in the 2027/2028 and
2028/2029 Reliability Pricing Model (“RPM”) Auctions, primarily driven by the
unprecedented addition of large loads, most notably data centers, in the PJM Region.  The
RBP framework, as the PJM Board of Managers (“PJM Board”) recognized in a
January 16, 2026 letter,2 is one part of a multi-pronged, ongoing effort by PJM to respond
to the challenges stemming from the influx of large loads into the PJM Region.  It is also

shortfall and avoids situations where load could effectively pay twice for capacity.  The

with PJM’s ability to maintain reliable operation of the system.

new load recognizes that the Initial RBP Target, based on the PJM Region Reliability
Requirement shortfall of 6,831.3 MW, is derived from the forecasted demand for the period
from 2027 up through the start of the 2028/2029 Delivery Year (or, for purposes of Demand
Resources and DER Aggregation Resources, through the second Incremental Auction).

$/MW-day UCAP.  Because the RBP is pay-as-bid, each committed RBP Resource has an
individualized “strike price.”
A committed RBP Resource must offer all committed RBP UCAP MW as a price
taker in the RPM, unless the resource qualifies for an exception under the rules governing
a committed RBP Resource participating in the RPM.  The “market price” is the RBP
Resource’s unit-specific weighted average resource clearing price (“WARCP”) of all RPM
Auctions in which the RBP Resource participated for the relevant Delivery Year, expressed
as $/MW-day UCAP.212
If the strike price of the total MW eligible to settle as a CfD is higher than the
market price, the buyer (load) must pay the difference between the strike price and market
price.  Alternatively, if the strike price of the total MW eligible to settle as a CfD is lower
than the market price the seller (RBP Resource) must pay back the difference between the
strike price and market price.
The proposed CfD settlement structure is just and reasonable.  First, the CfD
structure protects load from paying RBP Resources a windfall, while also protecting RBP
Resources from settling below market prices.  Thus, the CfD structure provides stability
during the lengthy term of an RBP Resource commitment and limits the potential risk of
long-term exposure.
Second, the CfD structure operates on the principle that payments for providing
RBP UCAP MW should net out the market revenues from the RPM.  In other words, CfD

RBP Settlement Credits and RBP Charges
RBP Resources that clear in the RBP are assessed RBP Settlement Credits, a daily
value measured in $/day UCAP,216 which may be positive (indicating a payment to supply)
or negative (indicating a reduction in payment to supply).
Load allocated responsibility for RBP UCAP MW will be allocated RBP Charges,
a daily value measured in $/day UCAP, which may likewise be positive (a reduction in
cost to load) or negative (a cost to load).217
For example, in a settlement where an RBP Resource is assigned a positive
$10,000/Day RBP Settlement Credit, load is assigned a corresponding negative $10,000
RBP Charge.
Likewise, in accordance with the rules in place today, all RBP UCAP MW that
offer and clear in the RPM will be assessed RPM Auction Credits, while load will be
assessed RPM Charges.

D.
Treatment of RBP Commitment in Event of Transmission Owner Exit
During Term of the Commitment (Proposed Tariff, Attachment DD,
section 18.7)
As RBP commitments will remain in place through the 2042/2043 Delivery Year,
i.e., for a fixed, 15-year term, PJM is also proposing rules that set forth the responsibilities
of various parties in the event a Transmission Owner exits from PJM.  While it is true that
no Transmission Owner has formally sought to exit PJM at this time, should such an event
occur during the term of an RBP commitment in an exiting Transmission Owner’s territory,

--- PAGE 98 ---
Honorable Debbie-Anne A. Reese
July 31, 2026
Page 91

clear and enforceable rules will ensure that RBP commitments are honored through the
term of the agreement.
Establishing those rules now, with the RBP proposal, will provide the requisite
notice of how RBP commitments will be honored and recognizes that a primary incentive
for developers to participate in the RBP is the cost certainty afforded developers in a
15-year commitment, which will aid the financing RBP Resources that can be developed
and placed in service quickly.  Without this 15-year guarantee, the financing terms and
conditions associated with developing an RBP Resource would likely be significantly
different, alter the total cost to build the resource, and potentially result in fewer resources
being offered into the RBP at higher prices.  Therefore, however remote the possibility of
a Transmission Owner exiting PJM during or prior to the 2042/2043 Delivery Year, it is
imperative that rules are in place to ensure that, even in the event of a Transmission Owner
exit, committed RBP Resources will be made whole for the entire term of the commitment.
If a Transmission Owner leaves PJM and the load taken with the Transmission
Owner is commensurate with the RBP Resources that are located in that Transmission
Owner’s territory, the Transmission Owner is expected to honor the remaining term of the
RBP commitment since it would need such resources to serve the loads in its service
territory.  However, in the event a Transmission Owner exits PJM and committed RBP
Resources are physically in such Transmission Owner’s service territory, it would be
unreasonable for PJM’s Members to continue paying for such excess RBP Resources if

--- PAGE 99 ---

they no longer can provide capacity to PJM (e.g., if the resource cannot be pseudo-tied into
PJM as further explained below).219
Similarly, in the event a Transmission Owner exits PJM with the net load that is
removed with the Transmission Owner’s departure, it would be unreasonable for PJM’s
remaining load to pay for the excess committed RBP Resources that remain in the PJM
Region.  To address these potential scenarios, proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, sections
18.7(a) and 18.7(b) put committed RBP Resources back in the same position they would
have been in had the associated Transmission Owner not exited PJM.
1.
Committed RBP Resources in exiting Transmission Owner territory
(proposed Tariff, Attachment DD, section 18.7(a))
Where a Transmission Owner exits PJM and committed RBP Resources are
physically in that Transmission Owner’s service territory, the Transmission Owner may,
prior to exiting PJM, enter into a separate agreement with the impacted RBP Resources
that would maintain appropriate rates, terms and conditions of the RBP commitments.220
The Transmission Owner effectively steps into PJM’s role in the RBP context.  In this way,
the Transmission Owner that leaves PJM will be responsible for honoring the commitment
to pay the impacted RBP Resources that may be located in such Transmission Owner’s
service territory for the remaining term of the commitment.  At the same time, the existence
of such an agreement would relieve PJM of any applicable RBP Obligations (“RBO”) made
to RBP Resources that are subject to such agreements.
