ALTERNATIVE REQUEST FOR REHEARING
If the Commission denies the clarification requested in section II.B above, AMP
seeks rehearing of the September 29 Order pursuant to FPA section 31338 and
Commission Rule 713.39 The Commission should grant rehearing of its determination that

approach.

1.
The Commission’s rejection of PJM’s RBP cost allocation
method was based on an apparent misunderstanding of that
method.
As discussed above, the Commission’s principal rationale for rejecting PJM’s
proposed cost allocation method was that PJM’s reliance “on Load Adjustments to
measure load growth may be inconsistent or not reasonably capture forecasted load
growth or actual large load growth.”43 While the Commission reasonably determined that
the cost causation principle requires RBP costs to be allocated based on “large load
growth,” the Commission erred in concluding that PJM’s as-filed proposal would not
accomplish this aim. The Commission’s conclusion appears to have been based, in
significant part, on a misunderstanding of the PJM methodology. As a consequence, the
Commission’s decision is not the product of reasoned decision making.44
The Commission expressed concern that, based on PJM’s discussion of its load
forecasting process in a prior proceeding, “it appears some large loads may never have
been accounted for as Load Adjustments because PJM accounted for them when
calculating the initial load forecast.”45 This observation misapprehends the PJM Load
Adjustment methodology used in allocating the Initial RBP Target. PJM’s proposed RBP
cost allocation method was based on the Load Adjustments in Table B9b in the 2026 PJM

The Load Adjustments reflected in Table B9b, which was developed after PJM’s filing in
Docket ER24-2447, are intended to include new large loads that might otherwise have
been reflected in the underlying econometric forecast. PJM has described51 the basic
methodology for Table B9b Load Adjustments to be as follows: First, PJM identifies zones
with existing data center loads. Second, PJM removes monthly data center load from the
commercial Energy Information Administration data underlying the load forecast. Third,
PJM removes hourly data center load from historical loads used in PJM’s model
estimation. Finally, total accepted load is added to the load forecast. Thus, PJM removes
large loads from the underlying forecast data and adds large loads back into the forecast
through the Load Adjustments.52 Table B9b is intended to capture the difference between
the final forecast and what the final forecast would be without any large load, not just
without Load Adjustment requests above the embedded value.

robust independent analysis to ensure that the proposals were evaluated based on
