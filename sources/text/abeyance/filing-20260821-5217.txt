I.
Introduction
Before the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC or Commission), the
Pennsylvania Office of Consumer Advocate (PA OCA) files this Protest in response to the PJM
Interconnection L.L.C.’s (PJM’s) July 31, 2026, Federal Power Act (FPA) Section 205 filing
containing revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (Tariff) and the Reliability
Assurance Agreement Among Load Serving Entities (RAA) to establish the Reliability Backstop
Procurement (RBP)1—a one-time procurement of new capacity to address resource adequacy
challenges in the PJM Region arising from insufficient commitments of capacity in the 2027/2028
and 2028/2029 Reliability Pricing Model (RPM) Auctions, primarily driven by the unprecedented
addition of large loads, most notably data centers.
The PA OCA represents the interests of Pennsylvania utility consumers or ratepayers before
FERC.2 “Ratepayers” refers to the millions of retail end users that are served by the PJM grid.

III.
Summary of Cost Allocation Portion of PJM’s Tariff Filing
In its filing, PJM proposes that the states or state utility commissions (PJM refers to state
utility commissions as the “Relevant Electric Retail Regulatory Authority” or “RERRA”)3
determine allocation of cleared RBP unforced capacity (UCAP) megawatts (MW) to LSEs,
through the RBO methods, within each Electric Distributor’s zone/area, as reported by each
Electric Distributor to PJM. If the Electric Distributors do not report the RERRA-approved RBO
methods to PJM, PJM proposes to allocate RBP costs to LSEs using existing FERC-approved PLC
values within the relevant transmission zone.
More specifically, PJM proposes the following as to cost allocation:
PJM is proposing that cost allocation to LSEs within an Electric
Distributor’s zone/area be based on “the state and/or RERRA-determined
allocation of cleared RBP UCAP MW to LSEs within each Electric Distributor’s
zone/area, as reported by each Electric Distributor to [PJM].” PJM has thus created

IV.
FERC’s Exclusive Jurisdiction to Determine PLC and NSPL Value Methodologies
In its filing, PJM does not recognize current FERC authority over PLC and NSPL value
methodologies. As is established in more detail below in this section, FERC currently asserts
jurisdiction over approving PLC and jurisdictionally analogous NSPL value methodologies, which
are set by transmission owners through voluntary OATT Attachment M-2 filings, made pursuant
to FPA Section 205. As a result, the PJM transmission owners, not Electric Distributors, establish
methods for determining PLC and NSPL values within their transmission zones, not Electric
Distributor zones. FERC currently permits the transmission owners to voluntarily file tariff
attachments to PJM’s OATT, Attachment M-2s, which establish and specify these methods for
determining PLC and NSPL values for individual retail customers for purposes of wholesale cost
allocation to LSEs.
FERC, PJM, and public utilities have placed PLC (capacity) and NSPL (transmission)
methodologies into the same category. For example, when a utility files an Attachment M-2 with

transmission peak value. An LSE's Network Service Peak Load is equal to its total
hourly load during the FirstEnergy zone’s highest peak value.9

and, by natural extension, PJM’s proposed RBP methodology for capacity. Because NSPL, PLC,

NSPL Determinations for Transmission Costs Allocations to LSEs

While many transmission owners have filed their methodologies for determining

VI.
Conclusion
In conclusion, FERC should find that PJM’s cost allocation proposal, within its RBP Tariff
filing, is unjust and unreasonable because it presents a jurisdictional problem and insufficiently
defines the Reliability Backstop Obligation method. FERC should remedy the unjust and
unreasonable tariff by (1) declaring FERC’s exclusive jurisdiction to determine RBO value
methodologies; (2) determining that PJM’s cost allocation proposal for RBP obligations is unjust
and unreasonable because it insufficiently defines the RBO method; (3) requiring jurisdictional
transmission owners within PJM to submit additional filings based on this docket to further define
the RBO method for determining individual customer’s RBO values within each transmission
owner zone; (4) mandating that the transmission owners make conforming Attachment M-2 tariff
filings under FPA Section 205, containing the RBO value methodologies (and PLC/NSPL value
methodologies); and (5) setting a time frame to resolve protests and comments to the transmission
owners’ tariff filings regarding the RBO methodologies.

Figure 3 - Concern Over State Apportionment of MW Obligation
OCA’s concern centers over the lack of state jurisdiction to establish an as-yet-to-be-developed
method of assigning reliability backstop obligation (RBO) MWs to retail customers.  This is shown
in the lower lefthand corner of Figure 3.  As compared to Figure 1 and Figure 2 – where assignment
of MWs is subject to federal jurisdiction per Amtrak v PPL – PJM now proposes to have states
establish this method. If states lack the jurisdiction to establish this method, then PJM will
implement its backstop cost allocation method that will allocate reliability backstop costs to all
consumers based on the PLC method. This result – which is outside of the state’s control due to
jurisdictional boundaries – would be unjust and unreasonable.
