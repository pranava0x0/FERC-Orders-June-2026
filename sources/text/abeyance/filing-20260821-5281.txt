PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.’s (“PJM’s”) application in this case to implement a one-
time Reliability Backstop Procurement (“RBP”) program to acquire new generating capacity
needed to serve forecasted Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) and other large data center loads should
not be adopted.1 It will unreasonably shift the risks and costs associated with acquiring that new
capacity from the data center loads to existing consumers in Ohio and elsewhere in PJM.  The
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC”) should reject the filing because it fails to
protect Ohio consumers and other consumers in PJM from the risks associated with this new
forecasted load, and from the associated costs of serving these loads.  PJM’s proposal will result
in excessive, unjust, unreasonable and unduly discriminatory and preferential rates for existing
consumers.
In the alternative, FERC should set PJM’s filing for evidentiary hearings and/or
settlement proceedings so that PJM and stakeholders can adequately consider and vet alternative
proposals for acquiring needed new supply for AI and other large data center loads, including

II.
PROTEST
OCC protests PJM’s filing because it will not minimize costs for consumer, nor
accomplish its objectives of acquiring new generating capacity in the region in a manner that
allocates risks and costs for the new capacity to those causing the costs to be incurred, i.e., AI
and large data center loads.  Instead, the proposal will result in shifting these risks and costs to
existing consumers who have not caused the need for new generating capacity and who are
unlikely to use the generating capacity provided by these new resources.  The risks and costs
include the realistic uncertainty that either generating capacity or the forecasted AI or large data
center loads will not show up.  Moreover, the proposal inappropriately and unreasonably relies
on state laws and regulations, that may not exist in many states, to ensure that these costs and
risks in fact are borne by the AI and large data center loads.
Ohio has no statutes governing allocation of large load-related generation capacity costs
among retail customers.  And while there is one case in which the Public Utilities Commission of
Ohio (“PUCO”) has directed allocation of some of these large load driven costs and risks to the
large load data center customers, there are no uniform regulations and tariffs that apply to all
utilities and LSEs in the state.25  Thus, PJM’s proposed RBP program leaves many Ohio
consumers at significant risk of bearing costs associated with these new large data center loads.
In contrast, the backstop procurement proposal prepared by the PJM IMM and proposals
discussed by other stakeholders in PJM would directly allocate the risks and costs of this new

C. The IMM proposed approach to resource adequacy in PJM will ensure
direct allocation of the risk and costs of needed new generation to the
responsible AI and large data center loads in the region.
On June 30, 2026, Monitoring Analytics, the PJM IMM, posted on its website its
proposal for resolving the resource inadequacy concerns created by the shortages in the
2027/2028 and 2028/2029 Delivery Years.  The IMM submitted this proposal to the PJM Critical
Issue Fast Path stakeholder process as an alternative to the PJM RBP proposal.42  As the IMM
states, “[t]he most important criterion for evaluating proposals to address how to reliably serve
new data center load is the assignment of the costs and risks associated with LSEs serving data
center load.”43  The IMM explains that “the failure to exclude new data center load from the
BRAs [RPM base residual auctions], as PJM proposes, would by definition impose significant
costs on other customers and therefore violate the basic principle that data center costs should
not be imposed on other customers.”44

III.
CONCLUSION
FERC may accept or reject a utility filing under section 205 of the Federal Power Act, 16
U.S.C. 824d, depending on whether  the utility has demonstrated that its proposal is just,
reasonable, and not unduly discriminatory or preferential.59  However, as PJM notes in its RBP
Application, FERC may not modify substantial elements of the proposal without PJM’s
consent.60
 Although OCC seeks rejection of PJM’s proposal in this case, OCC agrees that
something must be done soon to address the resource adequacy concerns in the PJM region over
the next few years.  Consequently, OCC urges that if FERC accepts the filing, it should do so
subject to refund and set the filing for hearing and/or settlement proceedings with directives that
the parties use the IMM RBP proposal as a starting point.
Such a process will provide stakeholders an opportunity, subject to Settlement Judge
oversight, to develop a just, reasonable and not unduly discriminatory or preferential solution
that will protect existing consumers in Ohio and elsewhere from the unreasonable shift of AI and
large data center load risks and costs to them, and from excessive, unjust, unreasonable and
unduly discriminatory and preferential rates.
