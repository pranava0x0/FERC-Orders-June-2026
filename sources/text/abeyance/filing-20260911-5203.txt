MOTION FOR ACCEPTANCE OF LATE-FILED COMMENTS AND
COMMENTS IN CONDITIONAL SUPPORT OF
THE ORGANIZATION OF MISO STATES, INC.

The Organization of MISO States, Inc. (“OMS”) submits this Notice of Intervention and
Conditional Comments in Support in response to proposed tariff revisions filed by the
Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (“MISO”) with the Federal Energy Regulatory
Commission (“Commission”) in the above-captioned proceeding.1 On August 18, 2026, MISO
filed proposed revisions to its Open Access Transmission, Energy, and Operating Reserves
Markets Tariff (“Tariff”) to establish a Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement
(“ZGIA”), associated study process and operational requirements, and market and resource
adequacy rules to accommodate Zero-Injection Generation Resources (“ZG Resource”) that intend
to serve load interconnecting at the same substation as the Generating Facility. OMS cautiously
supports the ZGIA proposal but highlights several critical reliability and resource adequacy
concerns for the Commission’s consideration.

II. COMMENTS
Over the course of the year, MISO and its stakeholders have worked to develop an
interconnection and study process that facilitates the expedited integration of large loads and
proximately located generation while maintaining system reliability and protecting customers from
unnecessary costs. As recognized by the Commission in its Show Cause Order, rapidly increasing
demand from data centers and other large loads presents new challenges for transmission planning,
generator interconnection, resource adequacy, and reliable system operations.4
OMS conditionally supports MISO’s Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement
(“ZGIA”) framework as a potential tool for addressing these challenges. However, MISO’s ZGIA
framework should be implemented only with appropriate safeguards to ensure that expedited
interconnection does not create increased reliability risks, preferential treatment, or costs shifted

OMS’s position has evolved through substantial review of MISO’s ZGIA proposal and

A. OMS Supports ZGIA as a Potential Tool to Interconnect Large Loads, Subject to
Appropriate Safeguards.

MISO began developing the ZGIA framework with the recognition that co-located
arrangements were necessary to quickly integrate large loads and their associated generation while
shielding existing customers from any resulting costs.5 The ZGIA framework is further premised
on the understanding that a generator that serves a co-located load and is prohibited from injecting
beyond the interconnecting substation may not present the same transmission impacts as a

conventional generating facility seeking firm interconnection service. As such, a properly designed
ZGIA process could reduce unnecessary network upgrades, improve utilization of existing
infrastructure, and facilitate timely development of generation and large loads.
OMS recognizes the potential value of ZGIA and the importance of practical pathways for
large load integration. The Commission’s Show Cause Order demonstrates that existing processes
may need to evolve to accommodate rapidly changing demand. MISO’s ZGIA Filing likewise
explains that ZGIA is intended to provide a defined interconnection and study process for
generation serving load at the same substation and voltage level while prohibiting injection onto
the broader transmission system. Therefore, OMS does not oppose the ZGIA Filing.
While OMS does not oppose the ZGIA Filing, the Commission should determine whether
the proposed ZGIA framework adequately protects the reliability and economic interests of
existing customers and whether the treatment of ZGIA resources is consistent with MISO’s
broader resource adequacy, market, transmission planning, and transmission service constructs.
OMS recommends that the Commission approve ZGIA only if those protections are clear and
enforceable.
B. Reliability Protections Must Be Fundamental to the ZGIA Framework.
The defining characteristic of ZGIA is the prohibition on injection beyond the
interconnecting substation. That limitation must be treated as a reliability requirement, not merely
a contractual condition. OMS remains concerned that financial penalties alone may not adequately
protect against inadvertent injection, no matter how momentary or transitory. Relevant
transmission system conditions should be studied prior to ZGIA execution rather than relying
solely on post-event penalties.

MISO appears to appreciate the importance of this distinction as its ZGIA Filing requires
detailed modeling of the generator, associated load, and protection facilities as well as proposed
operating requirements necessary to prevent injections beyond the interconnecting substation.
OMS remains concerned, however, that MISO has deferred establishing an explicit definition of
the appropriate Transient Tolerance Window to the development of its Business Practice Manual.6
It is paramount that the studies and the Transient Tolerance Window be developed and conducted
with sufficient rigor to ensure that ZG Resources do not cause adverse impacts to system reliability.
OMS recommends that the Commission ensure those operating requirements are
sufficiently specific, enforceable, and capable of physically or operationally preventing prohibited
injection. Depending on the facility, such mechanisms could include physical controls, generator
drawback controls, remedial action schemes, or other technologies and operating requirements
MISO determines are necessary. OMS also recommends that the ZGIA study process evaluate
both sides of the generation-load relationship, including but not limited to:
• the ZGIA generator tripping offline;
• the associated load tripping offline;
• the associated load rapidly reducing and increasing output;
• the generator being required to rapidly reduce output following a load reduction;
• simultaneous or correlated outages of the generator and load; and
• circumstances in which the generator and load do not behave as assumed in the
interconnection study.

C. The Initial ZGIA Framework Should Be Limited to Generation and Load at the
Same Substation and Voltage Level.

OMS continues to support a narrowly defined ZGIA framework. MISO’s ZGIA Filing
limits ZG Interconnection Service to generation and associated load located at the same substation
and voltage level. MISO explains that this can include configurations in which the generation and
load have separate points of interconnection or withdrawal at a common substation, provided the
required limitations on injection are maintained. 7  OMS supports this narrow approach and
recommends that the Commission retain the common-substation limitation.
OMS recognizes that MISO considered both a strict same-POI approach and broader
electrically proximate configurations (e.g., campus approach). MISO rejected the latter for this
proposal because of unresolved reliability, transmission service, and jurisdictional issues but
intends to address electrically proximate configurations through its forthcoming Large Load
Addition Resource Study (“LARS”) process. OMS agrees that those broader configurations should

D. MISO Must Prevent Cost Shifting When the Associated Generation or Load Does
Not Materialize.

A central benefit of ZGIA is the ability to avoid or reduce network upgrades that otherwise
could be required if associated load were evaluated without the ZG generation. That benefit
depends upon the continued existence and operation of the generation relied upon in the load study.
OMS supports MISO’s requirements for an agreement between the generator and associated load
demonstrating that the full output of the generating facility is intended to serve the associated load,
with an expected period of at least five years.8 MISO explains that this requirement is intended to
prevent speculative requests and provide sufficient certainty for MISO to rely on the generation as
an alternative mitigation in transmission-service studies.9
MISO also recognizes that if the generating facility ceases to serve the associated load,
MISO may need to restudy the load’s transmission impacts and potentially delay or suspend
service while an alternative mitigation is identified. Those provisions are important, but MISO’s
Tariff should go further by establishing clear responsibility for resulting costs. If network upgrades
subsequently become necessary because generation that supported the original study assumptions
does not materialize or ceases to operate, the costs of that incremental need should not
automatically shift to existing transmission customers.

E. Resource Adequacy Treatment Must Be Complete, Transparent, and Consistent
with Physical Capability.

OMS continues to have significant concerns regarding the treatment of ZGIA resources in
MISO’s Resource Adequacy (“RA”) construct. The fact that a ZG Resource cannot inject beyond
the interconnecting substation does not mean that it has no relevance to regional reliability.
Similarly, associated load cannot be treated as though it has disappeared simply because it is served
in part or in whole by on-site generation.
OMS recognizes that MISO’s filing proposes specific RA treatment. In particular, MISO
proposes to cap Seasonal Accredited Capacity (“SAC”) for a ZG Resource based on the applicable
portion of the Coincident Peak Demand forecast attributable to the ZG Associated Load, less SAC
assigned to that load, with an adjustment for avoided transmission losses.10 MISO also proposes

Of particular concern is whether different ZGIA-specific limitations produce inconsistent
outcomes among the various components of the RA construct. For example, OMS has identified a
potential mismatch if a ZG Resource’s availability is reduced because its associated load decreases,
but there is not a mechanism for such reductions to be accounted for or modeled in the LOLE
study. Consequently, resource class-level results in the LOLE study may over-estimate resource
performance. Although ZG Resources would have SAC constrained by the associated load’s
Coincident Peak Demand forecast, that cap takes place outside of the LOLE study. Without
addressing limitations, it appears possible that MISO could produce study results that suggest a
fleet of resources that appears capable of satisfying the PRM in one component of the RA construct

MISO should demonstrate that its methodology avoids such inconsistencies. The

F. ZGIA Should Provide a Clear Transition Path Without Circumventing the DPP.
OMS recognizes that MISO’s proposal expressly permits a ZG Resource to pursue another
form of interconnection service separately, including full integration and injection rights through
Network Resource Interconnection Service (“NRIS”) through MISO’s Definitive Planning
Process (“DPP”), with the subsequently effective service superseding ZG Interconnection Service.
OMS supports providing a clear transition path for a ZGIA resource that later seeks injection rights.
That transition, however, must not allow a ZGIA resource to circumvent or obtain an advantage
over interconnection customers already participating in the DPP queue. MISO should clearly
specify how such transitions will be sequenced, how queue position and study impacts will be
determined, and how transmission capability reserved or relied upon under ZGIA will be treated
when the resource seeks broader injection rights.
MISO characterizes ZGIA as a permanent feature in its Tariff rather than a temporary
program. Meanwhile, MISO and stakeholders are also developing separate processes for broader

G. OMS Supports Transparency and Meaningful State and Stakeholder Oversight.
The rapid development of large-load projects creates significant implications for state
regulatory commissions, load-serving entities, transmission owners, existing customers, and other
market participants. The ZGIA framework should therefore provide sufficient transparency for
regulators and stakeholders to understand:
•
the amount and characteristics of load relying on ZG Resources;
•
the amount and characteristics of associated generation;
•
the status of ZGIA projects;
•
material changes to ZGIA arrangements;
•
withdrawals or failures to materialize;
•
restudies and resulting network upgrades;
•
the treatment of ZG Resources in RA and markets; and
•
the reliability assumptions underlying ZGIA approvals.
This information is important not only for MISO’s operations and planning functions, but also for
state regulatory processes, including integrated resource planning, certificates of public
convenience and necessity, procurement proceedings, and other state proceedings concerning the
adequacy, cost, and reliability of electric service.
OMS is particularly concerned that a framework designed to accelerate large-load
development should not impair states’ ability to evaluate the costs and risks associated with serving
those loads. In its ZGIA Filing, MISO recognizes that ZGIA does not establish new transmission

H. ZGIA Should Complement, Not Displace, Broader Transmission and Large-Load
Reforms.
OMS supports ZGIA as one tool within MISO’s broader response to large load growth.
ZGIA should not, however, substitute for: cost-effective transmission expansion and regional
planning when objective and broadly distributed benefits clearly outweigh costs; improvements to
MISO’s generator and load interconnection processes; or development of appropriate large-load
transmission service.
MISO describes ZGIA as the first in a series of proposed solutions developed through the
stakeholder-led Large Load Working Group and identifies LARS as a forthcoming process for
addressing electrically proximate generation and load that do not satisfy the narrower ZGIA
requirements.11 The underlying growth in demand will continue to affect regional transmission
planning, resource adequacy, and system operations even when some large loads are paired with
generation behind the meter or at a common substation.
Consistent with OMS’s support for MISO and the MISO Transmission Owners’ abeyance
request, the Commission should therefore evaluate ZGIA as one component of a broader,
comprehensive framework for large load integration rather than as a standalone solution.12 That
framework should address the broader concerns identified in the Commission’s Show Cause Order,
including reliable and transparent transmission planning, meaningful consideration of alternative

III. CONCLUSION
OMS appreciates MISO’s efforts to develop a practical mechanism for facilitating the
timely integration of large loads and associated generation. OMS agrees that a well-designed ZGIA
framework can provide meaningful benefits by allowing generation that is dedicated to serving an
associated load to be evaluated in a manner consistent with its limited transmission-system impact.
OMS’s support, however, is conditioned on maintaining the fundamental principles of reliability,
cost causation, transparency, and equitable treatment of customers and market participants as
detailed above.
