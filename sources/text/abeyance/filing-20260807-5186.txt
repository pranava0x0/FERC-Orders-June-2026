Pursuant to Rule 2131 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“Commission”)
Rules of Practice and Procedure and the Commission’s Notice Establishing Answer Period, the
Organization of MISO States, Inc.2 (“OMS”) respectfully submits this Answer in Support of the
Motion to Hold Proceeding in Abeyance3 of the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc.
(“MISO”) and the MISO Transmission Owners (“MISO TOs”) in the above-caption proceeding.4
OMS supports the Abeyance Request because the issues identified by the Commission’s
Show Cause Order are significant, highly interconnected, and best addressed through a coordinated
stakeholder process followed by comprehensive filings under section 205 of the Federal Power
Act. An abeyance period will provide the necessary opportunity for MISO, MISO TOs, states,

I.
ANSWER
A. OMS Supports the Request to Hold this Proceeding in Abeyance.
OMS supports MISO’s and the MISO TOs’ Abeyance Request because the Commission
has identified a broad set of complex and interrelated issues that require robust participation from
all MISO stakeholders to develop durable, regionally tailored solutions. The Commission’s Show
Cause Order recognizes that the process for serving new large loads raises significant questions
concerning transmission planning including increased transparency, meaningful consideration of
alternative solutions, and the equitable assignment of costs and risks. MISO has made considerable
progress on several of these issues to date, but more time is needed.
OMS’s support is premised on the understanding that the abeyance period will be used to
develop comprehensive reforms addressing all issues identified by the Commission. The purpose
of the abeyance should not be simply to delay the proceeding, but rather to facilitate the
development of a complete record and meaningful reforms that can be evaluated as an integrated
package. To that end, OMS appreciates MISO and the MISO TOs’ recognition of the critical role

states play in managing affordability and their commitment to continued collaboration with the
states to develop and finalize solutions to the Show Cause Orders’ directives.5
A coordinated stakeholder process, followed by successive section 205 filings, provides
the best opportunity to produce reforms that are durable, regionally appropriate, and supported by
a broad set of stakeholders. Such an approach also provides stakeholders with a meaningful
opportunity to evaluate how proposed changes interact with one another before the Commission
is asked to determine whether the overall package satisfies the concerns identified in this
proceeding.
OMS also recognizes that the MISO region presents unique circumstances. While the
Commission’s inquiry was informed by developments occurring across multiple regions, including
PJM, the MISO region has distinct planning processes, regulatory structures, and load growth
patterns. Accordingly, reforms should be tailored to the operational and governance characteristics
of the MISO region while still adhering to the Commission’s objectives regarding the reliable,
efficient, and equitable interconnection of large loads.
B. The Show Cause Order Appropriately Identifies the Core Issues Requiring
Reform.
The Commission’s Show Cause Order appropriately focuses attention on issues that states
within the MISO region have increasingly observed as requests to interconnect and serve new large
loads continue to accelerate. States have attempted to properly align the costs and risks presented
by large loads using the regulatory tools available, but a comprehensive solution requires concerted
coordination and partnership between state and federal regulators. These developments have
significant implications not only for customers seeking service, but also for existing customers that

may ultimately bear the costs and reliability risks associated with investments required to
accommodate new large load customers.
OMS appreciates the Commission’s focus on large load interconnection issues including:
•
improving transparency regarding transmission costs associated with serving new large
loads;
•
addressing the potential for inappropriate cost shifting when anticipated load does not
ultimately materialize or materializes at a different scale or schedule than expected;
•
evaluating circumstances in which costs may shift among load-serving entities within a
transmission pricing zone;
•
ensuring that transmission-related reliability risks associated with delayed or uncertain
load development are appropriately considered;
•
improving transparency surrounding transmission service studies and study assumptions;
•
enhancing transparency throughout the transmission study process generally;
•
evaluating flexible transmission service options where appropriate;
•
considering the role of Grid-Enhancing Technologies and other Alternative Transmission
Technologies to efficiently address new load requests; and
•
addressing any additional issues identified by the Commission relating to the equitable
allocation of costs and the efficient integration of new large loads.

Several of these issues have been the subject of ongoing stakeholder discussions within
MISO. OMS appreciates MISO’s proactive leadership to develop workable solutions to these
issues, but we note that workstreams prioritized by MISO and stakeholders to date have been
limited to those issues supported by parties largely interested in developing and serving large loads.
The Commission’s Show Cause Order now provides an important opportunity to evaluate the
additional issues necessary to ensure MISO’s large load framework addresses the affordability,
reliability, transparency, and cost allocation concerns that are important to states, consumers, and
other stakeholders. OMS agrees that this broader review is both timely and appropriate, and we
expect that MISO will work with states on how best to prioritize these issues.

As MISO continues to evaluate reforms intended to facilitate service for new large loads,
it is equally important that the stakeholder process fully address the corresponding concerns of
existing customers, state regulators, and consumer advocates regarding affordability, reliability,
transparency, and equitable cost allocation. The Show Cause Order appropriately broadens the
discussion, beyond simply facilitating new service requests, to ensure that the resulting framework
fairly balances all affected interests.
C. OMS Intends to Participate in Developing Comprehensive Solutions.
OMS intends to participate throughout the MISO stakeholder process during the abeyance
period. States have a significant interest in ensuring that reforms promote reliable electric service
while protecting consumers from unreasonable costs and inappropriate cost shifting.
OMS expects that meaningful, long-term solutions will require a combination of wholesale
reforms to MISO’s Tariff and complementary actions by state regulators where retail issues or
state-jurisdictional cost allocation considerations exist. A collaborative stakeholder process
provides the appropriate forum for developing these solutions before they are presented to the
Commission through section 205 filings.
OMS likewise intends to participate fully in reviewing and responding to any filings
submitted by MISO and individual MISO TOs during the abeyance period and to assist in
developing a complete record addressing the Commission’s concerns as detailed in the Show
Cause Order.
D. All Issues Identified in the Show Cause Order Should Remain Open Throughout
the Abeyance Period.
While OMS supports granting the Abeyance Request, the Commission should make clear
that all issues identified in the Show Cause Order remain open throughout the abeyance period.
The Commission should evaluate the resulting section 205 filings as a comprehensive package

rather than treating individual issues as resolved based on isolated proposals developed in the
stakeholder process and interim 205 filings.
OMS appreciates MISO and the MISO TOs’ acknowledgement that reforms are needed to
improve transparency and ensure that the costs of serving new large loads are assigned fairly. OMS
intends to work constructively within the MISO stakeholder process to help achieve meaningful
reforms and will remain engaged throughout the proceeding and thereafter.
Accordingly, the Commission should treat all issues relating to the interconnection of large
loads including increased transparency in the planning process, equitable cost assignment,
protection from cost shifting, and meaningful consideration of alternatives as unresolved until the
record developed during the full abeyance period – including all filings submitted in response to
MISO and the MISO TOs’ ultimate response to the Show Cause Order – is complete. Only then
will the Commission be positioned to determine whether the package of reforms adequately
addresses the concerns identified in its Show Cause Order. Continued stakeholder engagement
with FERC will be imperative at the end of the abeyance period to ensure a final check on the
holistic set of reforms has been appropriately addressed.
II.
CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, OMS respectfully requests that the Commission grant MISO
and the MISO TOs’ Motion to Hold Proceeding in Abeyance. In doing so, the Commission should
make clear that the purpose of the abeyance is to facilitate the development of comprehensive
reforms addressing all issues identified in the Show Cause Order and that no issue should be
deemed resolved until the Commission has had the opportunity to review the complete package of
stakeholder-developed proposals, section 205 filings, MISO and the MISO TOs’ final response to
the Show Cause Order, and the responses of interested parties thereto.
