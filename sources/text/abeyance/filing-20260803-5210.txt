I.
BACKGROUND
A.
The Show Cause Order
On June 18, 2026, the Commission issued the Show Cause Order, instituting a proceeding
under FPA section 206 based on the Commission’s preliminary finding that the NYISO’s Open
Access Transmission Tariff (“OATT” or “Tariff”)9 appears to be unjust, unreasonable, or unduly
discriminatory or preferential.10  The Commission issued the Show Cause Order as part of a suite
of six coordinated orders directed to each of the RTO/ISO regions subject to the Commission’s
jurisdiction.
The Show Cause Order includes preliminary findings that the NYISO’s Tariff appears
unjust and unreasonable in five principal respects:
1.  Transmission Service Study Process for Large Loads.  The Commission
preliminarily found that the NYISO’s Tariff lacks sufficiently clear and consistent provisions
addressing: (a) a definition of large load as a distinct category of load; (b) a mandatory application
process, readiness requirements, and study procedures for Eligible Customers seeking
transmission service on behalf of large loads; (c) study timelines of no more than 60-90 calendar
days; (d) ongoing operational requirements – including telemetry, data reporting, remote
disconnect capability, ramp rate and ride-through requirements, and protection systems – for
transmission customers serving large loads; (e) evaluation of alternative transmission technologies

A.
Cost Recovery and Transmission Service Charges
1.
Network Upgrade Cost Recovery
The Commission preliminarily found in the Show Cause Order that the NYISO Tariff
appears unjust and unreasonable because it lacks adequate mechanisms to mitigate the risk of cost
shifting among transmission customers.  First, the Commission noted the lack of Tariff provisions
requiring NYISO to post in a single location on the NYISO website certain Network Upgrade and
cost data and other related information.28  Second, the Commission pointed to the absence of a pro
forma cost recovery agreement between NYISO, the relevant Transmission Owner, and the
Eligible Customer taking transmission service on behalf of large loads that helps ensure that
Eligible Customers bear the risk and are ultimately responsible for costs incurred to provide
transmission service, including Network Upgrade costs.29  The Commission also contemplated the
possibility of Transmission Owners entering into cost recovery agreements with large load
customers themselves rather than the corresponding Eligible Customers.30
The NYTOs plan to coordinate with NYISO, NYPSC, the New York Department of Public
Service (“DPS”),31 and other interested stakeholders to develop a process for the Transmission
Owner to recover from the Eligible Customer or large load customer, as applicable, an allocable
share of the cost of Transmission Owner Interconnection Facilities and Network Upgrades.
In a single-state ISO like the NYISO, a state public utility commission is in a good position
to address cost allocation issues associated with upgrades to accommodate large loads.  Among
other factors, the NYPSC will be in a good position to balance various benefits provided by

The Commission observed that the large load interconnection agreement is not included in

are not standardized and clearly specified.

be acceptable to the Commission for implementation without having to address any conflicts and

The NYISO is responsible for the reliability of the New York bulk power system.56  The
NYTOs are responsible for the reliable operation of their systems, including local (non-bulk)

York State law concerning the reliable operation of their systems.  With potentially large increases

withdrawal of the load and for supply paired with load, or that is behind-the-meter generation and
requests Energy Resource Interconnection Service (“ERIS”) and/or Capacity Resource

large load customers and suppliers will be required to satisfy operating requirements allowing for
dispatch, ramping, curtailment, interruption, and/or disconnection on an automated, NYISO-
controlled or Connecting Transmission Owner/Affected Transmission Owner-controlled, and
remote basis, depending on the circumstances.59
