ANSWER OF THE
SOUTHWEST POWER POOL TRANSMISSION OWNER GROUP
Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s Rules and
Regulations,1 American Electric Power Service Corporation, as agent for Public Service Company
of Oklahoma and Southwestern Electric Power Company, the Evergy Companies, Oklahoma Gas
and Electric Company, and Xcel Energy Services Inc. on behalf of Southwestern Public Service
Company (collectively, the SPP TO Group) submit the following answer to the July 8, 2026
Motion of L.M. Marlowe for the Establishment of Settlement Procedures and Convening of a
Settlement Conference, or, in the Alternative, for the Appointment of a Settlement Judge (Motion).
The Commission should deny the Motion because it is premature and would profoundly disrupt
the Commission’s well-reasoned decision to address the challenge of large load additions on a
regional basis through show-cause orders directed to Southwest Power Pool, Inc. and its
Transmission Owners2 and in parallel proceedings simultaneously directed at other regions
(collectively, Show Cause Orders).3

The Motion is premature, and Movant’s attempts to preempt arguments to the contrary fail.
Movant cites several items involving “Federal action on the reliability of large-load integration”
as the basis for its position that settlement procedures are necessary, claiming that “[a] proceeding
addressing an active and recurring reliability emergency—one already the subject of the highest
tier of reliability alert and of federal emergency orders—is not one in which movement toward
resolution can reasonably be characterized as premature.”4  Among those federal actions, Movant
cites the Show Cause Orders.  But Movant’s request for a nationwide settlement proceeding in lieu
of regional Show Cause Order proceedings is not “movement toward resolution.”5  Rather, it is a
rejection of the Commission’s pointed choice to address large load issues on a regional basis, rather
than impose a rigid nationwide rule, and to allow regional stakeholders the opportunity to develop
consensus solutions through a transparent stakeholder process.6  A nationwide settlement
proceeding would actively distract from resolution of the issues, and, as discussed below, would
be both less transparent and substantially more burdensome on stakeholders than regional
proceedings.  The Commission should deny the Motion and should not consider alternatives to the
nascent Show Cause Order proceedings at this time.
There is also no basis to grant the Motion.  The Commission has discretion whether to
establish settlement procedures, hold a settlement conference, or appoint a settlement judge.  The
Commission “may convene a conference of the participants in a proceeding at any time for any
purpose related to the conduct or disposition of the proceeding,” 7 but nothing in the Commission’s
rules require that action, especially where settlement procedures would be counterproductive.

Likewise, Rule 603 states that the Commission may order the Chief Administrative Law Judge to
appoint a settlement judge, but is not obligated to do so.8
Movant fails to mention the material burdens that a nationwide settlement proceeding
would present.  Hundreds of parties have submitted comments in response to the ANOPR, the
overwhelming majority of whom are located outside of Washington, D.C.  Movant’s request would
require those parties to undertake the costly process of hiring local counsel to participate in
settlement proceedings at the Commission.9  It would also be impracticable, if not impossible, to
effectively manage settlement proceedings involving the hundreds of parties that participated in
the ANOPR proceedings.  Besides presenting physical difficulties, it would be extremely time
consuming to manage confidentiality requirements, information presentations, roll-taking, etc.
Movant’s proposal almost certainly would create a procedural headache and would not address the
region-specific concerns that are the focal point of these proceedings.
Movant also claims that settlement here “would allow the participants—the RTO/ISOs,
transmission owners, large-load and generation interests, State representatives, ratepayer
advocates, and intervenors such as Movant—to work toward mutually acceptable reliability,
observability, and cost-allocation methodologies.”10  However, Movant ignores that the Show
Cause Orders grant Transmission Owners and the relevant RTO/ISO the opportunity to seek a 90-
day abeyance to provide time to “work through the stakeholder processes to develop FPA section
205 filings to revise the Tariff to address the issues raised in this order.”11  Thus, the Show Cause

Orders already provide for the exact cooperation Movant claims to seek, without the extra

Finally, the Movant’s particular interest in the proposed settlement procedures appears to
be based in some vague claim to intellectual property rights.12  As Movant states, “Movant is the
author and rights-holder of a framework responsive to these questions, and seeks its
consideration.”13  That is no basis for Movant to request the mandatory participation of the entire
utility industry in her self-centered, proposed settlement proceeding.  Every Transmission Owner
and System Operator has its own preferred “framework responsive to these questions.”  There will
be plenty of regional stakeholder proceedings for the Movant to present her views without
imposing substantial direct costs and administrative burdens on others.  The Movant alleges no
cognizable, concrete, or particularized injury from the Commission’s interlocutory Show Cause
Orders that distinguishes the Movant from any other person or entity with a policy preference.  By
contrast, the public utilities that the Commission has actually directed to respond to the Show
Cause Orders are responsible for developing and implementing solutions to the directives in the
Show Cause Orders.
For the reasons set forth above, the SPP TO Group respectfully requests the Commission
deny the Motion.
