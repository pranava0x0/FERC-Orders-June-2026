PJM Proposes Updates that Directly Address the Commission’s RBP Cost
Allocation Concerns.
Under FPA section 205, cost allocation must be grounded in the principle that costs
are assigned to those who cause them or benefit from the facilities and services provided.12
At the same time, courts have long recognized that cost causation is not a standard of
mathematical precision.  Indeed, courts have long recognized that “it would no doubt be
impossible, even if desirable, to formulate a rate scheme with such precision that each
customer—or even customer group—is made to bear the exact cost of the service he
received.”13  Therefore, costs need not be “allocate[d] . . . with exacting precision.”14
Rather, they need only “be allocated in a manner that is roughly commensurate with the

The proposed approach also satisfies the Commission’s cost-causation standards
because it assigns costs in a manner that is roughly commensurate with the benefits

Commission “is not bound to reject any rate mechanism that tracks the cost causation
principle less than perfectly.”31
