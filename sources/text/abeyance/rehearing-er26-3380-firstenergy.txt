BACKGROUND
Because the September 29 Order is the culmination of an expedited process that unfolded
over approximately nine months, several major events are relevant to this request.  On January 16,
2026, the National Energy Dominance Council and the governors of all thirteen PJM states issued
the Statement of Principles, urging PJM to "[p]rovide 15-year price certainty for new capacity
resources" through a reliability backstop auction and to allocate the costs of new capacity to LSEs
with data centers that have not self-procured new capacity or agreed to be curtailable.  Shortly
thereafter the PJM Board announced its decision in the Critical Issue Fast Path ("CIFP") process
addressing large load additions, which included accelerating a backstop capacity procurement.  In
April the PJM Board initiated a targeted CIFP process to develop the RBP. On June 30, 2026, the
PJM Members Committee voted on thirteen RBP proposals. Only one—sponsored by a coalition
of Electric Distributors and the Data Center Coalition ("Joint Proposal")—received the two-thirds
sector-weighted support needed for endorsement.  PJM's own proposal did not.  Then on July 14,
2026, PJM announced that the 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction ("BRA") cleared short of the
PJM Region's reliability requirement.  In response, on July 27, 2026, the PJM Board adopted PJM's
proposal, with three "targeted changes," rather than the Joint Proposal.  PJM filed its RBP proposal

FirstEnergy explained that the predictable consequence of this flexibility is that sellers will

investors are generally willing to build new generation on a straight-line, fixed-value basis.28

B.
The Commission Erred in Finding PJM's Resource Eligibility and Selection
Rules Just and Reasonable Without Considering FirstEnergy's Evidence
Regarding the Reliability Value of Dispatchable Resources.
PJM justifies the RBP as an extraordinary measure necessary to address the approximately
6,831 MW capacity shortfall identified in the 2028/2029 BRA and to preserve resource adequacy
in the PJM Region.33  FirstEnergy demonstrated that, because the stated purpose of the
procurement is to address a reliability need, the Commission should ensure that it prioritizes
resources that provide firm and dependable capacity.34  FirstEnergy supported that showing with
record evidence from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation ("NERC"), which has
identified that conventional generators provide much of the essential reliability services that
support reliable operation of the grid; that wind and solar resources lack the spinning mass and
traditional excitation systems that provide voltage control and stability; and that dispatchable
generators provide system inertia, dynamic reactive support, and frequency response for stable
grid operation.35  FirstEnergy further showed that PJM's selection process is designed to select the
"least-cost set of resources" without sufficient consideration of the relative reliability value of
different resource types.36
The September 29 Order finds PJM's eligibility requirements and selection process just and
reasonable.37  It disposes of the argument that the selection process should value dispatchable
generation's reliability attributes in a single sentence, stating that the Commission does "not find
such additional valuation metrics to be necessary for PJM's proposal to be just and reasonable"

The Commission Erred in Finding PJM's Supplier Performance and Credit
Provisions Just and Reasonable Without Addressing the Risk of Post-

FirstEnergy demonstrated that the Commission should ensure that customers are
adequately protected against supplier non-performance over the full fifteen-year commitment

The Commission Erred in Accepting PJM's Demand Response Gating
Criteria Without Resolving Whether Eligible Demand Response Must Be
Supported by Binding Contractual Commitments.

resources appear to require only a contract or agreement evidencing a "plan or evidence of
deployed enabling infrastructure," not a contract or agreement demonstrating a clear and binding
commitment to provide the MWs sold into the RBP.59  If demand response resources may satisfy

Commission to require PJM to clarify that eligible demand response resources must be supported
by binding contractual commitments.61

help to deter speculative resources from bidding into the RBP auction and ensure that the selected
RBP Resources are those most likely to go into service."63  The September 29 Order's analysis of

grant rehearing of the September 29 Order on the issues specified above.  Specifically, the
Commission should reject PJM's RBP filing, proceed under the Section 206 proceeding it has
instituted in Docket No. EL26-108-000, and establish settlement procedures.  In the alternative,
and at a minimum, the Commission should: (1) reject PJM's levelized average-price framework;
(2) direct PJM to strengthen supplier performance assurance and credit requirements for the full
commitment term and to clarify that eligible demand response resources must be supported by
binding contractual commitments; (3) reject PJM’s shortfall charge proposal because it fails to
ensure that customers are adequately protected against supplier non-performance over the full
fifteen-year commitment term; (4) vacate its preliminary findings that PJM's eligibility
requirements and selection process are just and reasonable; and (5) expand the scope of the paper
hearing to encompass the arguments identified herein that the September 29 Order did not address.
