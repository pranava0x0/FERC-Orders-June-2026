A.
The Motion Is Inconsistent with the Commission’s Regional Approach
As stated in the SPP Show Cause Order, the “[i]ndividual show cause proceedings
will allow the Commission to ensure that solutions to the problems the Secretary [of the
Department of Energy] identified are tailored to the specific, varied circumstances and
market constructs of each region.”10 The Commission goes on to state that the Show Cause
Orders “reveals the many ways in which those orders are customized for each market’s
unique circumstances and progress (or lack thereof) towards serving large load…and
“leave room for each market to tailor operational requirements for large loads that are
particular to their region [] and otherwise account for incumbent regional differences on
topics such as cost allocation and cost transparency, study processes, and network upgrade
procedures..”11 The Commission found that “[p]roceeding via [the Show Cause Order]
allow the markets (and their respective transmission owners) to explain, in the first
instance, how to address the Commission’s concerns.” 12

B.
The Motion is Premature
The Commission has only recently instituted these proceedings. Pursuant to the
SPP Show Cause Order, SPP is currently evaluating the issues identified by the
Commission and preparing its response. The Commission specifically afforded SPP the
opportunity to utilize its stakeholder process to develop potential tariff revisions and, if
appropriate, request additional time to do so. That process has not yet run its course.
Because no proposed tariff revisions have been filed and no administrative record has been
developed, there is presently no concrete controversy suitable for settlement. Settlement
procedures are designed to facilitate resolution of identified disputes—not to substitute for
the initial development of proposals that may ultimately become the subject of Commission
review. Granting the Motion at this early stage would invert the ordinary sequence
contemplated by Sections 205 and 206 of the FPA by requiring settlement discussions
before parties have articulated the proposals that might ultimately require resolution.
Finally, Although Rules 601 and 603 of the Commission’s Rules of Practices and
Procedure authorize the Commission to convene settlement procedures or appoint a
settlement judge, those rules are discretionary rather than mandatory. The Motion identifies
no extraordinary circumstance warranting departure from the Commission's recently
established procedural framework. General concerns regarding reliability and large load
integration—issues that motivated issuance of the Show Cause Order itself—do not
disposition of the proceeding, including submission and consideration of offers of
settlement or the use of alternative dispute resolution procedures.”).
