SUMMARY
The instant proposal and PJM’s separate RBP initiative address the same reliability
problem: unprecedented growth in new large loads—chiefly, data centers—that has
outpaced new generation and transmission development and produced consecutive years
of capacity shortfalls in PJM’s BRAs.9 RBP seeks to cover the immediate shortfall, though
does so by means that are unjust and unreasonable in myriad ways.10 IRAS also seeks to
preserve regional reliability in the face of an unprecedented resource adequacy challenge.
The mechanism facilitates the interconnection of new large loads but requires (or should
require) that they make a choice: either bring new capacity sufficient to meet their needs
or (assuming appropriate state action) face curtailment when circumstances necessitate
load reductions.
Joint Consumer Advocates recognize and appreciate that PJM’s IRAS proposal
seeks to navigate a difficult course, and we support the general thrust of the proposal. If
implemented correctly, the approach could go far toward maintaining reliability without
either stopping the interconnection of non-capacity backed new large loads or socializing
the reliability consequences of non-capacity backed large-load growth. We submit that
neither of those options was likely to gain regulatory traction or, even more importantly, to
be consistent with PJM’s obligation to ensure resource adequacy.11 IRAS rightly strikes a

In short, when considered in context and against alternatives, IRAS is a flawed step
forward. The Commission can and should take steps to modify the proposal. FERC should
exercise its FPA sections 205 and/or 206 authority and find IRAS unjust and unreasonable
as a compensatory resource adequacy measure. PJM should instead be directed to resubmit
IRAS as requiring non-capacity backed new large loads to take non-firm transmission
service. Indeed, PJM’s tariff already contains non-firm transmission services that appear
consistent with the proposed curtailment priority to be accorded IRAS.18 Finally,
acceptance of an amended IRAS proposal as urged here renders PJM’s proposed RBP
duplicative and unnecessary. The Commission should couple acceptance of an amended
IRAS proposal with the rejection of the RBP.

II.
IRAS SHOULD HELP EASE THE DEMAND PRESSURES
PLAGUING THE BRA.
In planning and administering the regional transmission system, PJM seeks “to
meet the demands for firm transmission service, and to support competition, in the PJM
Region.”41 The misalignment of supply and demand driven by projected new large loads
is an impediment to competition. In a supply-constrained market, new entry cannot perform
its function of disciplining the market power of incumbent resources:42
[C]ontinuing to simply accept the interconnection of large
data center loads that cannot be served reliably because there
is not adequate dispatchable capacity, is not a reasonable
path forward and is not an efficient or competitive market
solution and is not a solution of any kind. That path leads to
continued shortfalls, increased reliability issues, continued
maximum prices, and continued calls to abandon markets.43
The answer is not to throw out the BRA baby with the new large load bathwater.
“[C]urrent data center issues [should not be] a pretext for redefining fundamental elements
of the PJM market design.”44 PJM’s IRAS proposal offers an (albeit flawed) market-based
solution for new large loads that seek to connect to the grid while providing a pathway for
