RE: Transmittal of NCAI Resolution #MEM-26-016 – Urgent Request for a FERC
Moratorium on Hyperscale Data Center Approvals Pending Comprehensive
Grid Reliability and Ratepayer Impact Assessments
Dear Chairman Swett:
On behalf of the National Congress of American Indians (NCAI), the oldest and
largest national organization composed of American Indian and Alaska Native Tribal
governments, I am writing to formally transmit NCAI Resolution #MEM-26-016,
"Calling for a Moratorium on Hyperscale Data Center Permits Pending
Comprehensive Environmental and Tribal Impact Assessment."
This resolution was enacted at the 2026 NCAI Mid Year Convention in Memphis,
Tennessee. It represents the unified stance of Tribal Nations regarding the rapid
proliferation of hyperscale data centers and their unprecedented impact on the bulk
power system and transmission infrastructure overseen by the Federal Energy
Regulatory Commission (FERC).
While Tribal Nations support responsible economic and digital development, we are
deeply concerned about the immense grid demands associated with hyperscale
data centers requiring 100 megawatts or greater of power continuously. The rapid
interconnection of these massive load centers places extreme pressure on regional
electrical grids, accelerates the need for costly interstate transmission upgrades,
and threatens bulk power system reliability. Furthermore, without proper regulatory
safeguards, the substantial costs of these infrastructure upgrades are frequently
shifted onto existing residential and Tribal ratepayers.
The implementation of Executive Order 14318, "Accelerating Federal Permitting of
Data Center Infrastructure" (2025) exacerbates these risks. By directing federal
agencies to fast-track infrastructure approvals through new National Environmental
Policy Act (NEPA) categorical exclusions and streamlined FAST-41 processes, the
directive bypasses mandatory, government-to-government Tribal consultation and
undermines thorough evaluation of the cumulative impacts on energy transmission
networks.

To uphold the federal trust responsibility and protect the integrity of the power grid, NCAI formally calls
upon the FERC to impose an immediate moratorium on the issuance of any related certificates,
transmission approvals, or regulatory authorizations supporting hyperscale data centers until a
comprehensive, independent impact assessment is completed.
Consistent with the FERC’s statutory authority over interstate electricity transmission, reliability, and
wholesale rates, we urge the Commission to ensure the following:
●​
Cost allocation and ratepayer protection: Before any transmission planning approvals or
cost-allocation agreements are finalized for data center loads, binding conditions must guarantee
that all grid and infrastructure upgrade costs attributable to data center demand are borne entirely
by the facility operators, not passed on to existing Tribal or residential ratepayers.
●​
Grid reliability and impact assessment: The FERC must require rigorous, independent, and
conflict-free assessments of how these facilities impact regional resource adequacy and
transmission congestion, ensuring transparent economic analysis of the true costs required to
support their operations.
●​
Mandatory Tribal consultation: The FERC must engage Tribal Nations as sovereign partners at
the outset of any transmission planning or interconnection process related to these
developments, ensuring that routing and infrastructure decisions do not negatively impact treaty
rights, cultural resources, or sacred sites.
The Commission has a legal and moral obligation to conduct genuine consultation before making
regulatory decisions that affect the economic and energy security of Tribal Nations. Proper respect for
these sovereign interests will result in a more reliable, just, and resilient energy grid for all Americans.
We welcome the opportunity to meet with you and the Commission to discuss the implementation of this
moratorium.
