I.
MOTION FOR LEAVE TO ANSWER
The Large Energy Users respectfully request leave to file this Answer.  Rule 213(a)(2)
generally prohibits answers to protests unless such answers have been authorized by the decisional
authority.2  However, the Commission exercises its discretion to grant motions for leave to submit
answers when doing so will clarify the issues, assist in the Commission’s decision-making
processes, or otherwise ensure an accurate and complete record.3
The Large Energy Users are a collection of ad hoc associations of large end-users of
electricity, including manufacturers and educational institutions operating on thin margins, that
consume substantial amounts of electricity at various facilities throughout the Midcontinent
Independent System Operator, Inc. (“MISO”) footprint.  The Large Energy Users are also some of
the largest employers in the MISO footprint that continue to face increased operational costs as a
result of longstanding increases in the cost of electric service.  The Large Energy Users will be
most directly affected by the FERC’s determinations in this proceeding regarding MISO’s
Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads Proposal (“IRR Proposal”).  The Large
Energy Users support reforms that ensure just and reasonable practices that avoid adverse negative
economic consequences and unnecessary regulatory burdens to existing large loads, especially
existing manufacturing and educational institutional loads that have not triggered the regulatory and
legislative efforts responding to concerns associated with high-impact, large computational loads
whose electrical characteristics present system risks.  The Large Energy Users worked diligently
with other stakeholders to achieve a compromise solution that MISO filed with FERC.  The

A.
MISO’s Grandfathering/Transition Proposal is Just and Reasonable and
Consistent with the June 18, 2026 Show Cause Order’s Objective to Protect
Existing Commercial Arrangements.
The Commission explicitly stated that the Show Cause Order4 was issued to determine
prospective reforms related to Large Load interconnections and was not intended to disrupt
existing commercial arrangements.  Further, the Commission instructed MISO to ensure that any
Tariff revisions accommodate existing commercial arrangements through a reasonable
implementation timeline.  The relevant part of the Show Cause Order states the following:
We recognize that large loads, including data centers, are actively
working with transmission owners and other relevant entities to
interconnect to, and to take service from, the transmission system.
We appreciate that different large loads are currently at different
stages of that process.  We also understand that large loads and
Eligible Customers taking transmission service on behalf of large
loads are negotiating, entering into, and/or have executed various
agreements related to interconnecting to the transmission system
and/or the provision of transmission service.  This proceeding is not
intended to disrupt existing commercial arrangements, but rather to
establish prospective reforms.  Accordingly, any Tariff revisions
offered by MISO and/or the Transmission Owners in response to
this proceeding should (a) propose a reasonable implementation
period to ensure minimal disruption to such existing commercial

In alignment with FERC’s directive, MISO’s IRR Proposal includes a grandfathering
provision which exempts two categories of Large Loads: 1) Large Loads with a Service
Commencement Date on or prior to December 4, 2026; and 2) Large Loads associated with
transmission projects either approved for MISO’s Transmission Expansion Plan (“MTEP”) 2026
or submitted for Expedited Project Review (“EPR”) prior to June 18, 2026.6  The former category
will be entirely exempt from reliability requirements, and the latter category will be exempt from
any applicable ride-through and ramp requirements.7  This grandfathering provision respects
existing customers and existing contracts and presents a reasonable timeline for agreements that
are in process to conclude before the implementation of new reliability requirements.
As emphasized by Google LLC: MISO’s “pragmatic approach” includes a “reasonable
grandfathering of existing customers in a manner that respects reliance interests and investments
made under the rules in effect at the time.”8  Further, the proposed grandfathering provisions
prevent adverse economic consequences and unnecessary regulatory burdens to Large Loads,
especially existing manufacturing and institutional loads that have not triggered the regulatory and
legislative efforts responding to concerns associated with high-impact, large computational loads.

B.
The Public Interest Organizations and Consumer Interest Advocates Have
Not Demonstrated that Existing Large Loads are Causing the Reliability
Concerns Targeted by the Show Cause Order.
Large manufacturers and industrial customers have existed within MISO’s footprint for
years, operating safely and reliably, and therefore those longstanding existing loads do not present
the same reliability concerns associated with the electrical characteristics and high-impact, speed-
to-power demands of certain large, emerging computational loads.  The North American Electric
Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) has highlighted numerous reliability challenges presented by
emerging Large Loads, and electrical characteristics of computational loads specifically.9
The PIOs accurately discuss the Commission’s cost-causation principle10 and assert that
Large Loads must pay for reliability costs they impose on the transmission system.11  However,
the PIOs do not provide evidence that the existing Large Loads comprised of long-standing
industrial customers and manufacturers are causing the reliability issues facing the electric grid
today.  The Show Cause Order is aimed at recent and exponential growth of Large Loads, not at
large customers who have been reliably participating in MISO for decades.  Further, the Consumer
Interest Advocates generically assert a reliability threat caused by existing customers but fail to

C.
Concerns about Cost Shifting Risk among Transmission Customers are More
Appropriately Addressed in the Future Filing Directed by FERC and Planned
by MISO.
The Large Energy Users appreciate the cost shifting and transparency concerns raised by
the PIOs and Consumer Interest Advocates; however, these concerns are more appropriately
addressed in a future filing specifically focused on cost shifting risk among transmission
customers. MISO has proposed a cost shifting framework in its Large Load Working Group13
(“LLWG”) and plans to formally address cost shifting in a future FERC filing.  Therefore, the
PIOs’ stated concern that the grandfathering provisions presented in the IRR Proposal may
“deprive electric consumers of the cost shifting and other consumer protections that MISO has
been directed to address in the Show Cause Order”14 is appropriately raised in MISO’s
forthcoming filing on that topic.  The instant proceeding is about new interconnection reliability
