I.
INTRODUCTION
Pursuant to Rule 212 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (“FERC” or
“Commission”) Rules of Practice and Procedure,1 Silver Run Electric, LLC (“Silver Run”)
respectfully submits this Motion for Abeyance in response to the Order Instituting Proceeding
Under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act, issued in the above-captioned proceeding on June
18, 2026.2  Silver Run seeks a 90-day full abeyance of the current 60-day deadline to respond to
all issues raised in the Show Cause Order so that Silver Run may have the additional time
coordinate with PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”) and relevant stakeholders to prepare an
FPA section 205 filing.  Silver Run further requests that the Commission shorten the answer
period for this Motion to five days and issue an order on this Motion by August 13, 2026, so that
Silver Run will have certainty regarding its obligations in advance of the August 17, 2026
deadline to respond to the Show Cause Order.

II.
ARGUMENT
In the Show Cause Order, the Commission states that it will consider requests for
abeyances of up to 90 days, which should include both (1) a robust description of the content of a
potential future filing under FPA section 205 and (2) a reasoned and specific explanation of
when such a filing is expected to be made.3
Silver Run seeks additional time to ensure it can develop comprehensive, coordinated
tariff amendments in accordance with the Commission’s directives.  The issues the Commission
has raised are complex and substantial.  Addressing the Show Cause Order will require
substantial revisions to the PJM Open Access Transmission Tariff (“Tariff”)—potentially
including new pro forma contract(s)—and the incorporation of study and application processes
tailored to the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers on behalf of large loads.
Silver Run must therefore closely coordinate with PJM and other PJM TOs as the parties draft
new provisions and, as applicable, revise existing provisions.  PJM is separately seeking an
abeyance to engage in its stakeholder process, which requires a corresponding motion for

A.
Proposed Tariff Revisions
The Show Cause Order sets out four preliminary findings.  Because the Commission is
addressing its concerns related to co-located load in the separate and ongoing FPA section 206
proceeding in Docket No. EL25-49-000, the Show Cause Order is limited to the integration of
large loads that are not co-located with generation, and it accordingly makes no preliminary
finding regarding the rates, terms, and conditions of service that apply to co-location
arrangements.6  First, the Commission preliminarily found that the PJM Tariff appears to be
unjust and unreasonable because it lacks provisions that address, with sufficient clarity and
consistency, how PJM and/or the Transmission Owners will timely study — that is, within 60 to
90 days of receiving the request — the provision of transmission service to Eligible Customers
on behalf of large loads.7  The Show Cause Order directs PJM and the Transmission Owners, to
the extent the matters addressed implicate aspects of the Tariff over which they hold the filing
rights, either to explain whether the Tariff remains just and reasonable without an application
process, study procedures, and ongoing operational requirements that address the unique

operational requirements applicable to Eligible Customers seeking transmission service on behalf

large load; readiness and information requirements; the evaluation of alternative transmission
technologies; a pro forma service agreement memorializing operational terms; transparency; cost
recovery, associated financial security, and rate treatment provisions intended to mitigate cost
shifting among transmission customers; and a reasonable implementation period and effective
date designed to minimize disruption to existing commercial arrangements.  These matters
remain under development and may evolve through coordination with PJM and stakeholders.
Silver Run will coordinate with PJM and complete internal approvals that may be necessary in
time to comply with the Commission’s directives by November 16, 2026.

III.
REQUEST FOR SHORTENED ANSWER PERIOD AND EXPEDITED
TREATMENT
Silver Run respectfully requests that the Commission shorten the answer period for this
Motion to five days and grant expedited treatment so that it may issue an order on this Motion by
August 13, 2026.  Rule 213(d)(1) of the Commission’s Rules of Practice and Procedure provides
that an answer to a motion must be made within 15 days after the motion is filed unless the
Commission orders otherwise.17  The Commission should order otherwise here, for three
reasons.
First, a shortened answer period and expedited action will provide regulatory certainty in
advance of a deadline that is now imminent.  The Show Cause Order directs PJM and the PJM
TOs to respond within 60 days of issuance, which is August 17, 2026—14 days from the date of
this filing.18  If the ordinary 15-day answer period applies, the Commission will not be in a
position to rule on this Motion until after Silver Run’s response is already due.  Silver Run would
then be required to divert resources to preparing a merits response to the Show Cause Order
rather than to the coordinated work with PJM and stakeholders that is necessary to produce the
