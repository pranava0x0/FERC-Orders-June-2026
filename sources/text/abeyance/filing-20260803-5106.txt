ANSWER IN SUPPORT OF
THE PJM INDUSTRIAL CUSTOMER COALITION
TO THE MOTIONS FOR EXTENSION AND ABEYANCE
BY PJM INTERCONNECTION, L.L.C.

Pursuant to Rule 213 of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“Commission”)
Rules of Practice and Procedure and the requests for a shortened answer period of PJM
Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”),1 PJM Industrial Customer Coalition (“PJMICC”) hereby files
this Answer in support of PJM’s Motion for Extension of Time for Specified Compliance
Requirements and Request for Expedited Treatment of PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. in Docket No.
EL25-49, et al. (“Co-Located Load Extension Motion”) and Motion of PJM Interconnection,
L.L.C. to Hold Proceeding in Abeyance and Request For Shortened Answer Period and Expedited
Treatment in Docket No. EL26-67 (“Large Load Abeyance Motion”), both of which were filed on

July 28, 2026.2 These Motions both request that the Commission extend certain PJM filing
deadlines by 90 days.
In its Co-Located Load Extension Motion, PJM explains: “The compliance changes
directed in this proceeding significantly relate to or overlap with the directives in the Large
Load Order in three major areas: expansion of new transmission services, study procedures, and
operational requirements.”3 To facilitate the stakeholder process and to allow for coordination on
the Co-Located Load docket and the Large Load docket, PJM seeks a parallel ninety-day extension
for both proceedings, which will result in filings by mid-November.4
PJM further states:
Given the interrelated nature of a number of the other requirements in the
Compliance Order and Large Load Order, a coordinated approach on the
remaining compliance matters is appropriate for consistency and efficiency.
As noted above, both orders require PJM to propose Tariff changes to address the
study procedures to interconnect Co-Located/Large Loads, make new transmission
services available for customers that are willing and able to limit their withdrawals,
and specify operational requirements for Co-Located/Large Loads. PJM proposes
to address these interrelated requirements in a suite of filings to be submitted by
early to mid-November following a period of intensive stakeholder engagement and
feedback.5

PJMICC agrees with PJM’s proposed approach, which facilitates administrative
efficiencies and helps consolidate stakeholder and party resources and work efforts.6 PJMICC

believes that such a 90-day time frame is proportionate to the reforms the Commission is pursuing.
The Commission, PJM, and many stakeholders have been working hard to ensure all stakeholder
voices and perspectives are considered. Many of these participants have raised nuanced but
important issues that include significant downstream effects of the Commission’s major orders in
these dockets. To execute on the Commission’s reforms in a logical, coherent, and durable manner,
these nuanced issues must be addressed thoroughly and carefully in a coordinated fashion.
In summary, PJMICC agrees with PJM that the above-captioned proceedings raise
complex issues that are necessarily interrelated and that require significant analysis. In pursuit of
durable final orders that provide the industry with regulatory stability and clear direction toward
investment, growth, and resource adequacy, PJM’s plan to ensure robust discussion within the
PJM stakeholder process—and adequate time to ensure both proceedings are complementing and
not contradicting one another—is reasonable. Even a 90-day extension is still potentially an
aggressive timeline, but it better strikes the balance between the FERC’s urgent call for reforms
and the need for time to create a durable compliance filing. PJMICC therefore respectfully requests
that the Commission grant PJM’s Co-Located Load Extension Motion and Large Load Abeyance
Motion.
